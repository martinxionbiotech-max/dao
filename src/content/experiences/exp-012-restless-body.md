## Why this record matters

This is the archive's record of the **body's refusal to be still** — the phenomena that arrive when sitting gets established enough that the body, not just the mind, is the obstacle. The [lotus-pain record](/experiences/exp-010-lotus-pain/) covered the posture; this one covers what happens *inside* a working sit: the flung hand, the restlessness wave, the phlegm, the head pressure, and the flat period when the practitioner stops wanting to practice at all. It is also the archive's cleanest case of **practice-convergence with explanation-divergence**: the teacher's operational advice (ease off the breath work, increase exercise, practice in waves, don't force) aligns with modern periodization and over-breathing management — while his explanatory frame (habit-energy, expelling waste, blood pressure) is the tradition's own. The [three-languages](/blog/three-languages/) discipline keeps both halves visible, and the [restlessness problem page](/problems/restlessness-in-sitting/) is built on this record.

## The record (paraphrased, anonymized)

- **The reports:** a hand flung outward suddenly during sitting (recurring in sleep); restlessness arriving around the 25th minute — "not really pain, just wanting to move"; thick dark-yellow phlegm over a period without sore throat; head swelling after breath practice; and a stretch of aversion to sitting, sutra-reading, and practice generally, with the question "am I finished?"
- **The teacher's counsel, in its own terms:** the moving body is habit-energy — prostrations exist to exhaust it, and at the right point the body asks for stillness on its own; the 25-minute restlessness is what everyone starts with — do not hurry; the phlegm is the bronchia and lungs expelling accumulated waste — a good sign, spit it all out; the head swelling is rising blood pressure — ease off the breath practice and increase exercise to build heart and lung capacity; and for the aversion, the lute string: too tight, it snaps; too loose, it is mute — practice for a stretch, rest for a stretch, slowly.
- **The practice-adjacent note recorded:** a practitioner's observation that saliva flows when the mind is still — while another reported it at the start, when mind and breath were still coarse; the teacher's standing frame (from this series) being that beginners watch the breath through its stages without forcing.

## The three layers, kept apart

The practitioner reports (alarm at unexplained phenomena), the traditional framework (habit-energy, purgation, blood pressure, the lute string), and the alternative reading (benign relaxation movements, postural restlessness, the medical check for unexplained phlegm, over-breathing effects, motivational fatigue) are each reported as given. What the site carries forward is the **convergence**: rest between sessions, don't force the breath, keep the body's condition in view — the same operational core the [sitting protocol](/tools/sitting-protocol/) already teaches from the classical side.
