## Why this record matters

This is the archive's record of the **series' opening episode** — the one where the teacher is asked who he is, and answers with an origin story, a timetable, a retreat record, and a refusal. The site keeps it for four reasons: it is the source episode for the whole series the archive draws on; the origin story (a doctor's son, a hospital childhood, the fear of dying badly, a mantra tried on a sick body) is the tradition's most human entrance — no enlightenment, just a boy who did not want to die the way the patients did; the schedule is the archive's only full-time-table record of what a monastic sitting life actually looks like hour by hour; and the lineage refusal ("to name a school is to fall into discrimination") is the boundary-behavior the [source policy](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/) is built to respect — the site records the refusal, and does not resolve it.

## The record (paraphrased, anonymized)

- **The report:** his own account of the path — mantra, Buddha-name samādhi, huatou — and of its beginning in a hospital corridor.
- **The timetable:** 3 a.m. rise, hours of prostrations (864 full-body on his own schedule), one to three hours of sitting around liturgies, no breakfast, evening liturgy to 9, sitting to midnight, sleep at 10 — and in mountain retreats, sixteen hours of sitting a day.
- **The retreat record:** one tomato and one carrot a day; at peak, twenty hours of sitting with one hour of pacing per five; no sleep after the first days; at least forty-nine days indoors.
- **The refusal:** no lineage, no face, no money; "a hundred-flower mixed boxing"; judge the boxing, not the boxer.
- **The three layers, kept apart:** the self-report, the traditional frame (the stages named in his own terms), and the alternative reading (self-report ≠ finding; the intensive schedule's risks are documented on the site's [research](/research/farias-adverse-events-2020/) and [problem](/problems/breath-stopping-anxiety/) pages; the refusal is respected, not investigated).
