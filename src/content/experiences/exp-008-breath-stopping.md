**Anonymized note (paraphrased from a public forum thread).** A practitioner reports that during deep sitting the breath became progressively finer and slower, until at its subtlest it appeared to stop altogether — without any strain, air hunger, or sense of holding. The state ended when the practitioner chose to end it, and the question posed to the community was simply: is this normal?

**The community's distinction (as recorded).** The replies drew the line that the tradition itself draws: a breath that *subsides on its own* in stillness is read as a sign of depth — while *deliberately holding* the breath is warned against. The former is welcomed, the latter discouraged.

**Why this note matters.** The 'stopped breath' report sits on a boundary the knowledge base exists to mark. The tradition has a name for its positive pole — the Zhuangzi's 'the true person breathes from the heels' (真人之息以踵) — and a warning for its negative pole: forced breath control as the classic route to harm. The modern reading adds the obvious mechanism: deep relaxation lowers metabolic demand, and very shallow breathing can be experienced as cessation. All three readings are recorded; the safety floor is the community's own — no forcing, no holding, and anything that feels like strain is not this phenomenon.
