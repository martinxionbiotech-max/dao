## Why this record matters

Most of the entries in this archive describe what happens on the cushion: pain, breath, dullness, unusual states, the slow discovery that attention has wandered again. This one disputes the frame from its first line. It is not a report of an experience at all — it is a credo, a few hundred characters long, and its claim is that the interesting part of practice happens in the middle of an argument with a colleague.

That makes it unusually easy to test. You cannot check whether another person entered 定. You can check whether they kept their word when keeping it became inconvenient. The essay bets everything on that difference, and the bet is worth recording even where the reasoning underneath it is thin.

## The reflection

> Through studying the high-dimensional knowledge of Kunlun Righteous Culture I have come to see that one should not make cultivation out to be very mysterious. Cultivation is not simply sitting in Chan meditation, nor an obsession with chanting mantras and reciting sutras. Nor is it the cultivation of some secret method of divine power in pursuit of immortality, or the seeking of supernatural abilities. Cultivation is the continual correction of one's own wrong thoughts and behaviour. In daily life and work, in interpersonal relationships, constantly notice the arising of one's own thoughts, and promptly correct one's bad thoughts, words and deeds. Through the experience of all kinds of people, matters and things, train and temper one's abilities.
>
> Be kind to others, be glad to help others, do not close off your cognition, be willing to accept new knowledge, be willing to keep learning and raising your cognition. Establish a correct outlook on life, on values, on the universe. Ultimately cultivate a heart of great love, a heart of selfless dedication — be willing to serve the public, to serve society and the country.
>
> — Lingen, 3 November 2026

The original Chinese, as contributed:

> 我通过学习昆仑正义文化高维知识认识到，不要把修行看的很神秘，修行并不是简单的参禅打坐，也不是痴迷于诵咒念经！也不是修炼什么神功秘法想长生不老或求神通功能。修行就是不断的修正自己错误的思想和行为！在日常生活工作中，人际关系中，时时察觉自己的起心动念，并及时修正自己不好的思想与言行。通过各种人事物的体验和历练自己各种能力。
>
> 与人为善，乐于助人，不封闭自己的认知，愿意接纳新知识，愿意不断学习提升自己的认知。建立正确的人生观，价值观，宇宙观。最终修一颗大爱心，修无私奉献之心！愿意为大众服务，为社会和国家服务

## 1. The test is moved from the cushion to the transaction

The essay opens with three refusals and one assertion. Refused: sitting meditation as the definition; mantra and sutra recitation as the definition; secret methods aimed at immortality or supernatural power. Asserted: cultivation is the continual correction of wrong thought and behaviour.

Read carelessly, that sounds like a dismissal of practice. It is better read as a relocation of the test. The question a practitioner should ask is not *what state did I reach* but *what did I do to the person in front of me*. That is a different criterion, and a stricter one, because states are private and conduct is not.

The classical tradition supports the relocation without supporting the dismissal. 《大学》 makes cultivation the root of everything that follows: 自天子以至于庶人，壹是皆以修身为本 — from the Son of Heaven down to the common person, all alike take the cultivation of the self as the root. 《论语·学而》 gives the daily audit: 吾日三省吾身 — three questions, and all three are about conduct toward others, none about states of mind. 《孟子·离娄上》 states the rule for failure: 行有不得者，皆反求诸己 — when nothing you do works, turn back and look for the cause in yourself. 《论语·里仁》 adds the social version: 见贤思齐焉，见不贤而内自省也 — when you meet someone better, aim to equal them; when you meet someone worse, look inward.

And the 坛经 puts the location beyond doubt: 佛法在世间，不离世间觉；离世觅菩提，恰如求兔角 — the Dharma is in the world; awakening is not apart from the world; to seek bodhi apart from the world is like looking for horns on a rabbit. 《道德经》 ch. 54 gives the same priority in a single line: 修之于身，其德乃真 — cultivate it in yourself, and the virtue becomes real — with family, village, state and world as later tests rather than earlier ones. None of these texts says sitting is useless. They say the audit happens in the transaction, not only on the mat.

The essay's own formula is stronger than the classical one in one respect. The Confucian audit is retrospective — 三省 at the end of the day. The essay moves it forward, to 时时 — constantly, at the moment of arising. That is a harder instruction, and it is the one the rest of the text depends on.

## 2. Noticing the arising is the whole method

The operational sentence is 时时察觉自己的起心动念. Everything else in the essay is downstream of it: you cannot correct what you have not noticed, and most of what needs correcting has already been spoken by the time a daily audit gets to it.

The sharpest classical statement of why the moment of arising is the place to work is in 《传习录》:

> 一念发动处，便即是行了。

*At the moment a thought is set in motion, that is already action.*

Wang Yangming's point is not that thinking is as bad as doing. It is that the moral quality of an act is settled before the hand moves — the intention is already the deed in its first form — so the place to intervene is the arising, not the aftermath. The essay says *correct the bad thought*. Wang says something more precise: notice that the thought is already the act, which is why it cannot be deferred to later.

The method has a name in Chan, and it is not a flattering one. 神秀's verse in the 坛经:

> 身是菩提树，心如明镜台。时时勤拂拭，勿使惹尘埃。

*The body is the bodhi tree, the mind a bright mirror-stand; wipe it diligently at all times, and let no dust settle.*

时时勤拂拭 is the essay's 时时察觉 almost word for word. The verse is famous for being surpassed — 慧能's reply, 本来无一物，何处惹尘埃, is the sudden path, and the platform sutra presents it as the deeper answer. That contrast is worth stating rather than smoothing over, because the essay is unmistakably a 渐修 document. It assumes there is dust, that the dust can be seen, and that seeing it is a skill improved by repetition. The sudden path makes a different wager: that the mirror was never stained, and the work is to see that once. A reader may prefer either. What cannot be done is to hold both without noticing that they disagree about what practice is *for*.

## 3. The refusals: what they hit, and what they miss

Two of the three refusals are aimed at real targets.

长生 and 神通 are not straw men. The Daoist canon contains texts whose stated aim is 长生久视 — long life and enduring vision — and the 内丹 literature is a technology of transformation with an elaborate technical vocabulary. The 庄子's 藐姑射之山神人, who 不食五谷，吸风饮露, is a literary image rather than a manual, but it has been read as a model. And the contemporary market in 神通 — clairvoyance, healing at a distance, the reading of past lives — is large enough that a teacher insisting practice is conduct is making a useful intervention.

The first refusal is different in kind. 不是简单的参禅打坐，也不是痴迷于诵咒念经 is not aimed at a fraud; it is aimed at the ordinary disciplines. Here the essay's *not this* needs care, because it is not what the tradition says. Chan is a sitting tradition — 坐禅 is the name of the platform sutra's central chapter. Daoist practice includes 诵经, 存思, 服气 and 心斋. The essay's refusal is a corrective emphasis: a claim about what those practices are *for*, not a claim that they are worthless.

《庄子·知北游》 makes the underlying point coarsely and unforgettably. Asked where the Dao is, Zhuangzi answers 在蝼蚁 — in the ants; pressed, 在稊稗 — in the weeds; pressed again, 在瓦甓 — in the tiles; and finally 在屎溺 — in shit. The point is not vulgarity. It is that no domain is excluded, which is the same claim the essay makes when it moves practice into the office.

The distinction matters practically. If the sitting and the chanting are dropped, what trains the attention that 觉察 requires? The classical answer is that 定 is the base of 慧 — the cushion is where attention is trained under conditions simple enough to notice it wandering. Remove the training and the moment of arising is likely to be noticed, if at all, an hour late. The essay does not say to drop them. But a reader in a hurry will hear it that way, and that is the cost of a short text.

## 4. The standard the essay never states

The weakest word in the essay is 错误. Wrong according to what?

The text asks for two things that pull against each other: correct yourself according to the right standard, and do not close off your cognition to new knowledge. If the standard is supplied by the surrounding group — family, employer, the current consensus — then correction becomes conformity, and *do not close your cognition* is the first thing that gets corrected. That is the failure mode of every moral programme that begins with self-correction and never says who holds the ruler.

The essay does not resolve this, and it is worth being explicit that three readings are available rather than pretending the text chooses:

- **A conventionalist reading**: the standard is the group's, and the essay is advice about fitting in better. This contradicts the second paragraph, so it is probably not what the author means.
- **An intuitionist reading**: the standard is felt — 良知 — and the work is to stop overriding it. This is close to Wang Yangming, and it fits 起心动念 better than any rulebook, because a rulebook cannot operate at the speed of a thought.
- **A consequential reading**: the standard is harm. Does this thought, spoken aloud, increase it? This is the reading that survives contact with ordinary life, and the one the essay's own examples — 与人为善, 乐于助人 — point toward.

建立正确的人生观，价值观，宇宙观 is the sentence that would need the most work. It states that a correct outlook exists and should be built; it does not say how one tells a correct outlook from an incorrect one, or what to do when two people who each believe they are correcting themselves disagree. That is the work left undone, and it is not a small piece of work.

## 5. Service, and the failure mode of 无私奉献

The closing movement — 大爱心, 无私奉献, 为大众服务 — is the part most likely to be admired and least likely to be examined.

The classical resonance is real. 《道德经》 ch. 8: 上善若水，水善利万物而不争. Ch. 81: 圣人不积，既以为人，己愈有；既以与人，己愈多. Ch. 67 lists 慈 first among the three treasures. But note what ch. 7 says about heaven and earth: 以其不自生，故能长生 — precisely because they do not live for themselves, they endure. The logic is not self-erasure. It is that holding the self lightly is what preserves it. Read that way, 无私 is a way of carrying the self, not a way of spending it to nothing.

The distinction matters because 无私奉献 in ordinary use has a known failure mode: service without boundary, offered until the giver is depleted, then resentment, then withdrawal. The essay's own first paragraph guards against one version of this — 不封闭自己的认知, keep learning — but that guard is about cognition, not capacity. A person can be perfectly open-minded and still be exhausted.

There is also a small translation issue in the last clause. 为社会和国家服务 is modern civic vocabulary. The classical register would be 天下 or 众生 — wider than the state and not identical with it. This is not a fault in an essay written in contemporary Chinese. But it is worth noticing which words are classical and which are not, because the difference marks where the text is modern.

## 6. The frame the essay comes in

The essay attributes its understanding to 昆仑正义文化 and to what it calls 高维知识. This site does not assess that teaching, does not endorse it, and has no way to verify the cosmology behind the phrase. Saying so is not a dismissal; it is the boundary this archive keeps between a practitioner's frame and a finding.

What is worth noticing is that the argument does not need the frame. Strip the first clause and the rest stands: correct your conduct, notice what arises, keep learning, be useful. A reader who accepts the cosmology and a reader who rejects it can run the same experiment and will get the same evidence — which is the mark of a practice instruction as opposed to a metaphysical claim.

## What this does not say

- It is not the site's position. It was contributed by Lingen and published with permission; the argument is the author's.
- It does not say that seated meditation, chanting or study are worthless. It says they are not the definition — and the essay's own logic requires something that trains attention.
- It does not say that illness, poverty or misfortune are the result of wrong thinking. The essay does not claim this, and the claim would be false.
- It does not endorse 昆仑正义文化 or the phrase 高维知识.
- It does not claim that self-correction is a substitute for changing unjust conditions. 反求诸己 is a discipline for the person; it is not an account of the world.
