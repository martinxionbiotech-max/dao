Thinking and feeling are the six senses' grasping — only at the third dhyāna do they vanish, leaving only the two feelings of suffering and joy. Without concentration practice there is no liberation; reciting the Buddha-name and good deeds alone gain the lowest grade of rebirth in the Pure Land. The first dhyāna requires sessions of four hours — but do not rush, haste makes waste. The four dhyānas have depth: the fourth splits into two — its first half holds only the joy-mind and nothing else, sit until death in theory; the third still has the feeling of suffering, and unrest and agitation arise after long sitting, ending the session; the fourth has no such unease. Above the four come the four formless concentrations (boundless space, boundless consciousness, nothingness, neither-perception-nor-non-perception) — nine in all for ordinary people, ten counting the fourth's split; breaking through the last makes one an arhat. Then the arhat's nine dhyānas extinguish the nine discriminative minds and make a first-stage bodhisattva; the ten-stage prajñāpāramitās lead to the equal-enlightenment bodhisattva and then Buddhahood. The deepest of all is the cessation concentration — the Buddha's state — 'I am not a Buddha; I do not know what one sees there.' 'Being always in concentration — I have not achieved it myself; I still enter and leave, and can only answer from the sutras' theory.' An arhat's knowledge of former lives (five hundred lives each way) still requires entering concentration to see. An ordinary person's utmost in this human world is the fourth dhyāna; the rest is learned in the Pure Land or Tusita heaven"

## Why this record matters

This is the archive's record of the teacher's **complete dhyāna map** — the taxonomy the other episodes take for granted, here given in one answer: the definition of sitting and entry, the nine (or ten) concentrations, the arhat and bodhisattva stages, and the four-hour threshold. The site keeps it for three reasons: it documents a living tradition's ladder in the practitioner's own terms; its two closing honesties ("I have not achieved always-in-concentration"; "I am not a Buddha; I do not know what one sees there") are exactly the register the [source policy](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/) values — and they stand in deliberate contrast with the same teacher's hard line on posture in [EXP-014](/experiences/notes/exp-014-double-lotus-doctrine/); and the four-hour threshold is the concrete claim against which the site's own [session-length guidance](/experiences/questions/how-long-should-i-sit/) positions itself.

## The record (paraphrased, anonymized)

- **The reports:** the standing questions of the series' readers — what concentration is, whether it transcends consciousness, the four dhyānas' sensations, how long to sit, whether one can skip sitting and still be liberated.
- **The teacher's map, in its own terms:** sitting is the posture observing thought; entry is suddenly forgetting the body into a comfortable, doubly-sensed state; thought and feeling vanish only at the third dhyāna; four hours a session for the first dhyāna; the fourth dhyāna splits in two, its first half only joy, the third still carrying suffering's unrest; four formless concentrations follow — nine for ordinary people, and breaking the last makes an arhat; then the arhat's nine, then the bodhisattva stages; the deepest is the cessation concentration, the Buddha's own — and the teacher's two honesties close the ladder.
- **The three layers, kept apart:** the questions, the map, and the alternative reading (the taxonomy as the teacher's doctrine, recorded not adopted; the modern research typologies as the site's other register; the site's own session-length guidance; the episode's honesty kept as its distinguishing feature).
