## Why this record matters

This is the archive's record of the series' **breath-first episode** — the one where the teacher's whole system narrows to a single instruction, practiced all day. It is kept for three reasons: the all-day breath doctrine (not one or two hours, but walking, standing, sitting and lying — until the body stays slightly warm and the heat reaches the toes) is the series' standing answer to every fatigue-and-pain question, and the site records it as the teacher's system, not as fact; the episode's one hard number — **4 to 6 breaths per minute as the completion standard of the abdominal breath** — is the series' clearest quantified threshold, and the site flags its coincidence with modern slow-breathing research without adopting the channel theory that frames it; and the episode's moderate corners (eat more, the afternoon nap is a good habit, legs may hurt but dullness must stop the sit) are the series at its most convergent with ordinary prudence — the same convergence the site marks wherever it appears, from the [one-remedy episode](/experiences/notes/exp-019-one-remedy-body/) to the [prostrations episode](/experiences/notes/exp-021-prostrations-channel-circuit/).

## The record (paraphrased, anonymized)

- **The reports:** exhaustion after stopping the sits, with muscle soreness slow to recover; wet-cold knees and a lumbar-disc history; a heat sensation moving from the right sole through the body; dizziness and cold sweats on waking; breathing slowed from 12 to 4-6 per minute in a ninety-minute sit; images of the moon, sun and mountains seen in dullness — the moon ringed with dark clouds; and a full practice report (double lotus 35-40 minutes, mantra-walking with 200-300 recitations, 120-200 prostrations daily, three months of wavering before the meat vow settled, wandering thoughts reduced from continuous sheets to discontinuous patches).
- **The answers, in their own terms:** practice the breathing all day, everywhere — not one or two hours — until the body stays slightly warm and the heat current reaches the toes, and maintain that state; this brings energy and repairs the knees; the fatigue is glucose and oxygen spent on repair; the prayer wheel is a useful expedient, one turn as one recitation, keeping the mind from wandering; four to six breaths a minute means the abdominal breath is complete — the completed breath clears the consciousness even when sitting tired or with a headache, so try sitting two hours; in long sits the legs may hurt but dullness must stop the sit; eat more, two good meals a day; the afternoon nap is a good habit; and the key to opening the channels is the breath — practice until the body stays slightly warm, and one is then close.
- **The three layers, kept apart:** the reports; the teacher's framework (the all-day breath doctrine, the 4-6 standard, the heat-to-toes criterion, the glucose-oxygen repair theory); and the alternative reading — the slow-breathing convergence flagged without endorsement, the lay-physiology labeled, the moderate corners kept as ordinary prudence, the heat and imagery cross-linked to the site's [warmth](/experiences/questions/warmth-tingling-when-sitting/) and [light](/problems/seeing-light-in-sitting/) pages.
