## Why this record matters

The entries around this one are about what happens to a person who sits: pain, breath, dullness, the discovery that attention has wandered again. This one is about what happens between people. It is a charter rather than a confession — short, blunt, and written by someone who has watched a practice group long enough to know its characteristic failures.

That makes it a different kind of document, and worth keeping for a different reason. A group's technique is easy to describe and hard to fake; its manners are easy to fake and hard to describe. This note describes them.

## The reflection

> The immortal path is vague and distant; the human path is right underfoot. Start from life, take the cultivation of the mind as the foundation, and build the ground the practice stands on. We may discuss practice appropriately, but we should not quarrel — except in special circumstances, let cheerfulness be the rule. Today fellow practitioners attend only to 修身 and ignore 德行, which is to sacrifice the root for the branch. We welcome actual practice. Since we practise together, everyone is equal; do not be fond of teaching others, do not seek the spotlight and grow smug, and do not be hypocritical and eager to ingratiate.
>
> — Admin, 4 November 2026

The original Chinese, as contributed:

> 仙道飘渺，而人道就在脚下，坚持以生活入手，修心为基，筑修行之地基，我们可适当讨论修行，切莫争吵，除非特殊情况一般以开心为主，如今同修道友只顾修身，不管德行，实在是舍本逐末。我们欢迎实修既然同修大家一起修，人人平等，切莫好为人师，好喜出头洋洋得意，虚伪之心喜献殷勤

## 1. The path is not far from people

The opening contrast — 仙道飘渺, 人道就在脚下 — reads like a piece of practical advice, and it is. But it is also a very old argument stated in four characters.

《中庸》 puts it without ornament:

> 道不远人。人之为道而远人，不可以为道。

*The path is not far from people. To make a path that distances itself from people is not the path.*

The target there was not immortality but the habit of making the Way into something remote — a thing for specialists, for the past, for another order of being. The reply is that a path defined by distance from ordinary people is not a path at all. 《论语·述而》 makes the same move about virtue: 仁远乎哉？我欲仁，斯仁至矣 — is benevolence far off? I want it, and it is here.

So the note's first sentence is not anti-mystical. It relocates the test, which is a different and more defensible operation. It does not say that no immortal path exists. It says that whatever that path is, the one you can actually walk is the one under your feet, and a practitioner who cannot walk that one has no business discussing the other.

## 2. Technique without conduct is 舍本逐末 — and the tradition agrees

The central complaint is that fellow practitioners 只顾修身，不管德行, and that this is 舍本逐末 — sacrificing the root for the branch.

It would be easy to hear this as a Confucian objection to a Daoist group. It is not. The sharpest statement of it comes from a Daoist alchemist, in 《抱朴子·对俗》:

> 欲求仙者，要当以忠孝和顺仁信为本。若德行不修，而但务方术，皆不得长生也。

*One who seeks to become an immortal should take loyalty, filial piety, harmony, compliance, benevolence and trustworthiness as the root. If virtue is not cultivated and one attends only to techniques, one will not attain long life.*

That is 葛洪 — a man writing a manual of alchemy and elixirs — saying that the techniques do not work without the conduct. The note's complaint is not a moralist's addition to the tradition. It is the tradition's own warning, made by the person most invested in the techniques.

《大学》 supplies the image the note uses: 物有本末，事有终始，知所先后，则近道矣 — things have roots and branches, affairs have ends and beginnings; to know what comes first and what after is to be near the path.

There is, though, a terminological slide in the note that is worth naming, because it is the one place where the argument is weaker than it looks. In the classical vocabulary, 修身 already includes moral cultivation — 《大学》's 自天子以至于庶人，壹是皆以修身为本 is a programme of character, family and state, not of the body and its energies. The sentence 只顾修身，不管德行 is therefore only coherent if 修身 has been narrowed to mean body work and technique. That narrowing is real — it is what the word has come to mean in a great deal of contemporary practice culture — but it is a modern development, and the note is describing it rather than the classical term. The cure it proposes is, in effect, the word's older meaning.

## 3. Three vices, three classical answers

The last sentence names three behaviours, and each has an exact counterpart in the classical texts. This is worth setting out, because it shows that the note's instincts are older than its vocabulary.

**好为人师.** 《孟子·离娄上》: 人之患在好为人师 — the trouble with people is that they like to be others' teachers. Note the word 患: Mencius lists it among the things that damage a person, not merely among the things that are rude. What the line condemns is the *liking* to teach, not teaching as such. A person asked a question and answering it is not 好为人师; a person who cannot be in a room without correcting it is.

**好喜出头，洋洋得意.** 《论语·子路》: 君子泰而不骄，小人骄而不泰 — the noble person is composed without arrogance; the small person is arrogant without composure. The contrast is not between confidence and humility but between two kinds of ease: one that does not need an audience, and one that does.

**虚伪之心，喜献殷勤.** 《论语·学而》: 巧言令色，鲜矣仁 — clever words and a pleasing face are rarely benevolent. The target is not politeness but performed warmth: the ingratiating manner that substitutes for the thing it imitates.

Three vices, then, and all three are failures of the same kind: they are ways of using other people as a mirror. The person who must teach, the person who must shine, and the person who must be liked are all asking the room for something. The note's remedy — 人人平等, practise together — is a way of taking the mirror away.

## 4. The two norms that pull against each other

Here the note becomes a charter, and charters have to be judged by what they make possible rather than by what they intend.

It asks for two things at once: correct the conduct of the group, and do not quarrel; do not set yourself up as a teacher, and do not lecture. Remove quarrelling and remove teaching, and what remains is 以开心为主. That is a good tone for a group and a weak instrument for correction. The person whose conduct needs naming is precisely the person who will not hear it from a peer who has been told not to teach.

The escape hatch is the phrase 除非特殊情况 — except in special circumstances. It is the right instinct and it is not a procedure. Who decides that a circumstance is special? On what occasion, in what form, with what consequence? Without an answer, the exception will be available to whoever is most confident, most senior, or loudest — which is the 好喜出头 person the note has just warned about. A rule with an unwritten exception tends to be enforced by exactly the people it was written against.

This is not a small point. Every group that has tried to combine 实修 with harmony has met it. The usual solutions are procedural rather than moral: a named person to raise a concern, a private word before a public one, an agreement about what counts as correction rather than quarrel. The note does not offer any of these, and a reader who takes it literally will get the silence it did not intend.

## 5. 以开心为主, and the trouble with 实修

The note welcomes 实修 and also says that, generally, cheerfulness should be the rule.

Those are two different criteria, and they do not always agree. Actual practice is often boring, frequently uncomfortable, and sometimes disappointing; the parts that feel good are not reliably the parts that change anything. A group optimised for pleasantness will select for the practices that produce pleasant states and for the people who report them — which is one of the ways 修身 becomes a consumer activity in the first place, the very thing the note is complaining about two sentences earlier.

There is a reading that rescues it, and it is probably the intended one: 开心 describes the *tone* of the group, not the *test* of the practice. Do not turn practice into a fight; do not make every conversation a correction; let people be at ease. Read that way, the sentence is compatible with 实修 and is good advice. But the note does not distinguish the two readings, and a reader will take the easier one.

## 6. Equality, and the position the note speaks from

人人平等 is right in the sense the tradition gives it. 《孟子·告子下》 has 人皆可以为尧舜 — anyone can become a sage; the 坛经 has 不悟即佛是众生，一念悟时，众生是佛 — deluded, the Buddha is a sentient being; awakened, the sentient being is a Buddha. Standing is equal. Nobody's capacity is the issue.

Experience is not equal, and the note's flatness about it creates a practical problem. A group in which nobody may be a teacher is a group in which a beginner cannot easily receive correction — and correction is what 德行 requires. The distinction that makes both claims livable is between *standing* and *office*: everyone is equal as a practitioner, and someone still has to be responsible for the room. The note asserts the first and leaves the second unstated.

Which brings up something worth noticing without making an accusation of it. The note forbids 好为人师, and it is issued by an administrator — that is, from the position in the group that legitimately teaches and decides. That is not hypocrisy; a rule against self-appointed teaching can only be made by someone with the standing to make it. But it does mean the rule's force depends on the administrator keeping it as strictly as everyone else, and on the group being able to say so when he does not. The note says 人人平等; the structure says otherwise. That gap is where resentment grows, and it is the gap the note's own warning about 洋洋得意 is aimed at.

## What this does not say

- It is not the site's position. Contributed by Admin and published with permission; the argument is the author's.
- It does not say that discussion is bad. It says that quarrelling is, which is a narrower claim.
- It does not say that technique is worthless. It says that technique without conduct is 舍本逐末 — which is 葛洪's position, not a Confucian import.
- It does not claim that this particular group is guilty of the faults it names. It states norms, and norms are stated because they are easy to lose.
- It does not settle who decides what counts as 特殊情况. That is the note's unfinished business, and it is left as the author left it.
