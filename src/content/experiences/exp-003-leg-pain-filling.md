**Anonymized report (paraphrased from public community Q&A).** After two months of daily cross-legged sitting, a practitioner reported that progress was slower than expected, mainly because of foot pain: most sessions ended in scattered thought within half an hour of pain onset, and only once had the practitioner sat through the pain to the point where it passed. One ankle was swollen. Alongside this, a peculiar sensation developed: the lower abdomen seemed to fill with air, the filling rising until the chest, then the shoulders began to hurt. The practitioner felt the whole thorax and abdomen 'filled,' lower and middle dantian joined through, and asked the teacher, in almost the same breath: is this an illusion, or does this stage really exist? They added that their sensitivity to 'qi' was real but the sensation itself was weak and flickering.

**Teacher's traditional reading (as recorded).** The filling is normal — it is what a relaxed, energy-sufficient body feels like from inside; bone repair is painful and must be endured; keep practicing.

**Why this report matters.** It captures the two-sided reality of the early-stage reports this site documents: on one side, an entirely ordinary obstacle (leg pain, scattered attention) that any modern posture research would recognize; on the other, an interpretation demand — the practitioner is not content to notice the sensation, they need to know *which story it belongs to* (real stage or self-deception). The honest answer the knowledge base gives is the third option the source does not offer: the sensation is real as *felt experience*, while its *explanation* — as qi, as dantian filling, as relaxed musculature — is interpretive, with the tradition and modern physiology offering different vocabularies for the same report. Neither is asserted here as fact; both are labeled.

**Classification.** Anonymized practitioner report; traditional reading recorded as TRADITIONAL / LINEAGE INTERPRETATION. Swelling and persistent pain warrant professional medical attention regardless of practice context.
