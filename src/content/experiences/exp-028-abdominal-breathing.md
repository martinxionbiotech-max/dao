## The curriculum

**The teacher's core teaching, in its own order:** (1) the purpose is singular — change the chest-breathing habit into abdominal breathing, not to lengthen the breath (目的不在延长呼吸时间，而是练成腹式呼吸); (2) the mechanics — inhale by *releasing* the belly muscles (撤力, withdrawing the force, the belly expands on its own), exhale by *contracting* them hard (plus the lower contraction); the hissing SI sound is only a shortcut for strengthening the belly muscles and may be omitted when inconvenient; (3) the quantity — about 20% more air per breath, never straining; (4) the posture — practice standing, because standing keeps you sensitive to the head, and stop at the first discomfort; (5) the timing — any time you remember it, every day, but at least two hours after eating, morning and empty stomach best; (6) the standard — ten to twelve breaths per minute at rest; if you are already there, do not practice, just sit; (7) the division of labor — in sitting, do not observe the breath and do not control it; let the body breathe by itself; breathing *time* lengthens through sitting, not through daily practice; (8) the pace — three months to change a decades-old habit, and when the body pushes back (constipation, bloating), rest a month and let it catch up.

## Three layers, kept apart

- **The practitioner layer.** The readers' questions and reports, all registered as community testimony per the archive's rule: the office worker embarrassed to hiss in public (the teacher: skip the hiss, the key is the belly, not the sound); the counter-reading (one reader's minute-rate of 27); the chest-pain reader (told: your cardio is too weak, prostrations first); the cesarean-birth reader who reached both-feet detox in three weeks (the teacher's exhibit case); the reader who discovered her husband already breathes abdominally in sleep; the reader who found her belly rounding like a four-month pregnancy (it will flatten); the navel-itch reader (fascia stretching, no cause for alarm); the reader who felt a blockage at the stomach (the mid-belly nerves are dead at first; prostrations and belly-muscle work activate them); and the peer layer beside the teacher — fellow practitioners answering each other (the bad-breath answer from a peer: gut trouble, eat plain; the recitation-peer: the teacher holds that sitting must not observe the breath — get the breathing adjusted and go straight to recitation; the mantra-peer: feel only the sound of the mantra).
- **The teacher layer.** The full curriculum above, registered as the teacher's teaching at the same evidentiary level as the rest of the series. The Buddhist frame (merit, the precepts, the Buddha-field of recitation) is registered per the site's [standing boundary](/experiences/exp-013-buddhist-daoist-boundary/) — recorded as its tradition's doctrine, not adopted. Three items in this chapter carry the site's explicit flags:
  - **Health claims, registered not endorsed.** The gut-droop answer (breathing repairs the stomach; far worse chronic diseases "basically all get cured" by breathing and sitting), the sleep-reduction answer (more oxygen, less brain fatigue — sleep drops, a good thing), the fever-and-nausea mechanism (oxygen outrunning the brain's adjustment), and the detox-reading of foot color (dark purple to red) — all registered as the teacher's lay-physiology and health claims, per the site's [health-claims rule](/questions/can-meditation-cure-illness/), with the site's standing non-medical-advice declaration.
  - **Extraordinary claims, cross-linked.** The four-dhyana blood doctrine (a fourth-dhyana person's blood bright as red ink, dissolving in plain water in hours, carrying triple the oxygen) cross-links to the archive's [extraordinary-claims handling](/experiences/exp-016-nine-dhyanas-map/) per the standing rule — registered as the episode's content, not taken on.
  - **Safety rules, registered as the teacher's.** The menstruation rule (no forced abdominal breathing during the period — blood-flow increase), the pregnancy rule (never — the diaphragm pressing down harms the fetus), the full-stomach rule (eating then practicing is 作死, self-destructive), and the standing-monitoring rule (stop at first dizziness or head-pressure) — all registered plainly as the teacher's constraints, which the site's [sitting protocol](/tools/sitting-protocol/) neither adopts nor contradicts.
- **The alternative layer.** The chapter contains the teacher's sharpest departure from received scripture anywhere in the series: the **Anapanasati criticism** — the sutra's short-in-breath, long-out-breath instruction, practiced in sitting for two or three months, makes the body rebel (diaphragm stuck, lungs contracted, bowels stopped), and the teacher's conclusion is registered verbatim in paraphrase: the sutra's content may have been corrupted in translation and transmission; if you must sit, keep in-breath and out-breath *equal*. This is the archive's own moment of [source-criticism](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/), and the site files it beside its own version of the same discipline: a text's transmission is part of its content.
- **The methodological confession.** The chapter's most valuable passage for this site is the teacher's own account of why he works the way he does: the *Inception* elephant — "if I tell you not to think of an elephant, you think of an elephant" — so he never describes dhyana states, only breath-rates and body changes, "working the edges of the rules" (打擦边球), because naming attainments makes people chase them. It is the archive's own statement of the site's [description discipline](/questions/does-practice-need-faith/): describe the practice, not the state. And it arrives with its joke, which the site files in the [anti-display line](/experiences/exp-020-false-first-dhyana/): the practitioner who strains for warmth, and when it finally comes names it solemnly "fire dhyana" — 现实就这么滑稽，良师的作用就在这里 — reality is this comical, and *this* is where a good teacher matters. The naming of attainment is the joke; the attainment itself is not described.
- **The convergence point, stated plainly.** Beneath the Buddhist frame, the chapter's procedural core coincides with ordinary prudence on three points the site can state without endorsing any doctrine: gradual change (three months for a decades-old habit; a month's rest when the body pushes back), dose-limits (20% more air; ten to twelve breaths a minute; stop at discomfort), and the target defined as habit-change rather than achievement (the purpose is not to lengthen the breath). The site's [breath episode](/experiences/exp-023-breath-is-the-key/) holds the same teacher's one-line form of the whole chapter: the breath is the key.
