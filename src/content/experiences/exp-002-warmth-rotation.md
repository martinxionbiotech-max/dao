**Anonymized report (paraphrased from public community Q&A).** A beginner with only days of sitting practice reported the following sequence: after a period of whole-body warmth, a sudden internal 'shake' occurred, followed by a sensation of the entire body rotating, which the practitioner found pleasant. After ending the session, lying down immediately re-triggered the sensations — full-body tingling and a strange fading of control over the arms — and the practitioner, frightened, asked the teacher whether they had practiced wrongly and broken something.

Also reported: daytime energy noticeably improved — where seven hours of sleep previously left heavy eyelids, sitting now left the practitioner alert — with sleep need reduced.

**Teacher's traditional reading (as recorded in the source).** The sensations were normal: body warmth is the first-stage 'repair' of the body; more oxygen in the blood explains the alertness and reduced sleep; a beginner of days should not expect any 'state' (境界) yet. The reply explicitly defused the fear: keep practicing.

**Why this report matters.** It shows the gap the knowledge base exists to bridge. The practitioner experienced benign relaxation-and-arousal phenomena, but their only interpretive frame was fear of self-harm. The tradition's frame — that beginners commonly meet warmth, sweat, tingling, and movement sensations, and that these are handled by not-grasping and steady practice — is exactly what the [restlessness](/problems/restlessness-in-sitting/) and [sitting protocol](/tools/sitting-protocol/) pages document. Neither frame is asserted here as scientific; both are recorded with their sources.
