**Anonymized note (paraphrased from a public community article).** A beginner's first single-cross-legged session hit a wall at about ten minutes: the lower leg felt pierced by countless needles, and the urge to shake the leg loose was nearly irresistible. A senior practitioner's counsel was 'numbness is normal — endure it,' and the beginner describes gritting through, caught between trusting the advice and trusting the body.

**Why this note matters.** Leg numbness is arguably the most-reported physical difficulty in Chinese sitting communities, and the advice landscape around it is genuinely contradictory — which is why this knowledge base's [sitting protocol](/tools/sitting-protocol/) states the safety floor explicitly: adjust when needed, never force to injury, and treat numbness that persists after releasing the posture as a warning. Notably, the classical sources themselves already contain both poles — the endurance counsel of the training traditions and the 'know the fire and timing' (火候) caveat against over-forcing. The tradition is more careful than its loudest modern voices.
