## Why this record matters

Most entries in this archive are one person describing what happened to them on a cushion. This one is an argument in a chat room, and the argument is about the most basic word in the room. One participant insists that almost everyone is using 打坐 to mean the wrong thing; several others push back with the ordinary intuitions — surely you have to become still first, surely the body matters, surely this is impossible.

The useful thing is not who wins. It is that both sides are right about different questions while using the same word, and the transcript shows exactly where the word splits.

## The exchange

*Abridged: peripheral messages removed, participants reduced to A and B, wording retained.*

> **A:** Meditation is sitting; sitting is entering 定; entering 定 is ascending; where there is no entering or leaving of 定 and nothing to ascend to, that is 无余涅槃. Not the form — the one who watches the mind is utterly liberated.
>
> **A:** Is sitting anywhere sitting? Of course not. The office worker on 996 sits at a desk twelve hours a day — if sitting were 打坐, that lot would have attained long ago. They haven't. Having stability in the mind, knowing the present moment, watching the thought as it arises — that is 打坐.
>
> **B:** For the details of meditation you can check the usual material — ask an AI assistant, gather information from the platforms. For reference only.
>
> **A:** Understand this, and your eating and sleeping are also 打坐. Don't understand it, and you can sit there in full lotus and it still isn't 打坐.
>
> **B:** How long should one sit?
>
> **B:** As it comes, as it goes. When the heat is right, you can travel far.
>
> **A:** Sitting is not a matter of length. If you can truly sit still for five minutes, that beats two hours of dry sitting.
>
> **B:** I'm local to the area — is there a teacher here?
>
> **A:** Some people go dull the moment they sit; within minutes they are snoring. What is the point of two hours of sitting asleep? Or they sit there chasing thoughts — also pointless. So length proves nothing. The question is how many minutes you can actually be still.
>
> **B:** No stray thoughts — that feeling of high mountains and flowing water?
>
> **A:** Don't be in a hurry to sit. Don't assume sitting is simple — "it's just sitting, anyone can sit" — that is a serious mistake. Everything has two sides, and sitting is no different. If the theory is not clear, sitting blind is your poison: no benefit, real harm.
>
> **B:** Theory first, then practice — and someone to guide it.
>
> **A:** A lot of people have lost their minds doing this. They meet a state and cannot get out of it, and after that the head is full of spirits and ghosts. Sitting is a strong medicine, but it has to be learned correctly. Don't be blind about it. "I heard sitting is good, I heard it's fashionable, I'll join in" — that means nothing, and it is not as simple as you think. The basic precautions, the adjustments before and after — you have to know them.
>
> **A:** "My mind is a mess, I want some 静静" — and who is Jingjing? *(a pun: 静静 is both "quiet" and a woman's name)* Sitting is not the seeking of quiet; it is watching the mind. "Sitting feels so good, sitting is wonderful" — you're finished. Why are you finished? You will understand slowly.
>
> **B:** Being able to be still is impressive. Surely you have to become still first. And feeling comfortable in sitting is not easy either. The rest can come later. Sitting is not really a subject of its own — it is an incidental subject within practice, which is why it varies so much. For plain sitting, the first step is surely to still the mind.
>
> **A:** The seeking of quiet is itself noise. Toward every state, neither grasp nor reject; the mind not fixed on any one thing or one state — and this moment is 极乐. Does this most basic point really need repeating back and forth?
>
> **B:** Impossible.
>
> **B:** First train moving practice — get the body healthy and strong, have some martial ability, and then consider cultivation. The simplest practice is to read the Heart Sutra, the 枕中经, the Daodejing, the Diamond Sutra.
>
> **A:** The path is full of pits. You know that being scattered is bad, so you seek quiet — that is out of the dragon's pool and into the tiger's den. Scattered is wrong; so quiet must be right? That too is wrong.
>
> **A:** This is the most common mistake beginners make. You have to remind yourself constantly: nothing is obtainable, not a thread hanging. Quiet is not obtainable either; do not stick to quiet.
>
> **A:** Since nothing is obtainable, how could there be a quiet to obtain? How could there be a comfort to obtain, or a so-called 禅悦? There is only awareness, only clear knowing — it is not for you to get anything. The cultivator 损之又损，乃至无余 — how plainly it is put. Give up, and give up again, until there is nothing at all. That is real cultivation.
>
> **A:** So-called attainment: no path to be attained — that is called attaining the path. Attain what? Attaining nothing is the true path. Whatever you think you have attained is not the true path.
>
> **A:** The point is not quiet versus scattered. After all this, still not understood: the point is watching the mind. Scattered, you can watch the mind; quiet, you can watch the mind. Quiet and motion are only two marks. You have merely let go of the mark of scattering and grasped the mark of quiet.
>
> **A:** Not grasping — this moment is true 打坐, it is 极乐. Scattered, let it be scattered; quiet, let it be quiet. I only know scattered and quiet; I know all the marks and all the states. Among states, neither grasp nor reject: a good state I do not take, a bad state I do not push away.
>
> **A:** Cut through Buddha and demon alike. It does not mean hating the demon when it comes and rejoicing when the Buddha comes. Whatever you see, pay it no attention — no joy, no sorrow, unmoved as such. That is called 打坐.

## 1. The definition is thirteen centuries old

A's central move — sitting is not the posture — is not an innovation, and it is worth saying so before anyone treats it as one. The 《六祖坛经·坐禅品》 defines both words by function:

> 外于一切善恶境界，心念不起，名为坐；内见自性不动，名为禅。

*Toward all good and bad states without, the mind's thinking does not arise: that is called 坐. Within, seeing the self-nature unmoved: that is called 禅.*

There is no reference to legs, cushions or duration. A's "坐在哪里就是打坐吗？当然不是" is that sentence in modern Chinese, with an office worker added as evidence.

So the first round is not a dispute between tradition and modernity. It is a dispute between a text and a room that has forgotten the text.

## 2. 求静 is itself 乱 — the strongest move and its blind spot

The exchange's sharpest claim is that the wish to be still is itself agitation. B says: surely you have to become still first. A says: the seeking of quiet is noise.

This is not wordplay, and the tradition backs it. The same 坐禅品 closes the door on the position B holds:

> 此门坐禅，元不著心，亦不著净，亦不是不动……起心著净，却生净妄。

*This teaching's sitting is originally not fixing on the mind, not fixing on purity, and not immobility… To set the mind on purity is to produce a purity-delusion.*

That is A's argument, made in the 8th century, and it is a real argument: 求静 posits a state to be reached and a self who lacks it, and the seeking reproduces the division it is trying to close.

But A applies it to the weakest version of B's claim. B says 能静是很牛的 — being able to be still is impressive — and treats stillness as a by-product that makes the rest possible. That is not necessarily 著净. There is a difference between grasping at stillness and noticing that a settled mind is easier to work with, and A never separates them. The strongest form of B's position is never answered; only the weakest is.

## 3. 无所得 — and the seam where two traditions are stitched

A's "nothing is obtainable" is the 《心经》's 以无所得故, and the posture of neither grasping nor rejecting is the plain sense of the 《金刚经》's 应无所住而生其心. On this ground A is on solid classical footing.

The phrase 损之又损 is not from that ground. It is 《道德经》 ch. 48:

> 为学日益，为道日损。损之又损，以至于无为。

A renders it 损之又损，乃至无余. The substitution matters. 老子's ladder ends at 无为 — a way of acting in the world that does not force. 无余 is Buddhist: 无余涅槃, the end of rebirth. The two are not the same destination, and joining them is a habit of contemporary Chinese practice talk rather than a textual inheritance.

This is not a scandal. Almost every living tradition is a hybrid, and a speaker is entitled to build one. But the site's habit is to mark the seam, because a reader who goes looking for 损之又损，乃至无余 in the 道德经 will not find it, and will reasonably wonder what else was paraphrased.

The opening chain — 冥想就是打坐，打坐就是入定，入定就是飞升 — has the same quality. It is rhetorical escalation, not a doctrinal equation, and A signals as much by ending the sentence with the actual claim: 非形式而观心者究竟解脱.

## 4. 打坐 as 猛药: a right warning with a wrong proof

A warns that people damage themselves sitting, meet a state they cannot leave, and come out with "spirits and ghosts in the head", and concludes that 打坐 is strong medicine requiring correct instruction.

The direction is not fringe. The 《楞严经》 spends its final two fascicles on the 五十阴魔 — fifty categories of state that a practitioner may mistake for attainment — and 天台 止观 literature treats 魔事 as something to be recognised and managed rather than denied. Contemporary research has also begun documenting adverse effects of meditation, though far less systematically than the popular warnings suggest.

But the form of A's claim — 都是打坐打出来的 — is not supportable. It attributes the outcome to the practice alone, and says nothing about prior condition, dosage, supervision or the difference between a state that is frightening and a psychosis that was already forming. A is right that the risk is real and wrong about how to state it.

And A's own metaphor argues against A's conclusion. If sitting is a 猛药, then indication, dose and supervision are the whole question — which is exactly B's point about needing a teacher. A restates the danger and never answers the objection.

## 5. The question nobody answered: the body

B proposes moving practice first: get healthy, get strong, then consider cultivation. A does not reply to it at all.

That is the exchange's real gap. A's account of practice is entirely an account of attention. It has nothing to say about sleep, pain, illness, injury, or a body that cannot sit for five minutes without the mind going to the pain. For a beginner asking "how long should I sit", "train the body first" is not a competing metaphysics — it is a practical answer to a practical question, and A's reply ("five real minutes beat two dull hours") is a good answer to a different question, because it presumes a body and a mind that can manage five minutes.

The same silence covers B's other practical question — the teacher. A says blind practice is poison, which is an argument for supervision, and then does not follow it.

## 6. Who won

Neither, and the transcript is more useful for that.

A wins the definitional argument. 打坐 is not a posture, the classical text says so, and the office-worker line is a better proof than most sermons.

B wins the practical argument. A beginner needs a first step, a body that can take it, and someone to correct the errors — and none of those is answered.

The two positions are not actually in conflict. They are answering different questions with the same word: A means the state of awareness, B means the activity one schedules at six in the morning. Most of the heat in the exchange comes from that collision, not from disagreement about anything either side would defend if asked separately.

## Editor's judgment

This section is opinion, marked as opinion. It is the editor's reading, not a finding.

**A is right about the definition and wrong about the method.** The correction — posture is not practice, duration is not progress — is correct, classical, and worth saying loudly in a room where "how long should I sit" is the first question asked. The line I would keep from the whole exchange is the one a beginner can use tonight: *if you can truly sit still for five minutes, that beats two hours of dry sitting.*

**But the position is closed.** Every disagreement is diagnosed as 没明白; every settled state, including the one B describes, is diagnosed as 执静. A teaching that can absorb any counter-observation is not being tested by the conversation it is in — it is only being restated. That is comfortable for the teacher and unhelpful for the student, because the student cannot tell the difference between understanding and having been told they do not understand.

**The refusal to give a method is the real failure, and it is a pastoral failure rather than a philosophical one.** A says: don't sit blind, the theory must be clear first. A also says: there is nothing to attain, no state to reach, only awareness. Put together, a beginner is told that the thing they want is an error and the technique they need is dangerous — and is left with nothing to do on Monday morning. The classical answer to this is not more doctrine; it is a teacher, a form, and a small daily dose. A rejects the form and never supplies the teacher.

**B is right about the two things A never answers.** The body and the guide. Neither is a lower-stage concern to be outgrown; they are the conditions under which the awareness A describes becomes possible at all.

**And the strongest thing either side said is not in dispute.** Nobody in the room claimed that sitting more is better. The argument is about what the sitting is for — and on that, A's answer (watch the mind, grasp nothing) and B's answer (become still enough to see) are closer than the transcript makes them sound.

## What this does not say

- It is not the site's position. It is an anonymized transcript of a group conversation, with the editor's opinion clearly marked.
- It does not claim that A is authorized to teach, or that the exchange produced a correct doctrine.
- It does not claim that meditation causes psychosis. It says that adverse effects are documented, that A's blanket causal phrasing is unsupported, and that dosage and supervision matter.
- It does not say that posture and duration are worthless. It says they are not the definition — which is the classical position too.
- It does not settle whether a teacher is required. It records that the question was asked and not answered.
