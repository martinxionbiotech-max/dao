## The exchange

**The practitioner (long letter, paraphrased per the archive's rule — no verbatim quoting, no usernames):** a reader trained in a contemporary liberation-stages teaching writes that the four-dhyana ladder looks different there (entry by settling of mind-factors, not by body conditions), that the physical prerequisites for the first dhyana destroy beginners' confidence, that the Way should not be painful — and that they themselves practice *lying down*, not in the lotus: lying is the most relaxed, the awareness most open, and forcing the lotus through pain has already departed from the Way.

**The teacher's reply, in its own structure:** (1) the four-dhyana understanding in the letter is "what bookworms work out at their desks — the fault is not yours, many believe this and spread it"; (2) a standing challenge: describe, in plain language and with modern science, how the eighteen heavens from the boundlessness-of-space heaven down to the Brahma-assembly heaven evolve and how their beings change, in body and in mind — because "you cannot have a correct direction in dhyana practice if you do not know what the beings of that heaven are like" — with the closing sentence "I know what level your cultivation is at right now"; (3) on lying down: "I played with this in middle school; looking back, it was dreaming — sitting meditation enters stillness, lying down enters dreams, self-made beautiful dreams; there is the feeling of entering... and you come out because a fantasy arose"; (4) the auspicious reclining posture (jixiangwo) is a provisional method to help open the shanzhong point *before* the channels open, and afterwards lets qi travel the central channel slowly to keep the body nearest to stillness — "but it cannot compare with sitting"; (5) the Buddha's own experiment, told as the warrant: seeing that the dead feel nothing, he pushed the body toward death through fasting, until past a tipping point every cell signaled hunger — and concluded the body must instead be tuned to its most perfect health, the state where it produces the least sensation, which the tradition found in the cross-legged seat; (6) the ladder's time conditions: the first dhyana requires holding at least four hours of sitting, the second ten, the third can last days, the fourth until death — and once the fourth is reached, entering the first takes minutes or seconds; (7) the rule: "practice cannot be innovative; follow the footprints of predecessors — those who play at individuality fail in the end. The choice itself is not wrong; only the results differ. You wish to lie down and remain ordinary, continue; we wish to sit and be liberated, so we sit."

## Three layers, kept apart

- **The practitioner layer (the letter).** The lying-down argument, the openness-of-awareness claim, the "the Way is not pain" claim, and the alternative dhyana ladder — all registered as one practitioner's stated position, with its source framework (a contemporary liberation-stages teacher) named as the letter names it. The archive keeps the letter's claims unverified-by-the-archive: the letter is evidence of a *community position*, not of dhyana mechanics.
- **The teacher layer (the reply).** The eighteen-heavens challenge, the dreaming diagnosis of lying down, the provisional function of the auspicious reclining posture, the time-conditions ladder, the fasting narrative, and the anti-innovation rule — all registered as the teacher's own teaching, at the same evidentiary level as the rest of this series ([EXP-016](/experiences/notes/exp-016-nine-dhyanas-map/), [EXP-023](/experiences/notes/exp-023-breath-is-the-key/)). The physiological items (cells signaling hunger, the body's self-repair, the shanzhong point, the central channel) are registered as **lay-physiology** — the teacher's own explanatory idiom, not anatomy.
- **The alternative layer.** The letter's source framework — the contemporary liberation-stages teaching with its mind-factor-based dhyana ladder — is registered as an existing alternative tradition with its own internal coherence, and the archive does not adjudicate between it and the teacher's: the [progress page](/experiences/questions/what-counts-as-progress/) already holds the site's standing rule that stage-languages do not convert between systems.
- **Health claims, quoted without endorsement.** "Sitting in the lotus brings the body to its most perfect health" and "lying down is at most health-maintenance" are the teacher's claims, recorded as claims — not medical statements — and this page carries the archive's standing [non-medical-advice notice](/experiences/questions/can-meditation-cure-illness/). The fasting narrative is the teacher's retelling of the tradition's Buddha-story, registered as tradition, not as dietary instruction.
- **The convergence point, stated plainly.** The one point where the teacher and ordinary caution meet: lying down tends toward sleep — the archive's [drowsiness pattern](/experiences/patterns/drowsiness-vs-stillness/) documents the same boundary from the practice side. Beyond that convergence, the two positions do not reconcile, and the archive does not force them to.
