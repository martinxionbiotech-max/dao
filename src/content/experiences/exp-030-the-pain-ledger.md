## The episode

**A ledger of pains, kept by the teacher.** The episode opens with the archive's most typical report — a practitioner who had reached the comfortable one-hour sit, warm all over, who then extended to 1h25m and found the legs hurting again, with the feet still rosy and the pain feeling mechanical rather than blockage-based: do the long-sitters not feel their legs, or does everyone hurt again past a point? The teacher's answer is the episode's centerpiece, and it is a *schedule*: the skin replaces itself in 28 days, muscle and organs in 2–3 years, sinew in 3–4 years, bone in 7 years or more. While muscle and organs are still repairing, sitting hurts; once they finish, the legs sit comfortably and the channels count as open — but the sinews and bones are not done, and when the oxygen and glucose begin repairing *them*, the pain returns. The order is anatomical: the ankle first (all the body's weight has hung on it for decades — damage is guaranteed), then the knee, then the hip joint. And the timing is precise: the sinew-and-cartilage pain arrives between 90 minutes and two hours into the sit. This, the teacher says, is why the two-hour sit is a real number — 这个两小时不是随便说的 (this two hours is not said casually) — the repair that needs the extra oxygen begins there.

## Three layers, kept apart

- **The practitioner layer: the returns of pain.** The readers' reports fill the ledger's margins, and the site files them as the episode's texture: post-sitting pain extending into the next day with head-sweating during the sit (the reader notes it happens in double-lotus only, never in single, and never off the cushion); the left leg going numb when the body is shifted mid-sit while the right leg only hurts; the feet burning as if on fire after the half-hour mark while the leg-pain goes quiet; deep pressure marks on the thighs and shins after dismounting; a sitting posture that will not straighten (one leg forward, one back, the spine curving); and the practitioner who, while reciting the Heart Sutra, remembered his mother working abroad under hardship, transferred the thought into Buddha-recitation, and reported that the tears came and *all other wandering thoughts stopped* — the episode's only method report, and a vivid one. The site's [pain pages](/experiences/patterns/leg-numbness-pain/) hold the same reports in the site's own collection; this episode is the archive's native version.
- **The teacher layer, with the schedule as its claim.** The cell-turnover schedule is the teacher's framework, registered under the site's standing flags, not adopted: the repair timetable (skin 28 days, muscle 2–3 years, sinew 3–4 years, bone 7+) is lay-physiology in the same register as the [human-body chapter](/experiences/notes/exp-029-the-human-body/)'s boundary declaration — plausible-sounding, unverified, and framed by the teacher's own account of himself. The episode's derived claims follow it: the heat phase needs only three months of breath practice while the desire-realm dhyana needs 2–3 years, because bone repair takes 7 years and the doubled oxygen intake halves it (the archive's own numbers, cross-linked to the [nine-dhyana map](/experiences/notes/exp-016-nine-dhyanas-map/) per the extraordinary-claims rule); the head-sweating account (blood blocked below rises to the head, which relieves pressure by sweating); the leg assignment (降魔坐熬左腿，莲花坐熬右腿 — the demon-subduing sit is the left leg's trial, the lotus sit the right leg's); and the whole-body sequence (pain moves ankle → hips → back → neck; 全身都疼一遍就好了 — once the whole body has hurt, it is done). All registered as the teacher's, none endorsed.
- **The alternative layer, and the teacher's own injury.** The episode's most human moment is the teacher's self-report: as a novice monk he hoed vegetable plots always on one side, the body's weight all on the left hip joint, until the cartilage wore; the pain stopped when the fieldwork stopped, and then — years later, in sitting — it came back, deep in the bone, dragging him out of comfort mid-sit (那疼得真是深入骨髓). The site files this beside the schedule it motivates: the archive's own authority figure is, by his own account, sitting with a farm injury — the same genre as the archive's honesty markers elsewhere ([EXP-016's](/experiences/notes/exp-016-nine-dhyanas-map/) "I have not achieved it myself"). And the reader layer carries the episode's second notable feature: the readers *correcting each other's terminology* — 莲花坐 is the kneeling seat on the heels, the right-leg-on-top double-lotus is 吉祥坐 — a dispute the site registers as the archive's own evidence of how unstable the posture vocabulary is, which is exactly why the site's [cross-legged question](/experiences/questions/must-i-sit-cross-legged/) answers the practice question without importing the archive's posture doctrine.
- **The convergence point, stated plainly.** Beneath the schedule and the doctrine, the episode's procedural core coincides with ordinary prudence on the points the site can state: the pain is expected and gradual (the ankle-knee-hip order, the whole-body sequence — the same gradual-change rule as [EXP-003's](/experiences/reports/exp-003-leg-pain-filling/) filling-up account); the dose has a floor the archive claims and the site does not (the two-hour window is the archive's number — the site's [duration page](/experiences/questions/how-long-should-i-sit/) keeps its own); and the remedies are cheap (prostrations for the spine and circulation, slow running to clear the after-effects — the archive's standing pair). The site's [progress page](/experiences/questions/what-counts-as-progress/) keeps the boundary: pain in the archive is *repair made audible*; on this site, pain that persists or sharpens is a body signal to check, not a milestone to collect.

## Why it is filed here

Because this is the archive's most complete single statement of the *pain-as-progress* doctrine — the framework that turns the archive's hardest requirement (the two-hour sit) into a claim about the body rather than an act of will — and it belongs beside the site's [leg-pain pages](/experiences/patterns/leg-numbness-pain/) as the traditional-world counterpart of what the modern community reports: the same pains, one framework claiming to know what they mean, and the site keeping the framework in view without adopting its numbers. It also completes the archive's [body theory](/experiences/notes/exp-029-the-human-body/) on the sensation side: where the human-body chapter described the systems, this episode describes the *sound* they make while being rebuilt — and both are filed with the same discipline: the teacher's physiology is the teacher's, and the reader's body is the reader's.
