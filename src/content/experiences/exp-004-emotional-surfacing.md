**Anonymized note (paraphrased from a public forum thread).** A practitioner reports that as their sitting deepened, the mind's quiet was no longer fully under their control in one specific way: old unresolved material — things they had long decided not to think about again — began to surface during practice. The practitioner's community framed this as stillness acting as medicine: the quiet reveals what was buried, so that the person can face the origins, their own choices, and what was right or wrong in them, until the material no longer frightens.

**Why this note matters.** This is the practitioner-side counterpart of the tradition's claim that the mind is *originally* still and only disturbed by accumulated content — the Qingjing Jing's diagnosis in first-person form. It also marks a boundary the knowledge base must state plainly: the surfacing of difficult material is a recognized phenomenon in contemplative practice generally, and for trauma-related or overwhelming content, the appropriate response is professional support, not a meditation forum. The tradition's own counsel is non-forcing; the same applies here.
