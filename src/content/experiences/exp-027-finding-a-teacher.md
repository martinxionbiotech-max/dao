## The exchange

**The readers (paraphrased per the archive's rule — no verbatim quoting, no usernames):** one, who has followed a famous abbot for years, despairs of ever being accepted ("finding a teacher is so hard — my store of merit is not enough"); one asks the practical question — how do you even ask at a temple ("just walk up to a monk and say I want to learn Chan, ask him to introduce me to a teacher? will they say yes?"); one asks the hard question directly — "without a teacher's guidance, is it all demonic practice?"; one reports a local temple with no meditation and no contacts; one reports a wandering monk who seemed to read his past and future through his eyes; one, who has recited the Dizang sutra for two years and practiced the lotus for half a year, confesses she never dared to *start* sitting until recently, and is torn between three methods (mind-watching, the Guanyin ear-root method, concentrating spirit into the qi-cave), switching between them in one sitting; and one posts, with permission, her own teacher's dialogue — the teacher who answers "every day" when asked whether he sits, who says sitting means "just watching my thoughts — the watching is not a verb, not an ounce of force," and who answers the student's despairing "I may never reach it in this life" with: "if you really took me as your teacher it would be easy. You're just playing at it."

**The teacher's replies, in their own structure:** (1) to the abbot-seeker — the teacher-student bond is cultivated over many lifetimes, it is not met casually; do not rush; the key is the aspiration — the purer the aspiration, the likelier the good teacher; go try and see — it beats sitting and waiting; and the warning: an overactive imagination is also an illness, see 1 as 1, do not connect it into 10, it will only give you a headache; (2) to the temple-asker — they have people to receive you; usually they will have you practice on your own for about a year before taking refuge; go ask; (3) to the demonic-practice question — the direct answer, in four phrases: take the precepts as teacher, go deep into the canon, wisdom like the sea, self-study without a teacher also works; and when pressed for detail: read the sutras yourself, research yourself — these years are full of swindlers, few true persons; (4) to the no-meditation-temple reader — the conditions are not ripe; read my posts, practice the lotus on your own; when the affinity arrives, someone will come to lead you in; (5) to the mind-reading monk — one word: affinity; (6) the support teaching — do not worry about the future, look at the present; when you need help, the Buddhas and Bodhisattvas will support you and a new good teacher will appear; after Shakyamuni left, the Dharma carried 2,500 years — after I leave, will no one continue to spread the right teaching?; (7) the ladder of teachers — quoting the teacher Xuanhua: he raised you one step, I raise you the next, someone after will raise you further; keep practicing, keep the aspiration pure, and you will not fail to meet a good teacher; (8) to the Dizang-sutra reader — high praise for the two years of recitation, then the diagnosis of the three-method switching (jumping from book to book, trying each "supreme" method for a few days, wastes the best time; practice has no shortcut, only hard work); the great-bodhicitta instruction (the highest requirement of this transmission); the superman image (if you have a superhuman mind, the Buddhas will give you superhuman powers to keep the world's peace — a world full of infants who want only milk, and what the world lacks is supermen); (9) the fraud analysis — to those who met swindlers: look at yourself, why were you cheated? the cheat's pitch is always the shortcut ("spend some money and your business goes smoothly, or your practice jumps a level"); you get cheated because you want the cheap way instead of steady work; attainment cannot be bought — offering to a true bodhisattva earns merit, not sudden progress; (10) the meta-reaction to the reposted teacher-dialogue — "don't tell me this was by text message; your teacher is also quite the bore" — and then, when the poster admits her faults: "teachable — a person who knows her own problem has the possibility of changing it."

## Three layers, kept apart

- **The practitioner layer.** The readers' questions and hopes (the abbot-despair, the temple anxiety, the demonic-practice fear, the method-switching confession) and the reposted teacher-dialogue — all registered as community testimony, with the repost quoted only in paraphrase per the archive's rule.
- **The teacher layer.** The fate-bond doctrine, the aspiration rule, the try-and-see rule, the precepts-as-teacher answer, the swindler warning, the support teaching, the ladder of teachers, the no-shortcut rule, and the fraud analysis — all registered as the teacher's teaching, at the same evidentiary level as the rest of the series. The Buddhist frame (precepts, refuge, merit, the Bodhisattvas, bodhicitta) is registered per the site's [standing boundary](/experiences/notes/exp-013-buddhist-daoist-boundary/) — recorded as doctrine of its tradition, not adopted.
- **The alternative layer.** The reposted dialogue's teacher — a silent-illumination teacher with a different register (the watching that is not a verb; the respect that is practice) — is registered as a second teacher's voice inside the episode, and the episode's sharpest moment is the *friction*: Taiguanglin's own verdict on the other teacher ("also quite the bore") and on the student ("playing at it"). The archive keeps the friction visible rather than flattening it into one doctrine — the two teachers agree on the one thing the site elevates, and disagree on everything else.
- **Extraordinary claims, registered.** The mind-reading monk (the reader's report, met with a one-word answer) and the Buddhas-and-Bodhisattvas support teaching cross-link to the archive's [extraordinary-claims handling](/experiences/notes/exp-016-nine-dhyanas-map/) per the standing rule; the archive records them as the episode's content without taking them on.
- **The convergence point, stated plainly.** The teacher, the other teacher, and the site's own sources all land on the same discipline: *the teaching is what you do, not whose approval you hold*. Taiguanglin's version (practice has no shortcut, only hard work; read the sutras yourself), the silent-illumination teacher's version (the best respect for a teacher is to practice what the teacher says, well, and surpass the teacher), and the site's [description rule](/experiences/questions/does-practice-need-faith/) (say what you learned and from whom) are one sentence in three registers.

Because this is the series' *teacher question* — the direct answer to "is practice without a teacher demonic?" (no: the precepts are the teacher, the canon is the teacher, self-study works) — and the natural companion of the site's [teacher question page](/experiences/questions/do-i-need-a-teacher/), which holds the Zhuangzi's version of the same answer (the transmission chain runs through the son of ink — through texts).
