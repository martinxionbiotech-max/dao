## The chapter

**The archive's complete posture doctrine, in one chapter — eight sections, from the first single lotus to the opened channels.** The arc is the practice's own: section 01 draws the line (single lotus tilts the pelvis, cannot hold the four hours first dhyana needs, and is therefore preparation — though preparation that matters); section 02 hands beginners the drills (butterfly, frog, crane-riding); section 03 gives the doctrine its mechanism (only double lotus closes the circuit, and here the teacher inserts the chapter's most honest sentence: 我也不知道我们的身体为什么会这样 — *I don't know why our body is like this*); section 04 sets the rules (never after eating, rise at four, close down slowly); section 05 is the reaction ledger — the chapter's heart and the site's richest single catalog of sitting sensations; section 06 answers the ill and the disabled; sections 07–08 map the route out — the full-day protocol, the ren-du sequence, the heat between the kidneys that climbs the spine and comes home.

## Three layers, kept apart

- **The practitioner layer.** This is the archive at its most practical, and the readers' reports carry the texture: the reader who reached double lotus within a week of the butterfly drill; the reader who split a painful hour into two halves and was told *useful — at the beginning everyone endures it that way*; the reader whose feet went purple and photographed them, panicking, while the community chanted detox (and one commenter added the caution the archive itself repeats: 有强行盘腿强忍导致腿坏死的案例 — there are cases of legs ruined by forcing the posture); the reader who chose the ear-ringing as an object for ear-root meditation and was told he should hear nothing — the ringing is a nerve repairing, not a door; the reader with ankylosing spondylitis who asked whether his life still had an exit, and got the shadow-body method and the sentence *the Buddhas and bodhisattvas give up on no one — only people give up on themselves*; the mother asking about her father's hemorrhoids and diabetes and getting diet rules and a daughter's duty (dedicate merit); the reader whose double-lotus broke his lust into full flame and got the plainest advice in the archive: *run, slowly; recite the Ucchusma mantra; lust is normal, it passes.* The site files all of it with the standing anonymization (names removed, per the privacy rules) and the standing frame: these are practitioners' reports and the teacher's responses, not clinical data.
- **The teacher layer, with its claims flagged.** The doctrine's load-bearing claims are registered, not endorsed:
  - **The closed-circuit physiology.** The eyes, ears, tongue, fingers and soles all *spend qi*, so the posture seals them one by one — a loop that leaks least. This is the teacher's lay-physiology in the same register as the [human-body chapter](/experiences/notes/exp-029-the-human-body/)'s boundary declaration, and the site keeps both under the same flag: plausible, unverified, and framed by the teacher's own honesty clause in the same section (I don't know why the body is like this).
  - **The detox and repair schedule.** The purple feet as 浊气 leaving through the soles, the cell-turnover timetable (skin 28 days, muscle and organs 2–3 years, sinew 3–4 years, bone 7+), the 90-minute-to-two-hour pain window, the two-hour sit as the repair's minimum dose — the same framework as the [pain ledger](/experiences/notes/exp-030-the-pain-ledger/) (the NetEase repost of the same thread), now visible in its original official context. The cold-from-bone doctrine (skin-cold is outside air, bone-cold is creditors; recite mantras) cross-links to the archive's [extraordinary-claims handling](/experiences/notes/exp-016-nine-dhyanas-map/).
  - **The health claims, with the standing boundary.** The arthritis timetable (two hours daily, three months), the diabetes account (the pancreas needs oxygen to make insulin; reduce medication as blood sugar drops — the site's [illness question](/experiences/questions/can-meditation-cure-illness/) holds the boundary: the teacher's health advice is his, not medical counsel), the anemia rule, and the teacher's own regime (one meal, 864 prostrations, three hours' sleep — registered as self-report with the archive's own warning attached: 很多医学常识都不适用, much medical common sense does not apply to practitioners).
  - **The stay-or-stop discrimination rule — the chapter's safety core.** The teacher draws the sharpest line in the archive between mental and physical resistance: mental causes (wandering thought, lust, irritation, grief) are the *targets* — keep sitting, they are what practice exists to extinguish; but physical pain intensifying into labored breathing is a *problem to find and remove*, or 拖垮身体只是时间问题 — breaking the body is only a matter of time, the more diligent the faster. The site files this beside the [modern adverse-events research](/research/farias-adverse-events-2020/) and the [breath-stopping problem page](/problems/breath-stopping-anxiety/): the archive's own rule converges with the modern boundary — force is where the tradition and the research both say stop.
- **The alternative layer.** The chapter contains the archive's version of scripture-criticism in miniature: *there is no hand-gazing method in Buddhism — that is qigong or martial practice; choose dantian-gazing* — the same genre as the [Anapanasati criticism](/experiences/notes/exp-028-abdominal-breathing/) and the [body-contemplation correction](/experiences/notes/exp-029-the-human-body/): the archive draws its own borders against neighboring practice worlds. And the chapter's terminology question — the demon-subduing sit (降魔坐, right leg below) and lotus sit (莲花坐) as the two sides' trials — is the same vocabulary the [pain ledger's](/experiences/notes/exp-030-the-pain-ledger/) readers were caught disputing among themselves; the official chapter is where the site can point: the archive's own usage, stabilized.
- **The convergence point, stated plainly.** Beneath the doctrine, the chapter's procedural core coincides with ordinary prudence on the points this site can state without endorsing any mechanism: **gradual** (single before double, drills before force, 盘腿也不可能一次到位 — the posture cannot arrive in one step, take it slowly, anything double-lotus counts); **dosed** (sit until you cannot hold it — the time does not matter; split sessions work; never after meals; close down slowly; beginners not outdoors); and **body-signaled** (the end of a session is a quality, not a clock — the same rule the site's [timer question](/experiences/questions/should-i-use-timers/) states from the tradition's side). The [cross-legged question](/experiences/questions/must-i-sit-cross-legged/) keeps the site's own boundary: the archive requires double lotus *on its own physiology's authority*; the site answers the practice question without importing the doctrine.

## Why it is filed here

Because this is the chapter that the [pain ledger](/experiences/notes/exp-030-the-pain-ledger/) — the NetEase repost of this very thread — was excerpted from, and the site now holds both ends of that transmission: the repost on the community side and the original official chapter here, eight sections where the repost had one. It is also the archive's most complete answer to the site's [cross-legged question](/experiences/questions/must-i-sit-cross-legged/), the source of the [lotus-pain line](/experiences/patterns/leg-numbness-pain/), and the clearest single statement of the archive's posture theology — with the one sentence the site treasures most kept in the middle of it: *I don't know why our body is like this.* The archive that teaches the hardest posture in its tradition also writes down, in the same chapter, the limit of its own explanation.
