## The exchange

**The readers (paraphrased per the archive's rule — no verbatim quoting, no usernames):** one asks how "wine and meat pass the gut, the Buddha stays in the heart" squares with practice, and whether meditation requires celibacy and fasting; another asks what difference it makes to eat animals versus plants — if plants are alive too, and their fruit is their children and their stems their bodies, why is eating them not killing; a third asks the practical question — how to get enough protein, iron, and calcium on a vegetarian diet; and a community reader adds a caution from another forum: an eight-year unbalanced vegetarian who ended up diabetic, and the advice that beginners with jobs and families need protein, reduced gradually.

**The teacher's replies, in their own structure:** (1) to the sloganeer — a flat refusal to answer "pure theory" questions, and the forum's own completion of the slogan: "if the world imitates me, it walks into the demon-path"; (2) the ontology — animals have consciousness and eating them becomes karma; plants do not, and "don't ask how I know: like the earth orbiting the sun, plants grow of themselves when the conditions are met"; (3) the gate — meat-eating cannot reach liberation, cannot even enter the desire-realm dhyana; abstaining from meat is *the first step* of practice; those who stop killing and abstain will be reborn in the Four Heavenly Kings heaven; (4) the dream-test — the meat-habit is measured in dreams, in three stages: hungry in the dream, offered meat, and you eat it (the habit is not dead, even after years of abstention); later, you hesitate and still eat (practice insufficient); finally, hungry, offered meat, and you refuse — "I am a disciple practicing pure conduct; I would rather die than eat the flesh of sentient beings" — and this marks the completed abstention, with the frame: from beginning abstention to passing the dream-test takes at least seven years, give or take by the person; the sutras add that after seven years of abstention the devas stop avoiding you and begin to draw near and protect; (5) the eye-test — for a practitioner, look at the eyes first: meat-eaters' eyes carry a killing air, whatever their years and robes; the completed abstainer's eyes are soft and bright, the body without gloom, a faint fragrance; (6) the Buddha's two-stage narrative — the early teaching allowed meat, the early disciples spread a small-vehicle Buddhism that does not abstain; late in life the Buddha taught the great vehicle, saying the earlier meat was produced by his own powers and without consciousness, but after his nirvana no one has such powers, so eating meat is now bad karma — 杀心不除，尘不可出 (the Shurangama: while the killing mind is not removed, the dust cannot be escaped); the Mahayana spread north, which is how it reached China; (7) the honesty rule — you may eat meat, but do not spread the false doctrine that "threefold-pure meat" is fine; say the truth: "eating meat is wrong, and I have not yet extinguished the habit" — that itself is spreading the right teaching; those who want to eat and invent excuses, pushing the blame onto the Buddha, are making karma — "would a man who cut his flesh to feed a hawk and gave his body to a tiger be a meat-eater?"; (8) the nutrition exchange — to the protein question: "nonsense — ten years of one meal a day, vegetarian, no dairy and no soy milk, the best health of my life, no colds for seven or eight years, the spirit can rebuild the body; believe you'll be malnourished and you will be; believe the vegetarian practice brings merit and no illness and you will be healthy" — then the corrections: the body needs a transition period ("sudden abstention leaves you starry-eyed"); tofu daily is fine; no coffee, cola, or tea — anything addictive cannot coexist with samadhi, caffeine speeds the heart, and Pu'er tea is the choice for those who need detox, drunk in quantity in the meditation halls.

## Three layers, kept apart

- **The practitioner layer.** The readers' questions and the community's cautionary notes: the diabetes report from another forum, the gradual-protein advice, the seven-year practitioner's account of the diet naturally lightening (heavy food becoming unwelcome, no deficiency problems). All registered as community testimony, unverified by the archive.
- **The teacher layer.** The gate claim, the dream-test, the seven-year frame, the eye-test, the two-stage Buddha narrative, the honesty rule, and the nutrition answers — all registered as the teacher's teaching, at the same evidentiary level as the rest of the series. The frame is explicitly Buddhist: the Four Heavenly Kings, karma, the Shurangama, the great-vehicle spread — and the archive keeps the site's [standing boundary](/experiences/notes/exp-013-buddhist-daoist-boundary/): this is a Buddhist monastic dietary doctrine, recorded as a doctrine of its tradition, not adopted by the site.
- **The alternative layer.** The forum's own counter-voices (the gradual-protein reader, the diabetes caution, the slogan-completer) are registered as the community's internal corrections — the teacher's own exchange *contains* its moderating voices, and the archive keeps them visible rather than flattening the episode into a single doctrine.
- **Health claims, quoted without endorsement.** "Ten years one meal a day with the best health of my life, no colds in seven or eight years," "the spirit can rebuild the body," "believe you'll be healthy and you will be," the eye-test's physical markers, and the Pu'er detox claim are the teacher's claims, recorded as claims — not medical statements — with the archive's [non-medical notice](/experiences/questions/can-meditation-cure-illness/) standing. The dream-test's physiology (the eye's killing air, the body's fragrance) is registered as **lay-physiology** — the teacher's own idiom, not anatomy.
- **The convergence point, stated plainly.** The teacher and the cautious readers agree on the one point the archive elevates: *change gradually* — the body needs a transition period, sudden abstention starves it, protein should be reduced step by step, tofu is fine. That is ordinary nutritional prudence wearing both a monk's robe and a reader's caution, and the [diet question](/experiences/questions/does-diet-matter/) files it under the site's convergence rule.
- **The unusual claims.** The Four Heavenly Kings rebirth and the dream-test cross-link to the archive's [extraordinary-claims handling](/experiences/notes/exp-016-nine-dhyanas-map/) per the standing rule; the archive records them as the teaching's content without taking them on.

## Why it is filed here

Because this is the series' *diet gate* — the first systematic statement, in the site's archive, of a food doctrine attached to sitting practice, and the natural counterpart of the classical answer: the [xinzhai passage](/translations/xinzhai-passage/) demotes the food-fast ("that is the fasting of the sacrifice, not the fasting of the mind"), and this episode reinstates it, in a Buddhist frame, as the first step. The site's [diet question](/experiences/questions/does-diet-matter/) holds the two answers side by side without forcing a merger — and this page supplies the evidence that makes the comparison possible. It also extends the archive's [anti-display line](/experiences/notes/exp-021-prostrations-channel-circuit/) with a new instrument: the dream-test, by which even the practitioner who abstains for years is measured not by the plate but by the dream — the tradition's own way of saying that the habit is what happens when no one is watching.
