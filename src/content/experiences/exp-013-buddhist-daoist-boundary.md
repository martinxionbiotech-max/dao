## Why this record matters

This is the archive's record of the **boundary as lived**: three practitioners at different intensities — penance-prostrations, forced full lotus, hybrid visualization — and one teacher's answers, of which the centerpiece is the doctrinal line between Buddhist and Daoist sitting. The site has documented the historical borrowing (the [Zhiyi page](/people/zhiyi/), the [five-adjustments timeline](/timeline/xiao-zhiguan-five-adjustments/)) and the mixing question (the [combine page](/experiences/questions/combine-zuowang-mindfulness/)); this record adds the *community* register — what the boundary sounds like inside a working practice tradition, told by a teacher who enforces it.

## The record (paraphrased, anonymized)

- **The reports:** a practitioner in a prostration-penance phase (asking whether sweat extinguishes the self-mind, whether all suffering comes from attachment); a second who forced full lotus within months, found the left leg without sensation, stood on sunny hilltops doing full practice sets, and counted prostrations in the ten thousands; a third who sat whole days on one sweet potato, sat through drowsiness into a faint-joy state with no memory of self or place, felt a lower-dantian vibration stronger than the heartbeat, and layered a self-invented visualization — returning to age seven at the third eye, drawing sun-moon essence through the crown, a lotus of light opening on the inhale — onto the assigned breathing method, and asked whether a closing procedure was needed.
- **The teacher's counsel, in its own terms:** prostrations, breathing, and sitting together, with prostrations first; forced lotus before relaxation corrodes the knee — stop sitting, practice breathing standing, slow prostrations, walk through the leg pain, continue until belly-warmth reaches the knees; drowsiness is hypoxia — abdominal breathing (~10 breaths a minute) must be built before sitting; the dantian vibration is the water-fire not yet descending — open the abdominal muscles; the visualization is far too complex — before the channels open, simply observe the dantian; **and the boundary**: Buddhist practice does not inhale qi or absorb plant or sun-moon essence, because inhaling posits an inside and an outside and a subject and an object — it violates no-inner-outer-division; we attend only to our own body and consciousness; with the coda that Daoist theory can reach the fourth dhyāna but practice rarely passes the second, because all its work is on the body, and at the cold-warm perception or the eye faculty one resonates with the heavens and departs.
- **The convergence kept, the divergence kept:** the forced-lotus injury warning aligns with modern overuse-injury understanding — a genuine practice-convergence the [posture question](/experiences/questions/must-i-sit-cross-legged/) already carries. The hypoxia and reactive-oxygen accounts are lay-physiology, recorded as the teacher's. And the inner-outer boundary is a living tradition's line, not a history of the borrowing — both registers stay visible, per the [three-languages](/blog/three-languages/) discipline.

## The three layers, kept apart

The practitioners' reports (devotion, injury, invention), the traditional framework (the teacher's physiology and the doctrinal boundary), and the alternative reading (medical caution for unexplained drowsiness, the historical record of Buddhist–Daoist vocabulary traffic) are each reported as given. What the site carries forward: the injury warning (stop forcing the posture), the periodization (breathing before sitting), the simplification (observe the dantian, drop the lotus), and the boundary as a *living* fact — a tradition's self-description, not the site's own ruling.
