**Anonymized note (paraphrased from public community Q&A).** A practitioner reports that during sitting they would spontaneously belch and pass wind, and that after these episodes the stomach felt noticeably settled and comfortable — the effect attributed to the sitting itself rather than to anything eaten.

**Teacher's traditional reading (as recorded).** The body is 'regulating itself' in the first stage of practice; the digestive system adjusts as accumulated tension releases, expelling what was stagnant.

**Why this note matters.** Digestive-release reports are a stable, low-drama fixture of Chinese sitting communities — and a useful counterweight to the more spectacular warmth-and-qi reports: they anchor the experience system's claim that *many* early-practice phenomena are the body's ordinary regulatory systems operating under changed conditions. The physiological reading (parasympathetic activation, mechanical effects of slow breathing on the gut) and the traditional reading (expelling stagnation) describe the same events from different vocabularies; both are recorded, neither asserted.

**Classification.** Anonymized community report, medium confidence. No clinical claim made; persistent digestive symptoms warrant medical evaluation regardless of practice context.
