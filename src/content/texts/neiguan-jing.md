**Direct answer:** The *Neiguan Jing* (内观经, "Scripture of Inner Observation," full title *Taishang Laojun neiguan jing*) is a Sui–Tang Daoist scripture on inner observation (*neiguan*): "still the spirit and settle the mind, so that no wild thoughts arise" (靜神定心，亂想不起). It is the bridge text between the Han visualization methods of shouyi and the Tang systematization of zuowang — scholarship (Ding Peiren) holds that Sima Chengzhen's *Zuowang lun* directly builds on it, along with the *Qingjing Jing* and the *Dingguan Jing*.

## Dating and provenance

- Author unknown; scholarly dating: late Northern-Southern Dynasties to early Tang (隋唐之际).
- Connected with the Louguan (楼观) tradition of northern Daoism, with influence from Shangqing materials of the south.
- Preserved in the Ming Daoist Canon (洞神部本文类) and in *Yunji Qiqian* juan 17 (Tang encyclopedia).
- Attributed to Lord Lao (太上老君) — a traditional attribution, recorded as such.

## What the text teaches

1. **The premise:** the human mind is originally clear and still; it is polluted by the six emotions (六情) and sinks into the sea of suffering. "If the mind is clear and still, the ten thousand misfortunes do not arise" (心若清靜，則萬禍不生). Therefore cultivation is the cultivation of the mind.
2. **The method:** "The way of inner observation is to still the spirit and settle the mind, so that no wild thoughts arise and no false notions intrude; with eyes closed, search within, so that inside and outside are both empty and still" (內觀之道，靜神定心，亂想不起，邪妄不侵，周身及物，閉目思尋，表裡虛寂).
3. **The graduated emptying:** a sequence of emptying terms — empty the mind, no-mind; settle the mind, calm the mind; still the mind, rectify the mind; clear the mind, purify the mind (虛心無心、定心安心、靜心正心，清心淨心).
4. **Mind and body together:** inner observation is paired with bodily cultivation — keep jing and qi from scattering, so that "form and spirit merge with the Dao" (形神合道).
5. **The somatic map:** the text contains a detailed anatomy of inner deities and qi-flow (the *niwan* palace in the head, the five viscera spirits, the spirit offices) — the surviving trace of the visualization tradition into which the new, more abstract inner observation is set.

## The neiguan–zuowang link

Tang and Song sources explicitly equate the two: the *Daoshu* records Lü Dongbin asking "What is inner observation?" and Zhongli Quan answering, "It is what is called zuowang" (是所謂坐忘者也). This is the single clearest traditional statement that the two terms name one practice family. Modern scholarship (Ding Peiren) confirms the textual dependence: the *Zuowang lun*'s stages presuppose the *Neiguan Jing*'s vocabulary and structure.

## Interpretation cautions

- The text mixes registers: abstract emptying (Buddhist-influenced) sits alongside a concrete deity-and-qi anatomy (Shangqing heritage). Reading it as purely philosophical or purely technical flattens it.
- "Inner observation" here is not ordinary introspection: it is a *non-grasping* observation in which observed and observer both empty out — continuous with the *Qingjing Jing*'s ladder.

## Editorial synthesis

The *Neiguan Jing* is the missing middle term in the history this site documents: shouyi (Han visualization) → neiguan (Sui-Tang inward observation) → zuowang (Tang seven stages) → inner alchemy (Tang-Song onward). It shows the tradition itself renaming and re-absorbing its practice across centuries — evidence that "Chinese meditation" is one continuously reinterpreted lineage rather than several unrelated techniques.

**Uncertainty is preserved:** the Louguan attribution and the exact relationship between the *Neiguan Jing* and the *Dingguan Jing* rest on scholarly reconstruction, not direct evidence of transmission.
