**Direct answer:** The *Huangting neijing jing* (黄庭内景经, "Scripture of the Inner Landscape of the Yellow Court") is the early medieval scripture that systematized the **three dantian** (三丹田) — upper, middle, lower cinnabar fields — and thereby fixed the inner geography of Chinese contemplative practice for the next seventeen centuries. It is a visualization text: its practice is *cunsi* (存思), holding images of inner palaces and resident spirits, not the quiet emptying of the later stillness tradition. But its *map* outlived its method: the dantian system it codified became the default reference of internal alchemy and of modern sitting practice alike.

## The book

- **Title:** 黄庭 ("Yellow Court") — the inner space at the center of the body, imagined as a court or hall; 内景 ("inner landscape/scenery") distinguishes it from a companion 外景 (outer landscape) recension.
- **Form:** verse in heptasyllabic lines, dense with coded anatomy and spirit-names; the received text runs in sections keyed to the inner regions.
- **Attribution:** traditionally given to Lady Wei Huacun (魏华存, d. c. 334), a matriarch of the Shangqing (Highest Clarity) revelations. Dating is contested — proposals range from the late Han to the 4th century; the scholarly consensus places the received text in the 3rd–4th century CE.
- **Its signature line:** 三田之中精气微 — "within the three fields, the essence-qi is subtle" — the sentence that anchors the whole dantian system (see the [dantian glossary](/glossary/dantian/) for the full map).

## What it contributes

- **The fixed map.** Upper field (head/眉心), middle field (heart), lower field (below the navel) — three centers where essence (jing), qi, and spirit (shen) reside and transform. Later alchemy's great formula — refine jing to qi, qi to shen, shen to emptiness — presupposes this geography.
- **A practice of images, not of emptiness.** The Huangting's method is to *see* the inner court, its colors, its residents. The stillness tradition (zuowang, xinzhai) that this site centers runs in the opposite direction — emptying the field of images rather than filling it with them. The two streams coexist through the tradition; the Huangting documents the imagistic pole.
- **The source of a persistent confusion.** Because the dantian map is *traditional doctrine* — a contemplative geography, not an anatomical finding — modern warmth-sensation reports ("my lower dantian heated up") inherit an interpretive fork: traditional practitioners read the map as real inner physiology; modern observers read the sensations as body-mind events on which the map is overlaid. The [patterns page](/experiences/patterns/warmth-and-qi-sensations/) keeps the two readings separated, as this text page does.

**Uncertainty preserved:** author unknown, date contested, text layered. The Huangting is cited here as what it is — a received scripture of contested date whose vocabulary shaped the later tradition — not as a historical report.
