**Direct answer:** The *Qingjing Jing* (清静经, full title *Taishang Laojun shuo chang qingjing jing*, "Scripture of Constant Clarity and Stillness Spoken by the Most High Lord Lao") is a short scripture of under 400 characters that became one of the most recited texts in Daoism — first in the *Xuanmen* daily recitation canon (《玄门日诵早晚功课经》). Its thesis: the mind is naturally clear and still; desire disturbs it; clear the desires and the mind stills itself; then empty even the emptiness, and "if people can be constantly clear and still, heaven and earth all return to them" (人能常清靜，天地悉皆歸).

## Dating and attribution

The text claims to be Lord Lao's teaching, recorded by Ge Xuan after oral transmission — a standard Daoist attribution pattern. Scholarship dates the composition to the Six Dynasties or early Tang, noting clear influence from Madhyamaka Buddhist dialectics (the "emptying emptiness" ladder) and from early Tang Chongxuan (重玄, "Twofold Mystery") thought. The title and the claimed transmission line are traditional; the composition date is scholarly (labeled accordingly).

## The argument, in its own terms

1. **The Dao's negative theology:** "The great Dao has no form, yet gives birth to heaven and earth; no feelings, yet moves sun and moon; no name, yet nourishes the myriad things" (大道無形，生育天地；大道無情，運行日月；大道無名，長養萬物).
2. **The diagnosis:** "The human spirit likes clarity, but the heart-mind disturbs it; the human heart-mind likes stillness, but desires pull at it" (夫人神好清，而心擾之；人心好靜，而欲牽之).
3. **The remedy:** "If you can always dispel desire, the mind stills itself; if you can clarify the mind, the spirit clears itself" (常能遣其欲，而心自靜；澄其心，而神自清).
4. **The emptying ladder:** observe the mind — "the mind is not a mind"; observe the body — "the body is not a body"; observe things — "things are not things." Then: "Observe emptiness as also empty; emptiness has nothing to be emptied. What is emptied is already nothing; nothing is also nothing. Nothing being nothing, one rests in profound silence" (觀空亦空，空無所空。所空既無，無無亦無。無無既無，湛然常寂).
5. **The return:** from this stillness, respond to things — "constant responding, constant stillness: this is constant clarity and stillness" (常應常靜，常清靜矣).

## Why it matters for this knowledge base

- It is the most concise statement of the *removal* logic (dispel desire → mind stills itself) that the site's root texts establish (Daodejing 48; Zhuangzi 4).
- Its emptying ladder (mind → body → things → emptiness → emptiness of emptiness) is the clearest surviving example of Buddhist Madhyamaka dialectics absorbed into Daoist contemplative writing — a documented case of cross-tradition technique transfer.
- It shows the *recitation* function: the text was chanted daily, meaning the emptying ladder functioned as a repeatedly internalized script, not only as doctrine.

## Reception

Placed at the head of the daily recitation canon; commented on by numerous masters from the Tang onward; quoted across Daoist lineages, including inner-alchemy literature. Modern scholarship (e.g., the Taoist Association's critical edition) documents textual variants (e.g., 心扰之 vs. 情挠之), evidence of the text's wide copying history.

## Editorial synthesis

If the *Zhuangzi* supplies the phenomenology of emptying and the *Daodejing* the program, the *Qingjing Jing* supplies the *formula* — compressed, chantable, and endlessly commented. Its "observe that the observer is also empty" movement anticipates, in a different register, later non-dual contemplative instruction across traditions; but the genealogy is historical, not an equivalence claim.

**Uncertainty is preserved:** the composition date range is a scholarly inference; the traditional attribution is a religious claim, recorded here as such.
