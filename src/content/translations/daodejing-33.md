## Chinese text

知人者智，自知者明。胜人者有力，自胜者强。知足者富，强行者有志。不失其所者久，死而不亡者寿。

## Pinyin

Zhī rén zhě zhì, zì zhī zhě míng. Shèng rén zhě yǒu lì, zì shèng zhě qiáng. Zhī zú zhě fù, qiáng xíng zhě yǒu zhì. Bù shī qí suǒ zhě jiǔ, sǐ ér bù wáng zhě shòu.

## Literal rendering

To know others is wisdom; to know oneself is clarity. To win over others is strength; to win over oneself is power. To know sufficiency is wealth; to force the walk is purpose. To not lose one's place is endurance; to die and not be gone is long life.

## Term-by-term notes

- **The four-pair structure.** Each line is a matched pair — outer term first, inner term second — and each pair's verdict is the same: the inward version outranks the outward. 知人 (knowing others) gets 智 (wisdom); 自知 (knowing oneself) gets 明 (clarity). 胜人 gets 力 (force); 自胜 gets 强 (strength). The chapter is the book's most compact account of its whole practice: the *turn* — the same verbs, aimed home. The site's [seeing family](/glossary/jian-du/) — 观, 见独, 朝彻 — is this turn's vocabulary, and 自知者明 is its Daodejing charter.
- **明, the practice's word.** 明 is not brightness; it is the unclouded seeing the site's [light page](/problems/seeing-light-in-sitting/) keeps distinct from literal light — the 虚室生白 whiteness of an emptied room. Wang Bi's gloss makes the ranking explicit: knowing others is *only* wisdom; knowing oneself is *above* wisdom. The tradition's commentarial mainstream reads the chapter as the book's self-knowledge doctrine — the [settling](/concepts/jing/) that clears.
- **强行 — the forcing that isn't.** 强行者有志 is the chapter's famous difficulty: the book of [wuwei](/concepts/wuwei/) praising *forced walking*? The tradition's reading (Wang Bi: 勤能行之 — those who can practice it diligently) resolves it as perseverance, not coercion: the only forcing the book endorses is the forcing of *oneself to continue*. The site pairs it with ch. 64's 千里之行 — the [how-long question](/questions/how-long-until-results/) holds both as the tradition's own answer to the timetable question: the walk is forced, the distance is not promised.
- **死而不亡者寿 — the sharpest claim.** The chapter's close is the book's doctrine of survival in one line: to die and not be gone. The tradition's reading, registered in verification: what survives is the *Way* one held (身没而道犹存 — the body gone, the Way remains), not the person. The site's [stillness question](/questions/what-is-stillness/) and [death pages](/stories/drumming-basin/) keep this line: the book's immortality claims are about what does not belong to any of us, not about escaping the body's end. 寿 (long life) is redefined as *not-lost* — the same grammar as ch. 45's 大巧若拙: the highest value wears the face of its opposite.
- **不失其所者久.** The one non-paired clause: 所, one's place — the standing-ground the site's [settled sitting](/questions/how-long-should-i-sit/) translates as consistency, not posture. Endurance is a place, not a speed.
