## The passage

梓庆削木为鐻，鐻成，见者惊犹鬼神。鲁侯见而问焉，曰："子何术以为焉？"

对曰："臣工人，何术之有！虽然，有一焉。臣将为鐻，未尝敢以耗气也，必齐以静心。齐三日，而不敢怀庆赏爵禄；齐五日，不敢怀非誉巧拙；齐七日，辄然忘吾有四枝形体也。当是时也，无公朝，其巧专而外骨消。然后入山林，观天性；形躯至矣，然后成见鐻，然后加手焉；不然则已。则以天合天，器之所以疑神者，其由是与！"

## Translation (with notes)

**Artisan Qing carved a bell-stand** (梓庆削木为鐻). **When it was finished, those who saw it were astonished, as if it were the work of spirits** (见者惊犹鬼神). **The Marquis of Lu came to see it and asked: "What art have you used to make this?"** (鲁侯见而问焉，曰：子何术以为焉)

**He replied: "Your servant is a workman — what art would I have? But there is one thing. When I am about to make a bell-stand, I never let my qi scatter** (臣将为鐻，未尝敢以耗气也) **— I fast, to still the mind** (必齐以静心).

**"After fasting three days, I no longer dare to hold thoughts of congratulations, rewards, rank, or salary** (齐三日，而不敢怀庆赏爵禄). **After fasting five days, I no longer dare to hold thoughts of blame or praise, skill or clumsiness** (齐五日，不敢怀非誉巧拙). **After fasting seven days, suddenly I have forgotten my four limbs and my body** (齐七日，辄然忘吾有四枝形体也).

**"At that time there is no court and no ruler** (当是时也，无公朝) **— my skill is single and the outer world has fallen away** (其巧专而外骨消). **Then I go into the mountain forest and observe the heaven-nature of the trees** (然后入山林，观天性). **When the right form is present in the wood** (形躯至矣) **— then the bell-stand is already seen in it, and only then do I lay on my hand** (然后成见鐻，然后加手焉). **If not, I let it be** (不然则已).

**"This is heaven joined with heaven** (则以天合天). **That is why the vessel is suspected of being the work of spirits** (器之所以疑神者，其由是与)!"

## Word-by-word notes

- **未尝敢以耗气也** — "I never dared to let my qi scatter." The precondition is framed as *non-loss*: the craftsman protects his qi the way a practitioner protects stillness. Effort is spent preventing scatter, not generating force.
- **必齐以静心** — "must fast, so as to still the mind." 齐 here is 斋, fasting — the same word as xinzhai's "fasting of the mind" (see [xinzhai translation](/translations/xinzhai-passage/)). The craftsman's fast is the mind-fasting applied to work.
- **辄然** — "suddenly, all at once." The forgetting of the body is not wrestled into existence; it arrives when the earlier layers are gone. The stages are cumulative subtraction, and the last layer falls by itself.
- **外骨消** — literally "the outer bones dissolve." 骨 here is traditionally read as the scaffolding of external concerns; with the court gone, the outside world's hold on the work collapses.
- **观天性** — "observe the heaven-nature." The only positive action in the whole passage: after emptying, *looking*. The work is selection and release, not imposition.
- **则以天合天** — "then heaven joins heaven." The passage's formula for skill: one's own emptied nature meeting the material's nature, with no forcing between. The Dazheng chapter's craftsman-version of wuwei.

## Why this translation matters

This is the Zhuangzi's own stage-map of emptying — three days, five days, seven days — anticipating the Tang system of [zuowang](/concepts/zuowang/) by half a millennium, and using the same final formula (forgetting the body) as the [ch. 6 passage](/translations/zuowang-passage/). Where ch. 6 states the doctrine, ch. 19 walks it, day by day, in a workshop.

**Translation problems:** 鐻 (jù) is a frame for hanging bells/chimes — no clean English word; "bell-stand" is the usual choice. 骨 in 外骨消 is contested (some read it as "slip," 滑, in a variant text); the received reading above is the standard one. Both issues are flagged rather than silently resolved.
