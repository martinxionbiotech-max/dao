## Chinese text

谷神不死，是谓玄牝。玄牝之门，是谓天地根。绵绵若存，用之不勤。

## Pinyin

Gǔ shén bù sǐ, shì wèi xuán pìn. Xuán pìn zhī mén, shì wèi tiāndì gēn. Mián mián ruò cún, yòng zhī bù qín.

## Literal rendering

The valley-spirit does not die — this is called the mysterious female. The gate of the mysterious female — this is called the root of heaven and earth. Thread-thin and thread-thin, as if existing; drawn on, never exhausted.

## Term-by-term notes

- **The valley.** 谷神 — the spirit of the valley, or the valley-like spirit. The valley is the book's standing image for the [empty](/concepts/xu/) place that gathers: the [bellows](/glossary/tuoyue/) of ch. 5 works because it is hollow, and the [reversals](/translations/daodejing-22/) teach that the low place fills. The valley-spirit is the generative power *located in the hollow* — the chapter's first claim is that the source is not a summit but a depth. The [xuan-pin glossary](/glossary/xuan-pin/) carries the term's full anatomy; this page is the chapter around it.
- **The mysterious female.** 玄牝 (xuán pìn) — the book's most explicit maternal image, held in full on the [xuan-pin page](/glossary/xuan-pin/). Three images in one sentence — valley, spirit, mother — and the received gloss keeps it plain: the source that breeds the ten thousand things, an image of the Way. The site's [creator question](/experiences/questions/does-daoism-believe-in-a-creator/) reads this chapter as the book's alternative to a maker: not a designer but a *mother*, not an act but a *birth*.
- **The gate and the root.** 玄牝之门，是谓天地根 — the gate of the mysterious female is the root of heaven and earth. The [ch. 1 translation](/translations/daodejing-01/) holds the book's other gate (众妙之门, where the mysteries come out); this one is the only gate named as a *root* and attached to a mother. Gate and root are the chapter's one architecture claim: the world comes out of an opening, and what it comes out of is what it grows from — the same two directions the [大块](/glossary/da-kuai/) page documents for the Zhuangzi's great clod (breathing out the wind, carrying and releasing the forms).
- **绵绵若存 — thread-thin, as if existing.** The chapter's closing sentence is the tradition's canonical description of the source's felt quality, and the site's [practice pages](/practices/jingzuo/) use it as the standard sentence for what a settled sitting feels like: not vivid, not absent — continuous, as if existing. It is the direct counter to the beginner's demand for sensation, which is why the [nothing-happens question](/experiences/questions/i-feel-nothing-when-i-sit/) opens with it: the measured state of the source is *barely there*, and the [falling-asleep question](/experiences/questions/falling-asleep-during-meditation/) distinguishes it from sleep by exactly this — continuous, as opposed to gone.
- **用之不勤 — drawn on, never exhausted.** 勤 is read as 尽 (exhausted) per the standard gloss recorded on the xuan-pin page. The arithmetic is the [ch. 22](/translations/daodejing-22/) reversals' own: what seems barely there is what never runs out. The Zhuangzi's ladder answers it from the other side — 杀生者不死，生生者不生 ("what kills the living does not die; what generates life is not born," [the Nü Yu passage](/translations/yingning-passage/)) — the two books saying the same thing: the source outlives everything it generates, because it is the generating itself.
- **Not anatomy.** Later internal-alchemy readings locate 玄牝 inside the body; the site does not adopt them, per the boundary kept on the [xuan-pin page](/glossary/xuan-pin/) and the [source policy](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/).

## Where this passage sits

The book's shortest chapter and its densest cosmogony — with [ch. 1](/translations/daodejing-01/) (the gate where mysteries come out) and [ch. 14](/translations/daodejing-14/) (the shape of the shapeless), it forms the site's set on the book's non-object: the gate, the mother, and the continuous — three names for the one thing that cannot be named.
