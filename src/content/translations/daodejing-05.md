## Chinese text

天地不仁，以万物为刍狗；圣人不仁，以百姓为刍狗。天地之间，其犹橐籥乎？虚而不屈，动而愈出。多言数穷，不如守中。

## Pinyin

Tiāndì bù rén, yǐ wànwù wéi chúgǒu; shèngrén bù rén, yǐ bǎixìng wéi chúgǒu. Tiāndì zhī jiān, qí yóu tuóyuè hū? Xū ér bù qū, dòng ér yù chū. Duō yán shuò qióng, bùrú shǒu zhōng.

## Literal rendering

Heaven and earth are not benevolent — they treat the ten thousand things as straw dogs. The sage is not benevolent — he treats the hundred surnames as straw dogs. The space between heaven and earth — is it not like a bellows? **Empty, and not exhausted; set in motion, and more comes out.** Much speech runs out quickly — it is better to guard the center.

## Term-by-term notes

- **刍狗 (chúgǒu, "straw dogs"):** the sacrifice-object made of woven straw, honored while the rite lasts and discarded after — Wu Cheng's gloss, preserved in the commentaries, is the standard reading: the heaven-and-earth *does not cling* to what it makes, and neither should the sage. 不仁 here is the chapter's hardest word: not cruelty but **the absence of favoritism** — 天地无心于爱物, "heaven and earth have no heart set on loving things." The [ziran](/concepts/ziran/) concept page keeps this as the tradition's anti-favoritism claim: the world runs on its own, and the sage's indifference is the *correctness* of scale, not coldness.
- **橐籥 (tuóyuè, "bellows"):** the site's [tuoyue glossary](/glossary/tuoyue/) gives the full term (the leather bag and the blow-pipe); here the verse does its work: 虚而不屈 — empty, yet never used up — and 动而愈出 — the more it moves, the more comes out. Wang Bi's commentary draws the parallel to the Dao: the bellows has nothing of its own, so it can never be exhausted. This is the [xu](/concepts/xu/) concept page's founding image: emptiness as *capacity*, not absence.
- **多言数穷 (duō yán shuò qióng, "much speech quickly exhausts"):** the warning against the storehouse-mind's opposite — the mouth and the policy that never stop issuing. The variant 多闻 ("much learning") appears in some editions, and the page keeps both; either way, the contrast is with the center that follows.
- **守中 (shǒu zhōng, "guard the center"):** the line the whole sitting tradition inherits. The commentaries are explicit that this 中 is **not** the Confucian "middle way" — it writes through to 冲, the empty: 守中 = 守住虚静, "guard the empty stillness." The state's own study column records the same reading: 中通"冲"，指内心的虚静，不是儒家所谓的中庸. What is guarded is the bellows's interior — the empty center from which the motion comes.

## Where this passage sits

Chapter 5 is the book's physics of emptiness, and its last line is the site's shortest bridge from the [Daodejing](/texts/daodejing/) to the sitting manuals: "guard the center" is what the [sitting protocol](/tools/sitting-protocol/) operationalizes, and "empty yet inexhaustible" is what the [stillness question](/experiences/questions/what-is-stillness/) answers with. Paired with [ch. 25](/translations/daodejing-25/) (the cosmology: the self-so order) and [ch. 16](/translations/daodejing-16/) (the practice: empty to the utmost), the three translations together hold the site's DDJ spine.
