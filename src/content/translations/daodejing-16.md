## The passage

致虚极，守静笃。万物并作，吾以观复。夫物芸芸，各复归其根。归根曰静，静曰复命。复命曰常，知常曰明。不知常，妄作凶。知常容，容乃公，公乃王，王乃天，天乃道，道乃久，没身不殆。

## Translation (with notes)

**Reach emptiness at the limit** (致虚极), **keep stillness firmly** (守静笃).

**The ten thousand things arise together, and I watch them return** (万物并作，吾以观复). **Flourishing in their multitudes** (夫物芸芸), **each returns to its own root** (各复归其根).

**Returning to the root is called stillness** (归根曰静). **Stillness is called returning to one's destiny** (静曰复命). **Returning to one's destiny is called the constant** (复命曰常). **Knowing the constant is called clarity** (知常曰明).

**Not knowing the constant, one acts blindly — disaster** (不知常，妄作凶).

**Knowing the constant, one is all-embracing** (知常容); **all-embracing, one is impartial** (容乃公); **impartial, one is kingly** (公乃王); **kingly, one is of heaven** (王乃天); **of heaven, one is of the Dao** (天乃道); **of the Dao, one endures** (道乃久). **To the end of one's days, no peril** (没身不殆).

## Word-by-word notes

- **致虚极，守静笃** — the four characters that became the motto of the entire quiet tradition (the compound 虚极静笃 is still printed on practice-hall walls). 致 ("extend, carry to") is a verb of *arrival*: emptiness is a limit to be reached, not a state to be manufactured. 笃 ("firmly, unwaveringly") tells you the stance: not a flash of quiet, but quiet *kept*. Note the pairing: emptiness is reached (致), stillness is guarded (守) — two different verbs, two different tasks, one practice.
- **吾以观复** — "I watch the return." The first-person is emphatic: this is *watching*, observation, not control. The chapter's core claim: if you sit at the empty-still point, the movement of things *toward their root* becomes visible. You do not make things return; you watch them do it.
- **归根曰静** — "returning to the root is stillness." Stillness is not defined here as the absence of motion (things are moving — returning!) but as the direction of motion: back to the root. Stillness = the process of return itself.
- **静曰复命** — "stillness is returning to destiny/mandate" (命). The word 命 (the allotted life, one's own nature as given) makes the return *homeward*: to be still is to be re-assigned to what one is. The modern renderings "fate" or "destiny" risk importing predestination; "one's own nature as given" is closer.
- **妄作凶** — "blind action: disaster." The chapter's only warning, and it is aimed precisely at *action from non-knowledge* — the noise-making the whole tradition defines itself against.
- **The ladder** (容→公→王→天→道→久): knowing the constant does not stop at insight; it cascades through character (embracing → impartial → kingly → heavenly → of the Dao) to *endurance* — "to the end of one's days, no peril." The contemplative chapter ends on the note of *lasting*, not of bliss.

## Why this translation matters

This is the Daodejing's method chapter — the one that says *how* (empty to the limit, keep stillness, watch), not just *what* (the Dao is x). Every later practice text on this site stands on it: Zhuangzi's fasting ([xinzhai](/translations/xinzhai-passage/)) is its elaboration, the craftsmen's training ([three craftsmen](/blog/three-craftsmen-dazheng/)) is its demonstration, and [ziran](/concepts/ziran/) is its target — the things returning by themselves, 复命曰常, *that* is the self-so order.

**Translation problems:** 王 in the ladder is often read as "kingly/royal" (received text) though some manuscripts have 全 ("whole"); the reading above follows the received text and flags the variant. 复命's 命 has no clean English equivalent ("destiny," "mandate," "allotted nature" each carry different baggage) — the note above states the choice rather than hiding it.
