## Chinese text

其安易持，其未兆易谋；其脆易泮，其微易散。为之于未有，治之于未乱。合抱之木，生于毫末；九层之台，起于累土；千里之行，始于足下。为者败之，执者失之。是以圣人无为故无败，无执故无失。民之从事，常于几成而败之。慎终如始，则无败事。是以圣人欲不欲，不贵难得之货，学不学，复众人之所过，以辅万物之自然而不敢为。

## Literal rendering

What is at peace is easy to hold; what has not yet shown its omen is easy to plan for; what is brittle is easy to split; what is minuscule is easy to scatter. Deal with it before it exists; govern it before it is disordered. A tree that takes both arms to embrace is born from a downy tip; a terrace nine layers high rises from baskets of earth; a journey of a thousand li begins under the foot. The doer ruins it; the grasper loses it. Therefore the sage does not do, and so does not ruin; does not grasp, and so does not lose. People, in their affairs, always ruin them at the point of near-completion. Be as careful at the end as at the start, and there are no ruined affairs. Therefore the sage desires the not-desired, does not prize goods hard to come by, studies the not-studied, and turns back the crowd's overshoot — helping the ten thousand things be of-themselves, and not daring to act.

## Term-by-term notes

- **The four early stages.** 安 (at peace), 未兆 (omen not yet shown), 脆 (brittle), 微 (minuscule) — the chapter's opening is the book's doctrine of the *early*: everything is manageable before it is manifest. 为之于未有，治之于未乱 — act on it before it exists, govern before disorder — is the [ch. 63 translation's](/translations/daodejing-63/) own program (图难于其易: the difficult is a late stage) stated as law. The site keeps the two chapters as the book's matched pair on timing: ch. 63 says start with the easy; ch. 64 says start before there *is* one.
- **The three staircases.** 合抱之木，生于毫末；九层之台，起于累土；千里之行，始于足下 — tree from downy tip, terrace from earth-baskets, thousand-li journey from under the foot. The book's accumulation grammar, in three registers: plant, building, road. The last line is the tradition's most portable proverb (it survives intact in modern Chinese, with its own encyclopedia entry the site verified), and the site's [practice pages](/practices/jingzuo/) read it as their own founding sentence: the [day-count caveat](/questions/how-long-should-i-sit/) and the [feel-nothing question](/questions/i-feel-nothing-when-i-sit/) both answer to it — a practice is not built by intensity but by *baskets*, one sitting after another, each one a step from under the foot.
- **The finish-line warning.** 民之从事，常于几成而败之 — people ruin their affairs at near-completion. The chapter's middle is its sharpest practice sentence, and the site keeps it against the modern habit of quitting when results fail to arrive: the dangerous point is not the start (everything is easy to start) but the *near-end* (everything is easy to ruin there). 慎终如始 — be as careful at the end as at the start — is the chapter's one-line cure, the [stillness family's](/translations/daodejing-45/) evenness applied to time.
- **The disputed couplet.** 为者败之，执者失之 — the doer ruins, the grasper loses. The edition the site verified records the tradition's own doubt: one line of scholarship holds this couplet (and the closing 而不敢为) to be misplaced strips from [ch. 29](/sources/) — the site registers the doubt without adjudicating, per its [edition discipline](/translations/daodejing-22/). Whatever its home, the couplet is the [wuwei](/concepts/wuwei/) doctrine's negative arithmetic: non-doing is not passivity but the *refusal to ruin* — and the closing 以辅万物之自然而不敢为 (help things be of-themselves and not dare to act) is the [ziran concept](/concepts/ziran/)'s own chapter-ending: the sage's entire curriculum is the removal of the overshoot (复众人之所过 — turn back the crowd's overshooting).
- **欲不欲，学不学 — the double negation.** The chapter's last pair is the book's grammar of [subtraction](/translations/daodejing-48/) in desire and study alike: desire what is not desired, study what is not studied. The site holds it against both readings — asceticism (want nothing) and anti-learning (study nothing) — because the grammar is plain: the sage's wanting and studying are *redirected*, toward the not-wanted and the not-studied, which is exactly what the [practice pages](/practices/zuowang/) call the emptying.

## Where this passage sits

With [ch. 63](/translations/daodejing-63/) (the early, the easy, the small) and [ch. 48](/translations/daodejing-48/) (the daily lessening), ch. 64 completes the book's practice trilogy: start early, work small, hold to the end — and the whole doctrine is the accumulation the three staircases name.
