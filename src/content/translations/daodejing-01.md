## Chinese text

道可道，非常道；名可名，非常名。无名，天地之始；有名，万物之母。故常无欲，以观其妙；常有欲，以观其徼。此两者，同出而异名，同谓之玄，玄之又玄，众妙之门。

## Pinyin

Dào kě dào, fēi cháng dào; míng kě míng, fēi cháng míng. Wú míng, tiāndì zhī shǐ; yǒu míng, wànwù zhī mǔ. Gù cháng wú yù, yǐ guān qí miào; cháng yǒu yù, yǐ guān qí jiào. Cǐ liǎng zhě, tóng chū ér yì míng, tóng wèi zhī xuán, xuán zhī yòu xuán, zhòng miào zhī mén.

## Literal rendering

The Way that can be spoken is not the constant Way; the name that can be named is not the constant name. **The unnamed** is the beginning of heaven and earth; **the named** is the mother of the ten thousand things. Therefore: **constant without desire**, one observes its subtlety; **constant with desire**, one observes its boundary. These two emerge together and differ in name — together they are called the dark. Dark, and darker again: the gate of all subtleties.

## Term-by-term notes

- **道可道 — the founding untranslatability.** The first 道 is the Way; the second is the verb "to speak." The chapter opens the book — and this site's whole [translation policy](/blog/translation-policy/) — by warning that the saying is not the thing: 常道, the *constant* Way, is precisely what the sayable cannot hold. The [dao glossary](/glossary/dao/) keeps the term; the [zhuang-zhou page](/people/zhuang-zhou/) records how the tradition turned this opening into a style of talking that never claims to finish.
- **The two punctuation debates — both kept.** The line 无名天地之始 can be cut 无，名天地之始 ("the nameless — call it the beginning of heaven and earth": Wang Bi's reading, per the commentary tradition) or 无名，天地之始 ("the unnamed is the beginning": the received cutting). Likewise 常无欲以观其妙 vs 常无，欲以观其妙 — "constantly without desire, observe its subtlety" or "the constant-nothing: with desire, observe its subtlety." Both cuttings are ancient, both are documented in the teaching editions, and the site records both without ruling — the same [double-writing](/translations/daodejing-25/) discipline used for the 王/人 variant in ch. 25.
- **观其妙 / 观其徼 — the two observations.** 妙, the subtle, the unmanifest interior of things; 徼 (jiào), the boundary, the edge where the manifest runs out. The standard glosses (preserved in the commentary transmission) read them as the two directions of the [guan](/glossary/guan/): from the empty side one sees the mystery, from the full side one sees the limit. The chapter is the site's founding text for the observation-word the whole practice tradition later builds on.
- **玄之又玄 — dark, and darker again.** The [xuan glossary](/glossary/xuan/) carries the term: 玄 is the dark of depth, not of absence — the two (unnamed/named, or the two observations) share one root (同出而异名) and their shared root is what the chapter doubles. The doubling is the chapter's own refusal to let the dark be a *thing* one can finally name — the same recursion [ch. 48's 损之又损](/translations/daodejing-48/) applies to subtraction.
- **众妙之门 — the gate, not the house.** The chapter ends at a *door*. The book's first page positions itself as an entrance: what the gate opens onto is the remaining eighty chapters. The site's [reading order](/tools/reading-order/) is built on this modesty — the opening chapter promises access, not arrival.
