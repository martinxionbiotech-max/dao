## Chinese text

太上，不知有之；其次，亲而誉之；其次，畏之；其次，侮之。信不足焉，有不信焉。悠兮其贵言。功成事遂，百姓皆谓"我自然"。

## Pinyin

Tài shàng, bù zhī yǒu zhī; qí cì, qīn ér yù zhī; qí cì, wèi zhī; qí cì, wǔ zhī. Xìn bù zú yān, yǒu bù xìn yān. Yōu xī qí guì yán. Gōng chéng shì suì, bǎixìng jiē wèi "wǒ zìrán."

## Literal rendering

The highest: **they do not know there is one.** Next: they love him and praise him. Next: they fear him. Next: they insult him. Where trust is insufficient, there is no trust. How unhurried — his scarce words! When the work is done and the task accomplished, the hundred clans all say: **"We did it ourselves, of ourselves."**

## Term-by-term notes

- **The four ranks, measured by awareness.** The ladder descends as the ruler's *visibility* rises: not known to exist → loved and praised → feared → insulted. The best government is the one whose subjects cannot find it. This is the [wuwei concept](/concepts/wuwei/) in political form — the same logic the [ch. 37 translation](/translations/daodejing-37/) states as paradox (do nothing and nothing is left undone), here stated as *profile*: the ruler's absence from awareness is the achievement. The site keeps the ranking because it reverses every ordinary theory of leadership in one sentence: praise is second-best, because praise means the ruler is seen.
- **The variant, double-written.** Some received texts read 太上，**下**知有之 ("the highest: the *next-to-lowest* know of him"), others 太上，**不**知有之 ("they do not know there is one"). The site's [double-writing discipline](/translations/daodejing-25/) applies: both readings recorded, neither adjudicated — but the 不知有之 cutting is the one that carries the chapter's logic, since the four ranks would otherwise start at the same awareness-level as the second.
- **悠兮其贵言 — unhurried, and words are scarce.** The ruler's speech is *costly* in the root sense of 贵: rare, precious, sparing. The [ch. 22 translation](/translations/daodejing-22/)'s four 不自 are the same economy applied to reputation; here it is applied to speaking itself. For the site's [silence pages](/concepts/xu/) the line is canonical: the tradition's distrust of talk is not aesthetic — it is the same arithmetic as the reversals, what is not spent is not exhausted.
- **百姓皆谓"我自然" — the book's most beautiful political line.** When it all works, the people say *we did it ourselves* — and the phrase is the [ziran concept](/concepts/ziran/)'s own word: 自然, self-so, of-itself. The chapter's trick is that this is **the ruler's highest praise** — the moment the people take credit for what the ruler's non-presence made possible. The ruler vanishes, the people say "we did it," and the book says: exactly. No other political text in the tradition gives its ruler this vanishing as the trophy.

## Where this passage sits

Ch. 17 is the Daodejing's statement of the invisible best — the political face of the same arithmetic the site documents in [ch. 37](/translations/daodejing-37/) (the paradox) and [ch. 22](/translations/daodejing-22/) (the reversals). The three chapters together form the book's argument that the highest form of anything is the one you cannot see doing it — ruler, or sitter.
