## Chinese text

是其始死也，我独何能无概然！察其始而本无生，非徒无生也而本无形，非徒无形也而本无气。杂乎芒芴之间，变而有气，气变而有形，形变而有生，今又变而之死。是相与为春秋冬夏四时行也。人且偃然寝于巨室，而我噭噭然随而哭之，自以为不通乎命，故止也。

## Pinyin

Shì qí shǐ sǐ yě, wǒ dú hé néng wú gǎirán! Chá qí shǐ ér běn wú shēng, fēi tú wú shēng yě ér běn wú xíng, fēi tú wú xíng yě ér běn wú qì. Zá hū mángwù zhī jiān, biàn ér yǒu qì, qì biàn ér yǒu xíng, xíng biàn ér yǒu shēng, jīn yòu biàn ér zhī sǐ. Shì xiāngyǔ wéi chūnqiū dōngxià sìshí xíng yě. Rén qiě yǎnrán qǐn yú jùshì, ér wǒ jiàojiào rán suí ér kū zhī, zì yǐwéi bù tōng hū mìng, gù zhǐ yě.

## Literal rendering

When she first died, how could I alone not be shaken? But then I looked back to her beginning: before there was life, there was no life at all; and not only no life, but no form; and not only no form, but no qi. Mingled in the blur and dimness, something changed and there was qi; the qi changed and there was form; the form changed and there was life; and now there is another change, and death. These follow one another like the four seasons, spring, autumn, winter, summer. Now she lies at rest in the great chamber, and if I followed her wailing and weeping, I would show myself not understanding the decree of things. So I stopped.

## Term-by-term notes

- **概然 (gǎirán, "shaken"):** the text's first word about grief — one reading takes 概 as the wooden leveling-board (the heart unleveled), the other as the sigh of choked breath. Either way: the text begins from *being* shaken, which is the fact the later dispute circles. The [story page](/stories/drumming-basin/) reports both classical and modern readings; this translation keeps the plain sense.
- **本无生/本无形/本无气 (before life, before form, before qi):** three negations in descending order — the text does not say "nothing existed," it says each *layer* of what a person is was once absent. The sequence is the cosmology: qi is the first thing that *arrives* (变而有气 — "changed, and there was qi").
- **芒芴 (mángwù):** the "dimness and blur" — the pre-qi medium, the same compound the [qiwulun](/translations/qiwulun-wu-sang-wo/) uses for the state before differentiation. Not a god, not a void: an unformed hum.
- **变 (biàn, "change"):** four times in one breath — the verb that does all the work. The [peng-bird](/stories/peng-bird/) opening (fish into bird) and this passage share it: the tradition's one-word cosmology. Each 变 is a passage, not a replacement; the wife is not gone, she is *further along the sequence*.
- **偃然寝于巨室 (lying at rest in the great chamber):** 偃然 — settled, recumbent, at ease; 巨室 — the great room, the universe as the house. The word choice is deliberate and domestic: death as *resting at home*. The translator must not upgrade it to "slumbering in the cosmos" — the plainness is the point.
- **不通乎命 (not understanding the decree):** 命 here is the given order of things — the same term as the [ziran](/concepts/ziran/) family's "self-so." The text's claim is cognitive, not merely emotional: to wail on is to *misread* the event. Whether the composure that follows is equanimity or resignation is left open by the text itself — the page keeps it open.
- **故止也 (so I stopped):** three characters, the entire practice in miniature. The stopping is a *decision* made of an *understanding* — the same structure as the [zhizhi](/translations/daodejing-16/) "return" and the [zuowang](/practices/zuowang/) "forgetting": seeing, then ceasing.

## Where this passage sits

This is the theoretical core of the Zhile chapter — the book's chapter *on* "perfect joy" — and the fullest single statement in the *Zhuangzi* of the transformation cosmology. It is the text behind the [drumming-basin story page](/stories/drumming-basin/), and the site's standing citation for qi as the medium of change.
