## Chinese text

道常无名，朴。虽小，天下莫能臣。侯王若能守之，万物将自宾。天地相合，以降甘露，民莫之令而自均。始制有名，名亦既有，夫亦将知止。知止可以不殆。譬道之在天下，犹川谷之于江海。

## Pinyin

Dào cháng wúmíng, pǔ. Suī xiǎo, tiānxià mò néng chén. Hóu wáng ruò néng shǒu zhī, wànwù jiāng zì bīn. Tiāndì xiāng hé, yǐ jiàng gānlù, mín mò zhī lìng ér zì jūn. Shǐ zhì yǒumíng, míng yì jì yǒu, fú yì jiāng zhī zhǐ. Zhī zhǐ kěyǐ bù dài. Pì dào zhī zài tiānxià, yóu chuān gǔ zhī yú jiāng hǎi.

## Literal rendering

The Way is ever nameless — uncarved block. Small though it is, nothing in the world can make it a subject. If lords and kings could hold it, the ten thousand things would arrive as guests of themselves. Heaven and earth join, and sweet dew falls; no one commands the people, and they level themselves out. Once cutting begins, there are names; names having already appeared, one should also know where to stop. Knowing where to stop, one can be free of danger. The Way in the world is like streams and valleys flowing to river and sea.

## Term-by-term notes

- **The punctuation split, registered.** The two verified reproductions differ on the opening: one reads 道常无名，朴 ("the Way is ever nameless — uncarved block," the block as the nameless thing itself) and the other's unpunctuated form admits the traditional reading 道常无名，朴虽小… ("the Way is ever nameless; the block, small though it is…"). The site registers both without adjudicating, per its [variant discipline](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/), and notes what both readings share: the block *belongs to* the nameless — 朴 is not a name for the Way; it is the shape the nameless takes before names.
- **臣 — the political verb.** 天下莫能臣: "no one in the world can subject it." The character is the chapter's hinge: the Way cannot be made a *subject*, an official, a servant of any court — because a thing must be named to be summoned. The [ch. 1 translation](/translations/daodejing-01/) holds the opening claim (the nameable name is not the constant name); this chapter is the political consequence: whatever can be named can be conscripted, so the nameless is the one thing no power can press into service. The site's [ch. 13 translation](/translations/daodejing-13/) holds the companion freedom on the self's side (无身, the self with no position to be attacked).
- **The 自 series.** 自宾 (arrive as guests *of themselves*) and 自均 (level *themselves*) are the chapter's proof-clause, and they belong to the book's most important prefix: 自 — self- — which the site's [ziran page](/concepts/ziran/) holds as the book's root concept (自然, of-itself-so). The political claim is precise: when the ruler holds the nameless block, order is not *imposed*; things arrive and level of their own motion. The [ch. 37 translation](/translations/daodejing-37/) holds the same grammar one page later in the received order (万物将自化 — the ten thousand things transform of themselves).
- **始制有名 — naming is a cut.** 制 is the carpenter's verb (to cut, carve, institute — the same character as 制度, institutions), and the chapter is explicit that naming *begins* with a cut: the block is what exists before the first name, and every name is a cut into it. The chapter's famous answer is not "return to the nameless" but 知止 — *know where to stop*: once the names have appeared, the knowing of when to stop is the one discipline left. The site's [zhi-shui page](/glossary/zhi-shui/) holds the book's other stopping-word in the Zhuangzi's still-water image (止水, the water that stops and thereby mirrors); 知止 is the Laozi's version, and both stop not by refusing motion but by knowing the place.
- **The water ending.** 川谷之于江海 — streams and valleys to river and sea — is the book's standing water-politics (the [ch. 8 translation](/translations/daodejing-08/) holds the full water doctrine): the small flows to the great not by command but by *lowness*. The block and the water are the chapter's two images of the same thing: what cannot be conscripted (the nameless) and what cannot be summoned (the self-flowing) — and the chapter's own quiet joke is that both are images of what a ruler cannot hire.
