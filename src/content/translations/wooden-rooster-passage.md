## The passage

纪渻子为王养斗鸡。十日而问："鸡已乎？"曰："未也，方虚憍而恃气。"十日又问，曰："未也，犹应向景。"十日又问，曰："未也，犹疾视而盛气。"十日又问，曰："几矣。鸡虽有鸣者，已无变矣，望之似木鸡矣，其德全矣，异鸡无敢应者，反走矣。"

## Annotated translation

Ji Xingzi was training a fighting cock for the king. After ten days the king asked, "Is the cock ready?" — "Not yet. He is still **inflated with pride and leaning on his qi** (虚憍而恃气): *he performs his own strength; the puffing-up comes first, before anything real.*"

Another ten days, and the king asked again. — "Not yet. He still **answers to shadows and echoes** (犹应向景): *reacting to every movement and cry of other cocks — a pure reflex machine, all stimulus, no center.*"

Another ten days, and the king asked again. — "Not yet. He still **glances fiercely and brims with qi** (犹疾视而盛气): *the fighting intent itself is still in the eyes and the breath — the aggression hasn't been released, only suppressed.*"

Another ten days, and the king asked again. — "He is close now. **Even when other cocks crow, nothing in him changes; looking at him, he seems made of wood** (望之似木鸡). His *de* — his virtue, his complete power — is whole. No other cock will face him; they turn and run."

## Notes for the reader

- **The unit is ten days.** Forty days of subtraction, checked at fixed intervals — the passage is a training protocol in narrative form: four stages, each one the removal of a layer (display, reactivity, aggression), each verified by an outside observer.
- **The closing paradox carries the teaching.** The cock has become *invincible* by becoming *nothing to fight* — the same arithmetic as [zuowang](/concepts/zuowang/): subtract until only the whole remains, and the whole prevails. The word 德 (*de*) is used in its non-moral, power sense: integrity as *unbroken wholeness*, the site's [wuwei](/concepts/wuwei/) family.
- **"Like wood" is the goal, not the insult.** 木鸡 — the wooden cock — is the tradition's image of achieved stillness, so famous that "standing like a wooden chicken" entered ordinary Chinese as praise for composure (and, later, mockery — the story's two readings are both alive; this page keeps the Zhuangzi's, where it is the summit).
- **The same chapter, the other engine.** This passage is the subtraction pole; the chapter's [cicada-catcher](/translations/) and [Artisan Qing](/stories/artisan-qing/) are the concentration pole. The [Dazheng editorial](/blog/three-craftsmen-dazheng/) maps how one chapter holds both.

**Uncertainty preserved:** parable from the outer chapters (the Zhuangzi's middle stratum — see the [compilation timeline](/timeline/zhuangzi-compilation/)); the "ten days" is narrative rhythm, not a prescription.
