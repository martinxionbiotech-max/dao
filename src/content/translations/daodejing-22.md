## Chinese text

曲则全，枉则直，洼则盈，敝则新，少则得，多则惑。是以圣人抱一为天下式。不自见，故明；不自是，故彰；不自伐，故有功；不自矜，故长。夫唯不争，故天下莫能与之争。古之所谓"曲则全"者，岂虚言哉！诚全而归之。

## Pinyin

Qū zé quán, wǎng zé zhí, wā zé yíng, bì zé xīn, shǎo zé dé, duō zé huò. Shì yǐ shèngrén bào yī wéi tiānxià shì. Bù zìxiàn, gù míng; bù zìshì, gù zhāng; bù zìfá, gù yǒu gōng; bù zìjīn, gù cháng. Fū wéi bùzhēng, gù tiānxià mò néng yǔ zhī zhēng. Gǔ zhī suǒwèi "qū zé quán" zhě, qǐ xūyán zāi! Chéng quán ér guī zhī.

## Literal rendering

The bent — then whole. The crooked — then straight. The hollow — then full. The worn — then new. Little — then gained. Much — then confused.

Therefore the sage **embraces the One** as the realm's gauge. Not self-displaying, so clear; not self-asserting, so evident; not self-boasting, so meritorious; not self-proud, so enduring. Only through **not contending** can none under heaven contend with him. What the ancients called "the bent, then whole" — is that an empty saying! **Truly whole, and it comes back to you.**

## Term-by-term notes

- **Six reversals, one logic.** The chapter's engine is six pairs, each one the weak pole *yielding* the strong result: bend → wholeness, crooked → straight, hollow → full, worn → new, little → gain, much → confusion. The standard gloss (recorded in the Gushiwen teaching edition) reads them as the tradition's dialectic of yielding: 委曲保全, 虚怀若谷. The [wuwei concept page](/concepts/wuwei/) holds the family this belongs to — the paired reversals are non-action's arithmetic, the same sixfold grammar [ch. 45](/translations/daodejing-37/)'s neighbors use.
- **The manuscript variants, double-written.** The Mawangdui silk texts (as documented in the collations quoted during verification) read the opening pair as 曲则金，枉则定 — "the bent, then **gold**; the crooked, then **settled**" — and the middle line as 是以圣人**执一**以为天下**牧** ("holds the One as the realm's **shepherd**") against the received 抱一为天下式 ("embraces the One as the realm's **gauge**"). The site's [double-writing discipline](/translations/daodejing-25/) applies: both recorded, neither adjudicated — and the 金 reading is kept visible because its image (gold's ductility: bending without breaking) is the chapter's physics stated as metallurgy.
- **抱一 — embrace the One.** The verb is the [shouyi family's](/translations/daodejing-10-shouyi/) 抱, the same embrace as ch. 10's 抱一: the sage does not *apply* the reversals but holds the single thing underneath them. 天下式 — the gauge, the pattern, the instrument the realm is measured by: the chapter's word for what the sage *is* once the One is embraced.
- **The four 不自.** Not self-displaying → clear; not self-asserting → evident; not self-boasting → meritorious; not self-proud → enduring. Four negations, four positive results — the same self-emptying grammar the [xu concept page](/concepts/xu/) collects, here applied to reputation. Note the direction: the results are *given* (故), not seized.
- **诚全而归之 — truly whole, and it comes back.** The chapter's last line completes its own opening: 全 (wholeness) was the first reversal's reward, and the closing says the wholeness *returns*. The ancient saying 曲则全 is ratified not as wisdom but as fact — the book's most confident chapter-ending.

## Where this passage sits

Ch. 22 is the received text's densest statement of the yielding-logic, and the site places it between [ch. 37](/translations/daodejing-37/) (the paradox of non-action) and [ch. 10](/translations/daodejing-10-shouyi/) (the practice of holding the One): the paradox stated, the practice named, and here the arithmetic shown — six pairs of what bending buys.
