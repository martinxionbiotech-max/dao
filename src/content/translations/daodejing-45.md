## Chinese text

大成若缺，其用不弊。大盈若冲，其用不穷。大直若屈，大巧若拙，大辩若讷。躁胜寒，静胜热。清静为天下正。

## Pinyin

Dà chéng ruò quē, qí yòng bù bì. Dà yíng ruò chōng, qí yòng bù qióng. Dà zhí ruò qū, dà qiǎo ruò zhuō, dà biàn ruò nè. Zào shèng hán, jìng shèng rè. Qīngjìng wéi tiānxià zhèng.

## Literal rendering

The great accomplishment looks unfinished, and its use is never worn out. The great fullness looks hollow, and its use is never exhausted. The great straightness looks bent; the great skill looks clumsy; the great argument looks mute. Restlessness overcomes cold; stillness overcomes heat. The clear and still is the world's standard.

## Term-by-term notes

- **The five greats in their opposites.** 大成若缺 (accomplishment as unfinished), 大盈若冲 (fullness as hollow — 冲 is the [empty family's](/concepts/xu/) own word, the same as ch. 4's 道冲), 大直若屈, 大巧若拙, 大辩若讷 — five pairs, one grammar: the greatest instance of X *wears the face of* not-X. The chapter is the [ch. 22 reversals](/translations/daodejing-22/)' sibling (曲则全 — bend and be whole) grown into a typology: where ch. 22 gives the *dynamics* (the low pole wins), ch. 45 gives the *faces* (the great pole looks like the low one). The site keeps the two chapters linked as the book's matched pair on appearance: one says how the reversal works, the other says what the completed thing looks like.
- **大巧若拙 — the craftsman's chapter.** The 巧/拙 pair is the one the tradition's [craft pages](/stories/artisan-qing/) inherit: the finished skill looks like no skill at all — the [carpenter](/stories/yun-jin-cheng-feng/)'s axe-wind leaves the nose untouched, the [cook's](/stories/cook-ding/) knife falls through the joints as if doing nothing. The site cross-links the pair to the craft stories as the theory behind the workshop: mastery's final form is indistinguishable, to the unpracticed eye, from clumsiness.
- **The disputed couplet.** 躁胜寒，静胜热 — the final pairing is the chapter's most contested line, and the site registers the readings without adjudication: the plain-physiological (activity warms against cold, stillness cools against heat); the inner-state reading Wang Yijia records (inner restlessness overcomes *outer* cold, inner stillness overcomes outer heat — the inside, not the weather); and the psychological reading the site's verification also surfaced (restlessness *manufactures* heat — the agitated mind feels warm in the cold; stillness *measures* temperature truly — the still mind knows cold and heat as they are, and therefore 清静 is the standard). The received order 躁胜寒，静胜热 (restlessness-cold, stillness-heat) is kept as the base text, with the modern swapped arrangement recorded as a variant.
- **清静为天下正 — the book names the site.** The closing line is the tradition's definitional sentence on the [clear-and-still](/texts/qingjing-jing/): 正 here is the *standard*, the carpenter's square of the world — the chapter's last word is that stillness is not one state among others but the measure by which the rest are judged. The [qingjing-jing page](/texts/qingjing-jing/) (whose own title is this phrase's noun-form) and the [ch. 37 translation](/translations/daodejing-37/) (不欲以静 — not-desiring, and therefore still) and the [ch. 16 translation](/translations/daodejing-16/) (归根曰静 — the return to the root is stillness) form the site's stillness-trilogy, of which this line is the capstone: the root is stillness, the stilling is not-desiring, and the still is the standard.

## Where this passage sits

With [ch. 22](/translations/daodejing-22/) (the reversals) and ch. 41 (the great images: 大音希声，大象无形 — the site's [ch. 14 translation](/translations/daodejing-14/) cites it), ch. 45 completes the book's doctrine of the great-as-its-opposite — the chapter the craft stories and the stillness pages both answer to.
