## The passage

天下莫大于秋豪之末，而太山为小；莫寿乎殇子，而彭祖为夭。天地与我并生，而万物与我为一。既已为一矣，且得有言乎？既已谓之一矣，且得无言乎？一与言为二，二与一为三。自此以往，巧历不能得，而况其凡乎！故自无适有，以至于三，而况自有适有乎！无适焉，因是已。

Nothing in the world is larger than the tip of an autumn hair, and Mount Tai is small; no one lives longer than a child dead young, and Pengzu died young. Heaven and earth are born together with me, and the ten thousand things and I are one. Already one — how can there be anything to say? Already spoken as one — how can there be nothing said? The one and the saying make two; the two and the one make three. Going on from there, even the cleverest calculator cannot get to the end, let alone ordinary people. From nothing to something already makes three — how much more from something to something! Do not go anywhere; rest in the going itself.

## Why this passage matters

- **The chapter's own scale-bomb.** The Qi Wu Lun spends its length dismantling the tournaments — this versus that, right versus wrong, big versus small — and here it detonates the measuring rod itself: the tip of an autumn hair is *larger* than Mount Tai, and the longest-lived man in the tradition (Pengzu) died *young*. The claim is not a fact about physics; it is a fact about *measures* — every size and every lifespan is judged inside a frame, and the chapter has spent the whole page turning the frames. The site's [qiwu page](/glossary/qiwu/) holds the standing reading: this is the leveling in its purest form, and it is not relativism — the sentence does not say the hair and the mountain are the same size; it says the comparison has no place left to stand.
- **The oneness — and the trap in saying it.** The famous declaration follows at once: 天地与我并生，而万物与我为一. And then the chapter does what it always does: it catches its own sentence. If you *are* one with everything, there is nothing to say about it — but the moment it is *said*, the saying stands outside the one, and one becomes two, and two-plus-one becomes three, and the counting runs away forever (the verified journal article takes this exact puzzle as one of the chapter's hard passages). The site's [zhi-yan page](/stories/zhi-yan/) holds the book's general form of the same doctrine: every speech adds a shape, and the shape is not the thing. The oneness declaration is therefore not a creed to repeat but a place to *stop* — 无适焉，因是已: do not go anywhere, rest in the going itself. The site's [butterfly page](/stories/butterfly-dream/) shows the book's own preferred ending for this problem: not the sentence, but the dream.
- **The exit is the practice.** 因是已 — rest in *this* — is the chapter's recurrent exit, and the site's [tian-jun page](/glossary/tian-jun/) holds the balancing instrument it names. For the site's practice pages the passage works as a negative instruction: the measuring of one's sitting (how big, how long, how far) is the counting trap in miniature — the [progress page](/experiences/questions/what-counts-as-progress/) restates it in modern dress: measure practice, not attainment, because attainment-measuring is exactly the 一与言为二 machinery.
- **The smallness at the center.** Note which end of the scale the sentence chooses as the *largest*: not the mountain but the hair — the [peng-bird page](/stories/peng-bird/) holds the book's standing preference for the small and the near, and here it is the doctrine's summit. The smallest unit of the natural year (the fine new hair of autumn) is declared greater than the mountain the state worships: the book's entire politics of scale, in one clause.
