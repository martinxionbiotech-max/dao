## The story

Confucius, journeying westward to Wei, and his disciple Yan Hui asks the music-master Shi Jin how the Master's mission will fare. Shi Jin answers with the parable:

"Xishi had a pain in her heart, and frowned in her village. An ugly woman of the village saw it and found it beautiful; she went home and, holding her chest, frowned in her village too. When the rich of the village saw her, they barred their doors and stayed in; when the poor saw her, they took their wives and children and fled. **She knew the frown was beautiful, but she did not know why the frown was beautiful.**"

## Framing: the fable's original job is about institutions, not beauty

The idiom's afterlife (东施效颦, "Dongshi copies the frown" — the ugly neighbor's name and the four-character phrase both crystalize later, per the tradition's own dictionary record) has made it a general warning against blind imitation. The chapter's original aim is sharper:

- **The audience is Confucius.** The fable is Shi Jin's answer about whether the Master's mission — carrying the old institutions of Zhou into the new world — can succeed. The claim is not about cosmetics but about **rites and norms**: 礼义法度者，应时而变者也 — rites, duties, laws and measures are things that *change with the times*. Carrying them over unchanged is copying the frown without the ground that made it beautiful.
- **The pivot is 之所以 — the "whereby."** The fable's last clause is its whole point: she knew the frown was beautiful but not *why* it was beautiful (彼知颦美，而不知颦之所以美). Xishi's frown is beautiful *because Xishi is beautiful* — the gesture inherits its ground. The ugly neighbor copies the gesture without the ground, and the village empties. The [translation policy](/blog/translation-policy/) keeps this clause verbatim-friendly precisely because "whereby" is the site's standing question: what a practice's *ground* is.
- **The pair with the Handan walk.** The [Handan walk](/stories/handan-walk/) is the same warning from the loser's side (the imitator loses what he had); the frowning woman is it from the source's side (the beautiful thing cannot be transferred as a surface). Together they are the site's imitation-pair: the tradition's own two-part argument that practice transfers by *ground*, not by form — the exact principle behind the [posture question](/experiences/questions/must-i-sit-cross-legged/)'s treatment of copied forms.
