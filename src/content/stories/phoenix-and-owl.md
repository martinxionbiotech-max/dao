## The story

Hui Shi became prime minister of Liang. Zhuangzi went to see him. Someone told Hui Shi: "Zhuangzi is coming — he means to take your post." So Hui Shi, terrified, searched the state for him three days and three nights. Zhuangzi went to see him himself, and said:

"In the south there is a bird called the yuanchu — do you know it? The yuanchu sets out from the Southern Sea and flies to the Northern Sea; it will not **perch** except on the parasol tree, will not **eat** except the bamboo-seed, will not **drink** except the sweet spring. Now an owl had got hold of a rotten rat, and as the yuanchu passed overhead, the owl looked up and shrieked at it: '**Scram!**' — Do you mean to shriek *scram* at me over your Liang?"

## Framing: the shriek is the joke, and the friendship is the frame

- **吓 is the owl's own cry.** The word is onomatopoeia — the owl's shriek, hè — which Zhuangzi then redeploys as a transitive verb: "to shriek *scram* at." The fable's whole insult is built on the one sound that cannot be argued with, because it is not an opinion but a *possession reflex*: the owl does not believe the rat is fine food; it fears the rat will be taken. The modern reading tradition (recorded in the teaching editions) maps the three images — the yuanchu is the high-minded visitor, the owl the office-holder, the rotten rat the office — and the site keeps that map while noting what the source itself emphasizes: the comedy of the mismatch, not the moral of the phoenix.
- **The chapter's three Zhuangzi–Huizi episodes form one arc.** The [turtle of Pu](/stories/turtle-in-the-mud/) (the refusal of the Chu office), this passage (the refusal of Hui Shi's fear), and the [fish debate](/stories/fish-happiness/) (the friendship at its freest) — three registers of the same life. And the arc closes elsewhere in the book: the [carpenter Shi](/stories/carpenter-shi/) page records Zhuangzi at Hui Shi's *grave*, saying he has lost his whetstone — the man he mocked here is the man he mourned there. The site keeps the two pages cross-linked as the friendship's two ends.
- **The commentary tradition's gloss is the site's own discipline.** Guo Xiang's note on the passage, preserved in the Yihai Zuanwei: 言物嗜好不同，愿各有极也 — "creatures differ in what they savor; each desire has its limit." The yuanchu is not *better* than the owl; its three refusals (parasol, bamboo-seed, sweet spring) are its limit, exactly as the rat is the owl's. The passage is the [well-frog](/stories/jingdi-zhiwa/) chapter's lesson applied to appetite: the offense is not that the owl is low but that it *projects* — it measures the yuanchu by the rat.
