## The passage

北宫奢为卫灵公赋敛以为锺，为坛乎郭门之外，三月而成上下之县。王子庆忌见而问焉，曰："子何术之设？"奢曰："一之间，无敢设也。奢闻之：'既雕既琢，复归于朴。'侗乎其无识，傥乎其怠疑，萃乎芒乎，其送往而迎来，来者勿禁，往者勿止，从其彊梁，随其曲傅，因其自穷，故朝夕赋敛，而豪毛不挫，而况有大涂者乎？"

Beigong She was collecting taxes for Duke Ling of Wei to cast a bell, and built the stand outside the city gate, completing the upper and lower ranks of bells in three months. Prince Qingji saw it and asked him: "What technique did you set up?" She said: "In the space of the One, I dared set up none. I have heard: 'Carved and polished, and then returning to the uncarved block.' Blank as one without knowledge, drifting as one hesitating, gathering and vast — sending off those who go and welcoming those who come; those who come are not forbidden, those who go are not held; follow the strong and the overbearing, go along with the bent and the clinging; let each arrive at his own exhaustion. So collecting taxes from morning to evening, not a hair was bruised — how much more so, one who has the great road?"

## Why this passage matters

- **The most hated job, done by the least forceful man.** 赋敛 — tax collection — is the occupation the book's whole political line despises (it is exactly what the [empty boat](/stories/xuzhou-empty-boat/) does not do: it does not come *at* you). And the chapter's move is to hand that job to the man who sets up no technique at all — and have him finish in three months with 毫毛不挫, not a hair bruised. The [qiu-hao page](/stories/qiu-hao/) holds the hair-image in its scale-bomb form (the tip of an autumn hair, larger than Mount Tai); here the hair is the audit: the coercion-meter reads zero at the smallest unit.
- **一之间 — the technique that is not one.** Asked for his 术 (technique), Beigong She refuses the question's grammar: 一之间，无敢设也 — "in the space of the One, I dared set up none." The book's [wuwei page](/glossary/wuwei/) holds the general claim this instance instantiates: non-action is not no-doing but no-*forcing*, and the One is not a method to be installed. The phrase is the negative of every technique-peddler in the book — the [fisherman](/stories/ren-gongzi-fishing/) waited a year, and Beigong She waits out each contributor's own momentum.
- **既雕既琢，复归于朴 — the block, after all the carving.** The quoted line is the story's heart and the site's [ch. 32 translation](/translations/daodejing-32/) holds its companion text (朴 — the uncarved block, small, and nothing in the world can make it a subject). Beigong She's version adds the sequence the Laozi leaves implicit: the carving happens (the bell must be cast; the taxes must be collected — the work is real) and *then* the return to the block. 朴 here is not the untouched state but the finished state: the stand is complete, the hair unbruised, and the man still blank as one without knowledge.
- **The 自 grammar.** 因其自穷 — let each arrive at his *own* exhaustion — is the book's self-prefix in action, and the site's [ziran page](/concepts/ziran/) holds the prefix as the root concept: 自宾, 自均, 自化, and here the tax-payer's own momentum, followed rather than driven (从其彊梁，随其曲傅 — follow the overbearing, go along with the bent). The bell-stand is a lesson in the politics the site keeps returning to: the order that is not imposed is the order that completes — and the [hua page](/glossary/hua/) notes the same verb-family in the cosmos (things transform of themselves).
