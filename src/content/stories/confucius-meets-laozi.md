## The story

When Confucius went to Zhou to ask Laozi about the rites, Laozi said to him: "The men you speak of have long since rotted away with their bones; only their words remain. When a gentleman finds his time, he rides; when he misses it, he drifts with the wind and walks on. I have heard that a good merchant stores deep and looks empty, and a gentleman of full virtue looks foolish. Put away your pride and your many desires, your affected manner and your excessive ambition — none of these do your person any good. That is all I have to tell you."

Confucius returned and said to his disciples: "Birds — I know they can fly; fish — I know they can swim; beasts — I know they can run. What runs can be netted, what swims can be hooked, what flies can be shot. But the dragon — I cannot know how it rides the wind and cloud and rises to heaven. Today I have seen Laozi. He is like a dragon!"

## Framing: what this story is

This is the **founding encounter of the tradition, recorded four centuries after the fact.** The account stands in the *Shiji* (c. 100 BCE), Sima Qian's history — but the same chapter candidly admits the historians themselves did not know who Laozi was ("the world does not know which account is right"). The meeting of Confucius and Laozi is therefore recorded here as *traditional*: it may echo a real exchange between teachers of two schools, but as text it is Han-dynasty literature making a philosophical point — the Confucian sage himself deferring to the Daoist master.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

Three things in this short passage do real work for the whole tradition:

1. **The teaching content is pure subtraction.** "Put away your pride, your desires, your affected manner, your ambition" — the advice Laozi gives the younger sage is not a technique but a *removal list*, the earliest narrative form of the subtraction logic later compressed into Daodejing 48 ("pursuing the Dao, one subtracts daily").
2. **The dragon image fixes the contrast.** Confucius' taxonomy is knowledge by category — what runs, swims, flies, can be captured. The dragon evades category: Laozi is not describable within the grid of known skills. The image became the culture's standard figure for what lies beyond ordinary learning.
3. **The reverence runs both ways.** The story is often read as Daoist triumphalism, but its frame is Confucius' own voice praising Laozi — a meeting of respect, not a contest. The tradition preserved it that way.

**Uncertainty preserved:** historicity of the encounter is unverifiable; the dragon speech is recorded in Sima Qian's own text, but as reported speech of Confucius — tradition, not transcript.
