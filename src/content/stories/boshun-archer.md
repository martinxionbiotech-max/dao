## The story

Lie Yukou gave an archery demonstration for Bo Hun Wuren. He drew the bow to the full, set a cup of water on his elbow, and shot — arrow after arrow, one following the last before the first had struck, each new arrow set on the string as the previous left it. All the while he stood like a wooden statue.

Bo Hun Wuren said: "This is the shot of *shooting*, not the shot of *no-shooting*. Suppose you and I climbed a high mountain, walked out on a precarious rock, and stood over an abyss a hundred fathoms deep — could you still shoot?"

And he climbed the mountain, walked the precarious rock, stood over the hundred-fathom abyss, and backed toward the edge until two tenths of his foot hung in the air. Then he bowed Lie Yukou forward to join him.

Lie Yukou dropped to the ground. Sweat ran down to his heels.

Bo Hun Wuren said: "**The consummate person looks up into the blue sky, dives down to the yellow springs, casts his spirit through the eight extremes — and his spirit and qi do not change.** You now are trembling, with a dizzy look in your eye — your chance of hitting the target would be slim indeed!"

## Framing: the shot that is not a shot

- **射之射 and 不射之射 — the two shots.** The passage's hinge is a single distinction, and the site quotes it every time: 是射之射，非不射之射也 — "this is the shot of shooting, not the shot of no-shooting." The first shot is everything the [craft pages](/stories/artisan-qing/) document: skill so complete it looks like a statue (犹象人 — like a wooden man), the cup on the elbow that never spills. And the teacher calls it *the mere shot of shooting*. What it lacks is not skill but the *circumstance*: the [cicada-catcher's](/stories/cicada-catcher/) 用志不分 is trained in the mulberry grove, the [boatman's](/stories/boatman/) 忘水 is trained in the gorge — and the archer's stillness is trained on flat ground. The fable is the book's hardest line on training: **a skill untested at the edge is a skill unearned.**
- **The cliff is the exam.** Bo Hun Wuren does not argue; he *stages*. The mountain, the rock, the abyss, and the backing step until two tenths of a foot hang over nothing — 足二分垂在外 — and then the invitation. Lie Yukou, who shot like a statue, drops to the ground with sweat at his heels. The passage's target is not courage; it is the identity of the two skills: the [wooden-rooster page](/stories/wooden-rooster/) holds the book's subtraction (the trained bird looks like wood because everything extra has been removed), and this passage holds the addition's exam — the removal is only real where the fall is real. The site cross-links the two as the workshop's matched pair: subtraction trains, the edge tests.
- **神气不变 — the standard, named.** The teacher's closing sentence gives the book's word for what the test measures: 神气不变 — "spirit and qi do not change" — whether looking up into the sky, diving to the springs, or sweeping the eight extremes. It is the [stillness family's](/translations/daodejing-45/) own vocabulary at maximum scale: the [zuochi glossary](/glossary/zuochi/) holds the failure form (坐驰 — sitting while galloping, the spirit unsettled), and this passage holds the success form — the same 神气, undisturbed by a cliff. The dizzy eye (恂目之志) is the *inside* of the sweat: the body at the edge reports what the training did not remove.
- **The man who failed the test.** The passage names its archer Lie Yukou — the figure to whom the [Liezi](/texts/liezi/) is attributed — and lets him fail on camera, as the book's way of failing its own genre: the hero of this story is the one who *stands on the edge*, not the one who shoots on flat ground. The site keeps the identification with the [Liezi page](/texts/liezi/)'s uncertainty (the book and the man are distinct layers), and records the fable's own layer: an *outer* chapter passage, declared per the [layering rule](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/).

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

Because the site's [practice pages](/practices/zuowang/) teach on flat ground — and this passage is the book's own reminder that the practice is never finally tested until the edge. The [sitting protocol](/tools/sitting-protocol/) keeps the safety line; the fable keeps the honest line: the cliff is where you find out what the sitting removed.

**Uncertainty preserved:** an *outer* chapter passage (ch. 21, later stratum, declared); two independent full texts agree, with a CText parallel confirming the dialogue verbatim; 不射之射 rendered "the shot of no-shooting" (the standard reading); the passage is registered as fable — the book's account of a training principle, not a chronicle of the historical Lie Yukou.
