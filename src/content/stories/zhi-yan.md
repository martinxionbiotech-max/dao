## The passage

寓言十九，重言十七，卮言日出，和以天倪。

Lodged words, nine in ten; weighty words, seven in ten; goblet words pour out daily, harmonizing on the heavenly potter's line.

And the chapter explains each: the lodged words speak *through others* — "a father does not broker for his son" (亲父不为其子媒), because a father's praise of his son is suspected; the words must come from outside the family, from a borrowed voice. The weighty words rest on the elders — but only the elders who have the warp and weft, the root and branch: age without it is just an old person. And the goblet words: 卮言日出，和以天倪，因以曼衍，所以穷年 — daily, fresh, tipping into whatever the moment needs, and by this means, lasting out the years.

## Framing: the book's own instruction manual

- **The three registers, and the percentages.** The chapter is the book's self-description — the tradition has long read it as the closest thing the Zhuangzi has to an author's preface, and the Tsinghua close reading registers that consensus: lodged words (stories told through invented speakers — Confucius, Yan Hui, the craftsmen, this site's whole [story collection](/stories/cook-ding/)), weighty words (the authority of the aged and the canonical), and goblet words (the third register, the one that holds the other two together). Nine in ten and seven in ten are the book's own arithmetic of its material; the goblet words are not a percentage but a *mode* — 日出, they come out daily, the way the sun does.
- **The goblet is the argument.** 卮 is the ritual wine vessel, and the tradition's gloss (registered from the Guangming survey) is Guo Xiang's: full, it tips; empty, it rights itself — it never keeps its own shape. The goblet word is speech that holds *this* content now and empties for the next: the opposite of the fixed position, the -ism. The survey registers the alternative readings without adjudicating (Sima Biao's "branching words," Cheng Xuanying's "words without a mind," the modern "toast" and "jester" proposals) — the site keeps the goblet image as the common core: language that does not store.
- **The warrant sentence — why the book bets on its third register.** 非卮言日出，和以天倪，孰得其久 — *without the goblet words daily pouring, harmonizing on the heavenly line, who could last?* The chapter's own claim is that the fixed words age and the adapting words survive — and the site holds this against its own [translation policy](/blog/translation-policy/): the original is evidence and every rendering is testimony; the site's re-renderings of each chapter are its goblet words, poured for English readers, claiming no final shape. The [ch. 2 translation](/translations/daodejing-02/) of the site makes the same point from the Laozi side (the *xiang* pairs that generate each other).
- **The closing chain — words riding the same wheel as things.** The passage ends by fusing the speech-theory into the transformation-theory: 万物皆种也，以不同形相禅，始卒若环，莫得其伦，是谓天均。天均者，天倪也 — all things are seeds, passing forms to each other, beginning and end like a ring, no seam anywhere: this is the heavenly wheel, and the heavenly wheel *is* the heavenly line on which the goblet words harmonize. The site's [tian-jun page](/glossary/tian-jun/) already holds this wheel (the wheel, not the scale); this page adds the missing half: *the words are one of the forms riding it*. Speech is not outside the transformation; it is a thing among things, being reborn daily.
- **The note the tradition appends, kept.** The chapter's explanation of the lodged words contains the book's sharpest theory of its own reception: 与己同则应，不与己同则反 — what matches your view you accept, what does not you reject. The site's [qiwu page](/glossary/qiwu/) holds the same diagnosis from the other direction (the pivot that ends the tournament). The goblet words are the book's answer to that reflex: speech that changes its shape before the reader can fix it.
