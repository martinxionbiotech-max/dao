## The story

In Zheng there was a shaman named Ji Xian who could foretell death and birth, survival and ruin, fortune and misfortune, year and month — as surely as a god. The people of Zheng, seeing him coming, would drop everything and flee.

Liezi was dazzled by him, and said to his teacher Huzi: "I always thought your Way was the highest — but here is something higher still."

Huzi said: "I have shown you the ornament, not yet the substance. Come, bring him to me, and let him look at me."

The next day the shaman came and read Huzi. Going out, he told Liezi: "Alas, your master is dying — not ten days from now. I saw something strange: I saw wet ashes."

Weeping, Liezi reported this. Huzi said: "Just now I showed him the **earth pattern** — stilled, not stirring, not settling. He saw my virtue's mechanism blocked. Bring him again."

The next day the shaman returned and said: "Lucky man — your master has met me! He is recovering. There is life in him: I saw his blocked power stirring."

Huzi said: "Just now I showed him **heaven's soil** — names and substance did not enter, and the mechanism rose from the heel. He saw my goodness' mechanism. Bring him again."

The next day: "Your master is not settled," said the shaman. "I cannot read him. Have him steady himself, and I will read him again."

Huzi said: "Just now I showed him the **great surging without rival**. He saw my balanced-qi mechanism. Bring him again."

The next day the shaman came — and before he had even come to a stop, he lost himself and fled.

"After him!" said Huzi. Liezi ran, could not catch him, and reported: "He is gone — vanished, lost — I could not catch him."

Huzi said: "Just now I showed him that which had **never yet left my source**. I faced him with emptiness and yielded to him, bending like a blade of grass; he did not know who or what I was. Hence he fled."

Then Liezi knew he had not yet begun to learn. He went home, and for three years did not go out. He cooked for his wife, fed the pigs as if serving people, took no part in affairs, cast off ornament and returned to the unhewn, standing alone like a clod of earth amid the world — sealed in the unhewn, and in oneness to the end.

## Framing: what this story is

The shaman is a stock figure; the story is **parable, not history**. But it is the Zhuangzi's most explicit account of *stages of inner depth* — and it sits at the book's hinge, closing the final Inner Chapter (Ying diwang) as the culmination of the whole inner sequence. The chapter's coda — "the sage's mind is like a mirror: it does not go to meet things nor follow them away; it responds and does not store" (至人之用心若镜，不将不迎，应而不藏) — belongs immediately to this story's lesson.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

The four demonstrations are the tradition's earliest map of contemplative depth, and they are worth listing because later practice traditions (see [zuowang](/concepts/zuowang/)) built directly on this vocabulary:

1. **地文 (earth pattern)** — stillness so deep it resembles the stillness of earth; to an outside observer, it can look like lifelessness ("wet ashes"). Depth mistaken for death.
2. **天壤 (heaven's soil)** — the same stillness, but with the mechanism stirring "from the heel": stillness with life in it, invisible to the unskilled eye.
3. **太冲莫胜 (great surging, nothing prevails)** — balance so complete that no mark can be read on it at all; the observer's instruments give out.
4. **未始出吾宗 (never yet left my source)** — the person does not present any "state" at all. This is the passage's deepest claim: the final stage is not a deeper display but the withdrawal of display. The shaman flees because there is nothing to read.

Two cautions the text itself encodes: the stages are shown *by a master deliberately* — they are not achievements to chase — and the story ends not with the stages but with Liezi's three-year retreat into ordinary life: cooking, feeding pigs, dropping the project of being anyone special. The map is real; the destination is unremarkableness.

**Uncertainty preserved:** allegorical text of contested authorship; the stage-vocabulary is the tradition's own later elaboration built on this passage. The story is recorded as primary text, the stage-reading as TRADITIONAL / LINEAGE INTERPRETATION.
