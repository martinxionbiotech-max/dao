## The story

**Ran Qiu asked Zhongni: can anything be known about the time before heaven and earth existed?** (未有天地，可知邪？) Zhongni's answer is a single sentence, three words: **可。古猶今也。** — *Yes. The ancient is like the present.* Ran Qiu's question is dissolved on the spot — the text says 失問而退, he lost his question and withdrew.

The next day he is back, with the chapter's finest description of what an unanswerable answer feels like: *Yesterday I asked whether anything can be known of the time before heaven and earth; you said: yes, the ancient is like the present. Yesterday I was clear, today I am dim. May I ask what it means?* (昔日吾昭然，今日吾昧然). Zhongni's reply turns the diagnosis around — the clarity *was* the problem, or rather its location: *When you were clear, the spirit received it first; now that you are dim, you are searching for it through what is not spirit* (昔之昭然也，神者先受之；今之昧然也，且又爲不神者求邪). Then the four-character theorem that this passage contributes to the language: **无古无今，无始无终** — *no past, no present; no beginning, no end.* And the book's sharpest example, one sentence long: *for there to be children and grandchildren without there having been children and grandchildren — could that be?* (未有子孫而有孫子，可乎？) — as Xuan Ying's commentary unfolds it: children come from nothing into being all the time; why should heaven and earth be different?

Ran Qiu cannot answer. Zhongni closes the dialogue himself — and the closing is the chapter's doctrine, delivered as a sequence of refusals:

**不以生生死，不以死死生。** — *Do not let life give death to the living; do not let death give life to the dead.* Death does not kill the living, and life does not revive the dead; each is itself. **死生有待邪？皆有所一體。** — *Do death and life depend on each other? Each has its own integrity.* (Wang Xianqian: death and life do not wait on each other; each is a complete body.) Then the question that has been waiting under the first one: **is there something born before heaven and earth, and is it a thing? What things things is not a thing. Things do not emerge before things — and yet there are things** (有先天地生者，物邪？物物者非物，物出不得先物也，猶其有物也). The infinite regress that a material origin would require — a thing before things would itself need a thing before it — is cut off with one word: the origin of things is *not a thing*.

And the final turn is the one nobody sees coming: **犹其有物也无已，圣人之爱人也终无已者，亦乃取于是者也** — *because the having of things is without end, the sage's love of people is also without end: he draws it from this very source.* The chapter that began with cosmology ends with ethics, and says the two are one motion.

## Why it is told here

Because this is the chapter's **cosmology in miniature** — the passage that answers the oldest question in the book's universe (what was there before everything?) with the book's most economical tool: a grammar correction. The question "what was before heaven and earth" presumes a line, a before-and-after; the answer removes the line. Guo Xiang's note does it in nine words: heaven and earth always endure — *there was no time before they were*.

- **The answer is a time-denial, not a claim.** 无古无今，无始无终 is the site's [wu-shi-wu-zhong](/glossary/wu-shi-wu-zhong/) glossary entry in its original home — the four characters that cancel past and future, beginning and end, in one stroke. The dialogue's frame proves the point by its own shape: Ran Qiu asks about *the past* (before heaven and earth) and Zhongni answers about *the present* (ancient is like present). Time-talk is the trap; the [ziran](/concepts/ziran/) page holds the same move for the word "nature."
- **The child-and-grandchildren example is the whole argument.** It is not a metaphor but an observation: things come into being from no prior things all the time, in every generation. Xuan Ying's commentary makes the parallel explicit. The book's answer to the origin question is not a theory about the deep past; it is a fact about the visible present. The same move appears in the [four friends](/stories/four-friends/) story's "the great clod loads me with form" — the giver is present tense, not ancient history.
- **物物者非物 — the two-character refutation.** This is the passage's second contribution to the language: the agent that things things is not itself a thing. It is the answer to the site's [creator question](/questions/does-daoism-believe-in-a-creator/) in its most compressed form — the origin is real but not an object, not an item in the inventory. And it connects to the chapter's [Ran Qiu neighbor](/stories/dongguo-zi-asks/) and the same chapter's 通天下一气耳 — the whole world is one breath — both of which refuse to make the origin into one more member of the class it produces.
- **The last sentence is the surprise that makes the passage.** After all the negation, the text lands on *love* — 圣人之爱人终无已. The sage's unending love of people is not a warm addition to the cosmology; it is *drawn from it* (取于是者也): because things have no end, the sage's care has no end. The site's [drumming-basin](/stories/drumming-basin/) page marks the same structure — the text that dissolves death ends in the widest possible claim on the living. For the sitting tradition, this is the classical bridge between emptying the self and not despising other selves: [zuowang](/concepts/zuowang/)'s forgetting and this passage's love are the same grammar at different temperatures.
