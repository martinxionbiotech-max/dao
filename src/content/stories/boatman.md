## The story

Yan Hui asks Confucius: "Once I was crossing the deep of Shangshen, and the ferryman worked his boat like a spirit. I asked him, 'Can boat-handling be learned?' He said, 'Yes. A good swimmer can do it after a few tries. And a diver — one who has never even *seen* a boat — climbs straight in and handles it.' I asked him more, but he would not explain. May I ask what he meant?"

Confucius said: "A good swimmer can do it after a few tries because he has **forgotten the water**. As for the diver who handles a boat without ever having seen one — he sees the deep as a hill, and the boat's capsizing as his cart rolling backward. A thousand capsize-turns arrayed before him **cannot enter his abode** — how could he go anywhere without ease?

"Playing for **tiles**, one is skillful. Playing for **a silver belt-hook**, one is timid. Playing for **gold**, one goes stupid. The skill is one and the same — but when something is prized, the attention is **weighted to the outside**. **Whenever the outside weighs, the inside grows clumsy.**"

## Framing: the forgetting family's performance branch

- **忘水 — forgetting the water.** The ferryman's skill is the swimmer's forgetting applied to the craft: what is forgotten is precisely what threatens. The passage stands in the chapter the site has already mapped as the [workshop chapter](/blog/three-craftsmen-dazheng/) — [the cicada catcher](/stories/cicada-catcher/) fills the attention, [the wooden rooster](/stories/wooden-rooster/) empties it — and adds its own member to the forgetting family the site records elsewhere: the same chapter ends with 忘足，履之适也；忘要，带之适也；知忘是非，心之适也 — "forgotten feet are the shoe's fit; a forgotten waist, the belt's; forgetting right and wrong, the mind's." [Zuowang](/concepts/zuowang/) is this family's head; the ferryman is its practical arm.
- **The diver's abode.** 覆却万方陈乎前而不得入其舍 — a thousand capsizings cannot *enter* the diver's dwelling. The word 舍, the abode, is the same settled interior the site's [mirror passage](/translations/yongxin-ruo-jing-passage/) protects (应而不藏) and the [still water](/glossary/zhi-shui/) settles (唯止能止众止). Skill, in this chapter, is not the absence of threat but the interior no threat can enter.
- **The stakes are the diagnosis.** 以瓦注者巧，以钩注者惮，以黄金注者殙 — the three stakes (worthless tiles, a silver hook, gold) produce three performances from the *same* skill, and the chapter names the mechanism in one line the site quotes everywhere: 凡外重者内拙 — **whenever the outside weighs, the inside grows clumsy**. This is the tradition's own performance-anxiety theory: not a pathology of the timid but the structure of attention itself. It is also the answer to Yan Hui — the same Yan Hui of the [fasting of the mind](/translations/xinzhai-passage/) — and the answer's shape matches: the discipline is what one stops carrying.
