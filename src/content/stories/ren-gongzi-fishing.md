## The story

Lord Ren made an enormous hook and a thick black line, baited it with fifty oxen (五十犗以为饵), squatted on Mount Kuaiji, and cast his line into the Eastern Sea. He fished day after day — and for a full year caught nothing (期年不得鱼). Then a great fish took the bait: it dragged the hook, plunged down and surged up, rose with fins raised — white waves like mountains, the sea shaken, the sound like ghosts and spirits, terror for a thousand li. Lord Ren got his fish, split it and dried it, and from the River Zhi eastward to Cangwu northward, everyone ate their fill of it. Afterward, the clever-tongued commentators of later generations all startled and told each other about it (皆惊而相告也). But the man who raises a small rod, hurries to a ditch, and guards minnows — for him, catching a great fish is hard indeed. The one who trims a small tale to seek a county office is far from the great attainment. And those who have never heard of the Ren manner — they too are far from managing the world.

## Framing: the arithmetic of scale

- **The three absurdities, in order.** The bait (fifty oxen for one fish), the wait (a year of nothing), the result (waves like mountains, fear for a thousand li): the chapter stacks each scale past plausibility, deliberately — this is the book's *comedy of magnitude*, the same machine as the [Peng bird](/stories/peng-bird/) — nine-thousand-li wings against the dove's yard-hop. Ch. 26 is a *miscellaneous chapter* (declared per the [layering rule](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/)) — the book's later, wilder wing, where the jokes run longest.
- **The small-minded ones, and what they do with the news.** 后世辁才讽说之徒，皆惊而相告也 — the later clever-tongued retell the story in *amazement*: they get the spectacle, miss the arithmetic. Then the chapter's own diagnosis of them: 揭竿累，趋灌渎，守鲵鲋 — small rod, ditch, minnows — the [frog in the well](/stories/jingdi-zhiwa/) in fishing form: small equipment *is* small equipment, and guarding minnows guarantees minnows. The 灌渎 (ditch) is the chapter's image for every self-imposed smallness — the [useless gourd's](/stories/useless-gourd/) opposite number: Hui Shi couldn't use a thing too big for his jars; the ditch-men can't catch a fish too big for their rods.
- **The ledger the chapter keeps.** 饰小说，以干县令，其于大达，亦远矣 — the small tale seeking the county office, measured against the great attainment: the chapter's own cost-accounting, the [Cao Shang](/stories/cao-shang/) price-list inverted (he bought offices with cures; the small-tale sellers buy offices with tales). Lord Ren never appears in the comparison — he is the *unit of measure*, not a person: 未尝闻任氏之风俗 — "those who have never heard of the Ren manner" — the chapter's definition of the unteachable.
- **The wait is the doctrine.** The year of no catch is the story's center, and the site holds it against the [progress question](/questions/what-counts-as-progress/): fifty oxen of preparation, a year of nothing, *then* the sea rises. The chapter does not say the fish must come — it says the bait must be real. The [shepherd's rule](/stories/shanbao-zhangyi/) whips the lagging sheep; Lord Ren's rule baits for the fish he hasn't seen.
- **The drift, small and clean.** 任公子钓鱼 survives in modern Chinese as shorthand for *great ambition, long preparation* — the idiom keeps the scale and drops the year. The site registers it per its [idiom discipline](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/): what the idiom misses is exactly what the chapter guards — the wait is not the price of the fish; the wait *is* the fishing.
