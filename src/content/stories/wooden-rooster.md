## The story

Ji Shengzi was training a fighting cock for the king.

After ten days the king asked: "Is the cock ready?"

"Not yet," said Ji Shengzi. "It is still vain and carried by its own bluster."

Another ten days. "Not yet. It still answers every cry and shadow."

Another ten days. "Not yet. It still glares fiercely and is full of spirit."

Another ten days. "Close enough," said Ji Shengzi. "Now when other cocks crow, it does not change. Looking at it from a distance, it seems made of wood. Its virtue is complete. Other cocks will not dare face it — they will turn and run."

## Framing: what this story is

A parable from chapter 19 (Dasheng, "Mastering Life"), part of the Zhuangzi's later strata — like the [cook](/stories/cook-ding/), the trainer is a device, and the cock is the practitioner. The four ten-day stages are a compressed curriculum, and the punchline is in the reversal: **the modern idiom "dumb as a wooden rooster" means stupidity; the Zhuangzi means the opposite** — a stillness so complete it cannot be provoked, and the other cocks run away without a fight.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

The four stages map onto the sitting practices documented on this site with unusual precision:

1. **虚憍恃气 (vain, carried by bluster)** — the beginner's performance of practice: technique worn as display, energy spent on looking the part.
2. **犹应嚮景 (still answers cries and shadows)** — reactivity: the practitioner has settled somewhat but still jumps at every stimulus — the "wandering mind" stage the [restlessness page](/problems/restlessness-in-sitting/) documents.
3. **疾视盛气 (glares, full of spirit)** — the stage of effort: outer composure, inner strain. Stillness maintained by force rather than by nature.
4. **望之似木鸡，德全 (like a wooden rooster, virtue complete)** — the wuwei outcome: nothing external moves it, because nothing internal is being held up. The tradition's word for this is 德全 (virtue complete) — and note the causal claim, which is the text's whole teaching: the stillness is not the *absence* of strength but its *completion*. What looks dead is the most alive thing in the yard.

The story also guards against a misreading the modern community makes often: the "wooden" state is reached by *training* (forty days of graded work), not by imitating lifelessness. One cannot perform the fourth stage without passing through the first three.

**Uncertainty preserved:** parable, not report; the stages are the tradition's reading of the passage, and the "virtue complete" claim is a traditional claim, not an empirical one.
