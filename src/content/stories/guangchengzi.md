## The story

The Yellow Emperor had ruled the world for nineteen years. Hearing that Guangchengzi dwelt on the mountain of Kongtong, he went to see him. "I have heard you have reached the utmost Way," said the Emperor. "May I ask about its essence? I wish to take the essence of heaven and earth to help the five grains and nourish the people; I wish to regulate yin and yang so all living things may flourish. How should I go about it?"

Guangchengzi answered: "What you wish to ask about is the raw substance of things; what you wish to govern is the dregs of things. Since you began ruling the world, clouds rain before they gather, leaves fall before they yellow, and the light of sun and moon grows dim. A man with a glib heart like yours — how could he be fit to hear of the utmost Way?"

The Emperor withdrew. He gave up the world, built a solitary hut, spread white rushes, and dwelt there in retirement for three months. Then he returned to seek instruction. Guangchengzi lay with his head to the south. The Emperor, following the wind, crept forward on his knees, bowed twice, and asked: "I have heard you have reached the utmost Way. May I ask how to govern the body so that it may endure?"

Guangchengzi started up and said: "A good question! Come, I will tell you the utmost Way.

"The essence of the utmost Way is deep and dark. The extreme of the utmost Way is dim and silent.

"**See nothing, hear nothing, embrace the spirit with stillness — the form will right itself.**

"Be still, be clear; do not tax your form; do not agitate your essence — then you may live long. When the eyes see nothing, the ears hear nothing, and the mind knows nothing, your spirit will guard the form, and the form will live long.

"Guard your inner; close your outer; much knowing is defeat.

"I hold to the One and abide in its harmony — thus I have cultivated my body for twelve hundred years, and my form has not yet decayed."

## Framing: what this story is

This is **the closest thing the Zhuangzi has to a sitting instruction** — and it arrives dressed as myth. Guangchengzi is a legendary immortal, the "twelve hundred years" is deliberate hyperbole, and the chapter (Zaiyou, "Letting Be and Dwelling in Tolerance") opens with it as a political parable: the Emperor's first question — how to *take* heaven-and-earth's essence and *govern* yin-yang — is rejected outright as the wrong question. Only after three months of solitary retirement (the story's own demonstration of the practice) is he allowed to ask the right one: how to govern the *body*.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

The four-line core — 无视无听，抱神以静，形将自正 / 必静必清，无劳汝形，无摇汝精 — is the earliest complete formula of what later traditions systematized as jingzuo and zuowang:

1. **The instruction is negative all the way down.** See nothing, hear nothing — not visualization, not control of breath, not acquisition of content. The practice is the removal of input and the stilling of effort.
2. **Stillness is the mechanism, self-righting is the result.** "The form will right itself" (形将自正) — the text does not ask the practitioner to fix the body; it asks only for stillness, and claims the form corrects itself.
3. **"Much knowing is defeat" (多知为败).** The tradition's anti-cognitive clause, aimed squarely at the knowledge-project the Emperor began with.

**Uncertainty preserved:** the passage is primary text of contested authorship (the Outer Chapters were compiled later than the Inner); its teaching status rests on the tradition's adoption of it as canonical sitting doctrine. Recorded as such.
