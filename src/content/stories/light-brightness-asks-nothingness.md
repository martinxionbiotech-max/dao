## The story

**Radiance asks Absence a question — and Absence does not answer.** The setup is the chapter's joke turned inside out: 光曜 (guangyao, light-brightness) — the most radiant thing in the vocabulary — asks 无有 (wuyou, not-having) whether it has existence or has no existence. No answer comes. So Radiance does what no other questioner in the chapter does: it stops talking and *stares* — 孰視其狀貌，窅然空然 — examining the other's form, hollow and empty. All day it looks and does not see it; it listens and does not hear it; it reaches out and grasps nothing.

Then Radiance speaks the line that turns the fable into a ladder: **I can reach having-no-being, but I have not yet reached no-no-being** (予能有无矣，而未能无无也). To *have* the state of no-having is still a having — a possession of emptiness, a badge worn. The real thing is one step further out: not-having itself not-had. And the last sentence lets go of the ladder entirely: *and once one is that — how could one arrive here from anywhere?* (及爲无有矣，何從至此哉？) If you were truly Absence, there would be no "from" left to speak of, no journey, no questioner.

## Why it is told here

Because this is the chapter's **negation gymnastics** — the one passage in the whole book that shows the grammar of 无 (wu, no/not-having) working in layers, and it belongs beside the chapter's other two dialogues about knowing:

- **The chapter's trio.** [知问无为谓](/stories/zhi-wen-wuwei/) asks *about* the dao and gets silence — the best answer; [东郭子](/stories/dongguo-zi-asks/) asks *where* the dao is and gets the descent to the gutter; 光曜 asks *whether its interlocutor exists* and gets the stare. Three questions, three kinds of failure to transmit — and this third one is the only one where the questioner learns by *looking* instead of listening. The chapter's word for it is 孰視 — to look closely — the same verb the [fish-happiness](/stories/fish-happiness/) debate turns on. Here, looking closely yields: nothing to see.
- **The double negation, registered.** The site's [dao](/glossary/dao/) and [wu-sang-wo](/glossary/wu-sang-wo/) pages hold the vocabulary; this story holds the syntax. 有无 (having no-being) is still a state you can *hold* — the text's word is 能, can, ability. 无无 (no-no-being) is the ability dropped. The site files the distinction exactly as the text states it: the practitioner who has achieved emptiness has achieved a possession; the text's Absence owns nothing, not even its own absence.
- **The last line is the ladder's removal.** 何從至此哉 — *from where could one arrive here?* — is the chapter's final word on the journey metaphor. If [无何有之乡](/glossary/wu-he-you-zhi-xiang/) is the place without a there, this sentence is its border guard: there is no route to the route-less, because arriving requires a from, and the from is precisely what gets left behind. The site's [xu](/concepts/xu/) page quotes the same logic from the channel metaphor: what gathers must first be empty — here the text says the same about the gatherer.
