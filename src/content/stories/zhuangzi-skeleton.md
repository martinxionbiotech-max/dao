## The story

Traveling to Chu, Zhuangzi came upon an empty skull — dry, complete in its shape. He rapped it with his horse-whip and cross-examined it, asking by what disaster or failing it had come to this — the misfortunes of the living, question by question.

Then, done speaking, he took the skull, pillowed his head on it, and slept.

At midnight the skull came to him in a dream and said: "You talk like a debater. Looking at what you say — **all of it is the burden of the living; the dead have none of this.** Would you like to hear about death?"

Zhuangzi said: "Yes."

The skull said: "**In death there is no lord above, no minister below; none of the business of the four seasons. Free and easy, taking heaven and earth as my spring and autumn — even the pleasure of a south-facing king could not surpass it.**"

Zhuangzi did not believe it. "If I made the Director of Fate restore your living form — gave you back bone, flesh, and skin; returned you to your father and mother, your wife and children, your neighbors and acquaintances — would you want it?"

The skull frowned hard, knit its brow, and said: "**How could I abandon the pleasure of a south-facing king and go back to the toil of the human world!**"

## Framing: the refusal, the joke, and the book's double register

- **The resurrection refused.** The passage's whole weight is in its last line, and the site quotes it in full every time: 吾安能弃南面王乐而复为人间之劳乎 — "how could I abandon the pleasure of a south-facing king and go back to the toil of the human world?" Death is offered its body back and declines, frowning. The fable is the book's sharpest 齐死生 statement — and its funniest: the death joke told from the other side of the offer. The [drumming-basin page](/stories/drumming-basin/) (same chapter: Zhuangzi beating a basin when his wife dies) holds the argument's serious form — the transformation through 芒芴, qi, form, life, death — and this page holds its dream form: the same claim, staged as a skull's bedtime refusal.
- **A dream-speech, double-registered.** Like the [butterfly dream](/stories/butterfly-dream/), the skull speaks *in a dream*, and the site keeps the frame visible: the book does not assert "death is better"; it stages a dream in which a skull asserts it. The chapter itself closes with the sequel the site verified — Liezi's hundred-year-old skull, told that only the dead know there is neither death nor birth — so the book ends its death chapter by softening the joke twice: first a dream, then a riddle. The site's [life-death pages](/stories/sanghu-friends/) read the pair together: the skull's 南面王乐 is rhetoric in service of the chapter's actual claim — the living's burdens are not the last word about death.
- **The Director of Fate — and the book's gods.** Zhuangzi's offer invokes 司命, the Director of Fate, as a deity with the power to restore bone and flesh — and the skull, in a dream, outranks him by refusing. The site's [creator question](/experiences/questions/does-daoism-believe-in-a-creator/) holds the doctrine this scene illustrates: the book's world is *full of gods* (a fate-god who can resurrect bodies!) and has *no maker of the whole* — deities are officials, not creators, and the skull's freedom (从然, free and easy) is precisely freedom from officialdom: no lord above, no minister below.
- **The counterweight to the health pages.** The site keeps this passage linked to its [cure question](/experiences/questions/can-meditation-cure-illness/) and the [EXP-019 record](/experiences/notes/exp-019-one-remedy-body/) on purpose: the tradition that writes this death joke is the same tradition whose manuals promise health and longevity. The site's discipline is to keep both voices — the one that treats the body, and the one that raps its skull — because the book itself keeps both, in the same chapter.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

Because the practice the site documents is a *life* practice, and this passage is the book's standing reminder that the tradition's texts were never only that: they stage the question of death with the lightness the [sitting pages](/practices/jingzuo/) inherit as their own ideal — not solemnity, but the free-and-easy.
