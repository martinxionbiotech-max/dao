## The passage

東郭子問於莊子曰："所謂道，惡乎在？"莊子曰："無所不在。"東郭子曰："期而後可。"莊子曰："在螻蟻。"曰："何其下邪？"曰："在稊稗。"曰："何其愈下邪？"曰："在瓦甓。"曰："何其愈甚邪？"曰："在屎溺。"東郭子不應。莊子曰："夫子之問也，固不及質。正獲之問於監市，履狶也，每下愈況。汝唯莫必，無乎逃物。至道若是，大言亦然。周、徧、咸三者，異名同實，其指一也。嘗相與遊乎無何有之宮，同合而論，無所終窮乎！嘗相與無為乎！澹而靜乎！漠而清乎！調而閒乎！寥已吾志，無往焉而不知其所至，去而來而不知其所止。吾已往來焉，而不知其所終。彷徨乎馮閎，大知入焉，而不知其所窮。物物者，與物無際。而物有際者，所謂物際者也。不際之際，際之不際者也。謂盈虛衰殺。彼為盈虛非盈虛，彼為衰殺非衰殺，彼為本末非本末，彼為積散非積散也。"

Dongguo Zi asked Zhuangzi: "This thing called the Way — where is it?" Zhuangzi said: "There is nowhere it is not." Dongguo Zi said: "You must name a place, then it will do." Zhuangzi said: "In the mole-crickets and ants." "How so low?" "In the tares and weeds." "How lower still?" "In the tiles and shards." "How more extreme?" "In the dung and the urine." Dongguo Zi made no reply. Zhuangzi said: "Your question, sir, never reached the substance. The market inspector, asking how a pig's fatness is tested — by pressing down the leg: the *lower* you press, the *more evident* it becomes. Only do not insist on a fixed place, and nothing escapes being a thing. The highest Way is like this, and great words are the same. 周, 徧, 咸 — everywhere, all-around, complete: three names, one reality, pointing at the one thing. Let us try roaming together in the palace of no-why-there, and argue as one — will there be any end? Let us try not-doing together — still and quiet! Indifferent and clear! Blended and at ease! My intent has emptied out: I go without knowing where I arrive, depart and return without knowing where I stop. I have come and gone without knowing where it ends — roaming in the vast vacancy, the great knowledge enters it and does not know where it is exhausted. The thing-er of things has no boundary with things. That things have boundaries is what is called the boundaries of things. The boundary of the boundless, the boundlessness of the bounded — what is called fullness and emptiness, decline and decline-killing. What makes them full and empty is not full-and-empty; what makes them decline and die is not decline-and-death; what makes them root and branch is not root-and-branch; what makes them gather and scatter is not gather-and-scatter."

## Why this passage matters

- **The most quoted descent in the book.** The ladder is the passage: 螻蟻 (mole-crickets and ants), 稊稗 (tares and weeds), 瓦甓 (tiles and shards), 屎溺 (dung and urine) — each rung *lower*, and each rung still a place where the Way is. The interrogator's whole mistake is visible in his questions: 何其下邪 (how so low?), 何其愈下邪 (how lower still?) — he asks for the Way as if it were a *ranking*, and the answer is a ladder that runs downward on purpose. The site's [dao page](/glossary/dao/) holds the definition this passage enforces: the Way is not the highest thing but the thing that has no outside — a Way that was not in the dung would have a boundary, and a bounded Way is not the Way. 無所不在 — nowhere it is not — is the chapter's whole answer before the ladder begins.
- **每下愈況 — the idiom and its drift.** The proof is a market joke with a serious point: 正獲之問於監市，履狶也，每下愈況 — the inspector tests a pig's fatness by pressing the leg, and the *lower* he presses, the *more evident* the fatness becomes. The lower, the more evident: that is the original sense. Modern Chinese keeps the four characters as an idiom of *decline* — 每况愈下, "getting worse and worse" — which is almost the reverse of what Zhuangzi meant. The site registers the drift the way it registers all [meaning-drift](/glossary/liang-wang/): the idiom's modern life is evidence of the passage's fate, not of its meaning. What the book said about the *low* is now the phrase we use for falling into it.
- **The demand for a place is the error.** 期而後可 — "you must specify one, then it will do" — is Dongguo Zi's rule, and Zhuangzi's correction is grammatical: 汝唯莫必，無乎逃物 — only stop insisting on a fixed place, and nothing escapes being a thing. The Way is not *in* things the way a location contains an object; it is the thing-ness of things. The passage's closing sentences are the technical statement: 物物者，與物無際 — the thing-er of things has no boundary with things — and 不際之際，際之不際者也 — the boundary of the boundless. The site's [qiwu page](/glossary/qiwu/) holds the inner-chapter version of the same claim: the leveling of things is not the claim that all things are equal but the dissolution of the boundary-machine that separates them.
- **The palace of no-why-there.** The passage's invitation — 嘗相與遊乎無何有之宮 — let us roam the palace of no-why-there — is the book's own name for its favorite landscape, the 無何有之鄉 of the [xiaoyao page](/glossary/xiaoyao/) and the [useless-tree story](/stories/zhili-shu/): the place that exists precisely by being good for nothing. And the four adjectives that follow — 澹而靜，漠而清，調而閒 (still and quiet, indifferent and clear, blended and at ease) — are the chapter's sketch of the practice the site's [stillness pages](/glossary/jingzuo/) index: not a state to be reached but the removal of the asking posture itself. The question "where is the Way" dies of its own success once the questioner is standing in the answer.
- **Why it pairs with the chapter's opening.** The chapter began with [Knowledge's three questions](/stories/zhi-wen-wuwei/) and their silent answer; this passage is the same drama with a loquacious Zhuangzi instead of a silent Non-action-called. The difference is the audience: Dongguo Zi *needs* the ladder — he is the one who would not have heard the silence — and the ladder is built of the things his ranking-mind puts at the bottom. The site's [tushan-shuo page](/stories/tushan-shuo/) holds the companion use of low things from the book's middle chapters: the sheep-butcher who refuses office knows what Dongguo Zi does not — that the low is not where the Way condescends to be, but where it is not diluted by rank.
