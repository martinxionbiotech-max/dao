## The story

Zhuangzi, attending a funeral, passed Huizi's grave and, turning to his followers, said:

"A man of Ying had smeared plaster on the tip of his nose, thin as a fly's wing, and had the carpenter Shi cut it off. **The carpenter swung his axe like wind** — and, cutting by ear, took the plaster clean off without touching the nose; the man of Ying stood without a flicker.

"Lord Yuan of Song heard of it and summoned the carpenter: 'Try it for me.' The carpenter said: 'I was once able to cut it. But my **partner** has been dead a long time.'

"Since the Master died, I have no partner. **I have no one to talk to.**"

## Framing: the friendship the book's whole argument lives in

- **The last Huizi page.** The site's Huizi arc is now complete: the [gourd argument](/stories/useless-gourd/) (ch. 1), the [fish argument](/stories/fish-happiness/) (ch. 17), the [owl and the phoenix](/stories/phoenix-and-owl/) (ch. 17), the [snail-horn king](/stories/snail-horn-war/) (ch. 25, where Huizi brings in the hermit), and this — the grave. The book that spent four chapters arguing with Huizi ends the argument with a eulogy: **the opponent was the other half of the skill.** 吾无以为质矣，吾无与言之矣 — "I have no partner; I have no one to talk to" — is the tradition's plainest statement that thought is a *two-person* craft.
- **The carpenter is a figure the site already knows.** [Carpenter Shi's judgment](/stories/carpenter-shi/) (ch. 4, the useless tree) is the same 匠石, and the pairing is deliberate in the book's world: the one who knew what *not* to cut and the one whose cut *needed* the man who did not flinch. The word that carries both pages is the same: **质** — in ch. 4, the raw matter (质, the grain of the wood); here, the partner (质, the counterpart). The [zhi-shui glossary](/glossary/zhi-shui/) holds the family of doubled terms this page feeds: skill is always already a relation.
- **What the king cannot buy.** Lord Yuan of Song asks for the *performance* and is refused with the passage's whole point: 臣则尝能斫之，虽然，臣之质死久矣 — "I was once able to do it; but my partner has been dead a long time." The skill is not the axe-arm; the skill is the pair. The refusal is the eulogy's first half, and the direct address to the dead man is its second: the *follower*-facing story folds into a *friend*-facing sentence, and the book lets its hero miss someone.
- **The idiom today.** 运斤成风 survives in modern Chinese as the standing phrase for effortless mastery — one of the site's [idiom records](/stories/fish-in-the-dry-spring/), here with the meaning intact but the *object* of the mastery usually forgotten: the phrase remembers the wind, not the partner.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

Because the site's [source policy](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/) is itself a two-person craft — verification is the partner without whom the writer's axe is wind over nothing — and this is the tradition's own statement of that: **I have no one to talk to** is not loneliness; it is the recognition that thinking is done in pairs.

**Uncertainty preserved:** a *mixed* chapter passage (declared layer per the [layering rule](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/)); the story is fable (the carpenter Shi is also the ch. 4 figure, and the book reuses its cast); Zhuangzi's visit to Huizi's grave is the book's own scene, not an external biography; the reading follows the text and the teaching-edition notes.
