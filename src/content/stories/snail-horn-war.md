## The story

King Ying of Wei had a pact with Marquis Mou of Tian, and Marquis Mou broke it. King Ying, furious, would send an assassin. Xishou heard and was ashamed for him: "A lord of ten thousand chariots avenging himself like a commoner! Give me two hundred thousand armored men and I will take his country for you — drag off his people, rope his cattle, make his lord's inner heat break out on his back, then pull up his state, drive out the traitor, break his back, snap his spine." Jizi was ashamed for him too: "Building a wall ten fathoms high and tearing it down again — that's what convicts are made to suffer. The arms have been down seven years; that is your majesty's foundation. Yan is a troublemaker; do not listen." Huazi was disgusted with both: "Whoever argues for attacking Qi is a troublemaker; whoever argues against attacking is a troublemaker; whoever says 'those who talk of attacking or not attacking are all troublemakers' — is *also* a troublemaker." The king said: "Then what?" — "Just seek the Way, that is all."

Huizi heard of this and brought in the hermit Dai Jinren. Dai Jinren said: "There is something called a snail. Does your majesty know it?" — "Yes." — "On the snail's left horn is a kingdom called Chu; on its right horn a kingdom called Man. From time to time they quarrel over ground and go to war: **corpses by the tens of thousands, and the pursuit of the routed army takes fifteen days to come home.**" The king said: "Ah — that's a fiction, isn't it?" — "Allow me to make it real for you. Does your majesty suppose the four directions and above and below have a limit?" — "They are limitless." — "Then to know how to **let the mind wander in the limitless**, and come back to look at these reachable countries — are they not as if there, as if not there?" — "They are." — "Within the reachable, there is Wei; within Wei, there is Liang; within Liang, there is your majesty. And is your majesty distinguishable from Man?" — "**Not distinguishable.**"

The guest left, and the king stood dazed, as if he had lost something. When the guest had gone, Huizi came in, and the king said: "That guest is a great man — a sage would not be his equal." Huizi said: "Blow through a pipe and there is sound; blow across the tip of a sword and there is only a whisper. Yao and Shun — all people praise them; but speak of Yao and Shun before Dai Jinren and it is a whisper."

## Framing: the book's scale-argument, aimed at a war

- **Three advisors, one ladder.** The passage's first half is a compressed political debate: Xishou (attack), Jizi (don't — the seven years of peace are the foundation), Huazi (all of it is noise — 君求其道而已矣, "just seek the Way"). The [chapter's other scale passages](/stories/wangyang-xingtan/) teach by water and seasons; this one teaches by *shrinking the battlefield* — and the target is not philosophy but a **specific war that was about to be fought**.
- **The shrink is the argument.** 臣请为君实之 — "let me make it real for you": the hermit does not deny the snail story; he *installs* it, by one question (is space limitless?) and one chain (the reachable → Wei → Liang → **you**). The war of Chu and Man is exactly as real as the king's own — both are fictions of scale. The [frog in the well](/stories/jingdi-zhiwa/) is the same teaching aimed at a complacent frog; here it is aimed at an angry king, and the result is the passage's own record of effect: **客出而君惝然若有亡也** — the guest leaves and the king stands dazed, as if he had lost something. The book rarely shows its persuasion *working*; this is one of the places it does.
- **Huizi's whisper.** The closing exchange with Huizi (the same Huizi of the [fish argument](/stories/fish-happiness/) and the [gourd argument](/stories/useless-gourd/)) caps the passage: before Dai Jinren even Yao and Shun are a whisper — the scale argument applied to reputation itself. And the phrase 蜗角之争 survived into modern Chinese as the standing word for trivial quarrels — one of the site's [idiom-drift records](/stories/fish-in-the-dry-spring/), this time with the meaning intact.
- **The king is a real king.** King Ying of Wei (魏瑩, the King Hui who figures in the Mencius) is the passage's audience — a *mixed* chapter passage (declared layer), and the site records the episode as fable built around a historical name, per its [people-policy](/people/laozi/).

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

Because it is the tradition's strongest anti-war text — the argument that every war is a snail-horn war — and the site's [scale pages](/stories/jingdi-zhiwa/) now form the full set: the well, the sea, the snail — three containers, one measurement.

**Uncertainty preserved:** a *mixed* chapter passage (declared layer per the [layering rule](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/)); the snail kingdoms are fiction; King Ying of Wei is historical, the dialogue is the book's device; the reading follows the text and the attested parallel (Taiping yulan), not the popular self-help glosses.
