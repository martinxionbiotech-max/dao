## The story

Gongwen Xuan saw the Master of the Right and started: "What sort of man is this? Why one foot? Heaven — or man?"

"Heaven, not man. Heaven's engendering made him this one-footed one. A man's form is given as it is given — so I know it was Heaven, not man."

The marsh pheasant takes **ten steps for one peck, a hundred steps for one drink** — and does not ask to be kept in a cage. **Even kept like a king, it would not be good.**

## Framing: the chapter's shortest teaching, and the book's animal logic

- **Ten steps, one peck.** The pheasant's liberty is *expensive* — ten steps of work for a single peck, a hundred for a drink — and the passage does not pretend otherwise. The freedom it refuses to trade is not leisure; it is the *effort itself*. 神虽王，不善也: even if the cage treated it as king, it would not be good — the phrase is the passage's whole argument, and the site reads it as the [turtle of Pu](/stories/turtle-in-the-mud/)'s logic in animal form: the choice between the tiring open field and the honored cage is not a choice between comfort levels but between *being fed* and *being the one who feeds itself*.
- **The opening scene sets the theme.** The chapter stages Gongwen Xuan's shock at the one-footed officer — 天与？其人与？ heaven or man? — and answers: heaven. The [ziran](/concepts/ziran/) concept page holds the term this answer belongs to: what is given is given, and the pheasant passage follows as the same answer applied to appetite — the form is not to be corrected, and the freedom is not to be enclosed.
- **The chapter's four scenes.** The site now has the full chapter: the [cook](/stories/cook-ding/) (skill's measure), the pheasant (freedom's price), and [Qin Shi](/stories/qinshi-mourning/) (grief's measure) — the three scenes after the opening statement (吾生也有涯，而知也无涯). The pheasant is the middle term: between the blade that finds the spaces and the mourner who finds the measure, the bird that pays ten steps a peck.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

Because it is the shortest complete statement of the tradition's freedom: not the removal of effort, but the *refusal of the cage* — and the site's [practice pages](/practices/jingzuo/) are written on the same arithmetic: the practice costs ten steps a peck, and is not for sale as a king's keeping.

**Uncertainty preserved:** an *inner* chapter passage (earliest stratum), verified against the Wikisource full text; the figures are fable; the Taiwan study's reading (the pheasant as the natural against the fenced) is recorded as scholarly interpretation, not as the text's own.
