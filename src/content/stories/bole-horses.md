## The story

Horses, with hooves to tread frost and snow and coats to ward off wind and cold — nibbling grass, drinking water, lifting their feet and prancing: that is the horse's true nature (马之真性). Terraces and fine halls mean nothing to them. Then Bole arrives, saying: "I am good at managing horses." He brands them (烧之), clips their manes (剔之), trims their hooves (刻之), marks them (雒之), binds them with halter and reins (连之以羁馽), ranks them in manger and stall (编之以皂栈) — and two or three horses in ten are dead. Then he starves them, parches them, gallops them, paces them, aligns them, ranks them (饥之，渴之，驰之，骤之，整之，齐之) — bit and bridle-ornament threatening from the front, whip and switch from behind (前有橛饰之患，而后有鞭筴之威) — and the horses dead are already more than half.

The potter says: "I am good at managing clay. What is round fits the compass; what is square fits the square." The carpenter says: "I am good at managing wood. What is bent fits the hook; what is straight meets the line." Does the nature of clay and wood desire to fit compasses, squares, hooks and lines? Yet generation after generation praises them — "Bole is good at managing horses, and the potter and carpenter are good at managing clay and wood" — and this is the fault of those who govern the world (此亦治天下者之过也).

## Framing: shaping to the template

- **The arithmetic of harm.** Two death-tolls, escalating: 十二三 (two or three in ten) after the first discipline, 已过半 (more than half) after the second. The book's numbers do the arguing — the same device as [Cao Shang's price list](/stories/cao-shang/): harm, stated as a ledger. The first toll is the shaping (brand, clip, trim, halter); the second is the training (hunger, thirst, gallop, bit, whip) — the chapter counts both as one program, and the total is majority death.
- **治 — the verb is the charge.** 我善治马 — "I am good at *governing* horses." The word is the same one used for states, and the chapter's move is to translate it: what governing does, the stable shows — it treats the governed as material for a template. The potter's 圆者中规 (the round fits the compass) and the carpenter's 曲者中钩 (the bent fits the hook) are the same sentence as Bole's — and the chapter's question stands over all three: 夫埴木之性，岂欲中规矩钩绳哉？ Does clay's nature want to fit a compass?
- **Two craftsmanships.** The chapter sets the bad craftsmanship (shaping *to the tool* — the nature must fit the template) against the good one the book has already shown: [Qing the woodcarver](/stories/artisan-qing/) follows the wood's nature (以天合天 — matching heaven with heaven), the [cicada-catcher](/stories/cicada-catcher/) joins his nature to the task, [Wheelwright Bian](/stories/wheelwright-bian/) works with what is already in his hands. Bole and the potter and the carpenter of this chapter are their mirror: the same hands, the opposite direction — forcing the thing toward the tool. The site holds both families apart, and this chapter is the indictment page.
- **Death-by-improvement.** The story's deeper shape is the book's own warning pattern: [Hundun](/stories/hundun/) died of seven well-meant holes; the [Marquis of Lu's bird](/stories/luhou-yang-niao/) died of hospitality; the [marsh pheasant](/stories/marsh-pheasant/) trades ten steps for a peck of food in a cage. Bole belongs with them: improvement as the leading cause of death — the chapter's 真性 (true nature) is what every one of those pages defends, and this page is its charter.
- **The idiom's full inversion.** 伯乐 today names the one who *recognizes* talent — 千里马常有，而伯乐不常有, from Han Yu's 马说: the finder of hidden excellence. The Zhuangzi's Bole is the killer of it. The drift runs the whole length of the word's meaning — from destroyer to appreciator — and the site registers it as the most complete inversion in its [idiom-drift record](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/): the name survived, the role reversed.
- **The chapter's frame.** This is ch. 9, an *outer* chapter (declared per the [layering rule](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/)) — the book's naturalness polemic in its most systematic form: horses, clay, wood, and then (the chapter continues) the people whose 真性 the saints of benevolence and righteousness shaped to a template. The site quotes the opening, and the opening contains the whole.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

Because the practice this site studies — the [return](/practices/zuowang/), the [fasting of the mind](/practices/xinzhai/), the [settling](/practices/jingzuo/) — is, in this chapter's vocabulary, the un-managing of what Bole manages: the refusal to run one's own nature through bit and whip. The [refusal pages](/stories/bao-weng-guan-qi/) — the gardener who will not use the well-sweep — and the [unstrung](/concepts/ziran/) pages both find their sharpest statement here: the ledger of the improved horse.
