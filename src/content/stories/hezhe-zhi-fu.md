## The story

Zhuangzi's household was poor, so he went to borrow grain from the River Warden. The warden said: "Of course — I'm about to receive my fief's tax silver, and then I'll lend you three hundred gold pieces. Will that do?"

Zhuangzi's face darkened. "Coming here yesterday," he said, "I heard a voice crying from the middle of the road. I looked around and saw a carp in a cart-rut. I asked it: 'Carp, what are you doing here?' It said: 'I am a subject of the Eastern Sea's waves. **Do you have a dipperful of water to keep me alive?**' I said: 'Of course — I am about to travel south and persuade the kings of Wu and Yue to raise the waters of the West River to welcome you back. Will that do?' The carp's face darkened: 'I have lost my constant home, and I have nowhere to be. **Give me a dipperful of water and I live.** Saying what you say, you had better look for me early in the shop of dried fish!'"

## Framing: the help that arrives after the death

- **The three grandiosities.** The warden's offer is the fable's whole target, and the book stacks it: the fief's tax silver (not yet his), three hundred gold (for a loan of grain), and — in the embedded fable — the West River raised by kings. Each promise is bigger than the last and farther away; the dying carp's arithmetic is the answer to all three: 吾得斗升之水然活耳 — "a dipperful of water, and I live." The site's [practice-layer pragmatism](/questions/how-long-should-i-sit/) inherits this sentence: help is measured in dippers, not rivers.
- **The dying-fish pair.** The book has two dying fish, and the site now holds both: the [fish in the dry spring](/stories/fish-in-the-dry-spring/) (ch. 6: the spring gone dry, the fish moistening each other with breath — and the proverb's inversion: better to forget each other in rivers and lakes) and this one (ch. 26: the fish in the cart-rut, alone, asking for a dipper). The two passages are the book's matched experiments in *scarcity*: the spring-fish face each other and are told the answer is the river; the rut-fish faces a promiser and is told the answer is the dipper. Together they say: in abundance, forget each other; in need, hand over the water you actually have.
- **The idiom, intact.** 涸辙之鲋 survives in modern Chinese as the standing phrase for someone in desperate straits; 枯鱼之肆 ("the shop of dried fish") survives as the place grand promises will find you. Both meanings are **intact** — one of the site's [un-drifted idiom records](/stories/luhou-yang-niao/) — which the site flags: the fable is so precise that twenty-three centuries of use have not bent it.
- **Zhuangzi's poverty, self-portrait.** The passage is one of the book's autobiographical poverty scenes (with the [river crossing](/stories/xuzhou-empty-boat/)'s boat and the [bird pages](/stories/marsh-pheasant/)' hunger as neighbors): the author borrows grain, and answers the refusal with a fable that makes the refusal a category — 波臣, "subject of the waves," a fish *by profession*, so the dipper is not charity but the return of a misplaced subject to its element. The [fish-happiness page](/stories/fish-happiness/) (ch. 17) already holds the book's claim to know fish; this page is the book *being* one.
- **The warden is not evil either.** Like the [Marquis of Lu](/stories/luhou-yang-niao/), the River Warden means well — his silver really is coming, the three hundred is generosity. The fable's target is not malice but the *structure* of deferral: the help that would be real in three months is a refusal today. The site keeps the two together as its pair on well-meaning failure: the marquis over-feeds the bird to death with the wrong abundance; the warden under-feeds the fish to death with the right abundance at the wrong time.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

Because the site's [practice questions](/questions/zuowang-safety-without-teacher/) live in this fable's economy: what a beginner needs is the dipper — the concrete, small, now — not the river of a system that will arrive once everything else is in place. The tradition's own texts keep insisting on this, and this passage is the insistence at its sharpest.

**Uncertainty preserved:** a *mixed* chapter passage (later stratum, declared per the [layering rule](/sources/)); verified against two independent editions that agree, with the 监河侯/监何侯 variant recorded; the fable is the book's self-portrait, not an external chronicle.
