## The story

Yang Shi (1053–1135) had passed the highest examination at a young age, but gave up office to pursue learning. The story the tradition preserves is this: when he was already over forty, he and his fellow student You Zuo came to study with the great Cheng Yi (1033–1107), and called on the master one day while he was sitting quietly. Cheng Yi sat on in stillness. The two stood beside him, waiting. When Cheng Yi at last rose, the snow outside had fallen a foot deep.

## Framing: what this story is

The episode is recorded in the *Song shi* — the official history of the dynasty, compiled in the 14th century, some two centuries after the event. As history it is traditional rather than verified: the compilers were transmitting the lore of the Cheng-Zhu school, which by then was state orthodoxy and had every reason to burnish its founding images. As *tradition*, however, the story is documentary evidence of something real and important: how the school itself wanted quiet sitting to be seen.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

The story did for jingzuo what the butterfly dream did for Daoist transformation — it fixed an image. Every element carries meaning:

1. **Sitting is the master's activity.** Cheng Yi is not lecturing or debating; he is *sitting*. The image defines cultivation as something done in stillness.
2. **The student's work is waiting.** Yang Shi's standing is not passivity but the classical posture of the disciple: the time spent waiting is itself part of the learning.
3. **The snow makes the wait measurable.** The foot of snow is the story's clock — reverence measured in time endured.

This is the image behind the later jingzuo records (see the [concept page](/concepts/jingzuo/) and the [Zhu Xi timeline entry](/timeline/zhuzi-yulei-jingzuo/)): quiet sitting as moral cultivation, respect, and self-examination — the Confucian parallel to Daoist zuowang, sharing the posture but not the goal.

**Uncertainty preserved:** the episode's details are traditional transmission recorded by a later official history; it is labeled HISTORICAL EVIDENCE for the existence of the tradition, not for the snow.
