## The story

Zhuangzi walked in the mountains and saw a great tree, branches and leaves flourishing. The woodcutters stopped beside it and did not take it. Asked why, they said: "There's nothing it can be used for." Zhuangzi said: "This tree lives out its years **because it is no timber.**"

Coming down from the mountain, he lodged at an old friend's house. The friend was pleased and told the servant boy to kill a goose and cook it. The boy asked: "One can honk, one cannot — which shall I kill?" The host said: "**Kill the one that cannot honk.**"

Next day the disciples asked Zhuangzi: "Yesterday the mountain tree lived out its years for being no timber; today the host's goose died for being no timber. Where will you place yourself, Master?"

Zhuangzi laughed: "**I would place myself between timber and no-timber.** But between timber and no-timber — it *looks* like the place and it is not, so you are still not free of entanglement. Ride the Way and its power and wander, and it is different: no praise, no blame; now a dragon, now a snake, changing with the season, never committing to one course; now up, now down, taking harmony as the measure; **wandering at the ancestor of the ten thousand things; treating things as things and not being treated as a thing by them** — then how could anything entangle you?"

## Framing: the double standard, and the exit from both sides

- **The trap is the symmetry.** Ch. 4's scattered timber (the same 散木 logic, as Wang Nengxian's exposition notes) survives *because* useless; here the goose dies for the same reason. The passage does not resolve the contradiction — it *stages* it as the unanswerable question the disciples actually ask: 先生将何处，where will you place yourself? The [useless-gourd page](/stories/useless-gourd/) carries the first half of the theme (uselessness as room); this page carries the second: **uselessness is not a refuge either**, because the standard of use is not yours.
- **The laugh, and the between.** 周将处乎材与不材之间 — the answer is famous precisely because it is *given and withdrawn in the same breath*: 似之而非也，故未免乎累, "it looks like the place and it is not; you are still entangled." The site reads this as the book's own meta-answer: any position chosen *inside* the dilemma (timber / no timber / between) is still hostage to the dilemma's scale.
- **The real answer is a change of axis.** 乘道德而浮游 — riding the Way and its power — with its four marks: 无誉无訾 (no praise, no blame — the [translation of ch. 22's](/translations/daodejing-22/) four 不自 in narrative form); 一龙一蛇，与时俱化 (now dragon, now snake — **form follows season**, not identity); 以和为量 (harmony as measure); and 物物而不物于物 (**use things, don't be used as a thing**). The closing line the tradition keeps — 其唯道德之乡乎, "only the homeland of the Way and its power" — is the passage's true destination: the between-place was never the goal.
- **This is the survival chapter's opening.** Ch. 20 (Shanmu) is the book's *outer*-chapter essay on living through danger, and this first scene sets its method: the [ziran](/concepts/ziran/) reading — changing with the season is not strategy but nature; the dragon and snake are both given forms, and the wanderer takes them as they come.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

Because the site's [sitting guidance](/tools/sitting-protocol/) is built on exactly this refusal: the question "what posture is right" is the disciples' question — 材与不材 between postures is still entanglement; the practice pages answer with the season-changing principle instead, one dragon-day, one snake-day, the same sitting.

**Uncertainty preserved:** an *outer* chapter passage (declared layer per the [layering rule](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/)); the figures are fable; 雁 = domesticated goose per the scholarly note, kept as a translation decision; the bilibili-type popular readings (workplace survival tips) are not adopted — the site records the text, not self-help glosses.
