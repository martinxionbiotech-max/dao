## The story

The hook-forger of the Grand Marshal of Chu (大马之捶钩者) was eighty years old and had never missed by a hair's breadth (不失豪芒). The Marshal asked: "Is it skill — or do you have a Way?" (子巧与？有道与？) The old man said: "I have a keeping (臣有守也). Since I was twenty I have loved forging hooks; for other things I give no eyes, and only the hook gets my attention (于物无视也，非钩无察也). What is used, borrows from what is not used, and so keeps its use through length of years (是用之者，假不用者也以长得其用) — how much more so one who has nothing at all that he uses? Of what thing would he not have the use?" (而况乎无不用者乎！物孰不资焉！)

## Framing: the kept attention

- **有守 — the answer that outranks the question.** The Marshal offers the book's standard pair: 巧 (skill) or 道 (Way) — the same question [Confucius asks the cicada-catcher](/stories/cicada-catcher/) and the [swimmer](/stories/swimmer-lvliang/). The old man answers with a third word: 守 — *keeping, guarding*. The Qing philologist Wang Niansun (registered in the verification) holds that 守 *is* the old pronunciation of 道 — the word the tradition read as the Way was heard as *keeping*. The site keeps the double reading: the forger's humility (he will not claim the big word) and the philology (the word's oldest sound is guarding) say the same thing — the Way is what you keep.
- **The exclusion is the method.** 于物无视也，非钩无察也 — "no eyes for things; only the hook is examined." The formula is the book's whole craft-doctrine in one line, and it is the same structure the [cicada-catcher](/stories/cicada-catcher/) states (nothing but cicada-wings, 不以万物易蜩之翼 — the Su Yu gloss registered in the verification explicitly links them): attention works by *exclusion*. The site's [artisan family](/stories/cook-ding/) now has its fifth member — Cook Ding (the joint's gap), Wheelwright Bian (the un-wordable number), Artisan Qing (the heaven-matched wood), the cicada-catcher (the one thing), and the hook-forger (the kept thing) — and the family resemblance is this line: all of them became good by refusing to look.
- **The borrowed use.** 用之者假不用者也 — the user borrows from the not-used: the hands' precision is bought with the eyes' abstention. The chapter's formula is the [ch. 11 argument](/translations/daodejing-11/) (the useful by way of the empty) moved from the artifact to the person: the man's *work* is the emptiness's work. And the old man climbs past his own formula — 而况乎无不用者乎: how much more the one who has no using at all — the ladder the commentaries gloss as 无无 ("not even the not") — the book's signature escalation, this site's [non-action](/concepts/wuwei/) in its coldest form: use, then borrowed use, then no use at all — and *then* all things lend themselves (物孰不资焉).
- **The contrast that guards the story.** The site's [Shan Bao](/stories/shanbao-zhangyi/) also "gave no eyes to things" — and the tiger ate him. The difference is the *frame*: the forger's exclusion lives inside a trade (non-hook, no attention — inside the workshop); Shan Bao's exclusion lived inside a life (no people at all — outside the cliffs). The [two-deaths chapter](/stories/shanbao-zhangyi/) is the balance doctrine; this story is its precision version: keep the one thing — and keep it *inside* the world.
- **The drift, none needed.** 捶钩 survives only in the phrase itself; the story's idiom did not travel. The site keeps the verb pair 守/道 as the page's own pair — the tradition's oldest pun on what practice is: not having, keeping.
