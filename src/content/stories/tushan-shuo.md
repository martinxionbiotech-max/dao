## The story

When King Zhao of Chu lost his kingdom, Tu Yang the sheep-butcher (屠羊说) followed him into exile. When the king returned and prepared to reward his followers, the reward reached Tu Yang. The butcher said: "When Your Majesty lost the kingdom, Yue lost his sheep-butchering. When Your Majesty returned, Yue also returned to his sheep-butchering. My rank and salary are already restored — what reward is left?" (臣之爵禄已复矣，又何赏之有！)

The king said: "Press him." Tu Yang said: "When Your Majesty lost the kingdom, it was not my crime, so I do not dare face punishment for it; when Your Majesty returned, it was not my merit, so I do not dare accept reward for it."

The king said: "Have him come in." Tu Yang said: "By the law of Chu, one must have great reward-worthy merit before one may be received in audience. My wisdom could not save the state; my courage could not fight the enemy. When the Wu army entered Ying, I fled the danger and avoided the enemy — I did not deliberately follow Your Majesty. To break the law and void the covenant to receive me — this is not what I have heard of from the world."

The king said to Marshal Ziqi: "Tu Yang lives in a mean place, yet his stated principles are lofty. Have him brought with the three-banner rank (三旌之位)." Tu Yang said: "The three-banner rank — I know it outranks a sheep-stall. Ten thousand measures of grain — I know it outearns the butcher's profit. But may I, for greed of rank and salary, give my lord the name of one who *recklessly bestows* (妄施之名)? I dare not accept. I wish to return to my sheep-stall." And he did not accept (遂不受也).

## Framing: the refusal that escalates

- **Three refusals, three sizes of principle.** The king's three moves — reward (赏), compel (强), audience (见) — each draws a refusal on a larger ground than the last: first bookkeeping (I have my stall back — what's lost is restored), then the merit-arithmetic (no crime, no merit — nothing to settle), then the law itself (楚国之法: only merit earns an audience, and I have none). The butcher's weapons are the king's own instruments — the ledger, the merit system, the law of Chu — used against the king's favor. The structure is the point: refusal doesn't need philosophy; it needs the state's own arithmetic, run honestly.
- **The counter-page to Cao Shang.** [Cao Shang](/stories/cao-shang/) counted what he gained — a hundred chariots, indexed to degradation. Tu Yang counts three other columns: what he'd *lose* (the stall — 反吾屠羊之肆), what he *didn't earn* (非臣之功), and what the *king* would lose (妄施之名 — the name of reckless bestowal). The same court, the same reward system, the opposite answer — and the site pairs the pages as its two ledgers of [worth](/experiences/questions/zuowang-safety-without-teacher/).
- **The refusal family's tradesman member.** The [turtle](/stories/turtle-in-the-mud/) refused with an image (mud over temple); the [phoenix](/stories/phoenix-and-owl/) refused with disgust (rotten rat); Tu Yang refuses with accounts — and his is the family's most transferable voice: you don't need to be a sage to refuse a king, you need to remember what your stall is worth. The name itself says it: 屠羊说 — "the sheep-butcher called Yue" — a tradesman whose whole identity is his stall.
- **妄施 — the king's risk, made visible.** The refusal's final turn is the chapter's deepest: the butcher protects the *king* — accepting would make the king a reckless giver, and the butcher will not buy his own rise with his lord's reputation. Refusal here is not self-protection but mutual restraint: the same grammar the book uses for the [empty boat](/stories/xuzhou-empty-boat/) — the one who refuses to be a reward stops being a reward.
- **The mixed chapter, declared.** Ch. 28 is a *mixed* chapter (latest stratum, declared per the [layering rule](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/)), and the passage is one of the 让王 series — kings offering rank, farmers and butchers declining. The site registers the variant (又何赏之有 / 又何赏之言) and quotes the received wording.
