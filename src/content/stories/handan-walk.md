## The story

At the end of the Qiushui chapter, the philosopher Gongsun Long — famed for his logical paradoxes, his "white horse is not a horse" — reports being thrown into confusion by Zhuangzi's teaching. Prince Mou of Wei answers him with an opening parable:

"Have you not heard of the youth of Shouling who went to Handan to learn the famous Handan walk? Before he had mastered the capital's manner of walking, he had already lost his own way of walking — and he came home **crawling on all fours**. If you do not leave now, you will forget your own steps and lose your own craft entirely."

Gongsun Long's mouth fell open and would not close; his tongue stuck to the roof of his mouth; and he fled.

## Framing: a warning about methods, aimed at a logician

The fable does one precise job, and the site keeps it for the same job:

- **The lost thing is the *way of walking*.** The youth's error is not ambition but *substitution*: he put down one complete practice to acquire another, and the sequence cost him both. The target is not Handan — it is the abandonment of "故行," one's own established gait. The tradition's [ziran](/concepts/ziran/) claim is exactly this in general form: what is one's own, done in its own manner, is not improved by becoming someone else's.
- **The audience is the book's standing warning.** Prince Mou says it to Gongsun Long, the logician — the same figure the [fish debate](/stories/fish-happiness/) and the [carpenter story](/stories/carpenter-shi/) use as the mind's counterexample: the man whose *instrument* is argument. The fable's lesson lands on anyone whose practice is borrowed wholesale: not "don't learn," but "don't trade away what already walks."
- **The crawling is the diagnostic.** 直匍匐而归 — he comes home *crawling*: worse than before he left, and visibly so. The fable's scale is the whole body: a practice lost is not a possession lost, it is a *capacity* lost.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

Because this site's entire premise is people arriving from elsewhere — from mindfulness programs, from mixed communities, from [comparison pages](/comparisons/zuowang-vs-mindfulness/) — and the tradition's own warning about that situation is this fable. The [combining question](/experiences/questions/combine-zuowang-mindfulness/) and the [sitting protocol](/tools/sitting-protocol/) both implement the Handan lesson in policy form: keep what already works, add without substituting, and check — in the fable's own image — that you can still walk.

**Uncertainty preserved:** an *outer* chapter fable (the middle stratum — see the [compilation timeline](/timeline/zhuangzi-compilation/)); the Handan walk itself is proverbial, and the scene (Gongsun Long's rout) is literary, not a report. The idiom has since generalized into "blind imitation"; the page keeps the source sense per the [translation policy](/blog/translation-policy/).
