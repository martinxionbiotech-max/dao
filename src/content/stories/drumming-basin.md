## The story

Zhuangzi's wife died. Hui Shi came to condole — and found Zhuangzi sitting with his legs splayed, drumming on a basin and singing.

Hui Shi said: "You lived with her, she raised your children, she grew old with you. Not weeping is already enough — but drumming and singing? Is that not excessive?"

Zhuangzi said: "It is not so. When she first died, how could I alone not be shaken? But then I looked back to her beginning — before she was born there was no life; and not only no life, but no form; and not only no form, but no qi. In the dimness and blur, something changed and there was qi; the qi changed and there was form; the form changed and there was life. Now there is another change, and there is death. These follow one another like the four seasons, spring and autumn, winter and summer. She is now lying at rest in the great chamber, and if I followed her wailing and weeping, I would be showing myself not to understand the decree of things. So I stopped."

## Framing: the text's own honesty first

The sentence the debate usually skips is the text's first: **"When she first died, how could I alone not be shaken?"** The story does not present a man who was never grieved. It presents a man who grieved, and then *looked back* — and the cosmology of the looking-back is the point:

- **The four transformations.** No life → no form → no qi → the blur (芒芴) → qi → form → life → death. The whole arc runs in the medium of [qi](/glossary/qi/): transformation, not annihilation. The wife is "lying at rest in the great chamber" (偃然寝于巨室) — the universe as the room. The basin-drumming is the behavior that *follows* the cosmology, not a performance for visitors.
- **命 as decree, not fate-resignation.** "Not understanding the decree" (不通乎命) — the term the story uses is the same 命 the tradition uses for the given order of things. To wail on is to *misread* what has happened: not to feel too much, but to see it wrongly.
- **The posture is charged.** 箕踞 — legs splayed like a winnowing basket — was, in the ritual culture of the time, an insult in formal contexts. The story's staging matches its content: informality, refusal of the expected form, grief re-examined rather than grief performed.

## The interpretive split, reported without adjudication

The tradition and the scholarship genuinely disagree about the emotional register, and this page keeps both readings live:

- **The classical reading** (Cheng Xuanying and the mainstream line; Wang Fuzhi's *Zhuangzi Jie*: the chapter "takes death as the great joy") — the story shows grief *overcome*: the man who has understood transformation is at ease; the song is the equanimity itself.
- **The modern counter-reading** (the 2017 China Writers' Association essay, with Ren Jiyu and Chen Guying nearby) — "singing in place of weeping" (悲歌当哭): funerary dirges were sung, in the pre-Qin ritual world, *because* the grief was real (the Zhou ritual office of the wailing-singer; Confucius' own death-song); the basin-drumming is grief finding its form, "feigned detachment, real sorrow" (假旷达，真悲哀).
- **What both readings share** is the text's own claim: grief at first, then a *decision* — "so I stopped." Whether the stopping is equanimity or resignation (or the *ziran* acceptance of the inevitable, per Zhuangzi's "knowing what cannot be helped, and resting in it as the decree"), the story is not a manual for how mourners should feel. It is a picture of one man's reckoning, with the reckoning's cosmology attached.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

Because the site's core discipline — the [butterfly](/stories/butterfly-dream/) question, the [wu sang wo](/translations/qiwulun-wu-sang-wo/) translation — is exactly this: transformation as the medium of things. The basin story is the tradition's hardest version of that claim, and it belongs here *with its controversy attached*: a knowledge base that hides the split would be performing the equanimity it is supposed to examine.

**Uncertainty preserved:** an *outer* chapter story (middle stratum); the exchange is a scene, not a transcript; and the two readings are both scholarly positions about the text's tone, neither settled by the text itself.
