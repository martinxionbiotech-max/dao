## The story

Zhuangzi was wandering in the preserve at Diaoling when he saw a strange magpie come from the south — wings seven feet across, eyes an inch around — brush his forehead, and settle in a chestnut grove. "What bird is this?" he said. "Wings great but won't travel; eyes huge but won't see!" He hitched up his robe, crept forward, slingshot ready — and saw a cicada, just settled in lovely shade, **having forgotten its body**; a mantis, raising its arms to seize it, **having forgotten its form** in the gain; and the strange magpie, taking advantage of them both, **having forgotten its truth** in the profit.

Zhuangzi shuddered. "Ah — **things are bound to each other, and the two kinds summon each other!**" He dropped his slingshot and turned to run — and the gamekeeper chased him, shouting.

Back home, for three months he did not leave the house. His disciple Lin Ju asked why. Zhuangzi said: "I kept my *form* and forgot my *self*; I gazed into muddy water and lost myself in the clear pool. And I have heard the master say: *enter a place, follow its customs.* I wandered into Diaoling and forgot my self; the magpie brushed my forehead; it wandered into the grove and forgot its truth; and the chestnut grove's keeper took me for a thief. That is why I do not go out."

## Framing: the chain, and the man at the end of it

- **The forgetting chain.** The passage is the book's 忘 vocabulary in its failure mode: 忘其身 → 忘其形 → 忘其真 — three creatures, three forgettings, one chain. The cicada forgets itself in *shade* (comfort), the mantis in *grasping* (gain), the magpie in *profit* (advantage) — and Zhuangzi himself, stalking them, forgets his self under the magpie's wings. The site's [zuowang concept page](/concepts/zuowang/) holds the term's practice side ("sitting in oblivion" as the deliberate emptying); this page is the term's warning side: **forgetting is not automatically sacred** — the whole animal kingdom does it, and it is how the food chain works. The deliberate forgetting and the automatic forgetting are two different verbs wearing one word.
- **The observer observed.** The fable's one image the proverb lost: the man with the slingshot — the *observer of the chain* — is himself observed, by a gamekeeper who takes him for a poacher. The [proverb 螳螂捕蝉，黄雀在后](/sources/) ("the mantis stalks the cicada, the oriole is behind") comes from the Han text *Shuo yuan*, not from this passage — the site double-registers both: the Zhuangzi's chain (cicada-mantis-magpie-*Zhuangzi*-keeper, five links) is the earliest image, and the proverb's "behind you" is this passage's point made portable. What the proverb flattened into two links, the passage keeps at five: **there is always another link, and you are it.**
- **The two chains of the book.** The site's [kui-xian-chain page](/stories/kui-xian-chain/) (ch. 17) holds the book's other chain — the pity chain of the centipede and the wind, where every link runs by its own heaven-mechanism and the chain ends in the unanswerable. This passage is the chain's dark twin: there, each link moves itself (天机); here, each link is *moved by the next one's blindness* (相累, mutual entanglement). Two chains, two verbs — and the site cross-links them as the book's complete map of how things are connected: self-running from below, mutually binding from behind.
- **三月不庭 — the book's hero, rattled.** Three months without leaving the house: the passage lets its own author be caught, shouted at, and shaken — 吾守形而忘身 ("I kept my form and forgot my self") is Zhuangzi's own confession, in the same 忘 grammar as the animals. The [huzi-jixian page](/stories/huzi-jixian/)'s master who cannot be read is the ideal; this page is the practice: even the book's hero is one link in the chain, and the day's work is to *notice* the slingshot in your own hand — which he does, and drops it. The dropping is the teaching.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

Because it is the book's own warning about the site's central practice word: the [sitting pages](/practices/jingzuo/) teach forgetting-as-practice; this passage teaches that forgetting-as-automatic is the trap the practice exists to interrupt. The [zuochi glossary](/glossary/zuochi/) (坐驰 — "sitting while galloping") is the same warning inside the sitting posture; this page is the same warning inside the world.

**Uncertainty preserved:** an *outer* chapter passage (later stratum, declared per the [layering rule](/sources/)); verified against the full text with variants (樊/野; 见利/见得) recorded; the proverb's attribution is double-registered (Zhuangzi for the image, *Shuo yuan* for the two-link proverb); 三月不庭 read as "did not leave the courtyard for three months," the standard reading.
