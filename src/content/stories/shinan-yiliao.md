## The passage

市南宜僚见鲁侯，鲁侯有忧色。市南子曰："君有忧色，何也？"鲁侯曰："吾学先王之道，修先君之业，吾敬鬼尊贤，亲而行之，无须臾离居，然不免于患，吾是以忧。"市南子曰："君之除患之术，浅矣！夫丰狐文豹，栖于山林，伏于岩穴，静也；夜行昼居，戒也；虽饥渴隐约，犹且胥疏于江湖之上而求食焉，定也：然且不免于罔罗机辟之患，是何罪之有哉？其皮为之灾也。今鲁国，独非君之皮邪？"

Shinan Yiliao called on the Duke of Lu, who looked troubled. "Why the troubled look?" The duke said: "I study the ways of the former kings, I carry on the work of my ancestors, I revere the ghosts and honor the worthy, practicing it all personally, never leaving it for a moment — and still I cannot escape disaster. That is why I am troubled." Shinan Yiliao said: "Your method of avoiding disaster is shallow. The fine-furred fox and the striped leopard dwell in the mountain forests, hidden in the rocky caves — stillness. They go out at night and stay in by day — watchfulness. Though hungry and thirsty, they keep apart, foraging on the rivers and lakes — steadiness. And still they cannot escape the nets and the traps. What crime have they committed? Their *fur* is their disaster. And is the state of Lu not your fur?"

And the cure, when the duke asks how to reach the far country he proposes: 君无形倨，无留居，以为君车 — no proud bearing, no clinging residence: that is your carriage; 少君之费，寡君之欲，虽无粮，而乃足 — cut your expenses, thin your desires, and without grain you will have enough; and the farewell image the passage ends on: 送君者皆自崖而反，君自此远矣 — those who see you off will all turn back from the shore; from there you are far away. 故有人者累，见有于人者忧 — having people is a burden; being had by people is a worry.

## Why this passage matters

- **The pelt diagnosis.** The chapter's opening move is the site's whole [politics line](/stories/phoenix-and-owl/) in one image: the duke has done everything the tradition prescribes — the rites, the ghosts, the worthies, day and night — and is *more* endangered, not less, because the accomplishment itself is the target. The fox and leopard are the passage's proof-clause: the three virtues named (静, 戒, 定 — stillness, watchfulness, steadiness) are real and do not save them; the fur cancels all three. 其皮为之灾也 — the pelt is the disaster — and the pelt of a ruler is the state of Lu itself. The site's [marsh pheasant](/stories/marsh-pheasant/) is the same arithmetic from the other side (the free bird pays ten steps a time; the caged one is fed and has lost the ten steps), and the [useful tree](/stories/mountain-tree-goose/) holds the chapter's opening version of the same cut: whatever is good for something is cut down for it.
- **The far country is a practice, not a place.** 建德之国 — the country of built virtue — is described in the book's plainest utopian prose (the people simple and uncarved, few private wishes, working without hoarding, giving without expecting return, wild in their walking yet treading the great square), and then the passage spends the rest of its length on the only question that matters: *how do I get there?* The answers are all equipment-removals: the carriage is the removal of bearing and residence; the supplies are the removal of expense and desire; and the arrival is the shore where the senders-off turn back. The site's [wuwei page](/glossary/wuwei/) holds the pattern: the way is not traveled by adding vehicles but by shedding them.
- **The social self as the final pelt.** The passage's last line does the book's quietest radicalism: 有人者累，见有于人者忧 — *having* people is a burden, and *being had* by people is a worry — the two halves of every office, every rank, every audience. The site's [empty boat](/stories/xuzhou-empty-boat/) (same chapter) is the companion story: the boat that gets cursed is the one with someone in it. Shinan Yiliao's duke is the boat *with a kingdom in it* — and the passage's prescription is the same: unload, and the collisions stop being personal.
- **The reading discipline.** The passage's utopian country tempts a reading as a political program, and the site keeps the [claim discipline](/glossary/qiwu/): the chapter offers no route-map to 建德之国 except the unburdening itself, and the passage's own close (the shore, the turning-back) is an image of departure, not a charter. It sits on the site beside the [phoenix page](/stories/phoenix-and-owl/) as the book's standing answer to what a career costs — the answer being, precisely, the question.
