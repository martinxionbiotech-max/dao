## The story

The emperor of the South Sea was Shu (儵, "Swift"); the emperor of the North Sea was Hu (忽, "Sudden"); the emperor of the Center was Hundun (浑沌, "Undifferentiated-Wholeness").

Swift and Sudden often met in Hundun's territory, and Hundun treated them with great kindness. The two emperors planned how to repay his virtue: "Everyone has seven holes for seeing, hearing, eating, and breathing — he alone has none. Let us try boring some for him."

Each day they bored one hole. On the seventh day, Hundun died.

## Framing: the last word of the inner chapters

The parable *closes* the inner chapters — the book's own chosen ending. It follows immediately on the mirror passage ("the perfected person's mind works like a mirror — neither welcoming nor escorting, responding without storing"), and it is that passage's negative proof: the complete is complete; adding to it is subtracting from it.

The names do the argument. 儵 and 忽 together form the idiom 倏忽 — "in a flash, abruptly" — the tempo of haste and intention. 浑沌 is the undivided, the wholeness before distinctions: in cosmological myth, the world-egg before heaven and earth split; in the meditative vocabulary, the state before the senses (the seven holes) carve experience into channels.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

Because it is the tradition's earliest and strongest warning against the *engineering* reading of practice — and this site's own boundary, stated by its source:

- **The seven holes are the sensory-motor interface itself** — seeing, hearing, eating, breathing. The parable says the one without them was whole. This is the mythic statement of what [zuowang](/concepts/zuowang/) states as method ("drop the body, dismiss hearing and sight" — the very faculties, in the same order of idea, that the emperors tried to install).
- **The murder weapon is kindness.** Swift and Sudden meant well; they bored the holes *to repay a debt of gratitude*. The parable's target is not malice but improvement — the well-meant addition, the sincere refinement. In practice terms: the tradition warns against "improving" stillness with technique, against filling the quiet with operations.
- **The inverse reading is the practical one.** What the emperors drilled outward — the senses — the practitioner learns to rest inward; the holes remain but the grasping through them stops. The mirror passage that precedes the parable says how: respond, don't store.

**Uncertainty preserved:** cosmological parable, not history; the imagery (the world-egg, the faceless god) belongs to the mythic layer of the tradition, and this page keeps its claim at the level the text makes it — an image, an ending, a warning.
