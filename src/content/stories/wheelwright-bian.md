## The story

Duke Huan was reading above in his hall. Wheelwright Bian was carving a wheel below. He laid down his mallet and chisel, went up, and asked: "May I ask what words Your Grace is reading?"

"The words of the sages," said the duke.

"Are the sages alive?" — "They are dead."

"Then what Your Grace is reading is the dregs of the ancients."

The duke said: "I am reading, and a wheelwright presumes to comment! If you have a reason, well; if not, you die."

Bian said: "Your servant looks at it from his own work. Carving a wheel: too slow, and it is loose and won't hold; too fast, and it bites and won't go in. **Not too slow, not too fast — the hand gets it and the heart answers. The mouth cannot say it; there is a number in it** (得之于手而应于心，口不能言，有数存焉于其间). I cannot pass it to my son, and my son cannot receive it from me. So at seventy I am still carving wheels. The ancients died together with what they could not transmit. Then what Your Grace is reading — is it not the dregs of the ancients?"

## Framing: the anti-text text

The story is a deliberate provocation — placed in the *Tiandao* ("Way of Heaven") chapter, whose own opening warns against treating even its words as the thing itself. It is the book's self-criticism: **the Zhuangzi knows it is books**, and it keeps one story that attacks reading from within the reading.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

Because it names the exact limit this site is built around:

- **"There is a number in it"** (有数存焉于其间) — the classical statement of *tacit knowledge*: the tuned middle that exists only in the practiced hand. The craftsman's "number" is the same reality the [Artisan Qing](/stories/artisan-qing/) calls "heaven joined with heaven" and the [cicada-catcher](/stories/cicada-catcher/) reaches by stacking balls: it is real, it is trainable, and **it is not in the words**.
- **The transmission problem.** "I cannot pass it to my son" — the story's second claim: what matters most does not travel through language, only through the hand, the long apprenticeship, the person. This is the classical warrant for the tradition's insistence on *teachers* — and the basis of this site's own caution that no text, including these pages, is a teacher.
- **The dregs.** 糟魄 ("dregs, lees") is deliberately insulting — and deliberately true in one sense: reading the words without the practice *is* drinking the lees. The site's answer, on every page, is that texts are maps, not terrain — the [translation policy](/blog/translation-policy/) states it as doctrine, and this story states it as a joke with a death threat in it.

**Uncertainty preserved:** parable, with Duke Huan of Qi as the straight man — historical framing, not history. The story's attack on books is itself transmitted by a book: the tradition has always read it as a warning *inside* the reading, not as a command to stop reading.
