## The story

Artisan Qing carved a bell-stand out of wood. When it was done, those who saw it were astonished, as if it were the work of ghosts and spirits. The Marquis of Lu came to see it and asked: "What art have you used to make this?"

Qing answered: "Your servant is a workman — what art would I have? There is only one thing. When I am about to make a bell-stand, I never let my qi be scattered. **I fast, to still the mind.**

"After fasting three days, I no longer dare to think of congratulations, rewards, rank, or salary. After fasting five days, I no longer dare to think of blame or praise, skill or clumsiness. After fasting seven days, I have forgotten my four limbs and my body.

"At that point there is no court, no ruler — my skill has become single and the outer world has fallen away. Then I go into the mountain forest and observe the heaven-nature of the trees. When the right form is present in the wood, the bell-stand is already seen in it — then I lay on my hand. If not, I leave it.

"This is heaven joined with heaven. That is why the vessel is suspected of being the work of spirits."

## Framing: what this story is

The story is the second panel of the Dazheng chapter's triptych on trained stillness (with the [wooden rooster](/stories/wooden-rooster/) and the cicada-catcher). Artisan Qing is a device — the text's craftsman who does explicitly, in seven days, what the whole book's contemplative program describes: **a graded fast of the mind**, day by day, from the outermost claims (reward, rank) to the innermost (the body itself).

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

The seven-day fast is the Zhuangzi's own stage-by-stage map of emptying, and it deserves to stand beside the Tang system it anticipates:

- **Days 1–3: forgetting outcome** (庆赏爵禄 — reward, salary, congratulations). The outer layer: the project's social stakes.
- **Days 4–5: forgetting evaluation** (非誉巧拙 — blame, praise, skill, clumsiness). The inner layer: the self-assessment that watching eyes install.
- **Day 7: forgetting the body** (忘吾有四枝形体). The same formula as zuowang's "drop the body" (堕肢体) in ch. 6 — the text's own cross-reference, half a millennium before Sima Chengzhen's [seven stages](/concepts/zuowang/) (respect-faith → cutting ties → gathering mind → simplifying → true insight → deep stability → attaining the Dao).
- **Then, and only then: work** — and even the work is described as *perception* ("the bell-stand is already seen in the wood") rather than imposition. The famous closing formula, 以天合天 ("heaven joined with heaven"), is the craftsman's version of wuwei: skill as the meeting of one's own nature with the material's nature, with no forcing between them.

**Uncertainty preserved:** parable, not history; the seven-day sequence is literary and mnemonic, not a literal schedule — and the tradition's later stage-systems read it as their ancestor, a reading this page records as such.
