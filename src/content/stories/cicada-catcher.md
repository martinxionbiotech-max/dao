## The story

Confucius, traveling to Chu, came out of a forest and saw a hunchback catching cicadas with a stick, picking them off as easily as if he were scooping them up.

"You are skilled!" said Confucius. "Do you have a Way?"

"I have a Way," said the man. "For five or six months I practiced balancing balls on the stick: when I could balance two without dropping them, I lost few cicadas; with three, I lost one in ten; with five, it was like scooping them up.

"My body stands like a stump, my arm holds the stick like a withered branch. However vast heaven and earth, however many the ten thousand things — **I know only the wings of the cicada.** I do not turn, I do not lean, and I would not trade the cicada's wings for the ten thousand things. How could I fail to get them?"

Confucius turned to his disciples and said: "**Intention undivided, spirit condensed** (用志不分，乃凝于神) — is this not what the hunchbacked old man shows?"

## Framing: what this story is

The third panel of the Dazheng triptych (after the [wooden rooster](/stories/wooden-rooster/) and [Artisan Qing](/stories/artisan-qing/)). Note the casting: Confucius — the very figure the text elsewhere debates — appears as the *witness who praises*, and his closing sentence is the one that became proverbial. The Zhuangzi is deliberately showing the two traditions agreeing on concentration.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

This is the text's cleanest description of *trained attention*, and it answers the question practitioners actually ask — "how do I stay with the object?" — with a three-part answer:

1. **Progressive loading.** Five months of incremental difficulty (one ball, two, three, five) before any attempt at the real task. Skill is built at the edge of ability, not willed into existence. The text's word is 累 ("accumulate, stack") — concentration is accumulated like the balls themselves.
2. **Somatic anchoring.** "Body like a stump, arm like a withered branch" — the stillness of the body is not decorative; it is the attention's infrastructure, the same claim the [sitting protocol](/tools/sitting-protocol/) documents from the manuals.
3. **The narrow aperture.** "I know only the wings of the cicada" — not the forest, not the audience, not the outcome. The tradition's word for this narrowing, 用志不分, becomes the standard formula for the whole culture: intention undivided, spirit condenses.

And the counterpoint the chapter supplies by juxtaposition: the wooden rooster's stillness was trained by *emptying* (nothing provokes it), while the hunchback's was trained by *filling* (one thing fills him). The Dazheng chapter holds both — the two faces of concentration the later traditions inherited as [zuowang](/concepts/zuowang/) and [shouyi](/concepts/shouyi/).

**Uncertainty preserved:** parable; the "Way" the hunchback describes is the text's own teaching placed in a craftsman's mouth — recorded as literature, not report.
