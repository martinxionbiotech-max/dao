## The story

Ferrying a boat across the river, if an **empty boat** drifts into yours — even a hot-tempered man does not rage. But put one person on it, and you shout for him to pull aside; a first call unheard, a second call unheard, and by the third call abuse follows.

**Then not angry, now angry; then empty, now full. If a person can empty himself and travel the world, who can harm him?**

## Framing: the experiment with anger

- **The book's cleanest psychological experiment.** Same collision, two outcomes, one variable: whether there is someone on the other boat *to be angry at*. The passage is the tradition's earliest controlled study of anger's object — 向也不怒而今也怒，向也虚而今也实, "before, not angry; now, angry. Before, empty; now, full." The anger does not live in the collision; it lives in the *person found* in the collision. The site's [zhi-shui glossary](/glossary/zhi-shui/) (the still water that reflects) and this passage are the book's two laboratory moments — the water-table experiment and the boat experiment — and both conclude the same way: what the still thing shows and what the empty thing takes are, precisely, *nothing*.
- **The famous close, and its two readings.** 人能虚己以游世，其孰能害之 — "if a person can empty himself and travel the world, who can harm him?" The surface reading is self-defense: *become the empty boat and no one's abuse can land.* But the passage's own logic supports the deeper one, and the site registers both: the empty boat is not protected from collisions — the empty boat **is still hit**; what changes is the *anger*, yours and theirs. "Who can harm you" is not a promise of safety but of an end to the harm that anger itself does — the [mountain-tree-goose page](/stories/mountain-tree-goose/)'s companion truth: the book refuses uselessness as a shelter, and here refuses emptiness as armor. The emptiness is the end of the *fuel*, not the end of the hits.
- **虚己 — the self-emptying word.** The passage's verb is the [xu concept page](/concepts/xu/)'s practical form: 虚己, "empty oneself" — not empty the world, not empty the boat's attacker; empty the *self* the anger needs. The book's [practice vocabulary](/practices/zuowang/) is downstream of this sentence: every sitting form the site documents is, in the tradition's own terms, training to be the empty boat.
- **The comedy of the third call.** The passage is almost a sketch: one call, two calls, and *then* the abuse — anger as an escalation ritual that the empty boat walks through unharmed. The humor matters, and the site keeps it: the book's anger-theory is delivered as farce, which is itself part of the theory — the rage is a performance looking for a cast member, and the empty boat declines the role.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

Because the site's [experience records](/experiences/exp-004-emotional-surfacing/) constantly meet anger *rising in sitting* — and this passage is the tradition's one-line answer to that event: the anger needed a full boat; sit, and the boat empties. The [practice-layer question](/questions/what-is-stillness/) answers the "how" with the site's full protocol; this page supplies the "why it works" in the book's own experiment.
