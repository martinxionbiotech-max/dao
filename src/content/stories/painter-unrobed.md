## The story

Lord Yuan of Song was assembling painters for a project. The court painters arrived, received their instructions, stood at attention, wet their brushes and mixed their ink — so many of them that half had to wait outside. One painter came late, ambling in no hurry, received the instructions without standing at attention, and went straight to his lodgings. The lord sent someone to look in on him. He had taken off his clothes and was sitting cross-legged, naked. The lord said: "That's it. This is a true painter."

## Framing: protocol as the obstacle, not the path

The story runs on a clean opposition: every court painter performs *correctness* — standing, waiting, readying — and the lord, who ordered the performance, instantly recognizes the one man who skipped it. Three things carry the meaning:

- **The lateness is the point.** 儃儃然不趋 — "ambling, unhurried." The painter is not late out of carelessness; his unhurriedness *is* the state from which the work will come. The tradition's [jing](/concepts/jing/) is defined exactly so: the mind moving without being moved. The painter does not do stillness; he is already still.
- **The undressing is the method.** 解衣般礡 (jieyi panbo — later a fixed idiom for unconstrained creation): clothes off, legs spread, no audience, no pose. The painter's first act as a painter is to remove everything between himself and the work — the body arranged for its own comfort, the self removed from the scene. It is the [zuowang](/practices/zuowang/) move done as a practical joke: forget the self *including the dignity of the self*.
- **The lord's verdict flips the hierarchy.** The patron who staged the ceremony endorses its violation: "this is a true painter." The [ziran](/concepts/ziran/) claim in miniature — the standard is not the protocol but the capacity the protocol was supposed to produce. The same Lord Yuan of Song appears in the [carpenter story](/stories/carpenter-shi/) as the man who *wants* the skill without the conditions; here he is the man who can at least recognize the conditions when he sees them.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

Because the knowledge base is the protocol, and the story is the tradition's warning about protocols. Every page here — the [sitting protocol](/tools/sitting-protocol/), the [reading order](/tools/reading-order/), the verified quotations — is the court of painters standing at attention. The tradition's own texts keep the unrobed painter in view: the point of the forms is the state they prepare, and the state, when it arrives, forgets the forms. The site's [translation policy](/blog/translation-policy/) says the same about words: the apparatus serves the arriving, and should be dropped when it blocks it.
