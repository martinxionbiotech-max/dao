## The story

**The Grand Horsekeeper asked the belt-hook smith: do you have skill, or do you have a dao?** The smith is eighty years old and has never lost a hair's-breadth of precision — his hooks still come out exact, after sixty years of the same work. His answer is the shortest formula in the book's whole craftsman line: *I have something I hold to* (臣有守也). From the age of twenty he has loved forging belt-hooks; toward things he has never looked; whatever is not a hook he has never examined (於物无視也，非鉤无察也).

Then the text does what it does in every craftsman story — it hands the last line to the theory, and the theory lands like an axiom: **what uses it, borrows what does not use it, and so keeps its use long** (是用之者，假不用者也，以長得其用). The hook-maker's lifetime of *not looking* is the capital that funds his looking. And the chapter presses one step further, into territory none of the earlier craftsmen claimed: *how much more, then, one who has nothing he does not use — what thing would not lend itself to him?* (而況乎无不用者乎？物孰不資焉？)

## Why it is told here

Because this is the craftsman-line's **pure form** — the story that strips the formula down to its arithmetic. Where Cook Ding needed nineteen years and a butcher's blade, where the cicada-catcher needed five months of pellet-stacking, where Artisan Qing needed seven days of fasting, the hook-smith needs only the verb: *he never examined anything that is not a hook*. Sixty years of non-attention, one object — and the chapter tells you the exchange rate: the unused years buy the used ones.

- **The negative space is the skill.** The site's [xu](/concepts/xu/) page holds the theory end — the empty room gathers, the channel gathers because it is empty. The hook-smith is the same sentence told as biography: he kept his attention *unspent* everywhere except the hook, and that is precisely why the hook never wore out his precision. It is the [wheelwright's](/stories/wheelwright-bian/) "what I hold in my hands" with the word 守 (shou, to hold/to keep) said aloud — the archive's own vocabulary for the same gesture.
- **The final clause is the chapter speaking, not the smith.** Like [Cook Ding](/stories/cook-ding/)'s "I have heard the words of my lord's cook and learned how to nourish life" and the [cicada-catcher](/stories/cicada-catcher/)'s Confucius postscript, the story ends with the doctrine stepping onto the stage. Here the doctrine is the most radical in the book: 无不用者 — one who has *nothing he does not use* — a state the text places beyond the smith's partial discipline, where even the division between used and unused falls away. The site's [wuwei](/concepts/wuwei/) page marks the same gradient: the craftsman's is the worked form of not-doing; the text's last clause points past it.
- **One object, sixty years.** The story is the archive's hardest answer to the site's [progress question](/experiences/questions/what-counts-as-progress/): the measure is not the object's beauty but the length of the not-looking that produced it. The belt-hook is small; the 守 is total. Against every doctrine of breadth, the chapter files its minority report: *whatever is not a hook, he has never examined.*
