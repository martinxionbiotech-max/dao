## The story

Zigong, traveling south through Chu and turning back toward Jin, passed through Han-shade and saw an old gardener preparing his beds. He had dug a tunnel into his well and came out hugging a clay jar to pour the water — straining, sweat-dark, spending enormous effort for tiny results.

Zigong said: "There is a machine for this. In one day it waters a hundred beds, with next to no effort and great results. Wouldn't you like it?" The gardener looked up: "How does it work?" "A lever of wood, weighted behind, light in front — it lifts water as if pulling, fast as a boiling pot. It is called the well-sweep."

The gardener's face darkened — and he laughed. "I have heard from my teacher: **where there are machines, there are machine-dealings; where there are machine-dealings, there is a machine-mind. When a machine-mind lodges in the chest, the pure white is not whole; when the pure white is not whole, the spirit's life is unsettled; and where the spirit's life is unsettled, the Way will not carry it.** It is not that I do not know the thing. **I would be ashamed to use it.**"

Zigong hung his head and could not answer. Then the gardener asked who he was. "A student of Confucius." The gardener answered: "Are you not one of those who study wide to imitate the sages, who puff themselves up above the crowd, who pluck a single string and sing sad songs to sell a name to the world? You should be about forgetting your spirit-breath and breaking down your body — and even that would be a beginning! You cannot govern yourself, and you have time to govern the world? Be on your way. Do not block my work."

Zigong lost all color and walked thirty li before recovering himself. His disciples asked what had happened. Zigong said: "I thought there was one man in the world — Confucius — and did not know there was *this* man. I have heard from the Master: in affairs, seek the possible; in work, seek the completion; the one who spends little force and sees great results — that is the sage's way. Now it is not so. One who holds the Way is whole in power; whole in power, whole in form; whole in form, whole in spirit — *that* is the sage's way. ... Utility and machine-skill make a man forget his own heart. A man like this gardener will not go where his intent does not go, will not do what his heart does not do. If all the world praised him, he would not care; if all the world condemned him, he would not accept it. To be untouched by the world's praise and blame — that is what is called the man of complete power. And I — I am what is called a man of the wind-waves." (The gloss of Cheng Xuanying, preserved in the received edition: water is still by nature, but when the wind rises, waves rise; my heart is unfixed, like those waves — hence "a man of the wind-waves.")

He returned to Lu and reported to Confucius. Confucius said: "That man is one who practices the art of the Chaos-clan. He knows the one and does not know the two; he governs what is within and does not govern what is without. The one who, clear and plain, enters the undyed; who, through non-action, returns to the uncarved; who embodies his nature and holds his spirit, and so wanders the common world — should you really be startled by him? As for the art of the Chaos-clan — how could you and I even know it?"

## Framing: the machine, the mind, and the man who already knew

- **The refusal is informed, not ignorant.** The passage's hinge is one sentence, and the site quotes it in full every time: 吾非不知，羞而不为也 — "it is not that I do not know it; I would be ashamed to use it." The gardener has seen the well-sweep; he describes it better than Zigong does. The fable is not Luddism — it is the book's earliest argument that **tools train the mind that uses them** (有机械者必有机事，有机事者必有机心 — the three-link chain), two and a half millennia before the argument needed a smartphone to be obvious. What the gardener refuses is not the water but the 机心 — the machine-mind — that the machine breeds.
- **纯白 — the same white as the empty room.** The machine-mind's first damage is 纯白不备: the pure white is not whole. The site's [xushi-sheng-bai glossary](/glossary/xushi-sheng-bai/) holds the word's other home — 虚室生白, the empty room gives birth to brightness — and the two passages are one claim read forward and backward: the emptied room's white is the *birth* of brightness; the machined chest's lost white is the *settling* of the spirit into unrest (神生不定 — the spirit's life unsettled, which is exactly the [zuochi](/glossary/zuochi/) state, sitting while galloping, at the level of the whole life). The [xinzhai concept](/concepts/xinzhai/) is the practice answer to this passage: what the fasting of the mind empties is, precisely, the machine-mind.
- **The book's double voice.** The passage stages the strongest anti-Confucian street theater in the book — Zigong, named disciple, is taken apart by an unnamed gardener — and then *gives the last word to Confucius*, who praises the gardener as 假修浑沌氏之术者, "one who practices the art of the Chaos-clan," while marking him partial: 识其一，不知其二 (he knows the one, not the two), 治其内不治其外 (governs within, not without). The site's [hundun story](/stories/hundun/) (ch. 7, the primal Chaos bored to death by kindness) is the clan's other portrait, and the cross-link is deliberate: the gardener is the Chaos-clan's living member, and the chapter lets his own master be quoted against him — Zigong's 事求可，功求成 (seek the possible, complete the work) is *Confucius's* teaching, recorded verbatim in the same passage. The book does not choose; it shows both men quoting their masters.
- **The word's afterlife, narrowed.** 机心 survives in modern Chinese — but narrowed: the modern word means *scheming, calculating mind*, where the passage's chain is causal and specific (machine → machine-mind → spirit unsettled → the Way will not carry it). The site flags the drift honestly: the word kept its warning tone and lost its argument. And 抱瓮灌畦 itself survives as the standing idiom for the informed refusal of efficiency — the idiom, unlike most, still carries the scene's full irony: the one who refuses the machine is the one who best understands it.
- **What this is not.** The passage is not the site's endorsement of refusing technology, and it is not a health manual. The site registers it as an *outer chapter* text (attribution to Zhuangzi himself is, per the sources the site verified, contested), and reads it the way the chapter reads itself: as a question about what practice protects — the [zuowang concept](/concepts/zuowang/)'s forgetting is the gardener's 忘汝神气 in the mouth of the man he despises, which is the book's joke: the gardener and the Confucian are each quoting the other's remedy.

## Why it belongs in this knowledge base

Because it is the tradition's own warning about the site's own subject: a knowledge base *about* sitting is a kind of well-sweep — a machine for watering many beds. The passage asks, in its own voice, whether the machine that waters a hundred beds still waters the gardener. The site's answer is its structure: keep the [pure white](/glossary/xushi-sheng-bai/) as the measure, and treat the machine as what it is — a tool, watched.

**Uncertainty preserved:** an *outer* chapter passage (ch. 12, later stratum, declared per the [layering rule](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/); the chapter's authorship is disputed per the study notes the site verified); two independent full texts agree; the 风波之民 gloss is Cheng Xuanying's, from the received edition, recorded as commentary; Confucius's verdict and Zigong's self-indictment are quoted from the same passage, neither voice promoted over the other.
