## The term

大块 (dàkuài) — 块, a clod, a lump, a mass of earth; 大块, the great clod. The term appears twice in the site's [verified passages](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/), and the two uses bracket the book's cosmology:

- **As the breather** (ch. 2, opening the chapter): 夫大块噫气，其名为风 — "The great clod breathes out; the breath is called wind" — and the breathing's sound runs through every hollow: 是唯无作，作则万窍怒呺 — "only when it does not stir; when it stirs, the ten thousand hollows roar."
- **As the carrier** (ch. 6, the [four friends](/stories/four-friends/)): 大块载我以形，劳我以生，佚我以老，息我以死 — "the great clod carries me with a form, labors me with life, rests me with age, and quiets me with death."

## Why the term matters

- **The anti-creationist name.** The tradition's own gloss (the Zhuangzi yi commentary, verified on the passage) makes the point explicit: 塊者，無物也 — the clod is *that which has no thing-ness*; and 物之生也，莫不塊然而自生 — "the birth of things: nothing that is not **clod-like, self-born**." The word is chosen precisely because a clod has no face and no plan: the world-process is not a maker, not a person, not a mind — it is a mass that **breathes and carries** without being asked. The [ziran concept page](/concepts/ziran/) holds the family; 大块 is its heaviest member — the 自 in 自生 given the weight of earth.
- **The same clod does both jobs.** Ch. 2's clod *breathes out the wind* (the chapter's opening scene, whose hollows are the [吾丧我 passage](/translations/qiwulun-wu-sang-wo/)'s three sounds — earth, human, and heaven); ch. 6's clod *carries the body through its four seasons* (form, life, age, death). The site keeps the two together because the book does: the same impersonal mass gives you your body and your weather — which is exactly what makes death in ch. 6 *rest* (息我以死) rather than punishment: the carrier that labors you also quiets you, and the labor was never personal.
- **The wind's connection.** 大块噫气 is the site's anchor for the book's wind-family: the [pity chain](/stories/kui-xian-chain/)'s wind (ch. 17, roaring from the North Sea to the South) and this ch. 2 wind are the same breathing — and both passages end in the same refusal to find the driver (怒者其谁邪 in ch. 2; the chain's silence at the mind in ch. 17). The [tian-ji glossary](/glossary/tian-ji/) holds that refusal's vocabulary.

## Usage notes

The term stays in pinyin with the gloss "the great clod"; the clod's *self-birth* reading (块然而自生) is always carried with the term, because it is the term's whole point against maker-readings; both attested uses are cited, per the site's rule.
