## The term

朝彻 (zhāochè) — 朝, morning, dawn; 彻, to pass through, to penetrate to the far side. In the [Nü Yu ladder](/translations/yingning-passage/), the term names the stage after the three exclusions:

"...life put outside, then came the **dawn-clearing**. Dawn-clearing, then the seeing of the alone."
(已外生矣，而后能朝彻；朝彻而后能见独。)

## Why the term matters

- **It is a clearing, not a doing.** The ladder's grammar is the site's most quoted precision: 外天下, 外物, 外生 — three *acts* of putting outside, each with its day-count — and then the verb changes. 朝彻 is not performed; it *arrives*. The exclusions are the practitioner's work; the dawn is what happens when the work is done. The site keeps this distinction because it decides how the passage is taught: the tradition does not say "produce the dawn-clearing" — it says subtract, and the clearing comes.
- **The negative side is [jing](/concepts/jing/).** The ladder's stillness is not dullness; 朝彻 is the positive — the dawn is a *quality of light*, and the standard gloss (preserved in the teaching editions) is exactly that: 心境如朝阳一般清新明彻, the mind clear and fresh as the morning sun.
- **The pair with 见独.** The site's [jian-du glossary](/glossary/jian-du/) is this term's other half, and the two pages are written to be read together: 朝彻 → 见独 — clearing, then seeing. The dawn clears the sky; the seeing of the alone is what the cleared sky reveals. Without the clearing, no seeing; without the seeing, the clearing is only a mood. Together they are the ladder's turning point, where subtraction ends and culmination begins.
- **The earliest name for the moment subtraction pays.** The [ch. 48 translation](/translations/daodejing-48/) holds the book's subtraction verb (为道日损 — the daily lessening) and the [zuowang concept](/concepts/zuowang/) holds its practice name; 朝彻 is the Zhuangzi's single word for what the lessening yields — the sudden, unearned clarity. The dawn image says the rest: clarity comes like morning, on its own schedule, after the night's work is over — which is also why the ladder's day-numbers (three, seven, nine) are pedagogy, not a timetable, per the site's [day-count caveat](/experiences/questions/how-long-should-i-sit/).
- **Not a light.** The dawn here is the mind's clarity, not a visual phenomenon — the same boundary the [seeing-light page](/problems/seeing-light-in-sitting/) enforces for 虚室生白: the [xushi-sheng-bai glossary](/glossary/xushi-sheng-bai/) holds the emptied room's brightness, and both terms are state-metaphors. The site never merges them into a license for chasing inner lights.

## Usage notes

The term stays in pinyin with the gloss "dawn-clearing"; it is always introduced through the ladder's full sentence because the three exclusions are its definition; it is cross-linked with [jian-du](/glossary/jian-du/) (its pair), [yingning](/glossary/yingning/) (the ladder's summit), and [daodejing-48](/translations/daodejing-48/) (the subtraction it consummates).
