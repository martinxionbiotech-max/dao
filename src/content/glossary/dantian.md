## The term

丹田, "cinnabar field" — a *field* (田) in the sense of cultivated ground, holding *cinnabar* (丹), the traditional elixir image. The term names places in the body where vital substance is said to be stored and refined — the fixed geography of Chinese contemplative practice for nearly two millennia.

## What the sources say

The **Huangting neijing jing** (inner-scripture of the Yellow Court, Shangqing tradition, c. 3rd–4th c.) first systematized the system of **three dantian**: "essence-qi is subtle within the three fields" (三田之中精气微). The standard later map:

- **Upper dantian** — in the head, between the brows and inward (the "mud-pill palace," 泥丸宫);
- **Middle dantian** — at the heart (the "crimson palace," 绛宫);
- **Lower dantian** — below the navel (also called 关元 or 命门), where essence is stored ("men store essence, women store the fetus") — the center of internal-alchemy practice, and the field modern practitioners mean by default when they say "dantian."

The refinement sequence of later inner alchemy — refine essence into qi, qi into spirit — is located on this map: substance gathered and refined at the fields.

## The modern double life

Modern Chinese practice communities use "dantian" constantly, mostly in two registers:

- **Sensation language:** reports of warmth or "filling" at the lower abdomen during sitting (see [EXP-003](/experiences/exp-003-leg-pain-filling/) and the [warmth pattern](/patterns/warmth-and-qi-sensations/)) are routinely described as "the dantian responding" or "qi gathering at the dantian."
- **Attention instruction:** "guard the dantian" (意守丹田) functions as a modern sitting instruction — placing attention at the lower abdomen — descending from the older practice of guarding the One (shouyi).

**The discipline this site applies:** the dantian map is **traditional doctrine, not anatomy**. No dissected or imaged structure corresponds to any dantian; the "lower dantian" is a functional-attentional location, not an organ. When a practitioner reports warmth at the dantian, the *sensation* is real as experience while the *location* is interpretive. Both the traditional map and the modern physiological reading (interoceptive attention amplifying ordinary abdominal sensation) are recorded; neither is asserted as fact.

**For translators:** "cinnabar field" is the standard literal gloss, but "dantian" left transliterated is now conventional in English meditation writing, and "elixir field" is common in academic translations of alchemical texts.
