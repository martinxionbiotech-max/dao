## The term

齐物 (qíwù) — "the leveling of things": 齐, to make even, to treat as one; 物, things — everything, including the person and the person's claims. The term is the first half of the chapter title 齐物论, and the title itself carries the doctrine as a grammatical ambiguity the tradition has argued about for a millennium: read 齐物-论 and it is "the discourse on leveling things"; read 齐-物论 and it is "leveling the discourses about things." The chapter does both — and the ambiguity is the term's first lesson: the things and the claims about them are leveled *together*, because the claims are themselves things.

## Why the term matters

- **The pivot is the method.** The chapter's engine is the image it gives for its own procedure: 枢 (the hinge, the pivot) — stand at the pivot of the Way and answer to the endless yes/no's without taking a side. 齐物 is not a doctrine that things *are* equal (the chapter never denies their differences — it [counts them](/stories/three-in-the-morning/) meticulously); it is a *move*: view them from the pivot, where difference and sameness stop being rivals. The site's [three-in-the-morning](/stories/three-in-the-morning/) page is the term's parable, and the [wu sang wo page](/glossary/wu-sang-wo/) is its precondition — the leveling begins with losing the me that keeps the score.
- **The two readings, and what each protects.** The Song reading (level the *claims*) guards the chapter's epistemology: every school's 物论 — its theory of things — is one more thing to be leveled, and the chapter's parade of unanswerable disputes (is it this or that? [the butterfly](/stories/butterfly-dream/), [the shaman's readings](/stories/huzi-jixian/)) is the demonstration. The early reading (level the *things*) guards its ontology: the landing point is the things themselves, freed from the claims laid over them — the [hidden light](/glossary/bao-guang/) that stores all without sorting. The site holds both readings as the tradition's own, and adds the one observation the scholarship registers: the word 齐 never occurs in the chapter — the title is the editor's summary, and the doctrine's name came after the doctrine.
- **What the leveling is not.** The chapter's own warning, held by the site per its [claim discipline](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/): 齐物 is not the claim that all positions are equally true (that would be one more 物论, and the weakest one); it is the move that *ends the tournament* — 莫若以明, "better to use the light." The leveling is epistemic hygiene, not relativism: the chapter does not say the claims are equal; it says the claiming is the illness.
- **The drift.** 齐物 survives in modern Chinese as 齐物论 (the chapter name) and in philosophical vocabulary, but the verb 齐 today runs toward *uniformity* (整齐 — neat, uniform; 对齐 — alignment) — the standardizing sense the chapter itself refused, and which Mencius had already named against (夫物之不齐，物之情也 — the unevenness of things is their nature, Mencius 3A4, registered as the parallel the scholarship cites). The site keeps the term in its chapter sense: 齐 as the pivot's view, not the ruler's grid.
- **The recommendation.** Keep the term as "the leveling of things" and the title as "discourse on leveling things" (the Watson-tradition rendering), with a first-use note that the title's grammar is deliberately ambiguous — the site's own [translation of the chapter opening](/translations/qiwulun-wu-sang-wo/) carries the ambiguity in its note.
