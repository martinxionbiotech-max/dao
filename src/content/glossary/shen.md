**Shen** (神, shén) — "spirit," the third term of the classical triad [jing–qi–shen](/glossary/jing-qi-shen/) (essence–qi–spirit). In the meditative context it names the mind in its *luminous, animating* aspect: what condenses when attention becomes single ("intention undivided, spirit condenses" — 用志不分，乃凝于神, Zhuangzi ch. 19), and what is unsettled by contrivance ("when pure whiteness is not whole, the spirit grows unsettled; when the spirit grows unsettled, the Dao does not dwell there" — ch. 12). As the finest of the three treasures, shen is the summit of the alchemical path: refine essence to qi, qi to spirit, spirit to emptiness.

**Usage note:** shen overlaps with but is not identical to the modern "mind" (xin 心, [entry](/glossary/xin/)) — the tradition distributes mental life across both terms, roughly as the vessel (xin) and its light (shen). Translators have used "spirit," "numen," "spirituality," and "mind"; this site uses "spirit" with a gloss where the meditative sense matters.
