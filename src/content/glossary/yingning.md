## The term

撄宁 (yīngníng) — 撄, to disturb, jostle, entangle; 宁, stillness, peace. The Zhuangzi defines it in the [Dazongshi](/stories/fish-in-the-dry-spring/) chapter, at the end of the ladder of the sage-woman Nüyu:

"…it sends all things off, welcomes all things in, destroys all, completes all. Its name is **yingning**. Yingning means: *disturbed, and only then complete.*"
(其为物，无不将也，无不迎也，无不毁也，无不成也。其名为撄宁。撄宁也者，撄而后成者也。)

## Why the term matters

Three reasons, each structural to this site:

- **It corrects the most common misunderstanding of stillness.** 撄而后成 — "disturbed, and then complete" — the stillness is *made inside the disturbance*, not achieved by escaping it. The traditional commentary (as in the verified Shidian Guji edition) reads it exactly so: the work is done "in the midst of chaotic conditions" (从杂乱境缘中做出). This is the tradition's own answer to the objection that quiet only counts when nothing disturbs it — the same point the [drowsiness pattern](/patterns/drowsiness-vs-stillness/) makes against mistaking dullness for stillness, and the [restlessness page](/problems/restlessness-in-sitting/) inherits.
- **It is the ladder's summit, not its first rung.** The term arrives after the chapter's staged sequence — three days to put the world outside, seven for things, nine for the self, then the dawn-clearing (朝彻), the seeing of the alone (见独), the end of past and present, the unborn and undying. Yingning is what the whole ladder *produces*: not a stillness that avoids the world but a stillness that sends off, welcomes, destroys, and completes — 无不将也，无不迎也 — the exact verbs the [mirror passage](/translations/yongxin-ruo-jing-passage/) negates for the *person* (不将不迎) and affirms for the *Dao* itself. The perfected mind stops escorting and welcoming; the stillness it rests in is the Dao's own motion.
- **It names the site's working model of practice.** The [experiences archive](/experiences/) — the [night movements](/experiences/exp-005-night-qi-movement/), the [emotional surfacing](/experiences/exp-004-emotional-surfacing/), the [restless body](/experiences/exp-012-restless-body/) — is a catalogue of disturbance; and the site's whole editorial stance, from the [comparisons](/comparisons/zuowang-vs-mindfulness/) to the [protocol](/tools/sitting-protocol/), is that disturbance is not the opposite of the practice but its material. Yingning is the one-word name of that stance.

## Usage notes

The term enters the later tradition's technical vocabulary for the state "as if affected by things, yet not moved" — and it stands opposite [zuochi](/glossary/zuochi/), the "sitting while galloping" of the un-stilled mind: one is disturbance without stillness, the other stillness inside disturbance. The site keeps the term in pinyin with the gloss "stillness amid disturbance," per the [translation policy](/blog/translation-policy/).
