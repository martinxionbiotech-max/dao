## The term

玄牝 (xuánpìn) — 玄, dark, deep, mysterious; 牝, the female of an animal, the womb-side of the opposition. The term appears once, in [ch. 6](/translations/daodejing-06/) of the Daodejing, the book's shortest chapter:

谷神不死，是谓玄牝。玄牝之门，是谓天地根。绵绵若存，用之不勤。

"The **valley-spirit does not die** — this is called the mysterious female. The gate of the mysterious female — this is called the root of heaven and earth. Continuous, as if existing; drawn on, never labored."

## Why the term matters

- **Three images in one sentence.** The chapter stacks its whole cosmogony in twenty-five characters: the **valley** (the hollow — the [tuoyue glossary](/glossary/tuoyue/)'s bellows-family and the [xu concept](/concepts/xu/)'s emptiness, the same low place the [ch. 22 translation](/translations/daodejing-22/) pairs with fullness); the **spirit** (谷神 — the inexplicable generative power, the character 神 doing double duty as power and mystery); and the **mysterious female** — the book's most explicit maternal figure. The received gloss (kept on this page from the Gushiwen teaching edition) says it plainly: 玄牝 is the source that breeds the ten thousand things, an image of the Way.
- **The gate and the root.** 玄牝之门，是谓天地根 — the *gate* of the mysterious female is the *root* of heaven and earth. The [ch. 1 translation](/translations/daodejing-01/) holds the book's other gate (众妙之门, the gate of all mysteries) and the [guan glossary](/glossary/guan/) holds the watching that happens at it — but ch. 6's gate is the only one named as a **root**, and the only one attached to a mother. The site keeps the two gates linked because the book does: one chapter says where mysteries come out, the other says what the source *is*.
- **绵绵若存 — continuous, as if existing.** The chapter's closing phrase is the tradition's standard description of the source's felt quality: thread-thin, seemingly there, seemingly not — and 用之不勤, "drawn on, never labored" (勤 read as 尽, exhausted, per the standard gloss). It is the same arithmetic as the [ch. 22](/translations/daodejing-22/) reversals: what seems barely there is what never runs out. For the site's [practice pages](/practices/jingzuo/) this is the canonical sentence for what a settled sitting feels like — the state the [falling-asleep question](/experiences/questions/falling-asleep-during-meditation/) distinguishes from sleep: not vivid, not absent; continuous, as if existing.
- **Not anatomy.** The term's history includes later inner-alchemy readings that locate 玄牝 inside the body; the site does not adopt them. The glossary records the received-text meaning (the generative image of the Way) and the [source policy](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/) applies: physiology claims from later schools are registered on their own pages, not here.

## Usage notes

The term stays in pinyin with the gloss "mysterious female"; the valley-spirit line is always quoted in full because the term is only defined by its sentence; the reading follows the received text and the teaching-edition gloss.
