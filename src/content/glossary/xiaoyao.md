## The term

逍遥 (xiāoyáo) — a binome of motion: 逍, far; 遥, distant — not a place but a manner of moving: unmoored, unhurried, not fixed to any coordinate. The book opens with it — the first chapter is named after it (逍遥游, "the wandering of free wandering") — and closes ch. 1 with its own image: the useless tree planted in 无何有之乡 (the village of nowhere) and 广莫之野 (the wide wilderness), where one may 彷徨乎无为其侧 (roam idly at its side) and 逍遥乎寝卧其下 (wander free and sleep beneath it) — untouched by axe, harmed by nothing, because there is nothing to use it for.

## Why the term matters

- **Guo Xiang's definition: 无待.** The commentary tradition preserved in the verified edition defines the word by what it lacks: 待 — dependence, expectation, the condition of needing something to be provided. "If free wandering is tied to a place (系于有方), then even set loose, it is bounded — it has not yet reached non-dependence (未能无待也)." 逍遥 is not freedom *to* — it is freedom *from* needing: the book's answer to [what stillness is](/questions/what-is-stillness/) in the register of motion.
- **The second canonical usage: the business of no business.** Ch. 19's Bianzi speaks the word in its working form: 逍遥乎无事之业 — free-wandering in the task of no task — and glosses it with the Daodejing's not-claiming quartet (为而不恃，长而不宰 — the same formula the site holds in [ch. 10](/translations/daodejing-10-shouyi/) and [ch. 2](/translations/daodejing-02/)). 逍遥 is what *doing without claiming* feels like from inside — the book's own bridge between the [non-action concept](/concepts/wuwei/) and the moving body.
- **The tree-image is the whole.** The ch. 1 close makes the word a *location*: beside the tree nobody wants, in the nowhere that is safe for that reason. The site's [useless-gourd](/stories/useless-gourd/), [peng-bird](/stories/peng-bird/), and [bole-horses](/stories/bole-horses/) pages are the term's story-wings: 逍遥 is what the useless do, and the useful die of — the [survival arithmetic](/stories/zhili-shu/) in its freest form.
- **The drift, and one striking modern turn.** 逍遥 today means "carefree, at leisure" — the motion survives, the *无待* cosmology thins into mood. And in one modern legal usage the word kept its edge: 逍遥法外 — "wandering beyond the law" — where 逍遥 names *escape from accountability*, the word's freedom-flavor inverted by the law it escapes. The site registers both per its [idiom discipline](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/): the word still means what it meant — unfixed — and unfixed cuts both ways.
- **The recommendation.** Render 逍遥 as "free wandering" or "unfettered wandering" rather than the looser "carefree": the word is a manner of motion, not a mood — and the chapter's 游 (wandering) is the practice verb, matching the [mind's wandering](/glossary/xin/) the book teaches elsewhere.
