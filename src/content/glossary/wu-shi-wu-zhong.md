## The term

**无始无终** — *no beginning, no end* — appears in the Zhuangzi's [Zhi Bei You dialogue between Ran Qiu and Confucius](/stories/ran-qiu-asks-zhongni/), and it is preceded there by its twin: 无古无今, *no past, no present*. The four characters are the answer to a specific question — Ran Qiu's "can anything be known about the time before heaven and earth existed?" — and their function is to delete the line that the question presumes. Confucius answers first with a single sentence (*yes: the ancient is like the present*), then, when Ran Qiu comes back the next day dim instead of clear, with the full theorem:

**无古无今，无始无终。未有子孙而有孙子，可乎？** — *No past, no present; no beginning, no end. For there to be children and grandchildren without there having been children and grandchildren — could that be?*

The example carries the whole argument. Children come into being from no prior children in every generation; nobody finds this strange. The question "what was before heaven and earth" presumes that the world is different from children — that a world *needs* a prior thing to stand on. The chapter's answer is that it does not. Guo Xiang's commentary condenses the point to nine words: heaven and earth always endure — *there was no time before they were*.

The rest of the passage unpacks what the four characters imply. Death and life do not create each other — *each has its own integrity* (皆有所一體). The origin of things is not a thing (物物者非物). And the turn that gives the term its weight: because the having of things is without end, *the sage's love of people is also without end* (圣人之爱人终无已) — he draws it from this very source. No-beginning is not a claim about the deep past; it is a property of the present, and its ethical face is care without a cutoff.

## How it is used on this site

- **As the time-denial behind the origin question.** The site's [creator question](/experiences/questions/does-daoism-believe-in-a-creator/) answers "no" at the source-text level, and this term is one of the reasons: the Zhuangzi does not replace the creator with an older creator but removes the *before* that a creator would have to stand in. The [dao](/concepts/dao/) page's core sentence — the dao is not one more member of the class it produces — is the same move made with time.
- **As the frame for the chapter's no-question rule.** In the same chapter, [Zhi's three questions](/stories/zhi-wen-wuwei/) about knowing the dao are answered by silence, and the Beginningless's verdict is that the question itself is the error. 无始无终 is why: a dao with a beginning could be located, and therefore known and said. The four characters are what makes the dao unaskable — not too deep to answer, but without the edges that a question needs.
- **As the classical anchor for the practice's non-progress language.** The site's [progress question](/experiences/questions/what-counts-as-progress/) warns against measuring stages as rungs on a ladder with a top; the [ziran](/concepts/ziran/) page holds the same warning for the word "nature." This term is their common ancestor: the sitting tradition's refusal to promise a finish line is the meditation-practice form of the chapter's refusal to admit a starting line.

**One distinction the site keeps sharp:** 无始无终 is not the modern physics claim that time is an illusion, and it is not the promise of personal immortality. The chapter is doing grammar, not cosmology: it is answering a question about the origin of the world by correcting the shape of the question. The site's [translation policy](/blog/translation-policy/) registers this kind of term as "untranslatable-but-explainable" — the four characters survive as a unit, and the explanation travels beside them.
