**Xi** (息, xī) — "breath," and specifically, in the meditative vocabulary, the **finest of the four breath types** named by the [Xiao Zhiguan](/texts/xiao-zhiguan/): after wind (audible, labored), panting (noisy), and qi (silent but ordinary), comes 息 — the "rest" breath, fine and continuous, "as if present, as if absent." It is the target state of [tiaoxi](/practices/tiaoxi/) and the bridge word to [taixi](/practices/taixi/): the same character 息 sits at the heart of 胎息 ("embryonic breathing"), the tradition's name for the breath become so interior it seems to need no nose or mouth.

**Usage note:** 息 also means "to rest, to cease" (休/息) — and the Zhuangzi's "the true person breathes from the heels" (真人之息以踵) uses the noun at full depth: breath as the whole-body resting movement. The character's two senses (breath and rest) are not an accident in this tradition: the finest breath *is* the resting breath. This site renders it "rest breath" inside the four-breath ladder and plain "breath" elsewhere, so the ladder's technical sense stays visible.
