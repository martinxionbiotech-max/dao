## The term

天钧 (tiānjūn) — 天, heaven; 钧, the potter's wheel (also read 均, evenness, balance). The term appears in the Qiwulun's closing move, immediately after the [three-in-the-morning](/stories/three-in-the-morning/) fable:

"The sage therefore **harmonizes the rights and wrongs and rests on Heaven's wheel** — this is called **letting both go**."
(是以圣人和之以是非，而休乎天钧，是之谓两行。)

## Why the term matters

- **The wheel, not the scale.** The potter's wheel balances by *turning*: whatever rests on the rim is carried around, and the center stays still. The image is chosen against the scale's either/or — the sage does not adjudicate between 是 and 非 (this is right / that is right) but 和之, harmonizes them, and then **rests** (休). The resting is the practice-word: the [three-in-the-morning](/stories/three-in-the-morning/) keeper rests on the wheel, not on a verdict.
- **两行 — both go.** The term's companion definition: 两行, "the two both proceed." The site records the pair together because the passage defines one by the other — resting on Heaven's wheel *is* letting both go. The [xu concept page](/concepts/xu/) keeps the family's negative discipline (emptiness as the refusal to fill), and 天钧 is the positive image of the same refusal: the refusal to be the one who decides, pictured as a rotation one rides.
- **It closes the fable's argument.** The three-in-the-morning keeper fed the monkeys three in the morning, four in the evening — or four and three; the substance was unchanged, only the mood reversed. The passage's own gloss (preserved in the teaching editions and quoted on the [story page](/stories/three-in-the-morning/)) is that names and realities were never harmed, and anger and joy were *used* — and the sage's resting on the wheel is the same move raised to principle. The term is thus the chapter's last word on [this and that](/translations/qiwulun-wu-sang-wo/) — the whole debate machinery dissolved into a rim.

## Usage notes

The term stays in pinyin with the gloss "Heaven's wheel"; the site notes the graphic family 钧/均 (wheel/evenness) without choosing between the readings, and keeps the pair 天钧–两行 as one definition, per the passage's own grammar.
