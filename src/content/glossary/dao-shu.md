## The term

道枢 (dàoshū) — "the hinge of the Way." The term is born in the Qi Wu Lun's central sequence, one sentence before the better-known image it hands off to: 彼是莫得其偶，谓之道枢。枢始得其环中，以应无穷 — when that-and-this no longer get their counterparts, this is called the hinge of the Way; the hinge comes to rest at the ring's center, and from there answers the endless. 是亦一无穷，非亦一无穷也。故曰莫若以明 — the "is" is one endless, the "is-not" is another endless; therefore nothing equals using the clear light.

## Why the term matters

- **The hinge is what remains when the pairing stops.** The word 枢 is a door-hinge — the wooden pivot a door turns on — and the passage earns it: 偶 is the counterpart, the paired opposite (that/this, right/wrong, mine/yours), and the hinge is what is left when the pairs stop functioning. The site's [qiwu page](/glossary/qiwu/) holds the stakes: the leveling of things is not the assertion that all positions are equal (that is itself one more position); it is the *dissolution of the pairing machinery*. 道枢 is the name of the point where that dissolution lands — not a doctrine to hold but a pivot to stand on.
- **The three terms, one mechanism.** The site keeps this passage's family as three separate pages of one doctrine, and the order matters: 道枢 is the *hinge itself*; [环中](/glossary/huan-zhong/) is the *standing place* the hinge reaches (the ring's center, where the circumference's endless positions cannot grip you); [两行](/glossary/liang-xing/) is the *motion* from that place (walking two roads at once, harmonizing right and wrong while resting on the [heavenly balance](/glossary/tian-jun/)). The hinge precedes the place and the walk — and the passage says so with one small word: 枢始得其环中 — the hinge *for the first time* gets the ring's center. The pivot must be found; it is an achievement, not a default. The site's [true-ruler page](/glossary/zhen-zai/) holds the corresponding claim about the self: the ruler is not a thing in the inventory but what remains when the inventory stops.
- **The hinge answers; it does not refuse.** 以应无穷 — "in order to answer the endless" — is the clause that keeps the hinge from becoming an escape hatch. The center of the ring is not outside the wheel; it is what the wheel turns on, and its business is still *response* (the same grammar as the mirror's 应而不藏 on the site's [mirror passage](/translations/yongxin-ruo-jing-passage/): respond to everything, store nothing). The book's own claim about the endless is symmetrical: 是亦一无穷，非亦一无穷也 — the "is" and the "is-not" are both endless, which is exactly why no endpoint can be argued into existence; the hinge is what remains usable when both infinities are seen.
- **莫若以明 — the light is the method, not a conclusion.** The passage closes the hinge-sequence with its own recommendation: nothing equals using the clear light. The site's [three-in-the-morning page](/stories/three-in-the-morning/) holds the sentence in action (the keeper holds three-four and four-three as the same nuts and lets the monkeys' joy and anger do the rest): 明 is the clarity that sees both infinities at once without needing either to win. The drift warning stands: modern Chinese keeps 枢纽 (hinge/pivot) as an administrative word — the traffic hub, the political pivot — and has lost the clause that made it philosophical: the hinge is what the opposites *stopped* producing.
