## The term

朝彻 (zhāochè) — 朝, morning, dawn; 彻, to pass through, to be thorough, to be clear. In the [Nü Yu ladder](/translations/yingning-passage/), the term names the turning point of the whole sequence:

"...after he could put his own life outside, then came the **dawn-clearing**. Dawn-clearing, then the seeing of the alone."
(已外生矣，而后能朝彻；朝彻而后能见独。)

## Why the term matters

- **The ladder is gradual until it is sudden.** The ladder's three exclusions are dated and slow — [three days, seven days, nine days](/translations/yingning-passage/) — and then, with nothing left to remove, the turn arrives in a single word: the clearing is not a fourth subtraction but what the subtractions *release*. 朝彻 is the tradition's earliest structural claim about practice: preparation is gradual; the shift itself is sudden. The site's [jing](/concepts/jing/) concept page keeps the negative side (the stillness that is not dullness); 朝彻 is the positive — the dawn is a *quality of light*, and the standard gloss (preserved in the teaching editions) is exactly that: 心境如朝阳一般清新明彻, the mind clear and fresh as the morning sun.
- **It sits between the exclusions and the seeing.** The sequence is strict: 外天下 → 外物 → 外生 → **朝彻** → 见独 → 无古今 → 不死不生 → [撄宁](/glossary/yingning/). The dawn-clearing is *not* the summit; it is the condition for 见独, the seeing of the alone — the ladder still climbs after it. The site keeps the ordering because it corrects the most common misreading of sudden-illumination language: the clearing is earned, and the seeing comes after it.
- **It is the subtraction-tradition's one positive image.** The [DDJ48 program](/translations/daodejing-48/) speaks only in removals (损之又损); 朝彻 is what the removals look like from inside when they finish — not emptiness but *morning*. The term is the bridge between the [zuowang](/concepts/zuowang/) family's negative discipline and the [quiet-room light](/translations/virtual-room-passage/) the tradition later writes about (虚室生白).

## Usage notes

The term stays in the Zhuangzi's vocabulary in this site's records — the site does not extend it to later schools' sudden-enlightenment usage without source support — and it is kept in pinyin with the gloss "dawn-clearing," per the [translation policy](/blog/translation-policy/).
