**Zuochi** (坐驰, zuòchí) — "sitting while galloping": the Zhuangzi's **named failure state** of meditation. The body is seated; the mind runs. The term appears once, at the climax of the heart-fasting dialogue (ch. 4), as the negative of everything the passage has built: after "the empty room gives birth to brightness" and "auspicious things stop in the stopping," the text names the opposite — *if it does not keep stopping* (夫且不止), that is zuochi, the seated gallop.

**Usage note:** zuochi is the perfect negative twin of [zuowang](/glossary/zuowang/) (sitting in forgetting): same seat, opposite direction — one drops the mind's traffic, the other is nothing but the traffic, wearing the posture of stillness. The tradition's word for the gap this site documents in the [drowsiness-vs-stillness](/patterns/drowsiness-vs-stillness/) and [wandering-mind](/problems/wandering-mind-in-sitting/) pages: the *form* of sitting is not the *fact* of it. This site renders it "sitting while galloping," keeping the horse-image (驰) visible, because the classical term diagnoses precisely what modern practitioners report: the body still, the mind at full run.
