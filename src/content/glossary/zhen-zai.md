## The term

真宰 (zhēnzǎi) — 真, true; 宰, the steward — the one who carves, the one in charge (the same word as the butcher-priest at a sacrifice). The term appears in [ch. 2 of the Zhuangzi](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/) in its defining sentence:

若有真宰，而特不得其朕 — "**As if** there were a true driver — and yet its traces cannot be found."

The chapter then walks the body's hundred bones, nine orifices, six organs and asks which one is the master: 吾谁与为亲 — which do I love best? Are they all servants? Do they take turns ruling? And closes: 其有真君存焉 — "perhaps a true lord exists there" — before adding, in the same breath, that *finding* its nature or not changes nothing about its truth (如求得其情与不得，无益损乎其真).

## Why the term matters

- **The as-if is the whole doctrine.** The site's [source policy](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/) preserves what the commentaries make explicit: Guo Xiang's note on the passage — 起索真宰之朕迹，而亦终不得，则明物皆自然，无使物然也 — "search for the traces of the true driver and find none in the end; this shows that things are all *ziran*, there is no thing making them so." And Cheng Xuanying's subcommentary records the received equation (真君即前之真宰也) with the comment that the passage's questions are *posed in order to show there is no true lord* (假设疑问，以明无有真君也). The term is the book's way of **raising the creator-question in order to decline it**: the driver is not affirmed, not denied — it is *unfound*, and its unfindability is the argument for [self-so](/concepts/ziran/).
- **The family it completes.** The site's negated-cosmology set now stands: [大块](/glossary/da-kuai/) (the great clod — no face, no plan, things born clod-like of themselves), [天机](/glossary/tian-ji/) (the heaven-mechanism — runs itself, no driver known, 莫知其宰), and 真宰 (the driver-term itself — put forward as-if and left unfound). Three words, one refusal, and the site cross-links them as the book's answer to the question [whether the tradition has a creator](/experiences/questions/does-daoism-believe-in-a-creator/).
- **The steward-word matters.** 宰 is not "maker" but *steward* — the one who runs a household, carves at a sacrifice. The book declines even this lighter office: no steward, no carver; the hundred bones run their own household. The [hundun story](/stories/hundun/) (the shapeless one bored to death by gratitude) is the term's narrative form: the tradition's only figure who *could* be the driver is killed the moment well-meaning others try to give him the driver's face.

## Usage notes

The term always carries its 若 (as-if); the two commentary readings (Guo Xiang, Cheng Xuanying) are cited together as the tradition's agreed reading; the term is never translated as "God" — "true driver" with the as-if, per the site's [translation policy](/blog/translation-policy/).
