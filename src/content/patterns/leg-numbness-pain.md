**Recurring reports in the practitioner archive:** leg numbness and needle-like tingling typically begin within ten to thirty minutes of cross-legged sitting for beginners; pain is the most-cited reason sessions end early. Community advice splits visibly: one camp counsels endurance ('numbness is normal, endure it'), the other counsels adjustment. The split is real and documented in the sources themselves.

**The tradition's actual position is more careful than its loudest modern voices.** The training manuals do teach endurance of discomfort as a passage — but the same tradition encodes the counter-principle: know the 'fire and timing' (火候); do not let determination become injury; there are documented cases of irreversible damage from forced endurance. Practical traditional counsel includes adjusting posture gradually (scattered → single → full lotus), protecting joints from wind and cold, and slow, gentle release after sitting — massage the knees and ankles, wait out the numbness before standing.

**Mechanical reading.** Crossed-leg postures compress nerves and vessels; numbness is expected; numbness that persists after release, or pain at the joints themselves rather than in the musculature, is the traditional and modern warning line alike.

**Separation of evidence.** Community reports (CONTEMPORARY COMMUNITY OBSERVATION); traditional counsel (TRADITIONAL / LINEAGE INTERPRETATION); the mechanical account is common knowledge in health literature, not a research finding on Daoist sitting. `evidenceBasis` is `partially-researched` in the adjacent-domain sense only.
