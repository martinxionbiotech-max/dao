## The five-step check

**1. Find the chapter, not just the quote.** A floating phrase ("stillness overcomes agitation") is unverifiable; a located phrase is (Daodejing ch. 45). Search engines index full classical texts; search for the phrase plus a likely chapter title, and require the result to *show the surrounding text*, not just the sentence.

**2. Demand a second independent source.** One website's text is a rumor; two unrelated full-text sources agreeing is evidence. The site's standard: double-source verification for every direct quotation — and where they disagree, the disagreement is reported, not hidden.

**3. Check the variant.** Classical texts exist in editions (the Daodejing's received text vs. the Guodian and Mawangdui manuscripts; the Zhuangzi's inner vs. outer chapters). A quote is only as good as the note saying *which* text it is. When in doubt, prefer the received text for the reading most traditions actually used, and say so.

**4. Separate the text from its explanation.** "清静为天下正" is the text; "the quiet governs the world" is one rendering; "stillness makes you powerful" is an interpretation on top of the rendering. Verify the first; treat the second as translation (checkable); treat the third as commentary (attributable, not provable).

**5. Mark what cannot be checked.** Oral transmissions, practice experiences, and lineage claims have no textual verification at all. The correct result is not "false" but "not checkable" — and this site's pages say exactly that rather than converting it into a citation.

## Where to look

- **Wikisource** (zh.wikisource.org) — full received texts of the Daodejing, Zhuangzi, Baopuzi, and most scriptures this site quotes; occasionally rate-limits (403) — wait, then retry once.
- **Shidian Guji** (识典古籍) — scanned editions of classics, including the Baopuzi's Sibu Congkan edition.
- **Scholarly translations** — Legge, Watson, and Kohn are cited by name on this site; their translations are themselves checkable texts.
- **Avoid**: quote-aggregator pages without surrounding text, single-source blogs, and any page that will not show you the chapter.

## The one rule that governs this tool

A citation that cannot be located by chapter, confirmed by a second source, and separated from its interpretation is not a citation — it is an ornament. This site's pages carry their ornaments labeled; this tool exists so readers never have to take that labeling on faith.
