## The order, and why

**1. Daodejing (道德经)** — 81 short chapters, an hour of reading. Read it once *for exposure*, not for mastery: the text is deliberately paradoxical and compiled over centuries. What you take away: the subtraction logic (ch. 48), wuwei (chs. 37, 63), the water-softness images. Use any full translation; read the [text page](/texts/daodejing/) first for the caveats about versions.

**2. Zhuangzi chs. 2, 4, 6 (庄子)** — the three practice-relevant chapters: ch. 2 for the relativity-of-claims frame, ch. 4 for xinzhai, ch. 6 for zuowang. Skip the rest of the 33 chapters on first pass (they are a multi-author anthology; later reading is richer after the core). The [Zhuangzi page](/texts/zhuangzi/) lists what is traditionally attributed to whom.

**3. Qingjing Jing (清静经)** — under 400 characters; read the whole text in ten minutes. It compresses the emptying program into chantable form. See the [text page](/texts/qingjing-jing/) and [translation](/translations/qingjing-jing-opening/).

**4. Neiguan Jing (内观经)** — the "still the spirit, settle the mind" method text. Slightly longer and more technical (deity-and-qi anatomy in places); skim the anatomical sections, study the method sections. See the [text page](/texts/neiguan-jing/).

**5. Zuowang Lun (坐忘论)** — Sima Chengzhen's seven stages: the first systematic meditation treatise. Read fully; it is short, and it repays reading because it treats meditation as embedded in a way of life, not a technique. See the [text page](/texts/zuowang-lun/).

## Reading cautions (this site's own rules)

- **Read with the person/book distinction:** "Laozi said" and "Zhuangzi said" in these texts are often attributed speech — authority claims, not transcripts. The [Laozi](/people/laozi/) and [Zhuang Zhou](/people/zhuang-zhou/) pages document this.
- **Read with the tradition/science distinction:** the texts' claims about qi, spirit, and attainment are traditional physiology and soteriology; nothing here is a scientific claim, and the [comparison page](/comparisons/zuowang-vs-mindfulness/) documents what the research literature does and does not cover.
- **Do not skip the Daodejing's pessimism about words.** The first line warns you about the whole enterprise of reading: the Dao that can be told is not the constant Dao. The texts are aids to sitting, not substitutes for it.
