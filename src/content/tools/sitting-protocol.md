**What this is:** a session structure compiled from classical sources whose content this site has verified and cited elsewhere. It is an **editorial aid** — a modern arrangement of old instructions, not a lineage teaching, and not medical advice. Every step carries its source; where the sources are silent, the protocol is silent too.

## Before sitting

1. **Simplify the conditions** (Zuowang Lun stages 2–3: 断缘, 简事). The treatise's own order: reduce entanglements and trim commitments *before* expecting the sitting to deepen. Practically: keep the session in a part of the day not crowded by obligations.
2. **Set no goal for the session** (Daodejing ch. 48's subtraction logic; Qingjing Jing: dispel desire and the mind stills itself). The tradition's repeated counsel is removal, not achievement.

## The session

3. **Sit and settle the body** — Zhuangzi ch. 6's phrase is to *drop* the limbs and trunk: a release of holding, not a posture manual. The sources give no detailed posture instructions; sit in whatever way you can remain still and comfortable. (This is the classical lacuna; modern aids fill it.)
4. **Unify intention and descend the listening ladder** (Zhuangzi ch. 4): from attending to sounds, to attending to thoughts-as-namings, to resting in a receptive stillness that does not chase either.
5. **Do not force.** If agitation arises, end the session (Taiping Jing: leave the room; do not make the practice a fight). A short, quiet session kept today beats a long, forced one abandoned tomorrow.

## After sitting

6. **Carry stillness into affairs** (Qingjing Jing: "constant responding, constant stillness"; wuwei as non-forcing in activity). The tradition treats the sitting as practice for the rest of life, not as a separate compartment.
7. **Keep the practice regular and modest** (Zhuzi Yulei juan 116: the half-day formula was a *remedy* for one scattered student, not a schedule; but regularity as a principle runs through the jingzuo records).

## What this protocol deliberately omits

- Breathing counts, timings, and internal-energy procedures: not present in the cited classical core; later inner-alchemy material adds them, and that material has its own lineage context this protocol does not import.
- Attainment milestones: the *Zuowang Lun*'s later stages presuppose the earlier work and a framework of Daoist commitment; a secular protocol quotes the safety-and-setup stages only.

**Disclaimer:** this is not medical advice. For persistent distress, depression, or trauma-related symptoms, consult a qualified professional; no traditional protocol substitutes for care.
