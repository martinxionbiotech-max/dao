## The event

The book now called the *Zhuangzi* — the source of [zuowang](/concepts/zuowang/), [xinzhai](/concepts/xinzhai/), the [butterfly dream](/stories/butterfly-dream/), and the craftsmen of the [Dazheng chapter](/blog/three-craftsmen-dazheng/) — was not written by one person at one time. It accreted over roughly two centuries:

- **Mid-4th c. BCE:** Zhuang Zhou (see [person page](/people/zhuang-zhou/)) lives, writes, and dies; the seven **Inner Chapters** (内篇) — including the Qiwulun with its [opening vignette](/translations/qiwulun-wu-sang-wo/) and the Dazongshi with the [zuowang passage](/translations/zuowang-passage/) — are the traditional core attributed to him. (Modern scholarship mostly accepts the Inner Chapters as the earliest stratum, authored or largely authored by Zhuang Zhou.)
- **4th–2nd c. BCE:** the **Outer Chapters** (外篇, 15) and **Miscellaneous Chapters** (杂篇, 11) accumulate — works of followers, related schools, and later hands, including the craft parables of ch. 19 (Dasheng) and the sage-interviews of ch. 11 (Zaiyou).
- **Western Han:** the imperial bibliography (*Han shu, Yiwen zhi*) records the book at **52 chapters** — more than survive.
- **3rd c. CE:** Guo Xiang (郭象, d. 312) edits the book into the received **33 chapters** with his commentary, and this recension becomes the text the entire later tradition reads. (Some modern scholars argue Guo Xiang's recension dropped material; the 52-chapter Han text is lost.)

## Why it matters

- **Stratification is the key to citing it.** When this site says "the Zhuangzi," it means the received 33-chapter Guo Xiang text; when it says "the Inner Chapters," it means the stratum most plausibly close to Zhuang Zhou; when it cites ch. 19's craftsmen, it is citing the outer stratum. Every story and translation page states its chapter and stratum (see the [stories framework](/stories/wooden-rooster/) and [translation notes](/translations/qiwulun-wu-sang-wo/)).
- **The contemplative core is in the earliest stratum.** 心斋 (ch. 4), 坐忘 (ch. 6), 吾丧我 (ch. 2) — the emptiness vocabulary this site centers — is Inner Chapter vocabulary. The later chapters elaborate, dramatize, and popularize it.
- **The compilation itself is a tradition.** Two centuries of followers writing *in the master's voice* is not forgery in the classical sense; it is how a school perpetuates a teaching. The person-page (Zhuang Zhou) and the text-page ([zhuangzi](/texts/zhuangzi/)) carry this distinction through: the person is historical; the book is a school's anthology.

**Uncertainty preserved:** the Inner Chapters' authorship is a scholarly consensus, not a fact; the Guo Xiang recension's fidelity to the Han text is unverifiable (the 52-chapter text is lost). Both limits are stated wherever the text is cited.
