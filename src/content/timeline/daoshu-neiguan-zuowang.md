## The event

In the Southern Song (12th century), the scholar **Zeng Zao** (曾慥) compiled the *Daoshu* ("Pivot of the Dao" — the title itself taken from the Zhuangzi's "the pivot of the Dao," 道枢): forty-two juan, 108 chapters, gathering Daoist practice literature from the Han through the Northern Song — philosophy, alchemy, breathing, fetal-breathing, and meditation techniques, school by school.

Among its preserved dialogues is the sentence this site relies on most heavily for the identity of the two central practices: asked what *inner contemplation* (内观) is, the answer recorded is **"inner contemplation is sitting in oblivion"** (内观即坐忘).

## Why it matters

- **The tradition's own equation.** Modern scholarship separates "Daoist meditation" into distinct lineages; the Daoshu shows the Song tradition itself treating 内观 and 坐忘 as one practice under two names — the strongest single traditional warrant for this site's treatment of them as a unified field.
- **A compilation, not a system.** Zeng Zao gathered, he did not reconcile: the Daoshu preserves schools side by side, including methods (visualization, alchemy) quite different from the stillness tradition. It is evidence of *continuity and diversity*, not of a single orthodoxy.
- **The Zhuangzi as terminological sourcebook.** Even the book's title borrows from the Qiwulun — a reminder that by the Song, the Inner Chapters had become the common lexicon of all Daoist practice.

**Uncertainty preserved:** the "inner contemplation is sitting in oblivion" sentence is a dialogue attributed to the Zhong-Lü lineage, compiled at least two centuries after the alleged speakers; it records the Song tradition's view, not a Tang transcript. That is precisely how this site cites it.
