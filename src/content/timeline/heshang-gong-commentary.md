## The event, in layers

The anonymous Laozi commentary that became the received reading tradition:

- **The text:** the *Laozi Heshang Gong zhangju* — chapter-and-verse, the book's **81 chapters named** (体道, 养身 …), each glossed through two registers: governing the state (治国) and **governing the body** (治身) — cherishing qi, nourishing the spirit, guarding the five viscera's spirits, removing desire. This two-register reading is what carried the Daodejing from philosophy into the [practice corpus](/practices/shouyi/).
- **The name:** [Heshang Gong](/people/heshang-gong/) — "the Elder by the River" — is not a personal name but a description, and the tradition's delivery story (Ge Hong's *Shenxian Zhuan*: the ascension a hundred feet in the air, the two scrolls handed to Emperor Wen, the terrace built to watch for him) is the legend-layer. Scholarship conventionally dates the commentary's composition to the **Eastern Han** (some argue the Han–Wei transition), under a pseudonym — the same pattern of an honored pseudonym carrying an institutionalized reading that the site records for the [Liezi](/texts/liezi/) and [Huangting Jing](/texts/huangting-jing/).
- **The rise:** the recension became the standard received text; the **Tang dynasty** used it in the examinations; the **Dunhuang** Laozi manuscripts are mostly of this recension. This site's received-text variants — [ch. 25's 王亦大](/translations/daodejing-25/) and [ch. 5's 多言/多闻](/translations/daodejing-05/) — are documented from the Heshang Gong line, and [ch. 1](/translations/daodejing-01/)'s punctuation debates were fought through this recension's transmission.

## Why it matters

- **The book we translate is this book.** When the site says "the received text," it means, in large part, the Heshang Gong recension — 81 chapters, chapter-titled, practice-read. The [translation policy](/blog/translation-policy/)'s humility about variants is humility about this transmission: a text whose earliest full commentary is anonymous and legendary.
- **The body-reading is the bridge.** 治身 — governing the body — is the bridge between the [Dao chapters](/translations/daodejing-37/) and the practice tradition ([shouyi](/practices/shouyi/), [tiaoxi](/practices/tiaoxi/)): the commentary is where the Laozi stopped being only a philosophy and became a manual the meditation tradition could inherit. The site's timeline of the [practice's textual rise](/timeline/qingjing-neiguan-jing/) passes through this point.
