**Event:** In the recorded sayings of Zhu Xi (1130–1200), *Zhuzi Yulei* juan 116, the master tells a departing student Guo Deyuan — whose mind was scattered and unstable — that he should use "half a day for quiet sitting, half a day for reading books." The context makes it a remedial prescription for a specific condition (散乱), not a universal schedule. The same tradition records Zhu Xi and his teachers (the Cheng brothers) holding quiet sitting as a daily practice of moral self-cultivation, with warnings against sinking into emptiness or neglect of study.

**Evidence class:** recorded sayings compiled by disciples (13th c. compilation of 12th-c. teachings) — attributed teaching, close to the source but not verbatim guarantee.

**Why it matters here:** this is the most famous jingzuo passage in Chinese history and also the most misquoted — the half-day formula is conditional medicine, not a general rule. See the [jingzuo](/concepts/jingzuo/) page, where the context is quoted in full.
