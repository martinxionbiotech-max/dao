## The event

In the 310s–320s CE, Ge Hong — retired from office, in seclusion in the mountains of his native Jurong and later at Luofu in Guangdong — compiles the [Baopuzi](/texts/baopuzi/): **inner chapters** (20 juan) on alchemy, breathing, and visualization, **outer chapters** (52 juan as received; his own preface counts 50) of essays on government and society, ending with a partial autobiography.

## Why it matters for the sitting tradition

- **Scattered instruction becomes catalog.** Before this book, the practices of holding (shouyi) and of breathing (taixi) survive in sayings — the Daodejing's "embracing the One," the Zhuangzi's "breathing from the heels." After it, they are *enumerated*: the Dizhen chapter ranks guarding-the-One among thousands of visualization methods; the Shizhi chapter fixes taixi's definition. The tradition's curriculum phase begins here.
- **The book is honest about its own gaps.** Ge Hong repeatedly marks what he has practiced against what he has only heard, and notes that essentials remain oral and secret — the earliest documentation of the *limits of documentation* in this tradition, a limit this site inherits and states.
- **It anchors the dating of the practices.** Because the Baopuzi is securely dated, every practice it describes has a *terminus ante quem*: shouyi, taixi, and the visualization library were established, named, and ranked by c. 320 CE. This site's practice pages date themselves against this book.

## The context

Ge Hong wrote at the moment the Daoist tradition was consolidating — the Shangqing revelations were a generation away; the Huangting texts were circulating; the Han classics (Taiping Jing) were being organized into the heritage he collected. The Baopuzi is the hinge between the early aphoristic tradition and the systematized religion to come.

**Uncertainty preserved:** the precise years of composition are approximate (the autobiography gives the circumstances, not the dates); the juan counts vary across bibliographies, documented on the [text page](/texts/baopuzi/).
