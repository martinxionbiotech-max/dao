**Event:** In 1993, archaeologists excavated a Chu-state tomb at Guodian (Jingmen, Hubei) containing bamboo slips with the earliest known *Daodejing* fragments, sealed no later than ~300 BCE — possibly earlier. The slips preserve passages of the text in an order and wording that differ from the received version, proving the text was still forming at that date.

**Evidence class:** archaeological (primary); the seal-date is an inference from tomb typology (scholarly).

**Why it matters here:** the Guodian slips ground the *Daodejing*'s antiquity in material evidence independent of the Laozi legend, and complicate the tradition's single-author account. See the [Daodejing page](/texts/daodejing/).
