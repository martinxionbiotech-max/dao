## The event

Sometime between the 3rd and 4th centuries CE — the received text is associated with the Shangqing (Highest Clarity) revelation tradition — the **Huangting neijing jing** systematized the map of inner practice that would govern Chinese contemplative and alchemical work for the next seventeen centuries: the **three dantian** ("cinnabar fields"), with the upper field in the head, the middle at the heart, and the lower below the navel.

The line that anchors the system — "essence-qi is subtle within the three fields" (三田之中精气微) — became the standard reference for the body's inner geography. The later refinements of internal alchemy (essence → qi → spirit) all take place on this map, and the lower dantian became the default reference point of modern sitting practice (see [dantian](/glossary/dantian/)).

## Why it matters

- **The body becomes legible.** Before the Huangting system, inner practice references were scattered; after it, "where do you place attention" has a fixed, named answer.
- **It is a visualization-and-storage system, not a stillness system.** The Huangting's own methods are cunsi — visualization of inner palaces and resident spirits — which the later stillness traditions (zuowang) partially displace, while keeping the map. The map outlives the method.
- **The tension this site documents begins here:** the dantian map is *traditional doctrine, not anatomy* — a fact stated plainly on the glossary page, and the source of the interpretive fork every modern warmth-sensation report encounters.

**Uncertainty preserved:** the text's dating and attribution are traditional and contested (associated with Lady Wei Huacun; dated variously from the late Han to the 4th century); the received date range here is the scholarly consensus, not a fact of the text itself.
