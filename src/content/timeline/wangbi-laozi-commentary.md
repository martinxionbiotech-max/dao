## The event, in layers

The philosophical reading's founding document:

- **The text:** Wang Bi's *Laozi zhu*, composed in the 240s CE — 81 chapters, **no chapter titles** (unlike the [Heshang Gong recension](/timeline/heshang-gong-commentary/)'s named chapters), slightly longer than Heshang Gong's text mostly in function-words. With Han and Three-Kingdoms commentaries lost, this became **the earliest surviving Laozi commentary** and the book's standard philosophical recension.
- **The reading:** not philology but a system — mystery and emptiness (玄虚), 本末 (root and branches), and the principle the scholarship names: **名教本于自然**, "names and teachings are rooted in the natural." The site documents this reading the same way it documents the Heshang Gong reading: as a historical interpretation, not adopted as doctrine.
- **The fate:** the author died at 24 in 249, the year of the Gaoping Tomb coup; the commentary survived through a Ming reprint (Zhang Zhixiang's edition, collated against the Jingdian shiwen and Yongle dadian, per the Siku abstract), and became — with Heshang Gong's — one of the **four great transmitted-text families** (王弼本, 河上公本, 严遵《老子指归》, 傅奕本). The modern standard critical edition is Lou Yulie's (中华书局).

## Why it matters

- **The received text is two recensions.** Every time the site writes "the received text," it means the Wang Bi and Heshang Gong lines together: the [ch. 1 punctuation debate](/translations/daodejing-01/) is Wang Bi's cutting against the received one, and the [ch. 37 paradox](/translations/daodejing-37/) reaches us in his form. The two timelines — this one and the [Heshang Gong commentary](/timeline/heshang-gong-commentary/) — are the site's paired anchor for the [Daodejing text page](/texts/daodejing/).
- **The contrast is the lesson.** Heshang Gong read the book as body-cultivation; Wang Bi read it as discourse. The tradition has always had both readings, and the site's [translation policy](/blog/translation-policy/) inherits the tension: translate the practice terms carefully *and* the philosophy carefully, because the same book has been carrying both for eighteen centuries.
