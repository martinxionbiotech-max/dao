**Event:** Per the *Song shi* biography of Yang Shi (1053–1135), the future Neo-Confucian master and his fellow student You Zuo came to see Cheng Yi (1033–1107) when he was sitting quietly; Cheng Yi sat in stillness, so the two stood waiting beside him. When Cheng Yi rose, the snow outside was already a foot deep. The episode — "standing in the snow at Cheng's gate" (程門立雪) — became the canonical image of reverence for quiet sitting and teacher-deference in the Neo-Confucian tradition.

**Evidence class:** later dynastic biography (Song shi, 14th c.) recording a 12th-century tradition; widely accepted as the founding anecdote of Neo-Confucian jingzuo culture, though its details are traditional.

**Why it matters here:** it marks jingzuo's entry into the Confucian mainstream — quiet sitting as a practice of moral cultivation and respect, parallel to but distinct from the Daoist zuowang lineage. See the [jingzuo](/concepts/jingzuo/) page.
