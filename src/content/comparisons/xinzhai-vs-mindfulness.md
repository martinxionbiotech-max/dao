**Direct answer:** Xinzhai and mindfulness share a family resemblance — both are ways of *not interfering* — but they are not the same operation. Mindfulness trains a capacity: sustained, non-judgmental present-moment attention, usually for measurable psychological benefit (see the [Goyal 2014 meta-analysis](/research/mindfulness-meta-analysis-2014/)). Xinzhai is a *condition*: the mind emptied of its own interference so that "the Dao gathers in emptiness." Mindfulness is something you do; xinzhai is somewhere you get to.

## The structured comparison

| Dimension | Xinzhai (fasting of the mind) | Mindfulness (modern clinical) |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Zhuangzi ch. 4 | Kabat-Zinn's MBSR (1979 onward); research literature |
| Operation | Unify intention; stop listening with ears; let the Dao gather | Train present-moment attention, non-judgmentally |
| The self | Emptied — "the self that fasts" | Observed — thoughts noticed, not fused with |
| Goal | The Dao gathers; receptivity | Stress reduction; well-being; meta-awareness |
| Evidence base | A philosophical parable (not a manual) | A large clinical research literature |

## The overlap that matters

Both traditions agree on the core move: *stop imposing*. The Zhuangzi's fasting is the removal of the mind's own agenda; mindfulness's non-judgment is the same refusal in a psychological key. This is why the question of [combining them](/experiences/questions/combine-zuowang-mindfulness/) is a live one on this site — and why the answer there is careful: overlap in one move is not identity of projects.

## The asymmetry of evidence

This comparison is only honest if it states the asymmetry: mindfulness arrives with a modern research literature; xinzhai arrives with a fourth-century-BCE parable and a commentarial tradition. Neither fact makes either practice better — but treating the parable as if it had clinical data, or the clinical data as if it validated the parable, are both category errors.
