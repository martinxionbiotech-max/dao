**Direct answer:** Daoist and Buddhist meditation are different projects that borrowed each other's furniture for two thousand years. The Daoist aims — union with the Dao, guarding the One, quieting the body for longevity — sit beside the Buddhist aim of seeing through the construction of self. The shared vocabulary (sitting, breath, emptiness, stages) is real and also the main source of confusion: the same word often names different operations. This comparison takes each side from its own sources.

## The structured comparison

| Dimension | Daoist (zuowang / shouyi / xinzhai) | Buddhist (Chan / Tiantai sitting) |
|---|---|---|
| Central aim | Merge with the Dao; keep jing-qi-shen united; longevity | See through self-clinging; awakening |
| Foundational texts | Zhuangzi 4 & 6; Daodejing; Baopuzi; Zuowang lun | Tiantai manuals (Xiao Zhiguan); Chan lineage records |
| The self | A boundary to be *forgotten* (dropped limbs, dismissed knowing) | A construction to be *seen through* (no permanent owner) |
| The body | Valued; breath and qi work serve it | Posture as platform; body less central |
| Breath | Hierarchy of faults down to taixi (embryonic breath) | Counting and following breath as attention anchor |
| Stage talk | Borrowed from Buddhism after the Tang | Indigenous, elaborate |

## The borrowing that confuses everything

- **Tang Daoism borrowed Buddhist stage vocabulary** wholesale — the Zuowang Lun's ladder of stages reads Buddhist, but its summit is merger with the Dao, not awakening from self. Same scaffolding, different building.
- **The breath manual most cited on this site** — the Xiao Zhiguan — is itself Buddhist (Tiantai), which is why its four-fold breath analysis is kept on the [tiaoxi](/glossary/tiaoxi/) entry with its affiliation stated, and why it is evidence for *breath technique* rather than for Daoist claims.

## What this comparison cannot do

It cannot rank the traditions, and it does not. Each aims at what it aims at; "which is better" is a question the sources themselves refuse.
