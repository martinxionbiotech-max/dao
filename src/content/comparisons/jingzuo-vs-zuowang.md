## The short version

Both are seated stillness; the posture is nearly identical; the **goals are different in a way that matters**:

- **Jingzuo (quiet sitting, Neo-Confucian)** — stillness in service of *moral clarity*: collect the scattered mind, examine oneself, recover the calm from which right action proceeds. Its exemplar is the [student standing in the snow](/stories/standing-in-snow/) while Cheng Yi sits — reverence, not transcendence.
- **Zuowang (sitting in oblivion, Daoist)** — stillness in service of *emptying*: forget the body, drop knowledge, merge with the Dao. Its exemplar is [Huzi's four depths](/stories/huzi-jixian/) ending in "never left my source."

| canonical method | upright seated posture; daily short sessions; combined with reading | the seven stages: respect-faith → cutting ties → gathering mind → simplifying → true insight → deep stability → attaining the Dao |
| danger it warns against | quietism — stillness for its own sake | grasping — chasing states; forcing |

## Where they meet

The two traditions share more than posture:

1. **Stillness as restoration.** Zhu Xi explicitly framed quiet sitting as *restoring* a tired, scattered mind — and the Daoist manuals likewise describe the settled state as the body-mind's natural condition, not an exotic achievement.
2. **Both warn against force.** The Taiping Jing's "if restless, come out — do not force" and Sima Chengzhen's ladder of preliminary life-simplification both treat efforting as error. Neither tradition is ascetic in the self-punishing sense.
3. **Both feed the modern Chinese practice scene.** Contemporary "打坐" blends them freely — community practitioners rarely distinguish the lineages, which is exactly why this page exists.

## What does NOT transfer between them

- The Daoist goal of merging with the Dao (traditional/religious claim) is absent from Zhu Xi's account; he was explicit that sitting is a *means* for study and conduct, not an end.
- The Neo-Confucian social ethic (reverence serving family and state) is absent from the Daoist ideal; the Zhuangzi's figures famously refuse office.
- A practitioner adopting jingzuo is not thereby practicing zuowang — and vice versa. Shared posture, different project.

## Bottom line

If you want **calm, clarity, and moral steadiness in ordinary life** — the tradition that names this is jingzuo. If you want **emptiness, forgetting, and release of the self-construct** — the tradition that names this is zuowang. The honest answer to "which should I practice" is: know which goal you are actually pursuing, and use the word that names it.
