**Direct answer:** Jingzuo and zazen are the same posture with different engines. Jingzuo — quiet sitting — is the Neo-Confucian practice of sitting in stillness to collect the scattered mind for study and moral work. Zazen is the Chan/Zen practice of sitting to see one's nature directly. The Confucians borrowed the posture from the Buddhists they argued with, and they knew it: the tradition is explicit that jingzuo is *not* Buddhist sitting — same seat, different doctrine.

## The structured comparison

| Dimension | Jingzuo (quiet sitting) | Zazen (seated Zen) |
|---|---|---|
| Tradition | Neo-Confucian (Song and after) | Chan (China) / Zen (Japan) |
| Source | Zhuzi Yulei; Er Cheng waishu | Chan lineage records; later Zen manuals |
| Stated aim | Collect the mind for study and conduct | See one's nature; awakening |
| The mind's condition | Collected (收敛) — gathered from scattering | Settled into non-grasping — then inquiry (koan) or just sitting |
| Attitude to doctrine | Serves the Confucian program; not an end in itself | Is itself the heart of the tradition |

## The borrowed posture, the kept difference

- **Zhu Xi's program:** sitting quiets the mind so that reading and moral work can proceed — jingzuo is instrumental, a servant of study and conduct.
- **The Buddhist original:** sitting is the practice itself; the awakening it points at is not instrumental to anything.
- **The Confucians were explicit** that their sitting was not the Buddhists' — the borrowing was acknowledged and redefined, which is itself the cleanest evidence that the two were understood as different.

## What this comparison cannot settle

Whether quiet sitting "belongs to" one tradition or the other is a historical question, not a doctrinal one — the practices cross-pollinated (see [how religious is sitting?](/experiences/questions/is-sitting-religious/)). This page compares the two as their own sources define them, and ranks neither.
