**Direct answer:** Shouyi and concentration overlap in one technique — holding attention on one thing — and part company in what is being held. Concentration (as modern psychology uses the word) holds *attention on an object*. Shouyi holds *the One* — not an object but a state: the soul and body kept undivided, jing-qi-shen kept united. Concentration is a capacity you deploy; shouyi is a condition you inhabit. The Daodejing's question — "can you keep the soul embracing the One without separation?" — is a question about *unity*, not about focus.

## The structured comparison

| Dimension | Shouyi (guarding the One) | Concentration (focused attention) |
|---|---|---|
| What is held | The One — an undivided state of the person | An object of attention (breath, image, task) |
| Source | Daodejing 10; Taiping Jing; Baopuzi | Modern attention research; meditation traditions generally |
| Goal | Unity of jing-qi-shen; longevity; the Dao | Sustained, stable attention |
| Failure mode | Separation (离) — the One dividing | Distraction — attention wandering |
| Evidence base | Primary texts + lineage practice | A large modern research literature |

## Where they genuinely overlap

Both require that attention stop jumping. The practitioner who cannot hold attention at all cannot guard anything. This is why the site's [shouyi practice page](/practices/shouyi/) begins with the ordinary work of settling attention — and why the overlap is real rather than accidental.

## Where they part

Concentration can be deployed at anything — a chess problem, a spreadsheet, an enemy. Shouyi cannot: its object *is* the undivided state. A person concentrating hard is not necessarily guarding the One; a person guarding the One is, almost by definition, concentrated — but on what concentration cannot name.

The sibling comparison, [zuowang vs shouyi](/comparisons/zuowang-vs-shouyi/), treats how guarding relates to forgetting.
