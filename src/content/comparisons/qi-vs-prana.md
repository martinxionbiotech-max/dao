**Direct answer:** Qi and prana are both cultures' terms for a vital substance that is more than breath and less than soul — and the analogy between them is old, useful, and dangerous. Useful because it marks the same *kind* of concept across systems; dangerous because it invites reading one system's term as the other's. Qi is not prana translated; prana is not qi in Sanskrit. Each does different work inside its own cosmology, and the site's [qi glossary entry](/glossary/qi/) flags exactly this — the English word "energy" is a modern overlay on both, not a bridge between them.

## The structured comparison

| Dimension | Qi 气 | Prana प्राण |
|---|---|---|
| Tradition | Chinese: medicine, cosmology, Daoist practice | Indian: yoga, Ayurveda, Upanishadic thought |
| Core sense | Vapor, breath, the vital stuff that condenses and disperses | Breath, life-force, the animating current |
| Cosmological role | The blending that harmonizes yin and yang (DDJ 42) | The life-wind distributed through the subtle body |
| Practice system | Tiaoxi, taixi, moving qi (Baopuzi) | Pranayama — breath regulation and expansion |
| Modern overlay | "Energy" (a translation habit, not the classical term) | "Energy" (the same habit) |

## Why the analogy is useful

Both terms name something *between* breath and spirit, both sit at the center of a cultivation practice (breath work), and both were translated into English as "energy" for the same reasons. For a newcomer, saying "prana is the Indian cousin of qi" gets the *shape* of the concept across — the shape being: a vital substance you cultivate by breath work and stillness.

## Why the identity fails

- **Different bodies of practice:** tiaoxi and pranayama share family features but are not the same technique set; taixi (embryonic breathing) has no simple pranayama equivalent, and the reverse is true of several pranayama forms.
- **Different cosmologies:** qi blends yin and yang; prana distributes through a subtle-body anatomy the Chinese systems do not share.

## What this comparison cannot do

It cannot tell a practitioner which system is "right" — and the site's answer to [whether believing in qi is necessary](/experiences/questions/qi-belief-necessary/) applies here in both directions: the practice systems are what they are regardless of which vital-substance vocabulary a practitioner prefers.
