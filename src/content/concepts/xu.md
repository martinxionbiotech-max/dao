## The term

虚 (xū) — empty, insubstantial, hollowed out. In the Zhuangzi it is the *working state* of contemplative practice: the mind's condition when it has stopped imposing and begun receiving. It is the single most important technical term behind both xinzhai and zuowang.

## The original text

The term is defined in the xinzhai passage of *Renjian shi* (ch. 4), which closes its instruction in three stages:

> "Unify your intention. Listen not with the ears but with the mind; listen not with the mind but with qi. Listening stops at the ears; the mind stops at matching. **Qi is what is empty and awaits things. The Dao gathers in emptiness. Emptiness is the fasting of the mind.**"

And moments later, the passage's most quoted image:

> "Look into the closed room: **the empty room gives rise to white light**, and good fortune rests there. If the mind does not rest there, this is called *sitting while racing* (坐驰)."

## What the text claims

1. **Emptiness is receptivity.** Qi is defined as "what is empty and awaits things" (虚而待物) — the ability to receive without pre-forming. The Dao "gathers" (集) where this receptivity is present: emptiness is not the absence of content but the condition that lets content arrive unforced.
2. **Emptiness is the definition of xinzhai.** The passage makes the terms identical: 虚者，心斋也 — fasting of the mind *is* emptiness. The discipline of fasting (removing) and the state of emptiness (having removed) are one movement.
3. **The white light.** 虚室生白 — "the empty room gives rise to white light" — is the passage's image of the settled state: clarity arising *of itself* in the emptied mind. Traditional readings treat it as an image of the mind's natural luminosity in stillness; some modern commentators read it more concretely as a description of visual phenomena arising in deep quiet. Both readings are recorded here; the text supports the image without settling its interpretation.
4. **The named failure: 坐驰.** "Sitting while racing" — the body still, the mind galloping. This is the Zhuangzi's own term for *counterfeit sitting*: posture without emptiness. It matters because it is the earliest warning that sitting alone is not the practice — emptiness is.

## Historical development

- **Zhuangzi (ch. 4):** emptiness as the state where the Dao gathers; the origin of the term's contemplative use.
- **Later Daoist manuals (Qingjing Jing, Zuowang lun):** the term becomes the standard description of the goal-state — "empty and clear," "still and empty" — with the same claim: clarity arises from removal, not acquisition.
- **Neo-Confucian usage:** Zhu Xi and his school borrow the vocabulary cautiously — quiet sitting "empties" the mind of scattered thought, though the Confucian goal (moral clarity) differs from the Daoist one (release of self).

## Modern research boundary

No clinical research addresses "emptiness" as a contemplative state; the modern literature's nearest constructs (non-attachment, decentering, reduced self-referential processing) are correlates of *other* meditation forms and are not evidence about xu as described here. The claim that emptiness produces clarity is traditional; it is not tested, and this page does not present it as tested.

## Editorial synthesis

Xu is the tradition's answer to the question "what should the mind *do* in sitting?" — and the answer is: stop doing, become empty, and the Dao gathers. The image-pair the tradition left us — the empty room's white light, and the seated racer — remains the most economical diagnosis tool in Chinese contemplative literature: light in the room means the mind rests; racing while seated means it doesn't.
