**Direct answer:** Dao (道, dào) is the central term of the *Daodejing* and the *Zhuangzi*: at once the way things go, the way to go, and the unnameable source and pattern of everything. Both texts refuse a definition, then spend their length describing what it does and where it is found.

## The term

道 means "way, path, road" — and, as a verb, "to speak." The *Daodejing* opens on the pun: "The Dao that can be spoken is not the constant Dao" (道可道，非常道, ch. 1). The naming problem is the doctrine: the Dao is not a fixed referent that a word can catch. Translation practice is covered in the [glossary entry](/glossary/dao/).

## What the texts claim

1. **The naming problem (ch. 1).** "Nameless, it is the beginning of heaven and earth; named, it is the mother of the ten thousand things" (無名天地之始，有名萬物之母). See [Daodejing 1](/translations/daodejing-01/).
2. **Forced naming (ch. 25).** "There is a thing formed in confusion, born before heaven and earth… I do not know its name; I style it *Dao*; forced to give it a name, I call it *Great*" (吾不知其名，字之曰道，強為之名曰大). Every name in the passage is admitted to be a stopgap. See [Daodejing 25](/translations/daodejing-25/).
3. **Namelessness and smallness (chs. 32, 37).** "The Dao is ever nameless" (道常無名); "the Dao is ever without action, and nothing is left undone" (道常無為而無不為). See [Daodejing 32](/translations/daodejing-32/) and [Daodejing 37](/translations/daodejing-37/).
4. **The Zhuangzi's definition (ch. 6).** The closest thing to a definition in either text: "The Dao has feeling and trustworthiness, yet is without action and without form. It can be transmitted but not received; it can be obtained but not seen. It is its own root and its own trunk — before heaven and earth existed, it was there, of itself, from antiquity" (夫道，有情有信，無為無形，可傳而不可受，可得而不可見，自本自根，未有天地，自古以固存). See the [Zhuangzi](/texts/zhuangzi/) text page.
5. **Where it is found (ch. 22).** Dongguo Zi asks where the Dao is; the answer descends from heaven and earth down to "in the piss and the shit" (在屎溺) — the Dao is everywhere, and most reliably in what a person would not point at. See the [story page](/stories/dongguo-zi-asks/).

## Historical development

- **Pre-Daoist uses:** 道 as road, method, teaching; 天道 (the way of heaven) in Warring States cosmology.
- **The two texts:** the *Daodejing* makes it the cosmic principle; the *Zhuangzi* adds the inward turn — the Dao "gathers in emptiness" (唯道集虛), so the cleared mind is where it becomes accessible. See [xu](/concepts/xu/).
- **Later Daoism:** the Dao is personified — 太上老君 (Taishang Laojun) as its embodiment — a religious development the classical texts do not contain.

## Scholarly interpretation

- Why scholarship keeps the romanization: "Way" captures the path sense, "Nature" narrows it, "God" imports a creator — and no English word carries the "to speak" pun the opening line turns on.
- The first-line paradox (if it cannot be spoken, why speak?) is usually read as methodological rather than mystical: statements about the Dao are always approximations, kept honest by the text's own framing.

## Practice dimension

- **唯道集虚** — "the Dao gathers in emptiness": the practical claim behind all emptying practices. See [xu](/concepts/xu/) and [xinzhai](/concepts/xinzhai/).
- **Zuowang** — "sitting in oblivion" culminates in "merging with the Great Thoroughfare" (同於大通), the practitioner's version of the Dao. See [zuowang](/practices/zuowang/).

## Uncertainty

- Whether ch. 1's Dao is primarily a metaphysical claim or a political-ethical one (the "constant Dao" of the sage-ruler) remains debated; the text supports both.
- How far the *Zhuangzi*'s Dao departs from the *Daodejing*'s is a live question — the ch. 6 definition is either the same doctrine restated or a deliberate reworking.

## Editorial synthesis

Dao is the root node of this site's graph: every other core term (de, wu-you, ziran, fan, pu, xu) is a way of saying what it does or where it gathers. The discipline the site keeps: describe the Dao by its motions and its places, never by a final definition.
