**Direct answer:** *Ziran* (自然) — literally "so of itself," "self-so" — is the Daodejing's name for the way things go when nothing is forcing them. It is not "nature" in the modern English sense of forests and ecosystems; it is *self-arising order*: the condition of a process that runs from its own internal pattern, without external imposition. Where *wuwei* names the human side of the equation (not forcing), *ziran* names the world's side (nothing needing forcing). The contemplative tradition's entire enterprise — emptying, subtracting, forgetting — is one long way of approaching *ziran*.

## The core passages

- **Ch. 25:** "The Dao models itself on *ziran*" (道法自然). The chain runs: humans model on earth, earth on heaven, heaven on the Dao — and the Dao, at the top of the chain, models on *ziran*. Whatever the Dao is, its character is self-so-ness: it has nothing further to imitate, because its way of running *is* the standard.
- **Ch. 17:** "When the work is done and the task complete, the hundred families all say: 'We did it ourselves'" (功成事遂，百姓皆谓：我自然). The political metaphor doubles as a psychological one: the best order is the one nobody notices being imposed. *Ziran* is what remains when the agent's effort disappears from the experience.
- **Ch. 51:** "The Dao gives them life, Virtue rears them... and yet it does not presume to rule them" (生而不有，为而不恃，长而不宰) — the mother-passage of self-arising order: growth happens, and no supervisor is required.
- **Ch. 64:** "Assist the ten thousand things' self-so-ness, and do not dare to act on them" (辅万物之自然而不敢为) — the exact formula for the practitioner: *assist* (辅), never *force* (为). The whole of quiet sitting, in a single verb.

## What ziran is not

- **Not "nature" as a place.** The modern English "nature" (the outdoors, the biosphere) is a descendant meaning, not the primary one. *Ziran* is adverbial — *how* things go, not *where*.
- **Not spontaneity-as-impulse.** The popular rendering "spontaneous" imports the idea of doing whatever arises. The classical word is closer to *order that maintains itself*: rain falls, seasons turn, an unpushed child learns to walk — processes with internal grammar, not random discharge.
- **Not the enemy of practice.** Because *ziran* is the *result* of deep training, not its opposite — the Dazheng chapter's craftsmen (see [three craftsmen](/blog/three-craftsmen-dazheng/)) practice for months *in order to* arrive at not-forcing. Wuwei and ziran are endpoints on the far side of effort, not refusals of it.

## Ziran and sitting

The sitting tradition inherited ziran as its north star. The Daodejing ch. 16 — [translated here](/translations/daodejing-16/) — gives the method: reach emptiness at the limit, keep stillness firmly; then watch the ten thousand things arise and *return*; returning to the root is stillness; stillness is returning to one's destiny (归根曰静，静曰复命). The practice described is not effort *at* emptiness but the *observation of return* — of things going back by themselves. Zuowang's "forgetting," xinzhai's "fasting," the subtraction program of ch. 48: all are ways of getting the forcer out of the way so that what remains goes *so of itself*.

**Uncertainty preserved:** "道法自然" admits of a strong reading — that the Dao *imitates nothing but itself* — and the ancient commentators (notably Wang Bi: "自然者，无称之言，穷极之辞也" — "ziran is a word beyond naming, a phrase at the limit of speech") read it as the end of the modeling chain rather than a further entity. The entry point is uncontested: *ziran* is the standard to which even the Dao answers.
