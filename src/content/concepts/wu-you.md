**Direct answer:** Wu (无, "non-being, not-having") and You (有, "being, having") are the *Daodejing*'s founding pair — the terms through which the book explains generation, usefulness, and the return. The four passages that anchor the pair form a deliberate progression: naming (ch. 1), interdependence (ch. 2), function (ch. 11), origin (ch. 40).

## The pair in the Daodejing

1. **The beginning (ch. 1).** "Nameless, it is the beginning of heaven and earth; named, it is the mother of the ten thousand things" (無名天地之始，有名萬物之母). Wu and You appear as the two faces of the opening: the unmarked origin and the marked world. See [Daodejing 1](/translations/daodejing-01/).
2. **Interdependence (ch. 2).** "Being and non-being give rise to each other" (有無相生) — first of the chapter's six pairs (difficult/easy, long/short, etc.). Neither term stands alone. See [Daodejing 2](/translations/daodejing-02/).
3. **The empty hub (ch. 11).** "Thirty spokes meet in one hub; where it is not, there is the use of the cart" (三十輻共一轂，當其無，有車之用). Clay vessel, room — the chapter's three examples all locate usefulness in what is *not there*. "Therefore being is the benefit; non-being is the use" (故有之以為利，無之以為用). See [Daodejing 11](/translations/daodejing-11/).
4. **The origin (ch. 40).** "The ten thousand things under heaven are born of being; being is born of non-being" (天下萬物生於有，有生於無). The strongest ontological statement in the book. See [Daodejing 40](/translations/daodejing-40/).

## The Zhuangzi's ladder

*Zhi Bei You* (ch. 22) takes the pair one step further. Guangyao asks Wu-you ("Not-having") whether it is "having" or "not-having"; getting no answer, he finally asks: "Is it even possible to reach this?" The reply: "I can do not-having, but I have not yet reached *not*-not-having" (予能有無矣，而未能無無也). The point: even 无 can be reified — held as a possession — and the tradition keeps one move ahead of that trap. See the [story page](/stories/light-brightness-asks-nothingness/).

## Historical development

- **Wang Bi** (226–249) built his whole reading on the pair: 以無為本 — "taking non-being as the root." For him 无 is not empty nothingness but the unmarked, unconstrained root from which determinate things arise. See [Wang Bi](/people/wang-bi/).
- **Guo Xiang** (d. 312) reversed the priority: 无 cannot give rise to anything; things are self-so, and "non-being" is only a name for the absence of a maker. Both positions are recorded in the tradition without a settled winner.
- **Later usage:** the pair drifts toward Buddhist-informed readings of emptiness; classical Daoist usage keeps the functional sense (the hub's hole) primary.

## Scholarly interpretation

- Is 无 metaphysical nothingness or *functional absence* (the usefulness of the empty space)? The ch. 11 examples anchor the functional reading; ch. 40 pushes toward the metaphysical one. Modern scholarship mostly holds the two senses together rather than choosing.
- The pair should not be mapped onto Western being/nothingness debates without care; the Chinese terms are built from "having/not-having," which carries the practical register the Greek terms lack.

## Practice dimension

- **Xu (虚)** is the mind's version of the hub's hole: the emptied state through which the Dao gathers. See [xu](/concepts/xu/).
- **The 无无 discipline:** not clinging even to non-attachment — the contemplative reading of Guangyao's ladder.

## Uncertainty

- Whether ch. 40's 有生于無 is a cosmological claim, a logical claim, or a rhetorical reversal (consistent with [fan](/concepts/fan/)) is genuinely undecided; the passage is three words in the received text.
- The exact role of 无 in Wang Bi's system remains a contested point in Wei-Jin scholarship.

## Editorial synthesis

Wu and You are the pair that generates everything else in the *Daodejing*'s structure: naming, function, and origin all run through them. The site keeps the pair as one node — they are defined against each other and cannot be treated separately.
