**Direct answer:** *Jing* (静, "stillness") is the quality of mind that every practice on this site is named after or aimed at — the still water the [Daodejing](/texts/daodejing/) calls "returning to the root" (归根曰静) and the [Zhuangzi](/texts/zhuangzi/) pictures as the mirror: "responding without storing" (应而不藏). It is the *ground state*: not the absence of movement but the absence of disturbance. Movement continues — the body breathes, the world arrives — but nothing sticks, nothing ripples onward.

## The term's classical career

- **The Daodejing's definition.** Ch. 16 gives the canonical derivation: things proliferate, then *return*; "returning to the root is stillness" — and stillness is called "returning to life" (复命). Stillness is therefore not a pause from life but the direction of life itself, its homecoming. Ch. 45 states the practical law: "stillness overcomes agitation; the clear and still governs the world" — the quiet is *effective*, the more powerful term of the pair.
- **The Zhuangzi's image.** The perfected person's mind "works like a mirror — neither welcoming nor escorting, responding without storing, able to prevail over things without being harmed" (ch. 7). The mirror is the tradition's definitive picture of stillness: full responsiveness, zero retention. The same chapter's closing parable — the [Hundun story](/stories/hundun/) — warns what happens when stillness is improved upon.
- **The religious amplification.** The [Qingjing Jing](/texts/qingjing-jing/) makes the same stillness the center of a devotional program: "the human spirit is fond of clarity, but the mind disturbs it; the mind is fond of stillness, but desires draw it" (人能常清静，天地悉皆归).
- **The Confucian adoption.** When Zhu Xi and his school took up [jingzuo](/practices/jingzuo/), they took over the *word* jing — reverent stillness — and re-set its reference from cosmic return to moral composure. The Daoist root and the Confucian graft are historically distinct and both are documented on this site.

## What stillness is not

- **Not inactivity.** The mirror responds to everything that arrives; the still ruler in DDJ ch. 45 *governs*. Stillness in the Daoist sense is readiness, not torpor.
- **Not drowsiness.** The classical fault of [昏沉](/experiences/patterns/drowsiness-vs-stillness/) is precisely stillness' counterfeit: settled, but dark. The tradition keeps a separate vocabulary for the real thing (light, clear, aware).
- **Not forcing.** Stillness cannot be produced by tightening; the texts' verbs are *returning*, *letting*, *forgetting* — the [zuowang](/concepts/zuowang/) family — never *clenching*.

## Modern boundary, stated plainly

Stillness as a *felt state* (calm, low arousal, non-reactivity) is well described in modern research on contemplative practice — the Goyal 2014 [review](/research/mindfulness-meta-analysis-2014/) documents stress reduction across meditation programs. But the Daoist *account* of stillness — as return to the root, as the world's governing pole — is doctrine, not measurement, and this page keeps the two apart: the experience belongs to the practitioner, the mechanism to the tradition, the measurements to the research.

**Uncertainty preserved:** jing's definition is stable across the primary texts; its causal claims (stillness governs; return is life) are traditional, not empirical.
