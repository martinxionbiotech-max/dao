**Direct answer:** Yin-Yang (阴阳, yīnyáng) is the paired-aspects pattern of Chinese cosmology: change runs through alternating, interdependent poles — shade and sun, contraction and expansion, still and moving. It is *not originally a Daoist term*: it belongs to the shared Warring States cosmological vocabulary, systematized by the Yin-Yang school (阴阳家) and the medical tradition before it settled into Daoist usage.

## The term

阴 is the shady side of a hill, 阳 the sunny side — a single landscape seen from two aspects. The terms generalize to any complementary pair: dark/light, cold/warm, rest/motion, inner/outer. The pair is relational and reversible, not a moral binary: nothing is simply yin or yang, and each aspect turns into the other (the pattern formalized in [fan](/concepts/fan/)).

## Earliest appearances

1. **The Yijing commentary.** *Xici zhuan* shang ch. 5: "One yin, one yang — this is called the Dao" (一陰一陽之謂道). The line uses the word 道 itself, which is why it became the bridge text between the two traditions.
2. **The one Daodejing mention (ch. 42).** "The ten thousand things carry yin on their backs and embrace yang in their arms; the surging qi makes them harmonious" (萬物負陰而抱陽，沖氣以為和). See [Daodejing 42](/translations/daodejing-42/). This is the *only* occurrence of the pair in the received text — a fact that matters: the *Daodejing*'s own structure of paired opposites (ch. 2's 有无相生 sequence) works the same grammar without using the word.
3. **The medical systematization.** *Huangdi Neijing, Suwen* ch. 5 opens: "Yin-yang is the way of heaven and earth, the ordering thread of the ten thousand things, the father and mother of change, the root and beginning of life and death" (陰陽者，天地之道也，萬物之綱紀，變化之父母，生殺之本始). The medical canon turns the pair into a diagnostic and physiological framework.

## Historical development

- **Warring States:** the Yin-Yang school (Zou Yan and successors) built cosmology around the five phases (五行) and the yin-yang cycle; it was one school among many, later absorbed rather than preserved as an independent lineage.
- **Han synthesis:** yin-yang and five-phase thinking became the shared grammar of medicine, calendrics, divination, and ritual — including Daoist texts of the period.
- **Daoist internal practices:** later traditions (inner alchemy and its descendants) map yin-yang onto body and mind — e.g., the still/moving polarity that [jing](/concepts/jing/) and [xu](/concepts/xu/) describe. These mappings are later systematizations, not claims made by the classical texts.

## Scholarly interpretation

- The pair is usually read as *pattern*, not substance: yin and yang are not "two things" but two aspects of one movement.
- Modern scholarship stresses that projecting the full five-phase/yin-yang apparatus back onto the *Daodejing* is anachronistic; the book predates the systematization and uses the pair only once.

## Practice dimension

- **Balance as method:** the pair gives contemplative practice its vocabulary of alternation — stillness and movement, emptiness and fullness. See [jing](/concepts/jing/) and [xu](/concepts/xu/).
- **Health boundary:** this site's [problems](/problems/) pages keep the medical claims of the Neijing tradition clearly labeled as historical-text claims, not modern medical advice.

## Uncertainty

- Whether DDJ ch. 42's yin-yang line is original or a later insertion has been argued by some textual scholars; the received text includes it, and this site records the line as received.
- The exact relationship between the Yin-Yang school's cosmology and early Daoism is reconstructed from fragments; the school's own texts are largely lost.

## Editorial synthesis

Yin-yang is registered here as *shared equipment*: it explains the paired logic running through the Daoist texts without being their invention. Its one appearance in the *Daodejing* and its one appearance in the *Xici* are both quoted in full, so the boundary with the Yin-Yang school stays visible.
