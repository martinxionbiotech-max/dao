**Direct answer:** Stillness feels uncomfortable at first because it *works* — it removes the noise that covered the mind's own motion, and a mind in motion finds quiet hard, the way a ringing ear finds silence loud. The Zhuangzi names this as the starting condition itself: the [zuochi](/glossary/zuochi/) passage — "the body sits, the mind gallops" — is not a failure state to avoid but the honest description of where practice begins.

## What the sources say

- **Zhuangzi ch. 4:** in the very passage that defines the fasting of the mind, the text concedes the counter-case: the body can sit while the mind races. The tradition's answer is not to force the mind still but to *empty* the place thinking happens — [xinzhai](/concepts/xinzhai/), "the Dao gathers in emptiness."
- **The manuals' counsel, as transmitted:** discomfort in early stillness is information about how much motion was already running — and it settles with repetition, not with force.

## The boundary that matters

**Discomfort that is actually distress — emotional material that overwhelms, or pain that persists off the cushion — is a medical matter**, not a meditation matter. The tradition's texts are not a substitute for care, and neither is this page.

**Not medical advice:** persistent pain or overwhelming emotional states warrant professional evaluation regardless of practice context.
