**Direct answer:** A body that feels large, expanded, or without clear edges is a documented report from the earliest sources onward. The tradition reads it as the felt body loosening its claim on the person — a change in *perception* of the body, never in the body itself. The modern frame says something compatible: body perception is constructed by the brain from interoceptive signals, and it shifts when attention turns inward (see the [interoception roadmap](/research/khalsa-2018/)). What matters is whether the change stays on the cushion.

## What the sources say

- **Zhuangzi ch. 2:** "Heaven and earth were born together with me, and the myriad things and I are one" (天地与我并生，而万物与我为一). The sentence is cosmology, but the tradition has also long read it as the *felt* version of that cosmology: the boundary between this body and the rest of things is not a wall but a habit of perception.
- **The manuals' counsel, as transmitted:** sensations of the body changing shape or size in stillness are passed through, not collected — the same non-grasping rule applied to [light](/problems/seeing-light-in-sitting/) applies here. Neither chase the sensation nor fear it.

## What practitioners report

The community archives record the ordinary versions: a hand that feels far away, a body that feels vast, a head that seems to float. These are the same events as the [warmth and tingling](/experiences/patterns/warmth-and-qi-sensations/) reports in a different register — felt changes in the body map, not changes in the body.

## What research can and cannot say

Modern research describes the machinery — interoception, the brain's moment-by-moment map of the body's interior — and shows that this map shifts with attention and context. No study has measured body-image changes in Daoist sitting specifically, and this page does not cite any as if it had.

## The boundary that matters

**A persistent or distressing change in body image — one that continues off the cushion, interferes with daily life, or frightens you — is a medical matter**, not a meditation matter. The tradition's texts say nothing about neurological or psychiatric conditions, and neither does this page.

**Not medical advice:** new-onset or worsening changes in body perception warrant professional evaluation regardless of practice context.

The [qi](/concepts/qi/) page treats the framework these reports traditionally live inside.
