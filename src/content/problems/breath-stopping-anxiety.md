## The two versions, kept apart

**The spontaneous one.** Breathing becomes shallow and fine on its own; the pause, if it comes, comes *with* relaxation and reverses the moment attention moves. This is the phenomenon reported in [EXP-008](/experiences/notes/exp-008-breath-stopping/), and it has a precise classical name: the tradition's [taixi](/practices/taixi/) ideal — "one who has attained taixi can breathe without nose or mouth, as in the womb" — stated as an *arrival*, the result of long quieting, not an exercise. The manuals describe the whole trajectory: from the audible, labored "wind" breath through the "qi" breath to the "rest" breath (息) that is "as if present, as if absent."

**The forced one.** Breath held deliberately, raced to a pattern, or repeatedly interrupted with effort. This is the "wind" fault at intensity — and the Baopuzi's dietary note shows the tradition's own concern with breath that has become "strong and hard to close" (令人气强难闭): it warns against the conditions that produce *resisted* breath, and by extension against producing it directly. Forcing the breath has no classical warrant; the [taixi page](/practices/taixi/) states it flatly: taixi is a criterion of achieved quiet, not a technique to seize.

## How to tell them apart in one check

- **Effort:** spontaneous = effortless; forced = effortful. (The decisive test.)
- **Reversibility:** spontaneous reverses when you attend to it; forced is held *by* attending.
- **After-effects:** spontaneous leaves calm; forced leaves strain, lightheadedness, or a rebound gasp.
- **The community pattern:** threads on breath phenomena in sitting consistently sort into these two — the reports that describe "the breath almost disappeared" read as comfortable surprises; the ones describing "I tried to stop my breathing" read as warnings, and the replies say so.

## The boundary

Forced breath work can produce real symptoms (dizziness, chest discomfort, anxiety); no classical or modern source on this site recommends it. Spontaneous breath-stopping as a meditation phenomenon is reported but unstudied specifically in Daoist populations — and it is *indistinguishable at first glance* from conditions that need medical attention. The rule this site applies: comfortable and reversible is meditative; anything distressing, or any breathing problem outside sitting, is a doctor's question first.
