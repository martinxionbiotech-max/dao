**Direct answer:** Head pressure in stillness is reported in this site's archive, and the two available readings — the archive teacher's and the modern one — agree on the practical rule: **ease off**. The teacher read the reported head swelling after breath practice as a blood-pressure rise and told the practitioner to ease off the breath work and add exercise. The modern reading is the tension pattern: muscles of the scalp and neck held contracted for long periods produce exactly the band-like pressure described. What neither reading supports is pushing through.

## What the sources say

- **The archive reading.** In the [restless-body episode](/experiences/notes/exp-012-restless-body/), a practitioner practicing breath work reports head swelling and pressure afterward. The teacher's reading is registered plainly: a blood-pressure rise, treated by easing off the breath practice and increasing exercise to strengthen heart and lungs. The site files it as his framework — the episode's alternative layer notes the convergence with the over-breathing reading without adopting his mechanism wholesale.
- **The standing rule.** The [abdominal-breathing episode](/experiences/notes/exp-028-abdominal-breathing/) carries the same teacher's monitoring rule verbatim in paraphrase: **stop at the first dizziness or head-pressure**. Registered as his constraint, not the site's protocol.
- **What the tradition does not say.** No text in this site's corpus describes head pressure as a stage of practice, a channel opening, or a sign of progress. The manuals discuss breath quality and inner observation; the "pressure in the head" vocabulary belongs to the modern Q&A record, not to the received texts. A teaching that calls head pressure a milestone is newer than the sources this site holds.

## The physiological reading

- **The tension pattern.** Tension-type headache is sustained contraction of scalp and neck muscles producing bilateral pressure, "like a band." A long sit with a slightly held head and jaw recruits exactly these muscles; the pressure described by practitioners maps onto this pattern more closely than onto anything in the received texts.
- **The over-breathing neighbor.** As on the [dizziness page](/problems/dizziness-during-sitting/), forced breath work changes blood CO₂ and vessel tone; the archive teacher's blood-pressure reading is the folk form of the same region of physiology.
- **Attention itself.** Focusing on the head makes head sensations salient — the site's own [description discipline](/experiences/questions/does-practice-need-faith/): describe the practice, not the state.

## The boundary that matters

**New or worsening headache, headache that arrives with visual changes, vomiting, or neurological symptoms, or any head pain that continues off the cushion is a medical matter**, not a meditation matter. The archive rule — ease off, add ordinary exercise — is the practice answer; the medical question belongs to a doctor.

The sibling safety treatment is the [can sitting go wrong](/experiences/questions/can-sitting-go-wrong/) page; the breath-safety boundary is on the [breath-retention](/problems/breath-retention-daoist/) page.

**Not medical advice:** persistent head pressure warrants professional evaluation regardless of practice context.
