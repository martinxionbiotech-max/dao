**Direct answer:** The felt body receding in stillness is one of the oldest named experiences in the tradition — it is literally part of the definition of [zuowang](/concepts/zuowang/). "Dropping the limbs and body" (堕肢体) heads the list of what sitting-and-forgetting does. The tradition means the body's *claim* on attention relaxing, not the body being harmed and not the person losing touch with reality.

## What forgetting the body means

- **Zhuangzi ch. 6:** Yan Hui reports his progress in three stages — forgetting benevolence and righteousness, forgetting rites and music, and finally sitting-and-forgetting: "I drop away the limbs and body, expel perception, leave form behind, dismiss knowing, and merge with the Great Thoroughfare." Body-awareness dissolving is *stage three*, the arrival, not the injury.
- **The distinction that matters:** the text says the body's *felt solidity* drops away. It does not say the practitioner loses the ability to stand up, to notice pain, or to respond to the room. The tradition's [xu](/concepts/xu/) (emptiness) is receptive, not absent.

## What practitioners report

The archives record the ordinary versions — hands going distant, the body's outline blurring, periods of sitting in which the body is simply not on the agenda. The [yingning](/glossary/yingning/) entry treats the classical term for the settled version of this.

## The boundary that matters

**If the experience frightens you, persists off the cushion, or feels like watching yourself from outside — depersonalization — it is a medical matter**, not a meditation matter. The Zhuangzi is not a neurology textbook, and neither is this page.

**Not medical advice:** new-onset or distressing dissociative experience warrants professional evaluation regardless of practice context.
