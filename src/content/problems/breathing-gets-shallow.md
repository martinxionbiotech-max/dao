**Direct answer:** A quieting body breathes less loudly. The tradition grades breath from the faults — wind (audible), panting (not smooth), coarse (not fine) — down to the ideal, breath "as if present, as if absent," the threshold of [taixi](/practices/taixi/). So a naturally thinning breath is the direction of travel, not a malfunction. Modern research describes the physiology from the outside: slow breathing under roughly ten breaths per minute is associated with parasympathetic predominance (see the [Zaccaro review](/research/zaccaro-2018/)). What neither source licenses is *forcing* the breath shallow — and what neither source can hear is air hunger.

## What the sources say

- **The Xiao Zhiguan hierarchy:** the manual's four breaths run from wrong to right — wind, panting, coarse, and the fine breath of stillness. The [tiaoxi](/glossary/tiaoxi/) entry holds the full analysis.
- **The slow-breathing research:** voluntary slow breathing shifts autonomic balance toward the parasympathetic side, with effects on attention and emotion. The review's techniques are modern and voluntary; the Daoist ideal is traditional and *arrived-at* — the two are cousins, not the same practice.

## The boundary that matters

**The sensation of needing air is a signal to breathe.** Fine breath never includes air hunger; the moment breath feels insufficient, effort, or fright, it has left the tradition's category entirely.

**Breath discomfort, chest pain, dizziness, or any cardiac or respiratory symptom is a medical matter**, not a meditation matter. See the [breath-stopping anxiety](/problems/breath-stopping-anxiety/) page for the full safety treatment, and note that the [breath-stopping archive](/experiences/notes/exp-008-breath-stopping/) records how fear can attach to exactly this transition.

**Not medical advice:** persistent breath discomfort warrants professional evaluation regardless of practice context.
