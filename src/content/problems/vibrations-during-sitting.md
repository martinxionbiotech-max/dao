**Direct answer:** Vibrations in stillness are a named category in the tradition — felt movement of qi, the same frame that holds the [warmth and tingling](/experiences/patterns/warmth-and-qi-sensations/) reports. The counsel is uniform: observe without grasping, neither chase nor fight. The ordinary physiology is also real: a body settling into stillness releases held postural tension, and fine tremor while muscles unclench is common in any deep relaxation, meditation or not.

## The traditional frame

- **Qi-movement:** the manuals describe stillness as the moment when qi becomes *felt* — movement, warmth, tingling, vibration — and consistently teach that these are signs on the road, not destinations. The [qi](/concepts/qi/) page holds the framework.
- **Non-grasping:** the same rule as every other phenomenon in sitting: what arises in stillness is passed through. Chasing the vibration is the named error; fighting it is the same error in reverse.

## The physiological reading

No dedicated research exists on vibration sensations in Daoist sitting, and this page does not pretend otherwise. The ordinary explanations — postural muscles settling, residual motor activity during relaxation — are physiological common sense, not findings of the Daoist tradition.

## The boundary that matters

**Persistent tremor, one-sided shaking, or any movement symptom that continues off the cushion or interferes with daily life is a medical matter**, not a meditation matter. The tradition's texts say nothing about movement disorders, and neither does this page.

**Not medical advice:** new-onset or worsening tremor warrants professional evaluation regardless of practice context.
