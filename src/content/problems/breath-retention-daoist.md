**Direct answer:** No. The tradition's breath work runs in the opposite direction from retention. The ideal — [taixi](/practices/taixi/), "embryonic breathing" — is breath so fine, soft, and continuous that it seems to have stopped, and the texts frame it as an *arrival*: something long practice produces, not a technique you seize. Forced breath retention is not taught in the classical manuals this site covers, and it is the documented source of the fear treated on the [breath-stopping](/problems/breath-stopping-anxiety/) page.

## What the sources say

- **The Baopuzi (4th c.)** defines taixi and immediately attaches the anti-force conditions: the practitioner of moving qi must eat little and avoid raw or rich foods, because these "make the qi strong and hard to close" (令人气强难闭). The entire apparatus is built to make breath *soft enough to close on its own* — the opposite of gripping it shut.
- **The breath manuals' hierarchy:** the Xiao Zhiguan's four-part breath analysis runs from the faults (wind, panting, coarse) down to the ideal — breath "as if present, as if absent." The tradition's arrow points at fineness and continuity, never at holding.

## The distinction that matters

| | Taixi (the ideal) | Retention (the error) |
|---|---|---|
| How it arrives | After long practice, spontaneously | By force, on command |
| Quality of breath | Fine, soft, continuous | Gripped, blocked, effortful |
| The texts' stance | The completion of the Dao | Not taught; force is the named risk |

## The safety boundary

Holding the breath to force the experience of stoppage is not a shortcut to taixi — it is the shortcut to the fear, dizziness, and chest tension that the [breath-stopping anxiety](/problems/breath-stopping-anxiety/) page describes. **Anyone practicing breath work with a cardiac, respiratory, or anxiety condition should discuss it with a professional first. This page does not teach breath techniques.**

**Not medical advice:** chest pain, fainting, or severe shortness of breath warrant immediate professional evaluation.
