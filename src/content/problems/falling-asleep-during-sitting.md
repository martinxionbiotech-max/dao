**Direct answer:** Falling asleep in stillness is the loud version of the question the [drowsiness](/problems/drowsiness-in-sitting/) page treats, and the tradition's answer is the same: stillness and sleepiness are neighbors, not the same room, and the fix is posture, timing, and alertness — never guilt. An under-rested body will fall asleep in stillness exactly as it would anywhere else quiet; that is physiology, not failure.

## What the sources say

- **The manuals' counsel, as transmitted:** sit upright, at a time you are not already exhausted, and treat sinking as a signal to straighten the spine and wake the attention. The tradition does not moralize sleep; it engineers around it.
- **The archive case:** the site's archives record the ordinary arc — early sessions ending in sleep, stability arriving as the body adjusts to stillness. One practitioner's account (kept per archive rules) describes exactly this: sleep at first, stillness later, nothing forced in between.

## The practical factors

- **Sleep debt is the leading cause.** Sitting reveals how tired you are; it does not create the tiredness.
- **Posture matters.** Lying back invites sleep; upright sitting with a straight spine does not prevent drowsiness but does not cooperate with it.
- **Timing matters.** Sitting immediately after a meal or at the bottom of the afternoon invites the heaviness.

## The boundary that matters

**Persistent daytime sleepiness — the kind that shows up in daily life, not just on the cushion — is a sleep-medicine matter**, not a meditation matter. The tradition's texts say nothing about sleep disorders, and neither does this page.

**Not medical advice:** chronic daytime sleepiness warrants professional evaluation regardless of practice context.
