## The man and the arc

- **The youngest, who lasted longest.** The records agree on the frame: ordained at nineteen, Wang Chongyang's disciple the next year (1167), the youngest of the northern Seven — and dead in 1227 at seventy-nine, the longest-lived of them all. The site's [Wang Chongyang page](/people/wang-chongyang/) holds the founding act (the living-dead tomb, the burning of the hut, the eastward walk to "capture the horse"); Qiu Chuji is the sequence's last act: the one who *scaled* what the founder started. The organizer arc the site has been building — [Zhang Daoling](/people/zhang-daoling/) founds the group, [Kou Qianzhi](/people/kou-qianzhi/) courts the state, Wang Chongyang founds the last great movement — closes here: under Qiu Chuji the Quanzhen order became, in the standard account, the dominant Daoist institution of its century.
- **The thirteen years in the northwest.** The Daoist database record gives the specific and the site keeps it: six years in a cave at Panxi, seven at Longmen mountain, the straw cloak that became his nickname — and the mountain that became his lineage's name (Longmen, the Dragon Gate lineage, still the largest Quanzhen branch in the modern reckoning). The site's [practice pages](/glossary/zuowang/) note the pattern without inflating it: the northern tradition's classic shape — years of solitary sitting before years of institution-building — is the same shape the site's own [archive teacher](/experiences/notes/exp-017-why-he-began/) described in miniature.
- **The western journey — history and story, kept apart.** The journey is historical: in 1220 the seventy-four-year-old left Shandong for Genghis Khan's camp in the west, met him in the mountains (the "snowy mountain audience"), and came back with what the Beijing Evening News article registers as the concrete gains — tax and corvée exemptions for the order's disciples, the institutional basis of Quanzhen's rise. The *story* is the line he is remembered for — 上至帝王，降及民庶，尊卑虽异，性命各同耳 (from emperor to commoner, lives are alike) — and the "one word stops the killing" tradition (the Qianlong couplet: 一言止杀). The site registers the gap, per its [claim discipline](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/): the audience happened; the "stopping of the killing" is the tradition's favorite sentence about it, and the historical literature disputes it (the Beijing Evening News column, registered above; the Western assessment that reads the whole journey more harshly, also registered). Both are kept, neither adjudicated.
- **The lineage and the names.** Yuan Shizu titled him "Changchun Yandao Zhujiao Zhenren" (the True Man Who Spread the Way and Led the Teaching — the *organizer* title, note the verb 主教, leading the teaching), and Yuan Wuzong added "Quande Shenhua Mingying Zhenjun" — the standard two-stage imperial canonization the site's [Zhang Sanfeng page](/people/zhang-sanfeng/) documents as the template of later centuries. The names are registered as court acts, not as theology.
- **Why he belongs here.** Three of the site's standing lines meet in him. First, the organizer arc's completion (founder → reformer → cataloguer → organizer-at-scale). Second, the site's [reception-drift discipline](/people/wang-chongyang/): the tradition remembers the mountain meeting's sentence more than the tax exemption — and the site keeps both, because the exemption is what changed the order's fortunes and the sentence is what changed its story. Third, the site's [sitting lineage](/people/sima-chengzhen/) finds its northern counterpart: where Sima Chengzhen taught the Tang court the [Zuowang](/glossary/zuowang/) ladder, Qiu Chuji rode to the Mongol camp with nothing but a body of practice and a claim about lives — the same claim the site's [ch. 32 translation](/translations/daodejing-32/) holds as the book's own: 万物将自宾, things arrive of themselves.
