## Who he was

**Tan Chuduan (1123–1185)** was the Quanzhen disciple who came to the school on crutches. Born Tan Yu in Muping, a reader of classics and history, a fine calligrapher, generous and filial by reputation — and paralyzed. The sources give two versions of the paralysis's origin: the encyclopedia account has him sleeping drunk in wind and snow and waking with wind-paralysis (风痹); all sources agree on what happened next. He had tried every treatment without effect, and in 1167, hearing that Wang Chongyang had come to Shandong, he went on his crutches to ask for a cure. The Daoist Culture Centre Database records the scene in full: Wang Chongyang *barred the door and would not let him in; Tan held his ground through the night, and suddenly the door opened of itself* — Wang took this as the fated encounter (仙缘), had him sleep in the same bed, and by morning the old illness was gone. The crutches stayed at the door; Tan asked to serve at Wang's side, and did so for life.

**The Luoyang years.** After burying Wang Chongyang at the ancestral shrine, Tan went west with Liu Chuxuan — not to a mountain cave but to Luoyang's streets. The archive's phrase for this period is the most striking in his biography: *he went in and out of the red streets and purple lanes, amid the groves of wine and arrays of flowers, refining the mind against circumstances, until facing circumstances his mind did not stir* (出入红街紫陌，花林酒阵之中，对境炼心，直至对境无心). The site's archive of sitting experiences marks the same structure in the [restlessness](/problems/restlessness-in-sitting/) problem page: the tradition's own word for training attention in hard conditions is to stay in them, not to flee them. Tan's version is the most urban form of that doctrine on record.

**The third patriarch.** After Ma Yu's death, Tan succeeded as the third patriarch of Quanzhen, and died in 1185 in Luoyang — the wiki dates give May 2 at the Chaoyuan Palace. The posthumous titles came later, as they always do: Yuan Shizu's 长真云水蕴德真人 (the wiki records the second character as 荡德 — the site registers both without adjudicating), and Yuan Wuzong's longer 长真凝神玄静蕴德真君. The school he founded, the Nanwu (南无) lineage, lasted into the late Ming, though never at the scale of Qiu Chuji's Longmen.

**The teacher who softened the school.** Tan's recorded thought contains the sentence that changed Quanzhen's course: **忠孝仁慈胜出家** — *loyalty, filial piety, and mercy surpass leaving home.* Ma Yu's teaching had made renunciation the condition of practice; Tan revised it: one can practice at home. The Daoist Culture Centre Database states the consequence plainly — this modification is what let Quanzhen spread to ordinary households. For this site, the sentence matters twice over: it is the historical ancestor of the [do-i-need-a-teacher](/questions/do-i-need-a-teacher/) and [is-sitting-religious](/questions/is-sitting-religious/) pages' answer that the practice does not require a monastery, and it is the classical counterweight to the archive's harder [double-lotus doctrine](/experiences/exp-014-double-lotus-doctrine/) — the tradition itself has always contained the softer door.

## Two anecdotes, kept in their layer

- **The turtles and snakes.** Tan was famous for practicing the two characters 龟 and 蛇 every day until, the tradition says, they seemed ready to fly from the page, and believers collected his calligraphy as treasure. A record about handwriting — but it sits inside the site's broader file on the tradition's [aesthetics of discipline](/stories/cicada-catcher/): one object, daily, for decades.
- **The two teeth.** The *Jinlian zhengzong ji* records that Tan once begged leftover food at a monastery; the Chan master flew into a rage and struck him, knocking out two teeth; Tan swallowed them with the blood, smiled, bowed, and left — and the story says his fame spread through the capital and Luoyang. The site registers this in its traditional layer (the same layer as Wang Chongyang's [living-dead-man's tomb](/people/wang-chongyang/) and the [iron-foot immortal's](/people/wang-chuyi/) cliff-standing) and notes the genre: hagiographic endurance records are the tradition's way of marking the practice's hardest claim — that non-reaction under insult is trainable. The [empty boat](/stories/xuzhou-empty-boat/) story is the same claim in philosophical form.
