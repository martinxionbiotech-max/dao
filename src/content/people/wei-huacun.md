## Who she was, and how the sources treat her

Wei Huacun is the founding woman of the Shangqing ("Highest Clarity") tradition — recorded as a historical person, an aristocrat (daughter of Wei Shu, a Jin minister), a Celestial Master practitioner, and a mother, who late in life retired to religious practice. The tradition gives her dates as 251/252–334 (the sources split the birth year by one, and the page keeps both). After her death she became **the Lady of the Southern Marchmount** (南岳夫人, "Nanyue Furen") and, in Shangqing's revelation record, the matriarch who transmitted the tradition's scriptures — with her spirit continuing to instruct the mediums and scribes of the 364–370 revelations.

For this site she matters on one specific point: **the traditional attribution of the [Huangting Jing](/texts/huangting-jing/)** to her — the tradition that she received and transmitted the "inner scenes" scripture that became the visualization tradition's map-document. That attribution is exactly the kind of claim this site layers carefully: *the person is historical; the scripture's attribution to her is tradition; the scripture itself is dated by modern scholarship centuries later than her lifetime.*

## The three layers, kept separate

- **Historical layer:** an aristocratic Jin woman who practiced and taught, dates transmitted as 251/252–334. Her father's identity (Wei Shu) is recorded; her life is recoverable in outline, not in detail.
- **Tradition layer:** her posthumous titles and the Shangqing revelation lineage — the claim that she became an immortal matriarch instructing later mediums (Yang Xi's revelations, c. 364–370). This is the tradition's own register, reported as such.
- **Text layer:** the *Huangting Jing* attribution. The [text page](/texts/huangting-jing/) documents the scholarly dating (the received *Inner Scenes* scripture is placed centuries later, in the context of the Shangqing corpus itself) — meaning the attribution tells us what the tradition *wanted to claim* about the scripture's authority, not who wrote it.

## Why she belongs in this knowledge base

Two reasons, both structural. First, she is the canonical example of the site's [text-people separation](/blog/three-languages/): a real person, a deified lineage, and a contested text attribution — three registers that collapse into each other in popular accounts, and that this site keeps apart by design. Second, she anchors the **visualization pole** of the tradition: the [cunsi](/practices/cunsi/) practice page and the [jing-qi-shen](/glossary/jing-qi-shen/) body-map both descend through the Shangqing visualization lineage she heads — the *filling* pole that stands opposite the [zuowang](/practices/zuowang/) emptying.

**Uncertainty preserved:** the birth-year split (251/252) is kept; the revelation lineage is tradition, not history; and the Huangting attribution is reported as the tradition's claim, with the text page's scholarly dating as the counterweight.
