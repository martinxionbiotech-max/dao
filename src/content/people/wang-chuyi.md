## The man and the arc

- **The ascetic wing of Quanzhen.** Wang Chuyi is the other pole of the school the site's [Qiu Chuji page](/people/qiu-chuji/) describes: where Qiu Chuji walked the court path — the Western journey, the emperor's favor, lives saved by politics — Wang Chuyi walked the mountain path. The records agree on the frame: he was taken by [Wang Chongyang](/people/wang-chongyang/) in 1168 (the same year as Qiu Chuji), and then spent the standard-record nine years alone in the mountains at the Yunguang Cave in Wendeng, practicing what the records describe as bitter seclusion (苦修深山整九年). The site's [organizer arc](/people/zhang-boduan/) holds the school's founding logic — the master gathered seven disciples, and the disciples split the work: the court wing and the hermit wing. One master, two ways, same year.
- **The Iron-Foot Immortal.** The nickname 铁脚仙 is the record's most durable fact about him, and the site registers it at its honest level: the sources agree on the name and on its connection to his seclusion practice — the popular tradition fills in the detail (standing one-footed at cliff edges through the years), and the site files the detail as the tradition's idiom, the same physical-severity language the [standing-in-snow story](/stories/standing-in-snow/) preserves for the Confucian side: standing still for a long time, as a form of asking. What is historical is the nine years and the name; what is traditional is the standing itself. The site's [sitting practice page](/practices/jingzuo/) holds the boundary: stillness as a form of devotion belongs to the practice tradition, registered, not prescribed.
- **The mother, and the name-cluster.** The records carry two anchors the site keeps because they are rare in the tradition's biography genre: he was fatherless from childhood and devoted to his mother (幼年丧父，对母亲十分孝顺 — the Taiwan database entry), and his name underwent changes the sources cannot agree on (玉阳子 as the style-name in most records; 玉阳 as the courtesy name with 介阳子/全阳子/华阳子 as the style in the Wikipedia cluster; the earlier name 伞阳子 in the Taiwan database, later changed to 玉阳子). The site registers the whole cluster without adjudicating — a name is a record, and disagreement about a name is part of the record. The filial anchor matters to the site's [teacher line](/experiences/questions/do-i-need-a-teacher/): the tradition's own exemplars of severity begin from ordinary duty, not from escape from it.
- **The court's trust during the suppression.** The Taishan shrine account records the sharpest political fact: even during the Jin dynasty's suppression of Quanzhen, Wang Yuyang did not lose the court's trust (即使是在金朝打击全真教的时期，王玉阳仍不失却朝廷的信任) — the hermit retained what the school as a whole was losing, and the site files the irony beside the [Qiu Chuji page](/people/qiu-chuji/): the ascetic's reputation was the school's diplomatic asset, exactly because he asked for nothing. The Yuan-era canonization completed it: 玉阳体玄广度真人 — the title the records give him, the same posthumous machine that made Qiu Chuji a god of the state.
- **The branch, registered.** The seven disciples each founded a branch of the teaching, and the records disagree about his: the Kaiwind list names it 全真派, the standard account names the 嵛山派 (Yu-Mountain branch, from the Kunyu mountain range of his home region) — the site registers both without adjudicating, per its [dual-registration discipline](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/). The disagreement is itself the lesson: the branches were named by their successors, not by their founders, and the founder's own name-cluster was already multiple.
- **Why he belongs here.** He completes the site's Quanzhen portrait: [Wang Chongyang](/people/wang-chongyang/) the founder (the mad-sage idiom, the grave-house years), [Qiu Chuji](/people/qiu-chuji/) the court wing (the Western journey, the refusal of the elixir), and now Wang Chuyi the mountain wing (the nine years, the iron feet, the mother, the trust). The site's [madness line](/people/bai-yuchan/) gets its northern mirror: the southern lineage's craftsman-mystics (barrel-hooper Chen Nan, mad-poet Bai Yuchan) and the northern school's severity-saints are the same tradition speaking two dialects — and this page registers the dialect that made nine years alone in a cave the school's standard of proof.

On the companion left behind on Kunyu Mountain, see [Hao Datong](/people/hao-datong/).
