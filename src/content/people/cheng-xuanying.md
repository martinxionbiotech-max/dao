## The man and the book

- **The second half of the standard Zhuangzi.** Every quotation of the Zhuangzi on this site that carries a "sub-commentary" reading — the [cripple Shu's](/stories/zhili-shu/) 忘形 as the ancestor of forgetting, the [half-shadow's](/stories/wang-liang-wen-jing/) 天机自张, the [true ruler's](/glossary/zhen-zai/) 或曰 — is Cheng Xuanying's voice, filtered through the tradition's editors. The [Daoist catalogue record](/people/guo-xiang/) is explicit: Guo Xiang's commentary and Cheng's sub-commentary were transmitted as one book (the Nanhua zhenjing zhushu, 35 juan, in the Ming canon), and Guo Qingfan's definitive Zhuangzi jishi incorporated the pair whole. The site's [layering rule](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/) rests on the Guo Xiang recension; the *glosses* the site quotes rest, more often than not, on this man.
- **The dates and the title.** 608–669, courtesy name Zishi, from Shanzhou: summoned to court at 23 (Zhenguan 5, 631) and titled Xihua fashi — the "Western Florescence" of his name pointing, the record notes, to the western mountain of his later retreat (Yuntai, modern Lianyungang), and the title itself to the Tang court's Daoist patronage. The [dating rule](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/) applies to what the record does not give: his teachers and his death's circumstances are not in the verified sources, and the page does not supply them.
- **The Chongxuan school, in one phrase.** 重玄, "twofold dark," is built from the [first chapter's](/translations/daodejing-01/) closing words — 玄之又玄, the dark and darker still — read as a *method*: the first dark dissolves the things; the second dissolves the dissolving. The site's [xuan page](/glossary/xuan/) already holds the term as depth rather than secrecy; Cheng Xuanying is the thinker who made that depth a school — the verified record says the school "became the mainstream of early Tang Daoist philosophy" under him, and the Daoist catalogue record adds the precise inheritance: his sub-commentary builds on Guo Xiang's 独化 (each thing's self-transformation) and extends it through the twofold-dark method. The site's [ch. 2 translation](/translations/daodejing-02/) notes the same negation-stacking in the Laozi's own pairs; the Chongxuan school is that stacking, systematized.
- **The Buddhist question, registered whole.** The record's sharpest item is the contemporary Buddhist charge (Chengguan, quoted in the Gong Pengcheng study): Cheng "broadly cites the Buddhist teachings" but "sees small verbal similarities and misses the great difference in meaning." The study adds the frame the site keeps: Cheng was a committed Daoist — the huahu thesis (Laozi went west and taught the barbarians, i.e., the Buddha), the claim that Laozi taught all three traditions — who nevertheless absorbed Buddhist vocabulary because the early Tang court ran *three-teachings debates* (the Wude 7 record) and a polemicist must know the other side. The site's [borrowing boundary](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/) — which keeps Buddhist *terminology* and refuses Buddhist *doctrine* — is, in a precise sense, Cheng Xuanying's own policy, six centuries early: use the words, keep the Way.
- **Why he belongs here.** He closes the site's [commentary arc](/people/wang-bi/): Wang Bi made the Laozi philosophical at 23; Guo Xiang made the Zhuangzi standard; Cheng Xuanying made the pair *Daoist* — institutional, two-thirds of the canon's hermeneutic apparatus, and the last name in the site's own chain of quoted glosses. And he belongs to the site's [practice lineage](/people/tao-hongjing/) as its counterweight: where Tao Hongjing compiled the revelations into a church, Cheng Xuanying compiled the commentaries into a philosophy — the same century, the same three-teachings competition, the two halves of Tang Daoism.
