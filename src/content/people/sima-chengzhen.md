**Direct answer:** Sima Chengzhen (司马承祯, 647–735) was the twelfth patriarch of the Shangqing (Highest Clarity) school of Daoism and the author of the *Zuowang Lun* — the treatise that converted the classical ideal of sitting in oblivion into a graduated seven-stage system. He is the single most important named figure in the history of Daoist seated meditation, and among the best-documented Daoists of the Tang.

## Life (historical record)

- **Origins:** from a prominent family of Wen County, Henei (Henan); great-great-grandson of a Northern Zhou regional inspector.
- **Ordination:** became a Daoist at twenty-one, studying under Pan Shizheng on Mount Song, receiving the Shangqing scriptural and practice transmissions (including the *Jingen shangjing*, the *Sandong milu*, and daoyin, bigu, and drug-taking arts).
- **Tiantai:** lived in seclusion at Tongbo Abbey on Mount Tiantai for decades, where he founded what later tradition calls the Tiantai lineage of Shangqing.
- **The courts:** invited to the capital by Empress Wu Zetian and by Emperor Ruizong to expound the Dao; in 721 (Kaiyuan 9) Emperor Xuanzong summoned him and personally received the *falu* (ritual registers); in 724 Xuanzong had him choose a site on Mount Wangwu, where the Yangtai Abbey was built with the emperor's own calligraphy for its plaque.
- **Death and honors:** died at Mount Wangwu in 735, aged eighty-nine; buried at Songtai; Xuanzong composed the epitaph and conferred the posthumous title Zhenyi xiansheng (贞一先生, "Master of True Unity") and the honorific rank of Yinqing guanglu dafu.
- **Cultural orbit:** counted among the "Ten Friends of the Immortal School" (仙宗十友) with Li Bai, He Zhizhang, and Meng Haoran — evidence of his prestige in Tang literary culture.

## Works

| Work | Content |
|---|---|
| *Zuowang Lun* (坐忘论) | The seven-stage meditation treatise (see [text page](/texts/zuowang-lun/)) |
| *Tianyinzi* (天隐子) | Cultivation in dialogue form, on simplicity and gradual practice |
| *Fuqi jingyi lun* (服气精义论) | On qi-absorption and its subtleties |
| *Xiuzhen mizhi* (修真秘旨) | Twelve chapters, composed at Yangtai Abbey |

He also systematized the Shangqing "seven signs" (七候) of practice progress — from improved health through stages of transformation to "refining spirit to merge with the Dao" — a graded-achievement scheme parallel to his seven meditation stages.

## His theoretical contribution

Sima Chengzhen's distinctive claim: "The immortals are also human" (神仙亦人). Immortality is the natural completion of human potential, approached through emptying the mind ("cultivate my empty qi, follow my self-so-ness") — not through external elixirs alone. He integrated Buddhist contemplative vocabulary into the Shangqing practice structure, producing the synthesis that defined Daoist meditation for the rest of the tradition. His work influenced Song-Yuan inner alchemy and — through the shared vocabulary of mind-collecting — the Neo-Confucian jingzuo tradition.

## Interpretation cautions

- Much of his biographical detail comes from Tang official histories and stele records — generally reliable, but honorific in framing (the sources are court-facing).
- The traditional account of his transmission lineage (Shangqing patriarchal list) is a religious construction of continuity, labeled as such here.

## Editorial synthesis

Sima Chengzhen is the pivot between two eras: before him, Daoist sitting practice was a cluster of lineages and manuals; after him, it has a canonical treatise, a named seven-stage architecture, and an imperial imprimatur. Anyone studying Chinese meditation eventually arrives at his door — the *Zuowang Lun* is the standard by which the tradition organized itself.
