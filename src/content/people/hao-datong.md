## Who he was

**Hao Datong (1140–1212)** was the Quanzhen Seven Perfected's reader of the *Book of Changes*. Born Hao Lin in Ninghai, he grew up on the *Laozi*, *Zhuangzi*, and *Liezi* and especially on the *Yijing* — the sources say he understood yin-yang, calendrics, and divination, and that he disliked the scramble for office so much that he hid his gifts behind a diviner's stall, telling fortunes to keep people from finding out what he was. Two dates for his birth circulate (1140 and 1149); the site registers both without adjudicating, as it does the two dates for his conversion (1167 or 1168). What the sources agree on is the shape of the life: the *Yijing* man who became a silent ascetic.

## The years that made him

- **The diviner who joined.** In 1167 (or 1168) Hao entered Wang Chongyang's school. When the master left for Guanzhong in 1169 with Qiu, Liu, Tan, and Ma, he did not take everyone: **Hao and Wang Chuyi were left to practice on Kunyu Mountain** — the archive's mark of the inner circle of four (whom Wang Chongyang, in the surviving metaphor, called his brother, nephew, and sons) and the second ring around them.
- **The six years under the bridge.** The signature Hao story is dated to 1175: begging in Wozhou, he had a sudden insight, and spent **six years practicing under a bridge**, silent. People called him "the master who does not speak" (不语先生). The site files the story in its traditional layer and notes its function: it is the Seven Perfected's most literal version of the [empty boat](/stories/xuzhou-empty-boat/) claim — a man who removed himself so completely that speech itself was dropped, and the silence was the teaching.
- **The northern mission.** After the nine-fold work was, in the tradition's word, completed, Hao went north to teach — the record that completes the Seven Perfected's geographical map: Shanxi, Hebei, and the north for Hao, as Luoyang had been for Tan and Liu.

## The lineage and the books

Hao founded the **Huashan (华山) lineage**, the branch of Quanzhen named for Mount Hua and anchored, in the tradition's later reckoning, in the *Yijing*-framed inner alchemy that was his specialty — the baike entry's phrase is 卦爻周天丹道: the hexagram-and-line orbit alchemy, using the *Changes* to lay out the stages and fire-phases of the practice. His recorded works run the same line: the *Sanjiao ruyi lun*, the *Shijiao zhiyan*, a *Heart Sutra* commentary, a *Zhouyi cantong qi jianyao shiyi*, the *Taiyi tu* — collected in the *Taigu ji* preserved in the Daoist canon.

## Boundary notes, stated plainly

The bridge silence and the sudden insight are traditional records, registered in the tradition's layer; the historical core is the disciple, the school, and the lineage. The birth-year and conversion-year discrepancies are kept visible rather than smoothed over, per the site's standing rule for the Seven Perfected's dates. What no source disputes: Hao Datong was one of the seven disciples, the founder of the Huashan lineage, and the Quanzhen master whose practice vocabulary ran through the *Book of Changes*.
