## Who he was

Tao Hongjing (456–536), courtesy name Tongming, style-name the Huayang Recluse, posthumous title Master Zhenbai — born to an aristocratic family in Danyang Mòlíng (present-day Nanjing). A prodigy: at ten he read [Ge Hong's](/people/ge-hong/) *Traditions of the Immortals* and formed his ambition; at fifteen he wrote the *Seeking the Mountains* essay; at twenty he entered court service as reader to the princes and became a general of the palace guard. He studied with the Daoist master Sun Youyue, and in **488** — the hinge of his life for this site — he obtained at Maoshan the **original manuscripts of [Yang Xi](/people/yang-xi/) and Xu Mi**, the medium-circle records of the 364–370 revelations. In **492** he resigned office and withdrew to Jurong's Mount Juqu (Maoshan), where he stayed forty-odd years, building the Huayang lodge, transmitting the Shangqing scriptures, and founding what became the Maoshan lineage (which later numbered him among its earliest masters).

The Liang emperor Wu consulted him by envoy on state affairs so often that the age called him 山中宰相 — "the prime minister in the mountains." The stories capture the refusal without the withdrawal: asked to return to office, he painted two oxen — one free in the grass, one gold-harnessed and driven with a stick — and sent the painting as his answer.

## What he compiled — and why the compiling matters

Tao Hongjing is the site's archetype of the **compiler-editor**, the figure who turns scattered household records into a tradition's charter:

- **The *Zhengao* (Declarations of the Perfected)** — his edition, with annotations, of the Yang–Xu revelation records, built on Gu Huan's earlier *Zhenji*. The [Shangqing timeline](/timeline/shangqing-revelations/) anchors this as the moment the revelations became a corpus: without the *Zhengao*, Yang Xi's sessions would be family papers; with it, they are the founding documents of a lineage. The same pattern the site records later in [Zeng Zao's Dao Shu](/timeline/daoshu-neiguan-zuowang/) — a compiler, a century after the material, giving it its canonical form.
- **The practical corpus** — the *Dengzhen Yinjue* (Hidden Instructions for Ascending to Perfection), the *Yangxing Yanming Lu* (Extending Life through Nourishing the Nature), and the *Zhenling Weiye Tu* (Chart of the Ranks and Positions of the Perfected), the last of which systematized the celestial bureaucracy that the [Wei Huacun](/people/wei-huacun/) tradition claimed — the institutional order behind the [Huangting Jing's](/texts/huangting-jing/) interior maps.
- **The three-teachings stance** — he took Buddhist precepts at the Asoka stupa, kept Buddhist and Daoist halls at his lodge with alternate-day worship, and held that the hundred methods all converge in the three teachings (百法纷凑，无越三教之境). He is the earliest *institutional* instance of the coexistence the site records on the [Zhiyi](/people/zhiyi/) page from the Buddhist side: the two poles of the [vocabulary-borrowing](/blog/three-languages/) boundary, one generation apart.

## The three layers, kept separate

- **Historical layer:** the dates (456–536), the offices, the 488 manuscript discovery, the 492 retirement, the emperor's consultations — documented and consistent across sources.
- **Scholarly layer:** his role as *editor* — the *Zhengao's* compilation and annotation, the systematizing works — the register in which his importance is least contested.
- **Tradition layer:** the revelation claims themselves, and his later place in the Maoshan lineage's forty-five generations — the tradition's account, reported as such.

## Why he belongs in this knowledge base

He closes the site's Shangqing arc: [Wei Huacun](/people/wei-huacun/) (the claimed source) → [Yang Xi](/people/yang-xi/) (the medium) → **Tao Hongjing (the editor)** → the Maoshan lineage that raised [Sima Chengzhen](/people/sima-chengzhen/) and the [Zuowang Lun](/texts/zuowang-lun/). The arc is the site's standing demonstration that a tradition is not only a revelation but a *filing system* — and that the filers deserve pages as much as the revealers.

**Uncertainty preserved:** dates and offices are documented; the revelation-material's authenticity is the tradition's claim, not a finding; the exact scope of Gu Huan's earlier collection versus Tao's own editing is recorded in the sources as a matter of scholarly reconstruction.
