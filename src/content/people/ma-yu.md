## The man and the arc

- **The school's most expensive convert.** The records agree on the arithmetic that makes Ma Yu unique in the Quanzhen founding: he was the rich one. 马半州 — "Ma of half the prefecture" — was already a byword for the family fortune when [Wang Chongyang](/people/wang-chongyang/) walked into Ninghai in 1167, and the Weihai account preserves the sequence: the couple received the stranger with tea, talked, and ended by *funding the building* — the 全真庵, the Hermitage of Complete Truth, from which the whole school takes its name. The site's [founder page](/people/wang-chongyang/) holds the arc from the other side (the grave-house years, the eastward journey); this page holds the receiving end: the school's first act in Shandong was paid for by the man who then joined it. The site's [dropped-jade story](/stories/lin-hui-qi-bi/) is the book's own version of the arithmetic — the thing of value is dropped when the pressure comes, and what is kept is the living bond — and Ma Yu is the tradition's case study: the fortune went to the sons, and the man followed the master.
- **The renaming, and the hesitation before it.** The Daoist Association's profile preserves the traditional machinery of the conversion: Ma Yu's recorded dream-poem (烧得白，炼得黄，便是长生不老方 — burn it white, refine it yellow, that is the ageless recipe), after which Chongyang gave him the name 钰 with the style 丹阳子 — the dream as the traditional account's seal on the renaming. And the profile is equally specific about the cost: 初为家事所累 (at first burdened by family affairs), unable to decide, he was persuaded — the traditional account's device is the divided pear (分梨, a pun on 分离, separation) — and only then handed the family wealth to his sons and left with the master. The site's [teacher-line question](/experiences/questions/do-i-need-a-teacher/) holds the pattern this makes visible: in the tradition's own record, even the school's first convert did not leave easily — the departure is depicted as an extraction, not a flight.
- **The three years of mourning.** 1170: Wang Chongyang dies in Bianliang, and the four disciples — Ma, Tan, Liu, Qiu — carry the coffin west to Liujiang village near Zhongnan. Ma Yu then keeps the mourning hut for three years, and the records quote the severity in a single breath: 修真功，积真行。服纸麻之服，食秣粮之食。隆冬祁寒，露体跣足，怡然不之顾，唯一志于道 — paper-hemp clothes, fodder-grain food, midwinter bare-bodied and barefoot, undisturbed, intent on the Way alone. The site's [ascetic-wing page](/people/wang-chuyi/) holds the school's parallel severity (the nine years in the cave); this page holds the mourning version — and the pair shows the founding generation's shared grammar: proof, in Quanzhen's record, is measured in what the body endures, and the [sitting practice pages](/practices/jingzuo/) keep the boundary on that grammar for the modern reader.
- **The rule of the second patriarch.** After the mourning, Ma Yu returned east and taught between the capital region and Shandong — and the records' summary of his conduct is a rule, not an anecdote: 安贫慈下，不用人一钱，不接人一物 — content in poverty, kind to the lowly, taking no one's coin, accepting no one's thing. The man who had funded the school now refused its money. The site's [Qiu Chuji page](/people/qiu-chuji/) holds the court wing of the same school (the Western journey, the emperor's favor); the records make the contrast explicit in one portrait sentence — 其安心定性则清虚淡泊，其接物导人则慈爱恺悌 (settling the mind with clear emptiness, guiding people with loving courtesy) — by which, the records say, people from near and far flocked to him, and the Yuxian branch (遇仙派, the Encountering-Immortals branch) was founded. The founder gathered seven disciples; the second patriarch set the school's two wings in motion, and this page registers the one that asked for nothing.
- **The dates, registered.** The records disagree about the death year: most give 1123–1183, the Wikipedia article gives 1123年6月15日—1185年1月25日. The site registers both without adjudicating, per its [dual-registration discipline](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/) — and the same discipline covers the small disagreements that trail the record (玄宝/玄玉, 马半州/马半街). A founder's biography is a record with edges, and the edges are part of the record.
- **Why he belongs here.** He completes the site's Quanzhen founding portrait as the *material* term of the sequence: [Wang Chongyang](/people/wang-chongyang/) the founder who made the grave-house years the school's origin myth, Ma Yu the host who made the school's name out of his own house, [Qiu Chuji](/people/qiu-chuji/) the court wing, and [Wang Chuyi](/people/wang-chuyi/) the mountain wing. The site's [craftsman-mystic line](/people/chen-nan/) gets its northern mirror once more: the southern lineage's founders came from the trades (the barrel-hooper Chen Nan); the northern school's first disciple came from the *fortune* — and both traditions record the same conversion: the thing that was theirs became the thing they gave.

The most prominent woman of the Seven Perfected has her own page: [Sun Bu'er (孙不二)](/people/sun-buer/).
