## The man, and the book that swallowed him

- **The edition that became the book.** The site's single most important fact about Guo Xiang, verified across the sources: of the several dozen Zhuangzi commentaries written in the Wei-Jin, **all but his were lost by the Tang**, and from the Song onward his edition circulated alone and fixed the chapter order. The 33-chapter book this site quotes — the [inner seven, outer fifteen, mixed eleven](/texts/zhuangzi/) division on which the site's whole [layering discipline](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/) rests — is Guo Xiang's edition. Every 内篇/外篇/杂篇 judgment on this site is a loan from his scissors. The site keeps this on the [zhuangzi text page](/texts/zhuangzi/) and here, because it is the tradition's deepest lesson in textual authority: the book we read is a *selection*, and the selector's name is recorded.
- **The theft, double-registered.** The [Shishuo xinyu's](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/) story is quoted in full in the sources above, and the site registers both halves without adjudicating, per its [edition discipline](/translations/daodejing-22/): the accusation (Guo Xiang, "of thin conduct and fine talent," took Xiang Xiu's commentary as his own) and the counter-evidence (the Siku zongmu's collation against Xiu's remnants found passages where Xiu has what Guo lacks, where they differ outright, and where they interpenetrate — "his punctuating-revision is not without evidence"). Tang Yijie's formulation, recorded in the sources, is the site's adopted summary: Guo's work is Xiu's "continued and broadened" (述而广之). The controversy itself is the teaching: **commentary is authorship**, and the tradition knew it seventeen centuries before the word "compilation" had a literature.
- **"It is the Zhuangzi commenting on Guo Xiang."** The Chan master Zonggao's verdict, preserved in his recorded sayings and quoted in the site's sources: 曾见郭象注《庄子》，识者云：却是《庄子》注郭象 — "I have seen Guo Xiang's commentary on the Zhuangzi; those who know say it is the Zhuangzi commenting on Guo Xiang." The page keeps it as the tradition's sharpest warning about itself: Guo's commentary is the most influential reading of the book *and* the reading that reversed the book — the sources record his treatment of the Xu You passage (the book praises the hermit who refused the throne; Guo praises Yao who kept it, and dismisses the hermit's "hollow silence in the mountains" as what made the Lao-Zhuang talk useless to those in power). Qian Mu's charge (曲说媚势, bending doctrine to flatter power) is registered as the harshest form of the same observation, and the [people pages](/people/fu-yi/) keep the pattern: the tradition's textual heroes were often its doctrinal revisionists.
- **The doctrine he installed.** The verified summary: the ten thousand things **self-generate and self-transform** (自生自化), against both the veneration of non-being and of being; and 名教即自然 — the names-and-teachings (the Confucian order) *are* the natural. The first claim made Guo the Zhuangzi's philosophical heir — the [大块](/glossary/da-kuai/) and [真宰](/glossary/zhen-zai/) pages this site holds are, in their received reading, Guo's doctrine (块然而自生 is his word-family) — and the second made him its political tamer. The site's [does-Daoism-believe-in-a-creator question](/questions/does-daoism-believe-in-a-creator/) answers from the texts *as received through him*; this page is the receipt.
- **The speaker.** Wang Yan, the era's greatest judge of talk, heard him and said: 如悬河泻水，注而不竭 — "like a river pouring from a hanging cliff, never exhausted." The site keeps the image as the man's one undisputed monument: whatever else he took or bent, the flow was his.

## Why he belongs in this knowledge base

Because this site cannot cite the Zhuangzi without citing him. The [zhuangzi text page](/texts/zhuangzi/) is his edition; the [zhuang-zhou page](/people/zhuang-zhou/) is his portrait of the author; the layering discipline is his table of contents. A knowledge base about the book that does not name the editor is quoting a ghost — and the site names him, theft-controversy and all.

**Uncertainty preserved:** the birth year is not recorded (c. 252 is modern scholarship, questioned by Xu Hangsheng); the death year (312, Yongjia 6) follows the Jin shu's "died at the end of Yongjia"; the theft controversy is registered with both camps and not adjudicated; Tang Yijie's 述而广之 is the site's adopted formulation, attributed; the doctrinal summary follows the CText wiki's characterization of the commentary.
