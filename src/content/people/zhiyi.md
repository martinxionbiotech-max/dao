**Direct answer:** Zhiyi (智顗, 538–597) was the founding systematizer of the Tiantai (天台) school of Chinese Buddhism and the author of the most influential meditation manual in Chinese history — the short *Xiao zhiguan* ([text page](/texts/xiao-zhiguan/)). This site documents him not as a Daoist figure but as the historical source of the *operating vocabulary* that Daoist and Confucian sitting instruction later absorbed: the five adjustments and the four faulty breaths. His historicity is as solid as any figure's in the field.

## Life (from Buddhist biographical sources)

- Born 538 in the Liang dynasty; became a monk young and studied under Huisi (慧思), the second patriarch of what became the Tiantai lineage.
- 567: went to Mount Tiantai (天台山) in Zhejiang, where the school took shape and its name.
- Served as spiritual teacher to Sui dynasty royalty; the school's political reach made its texts and vocabulary normative across China.
- 594: at Yuquan Temple (玉泉寺) in Jingzhou, dictated the *Mohe Zhiguan* ("Great Calming-and-Insight") — the mature statement of his meditation system — recorded by his disciple Guanding (灌顶, 561–632). The lecture series was left unfinished at Zhiyi's death in 597.
- Among his many works, the *Xiao zhiguan* — a popular condensation of the same system, written for lay practitioners — became the traveling text: short, plain, and copyable.

## Why he is on this site

Zhiyi matters here for one specific reason: **his beginner's manual became the common operating language of Chinese sitting**. Three concrete transfers:

1. **The five adjustments (调五事)** — diet, sleep, body, breath, mind — passed from Tiantai instruction into Daoist health manuals and Neo-Confucian quiet-sitting teaching. The modern Chinese word for breath regulation in sitting, 调息, is his (see [tiaoxi](/glossary/tiaoxi/)).
2. **The four faulty breaths** — wind, panting, gasping, resting — the standard diagnostic grid for breathing faults, reused verbatim in later non-Buddhist manuals.
3. **The sinking/floating pair (昏/浮)** — dullness versus scattering — which the Zhu Xi school's quiet sitting inherited, and which this site's [drowsiness](/problems/drowsiness-in-sitting/) and [restlessness](/problems/restlessness-in-sitting/) pages treat as the received distinction 昏沉 vs 散乱.

**The boundary:** Zhiyi's *system* is Buddhist samatha-vipasyana with Buddhist metaphysics; what the Daoist tradition borrowed is the *technique vocabulary*. This site documents the borrowing, states the provenance on every page, and adopts none of the Buddhist doctrinal claims. Zhiyi is recorded here as a historical person whose manual crossed traditions — not as a Daoist.

**Uncertainty preserved:** biographical details rest on Buddhist hagiography and doxography; the dates and institutional facts are well established, the anecdotal texture less so.
