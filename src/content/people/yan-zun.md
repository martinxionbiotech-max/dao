## The man, and why the counter mattered

- **The fortune-teller of Chengdu.** Yan Zun — whose original surname was Zhuang (the surviving editions list him as 庄遵; the 严 form is the later standard) — is the tradition's case of the *working* hermit: in the Chengdu market he divined for a hundred cash a day, and when the hundredth coin arrived, he closed the curtain and taught the Laozi. The Hanshu's line is the biography's heart: the Shu people loved and honored him, and praise him to this day. The site keeps the counter because it is the [reading-order tool](/tools/reading-order/)'s earliest ancestor: a classroom that opened at the price of a day's food and closed at the same price.
- **The teacher of Yang Xiong.** The young Yang Xiong studied under him — and the biography preserves the famous test: the governor Li Qiang asked Yang Xiong whether he could recruit the hermit; Yang Xiong answered that Junping could be visited with proper ceremony but could not be made to bend (可见而不可得诎也); the governor came with ceremony, and in the end did not dare offer him the post. The site registers the scene with its [refusal-pages](/people/chen-tuan/): the pattern — power comes to the hermit and leaves without him — runs from Junping's counter through [Chen Tuan](/people/chen-tuan/)'s two imperial audiences to the present.
- **The rich man's arithmetic.** Asked why he did not seek office, he answered that office is self-inflicted trouble; offered carriages and clothes by a rich man, he refused with the passage's best line: the rich man, he said, works day and night and is never satisfied, while he himself earns coins without leaving his bed and has hundreds left over with nothing to spend them on — "so it is I who have surplus and you who lack" (以不足补有余 — you are filling the lacking from the surplus). The site records the exchange as the tradition's earliest *contentment arithmetic*, the [practice-layer answer](/questions/what-is-stillness/) in biographical form.

## The book: Laozi zhiguì

- **The earliest surviving Western Han commentary.** The thesis the site verified states it plainly: among Han Laozi commentaries, Yan Zun's *Laozi zhiguì* (老子指归, also 道德真经指归, "The Laozi's Pointing Home") is the only one from the Western Han that survives — the bridge between the Huang-Lao synthesis and Wei-Jin xuanxue, and a documented influence-line toward Yang Xiong's *Taixuan*, Wang Chong's natural philosophy, and — per Wang Deyou, Na Wei, and others — [Wang Bi](/people/wang-bi/) himself. The site's [commentary pages](/people/heshang-gong/) now hold the tradition's three earliest readers of the [Daodejing](/texts/daodejing/): [Heshang Gong](/people/heshang-gong/) (the anonymous practice reading), Yan Zun (the market-counter philosophical reading), and [Wang Bi](/people/wang-bi/) (the court philosophical reading, dated, at twenty-three).
- **The self-transformation line.** The thesis's key finding for this site: in a Han world saturated with heaven-human resonance (Dong Zhongshu's system), Yan Zun **kept to the Daoist line** — emphasizing 万物自化, the self-transformation of the ten thousand things, and stepping away from the resonance cosmology. The site's [ziran concept page](/concepts/ziran/) marks him as the term's great Han carrier: the Western Han's one major Laozi reader who did *not* hand the book to the correlative cosmologists.
- **A lecture text.** The zhiguì's structure — a passage of the Laozi followed by the teacher's free elaboration — is recorded in the sources as likely a student compilation of his lectures. The site notes the parallel with its own [experience records](/experiences/): the tradition's foundational texts were often *heard* before they were written, the teacher's voice at one remove.

**Uncertainty preserved:** dates follow the standard accounts (b. 87 BCE, d. c. 6–7 CE, aged over ninety — the sources vary between 九十余 and 95, recorded as approximate); the Zhuang/Yan surname question is registered without asserting the taboo explanation; the zhiguì's student-compilation status follows the sources' own statement; the Wang Bi influence-line is recorded as scholarly argument, not fact.
