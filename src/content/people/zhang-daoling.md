## The man, in three layers

- **The historical layer: a movement on record.** The one fact every source agrees on: in the mid-second century CE, in Shu (modern Sichuan), there was a religious community whose members contributed five pecks of rice — the Five Pecks of Rice Way, attested in the [Hou Han shu and Sanguo zhi](/timeline/heshang-gong-commentary/), the earliest organized Daoist church in the record. The site's [Taiping jing page](/texts/taiping-jing/) holds the contemporary movement in the east; this page holds its western sibling — and the two together are the site's answer to the question of when the practice traditions first acquired an *organization*.
- **The traditional layer: the founder's biography.** The tradition's own account, registered as tradition: a scholar of the classics who left office, declined three summonses, and retired to Heming mountain; the founding revelation of Han'an 1 (142) — the Most High conferred the Three Heavens' orthodox law and named him Celestial Master (天师); the twenty-four parishes (二十四治) of Han'an 2 (143); the registers, the demon-pacifying swords, the covenant with the old powers of Shu. The site keeps this account in the tradition's own terms — the [revelation pages](/timeline/shangqing-revelations/) hold the genre: a founding revelation is a tradition's constitution, recorded as claimed, not as chronicle.
- **The legendary layer: the arithmetic of ascent.** The white-day ascent at 123, the dragon-tiger elixir that named Longhu Mountain, the subjugation of the six demon kings — registered as legend. The site notes the numbers' neatness without adjudicating: the traditional birth year (34 CE) plus the traditional age (123) equals the traditional death year (156) exactly — hagiographic arithmetic, flagged as such per the [source discipline](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/).
- **The name.** 张陵 or 张道陵 — the sources record both, with 道陵 (Way-mound) plausibly a later honorific; the site uses the common name and notes the variant. 天师 (Celestial Master) — the title his line still bears — is the page's real subject: an office that outlived its founder by nineteen centuries.

## Why he belongs in this knowledge base

Because this site's practice pages — [shouyi](/practices/shouyi/), [cunsi](/practices/cunsi/), the [breathing](/practices/tiaoxi/) — trace back to texts and lineages, and Zhang Daoling is the moment those became a *church*: parishes, registers, a paid-for membership (the five pecks), and a hereditary succession that the [Kou Qianzhi page](/people/kou-qianzhi/) shows being reformed four centuries later. The site's [Taiping jing](/texts/taiping-jing/) asks what the practices *were*; this page asks who *organized* them — and the answer, however layered the legend, is the first name in the record. He is the boundary stone between [texts](/texts/zhuangzi/) and [institutions](/timeline/kou-qianzhi-reform/) — and the site keeps the layers separate: history (the movement), tradition (the revelation), legend (the ascent).

**Uncertainty preserved:** the movement is historical; the founder's dates and deeds are traditional; the ascent is legend. The 34/123/156 arithmetic is recorded as the tradition's own, without the site taking a position.
