## The man, and the two records that must be kept apart

- **The historical record.** Wang Bi, courtesy name Fusi, of Gaoping in Shanyang (present-day Shandong), born 226, held the post of *shangshu lang*, and died in the autumn of 249 — the year of the Gaoping Tomb coup — of a febrile illness, aged 24. He annotated the *Laozi* and the *Zhouyi*; the Tang court later made his *Zhouyi* commentary the basis of the official *Zhouyi zhengyi*. The sources agree on all of this, and the site records it as history.
- **The intellectual record.** With most earlier Laozi commentaries lost, **Wang Bi's Laozi commentary is the earliest surviving philosophical reading of the book** — the text through which the "dark learning" (xuanxue) of the Zhengshi era read the Laozi. The contrast the scholarship draws (and the site keeps) is with the [Heshang Gong commentary](/timeline/heshang-gong-commentary/): Heshang Gong reads the book as body-cultivation and qi-work (治身), Wang Bi reads it as discourse on mystery and emptiness (谈玄说虚) — 81 chapters, **no chapter titles**, slightly longer than the Heshang Gong text mostly by function-words. Together the two recensions are the received text's dual foundation: the [translation pages](/translations/daodejing-01/) document variants from both lines.
- **The site's specific dependence.** [Ch. 1's punctuation debate](/translations/daodejing-01/) — 无，名天地之始 vs 无名，天地之始 — is the Wang Bi reading against the received cutting, and the site double-writes it. The [ch. 37 paradox](/translations/daodejing-37/) is quoted in the philosophical form his recension carried. The person page exists so these editorial debts have a face.

## Why he belongs in this knowledge base

Because the site's [Heshang Gong page](/people/heshang-gong/) ends with the oldest *practice* reading of the Laozi being anonymous — and Wang Bi is the counterweight: the oldest *philosophical* reading, by a named, dated, twenty-three-year-old. The pair is the site's standing lesson about the [source policy](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/): the tradition's two founding readings of the same book are one legendary name and one historical one, and the discipline is to know which is which on every page.

**Uncertainty preserved:** the biography follows the standard sources (226–249, the post, the death at 24); the intellectual characterization follows the scholarly account recorded above; the site does not adopt the xuanxue reading's metaphysics — it documents it, exactly as it documents the Heshang Gong reading without adopting its physiology.

The commentary that fixed the received text is dated at [Wang Bi's Laozi Commentary (c. 249 CE)](/timeline/wangbi-laozi-commentary/).
