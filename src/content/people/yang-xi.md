## Who he was, and how the record treats him

Yang Xi is the **instrumental figure** of the Shangqing ("Highest Clarity") tradition — the medium through whom, the tradition records, the deified [Wei Huacun](/people/wei-huacun/) and other celestial figures descended at Jurong (modern Jiangsu) during the reign of Emperor Ai (c. 364–370), dictating the scriptures that became the *Shangqing* corpus. The records describe him as a young Celestial Master adept attached to the Xu family — Xu Mi, a court official, and his son Xu Hui — writing the revealed texts in clerical script, in the course of the nightly sessions that the [timeline page](/timeline/shangqing-revelations/) anchors.

For this site he matters on two counts. First, he is the **bridge figure** between the [Wei Huacun](/people/wei-huacun/) page and the Shangqing corpus: the person through whose claimed transmissions the Lady of the Southern Marchmount became the tradition's matriarch — which is why the [Huangting Jing attribution](/texts/huangting-jing/) routes through him. Second, he is the site's clearest case of the **medium as author**: the tradition's own account makes the scriptures' origin a *receiving*, not a composing — and the modern reader stands exactly where the historical scholar stands, needing to keep the person (a real figure of the 360s, with a recorded place in the Xu household and in the later Maoshan lineage) separate from the mediumship (the tradition's register, reported as such).

## The three layers, kept separate

- **Historical layer:** a Celestial Master adept at Jurong in the 360s, in the Xu family's circle, whose written records became the nucleus of the Shangqing corpus — his dates beyond the revelation years are **not recorded** in the sources this site verified, and the page does not supply them.
- **Tradition layer:** the mediumship — the descents, the dictations, the celestial titles — reported as the tradition's own account, with the [timeline](/timeline/shangqing-revelations/) dating each element.
- **Text layer:** what the revelations *became* — the Shangqing scriptures, Tao Hongjing's *Zhengao* with its annotations (the fifth-century *Zhenji* of Gu Huan having been the earlier collection), and the Maoshan lineage that institutionalized them. The texts are real and dated; their origin story is the tradition's claim.

## Why he belongs in this knowledge base

Two structural reasons. First, he completes the site's practice map on the **visualization pole**: the [cunsi](/practices/cunsi/) practice page descends from the Shangqing corpus he mediated, and the [jing-qi-shen](/glossary/jing-qi-shen/) body-map is Shangqing through and through — the *filling* tradition standing opposite the [zuowang](/practices/zuowang/) emptying, both of which the site documents as the two poles of one inheritance. Second, his page is the site's standing exercise in the **three-register discipline**: person, mediumship, and text kept apart — the same separation the [Wei Huacun](/people/wei-huacun/) and [Huangting](/texts/huangting-jing/) pages perform, and the [translation policy](/blog/translation-policy/) generalizes.

**Uncertainty preserved:** dates beyond the revelation window are not stated (not recorded in the verified sources); the mediumship is the tradition's register, not a historical finding; the exact division of the scriptures between Yang Xi and the Xu scribes is the tradition's account.
