## The man, and the records that must be kept apart

- **The historical record.** Chen Tuan, courtesy name Tunan, of Zhenyuan in Bozhou (present-day Luyi, Henan, per the Song shi — his birthplace is otherwise disputed). Born 871; failed the *jinshi* examination amid the late Tang wars; turned to the mountains — Wudang, then Huashan, then Shaohua — where he practiced *bigu* (grain-avoidance) and the *sleeping* practice that made his name. Two emperors summoned him: Zhou Shizong gave him the title **Baiyun xiansheng** (Master White Cloud) after a famous exchange — the emperor asked about the alchemy of yellow and white, and Chen Tuan answered: "Your majesty is lord of all within the seas; your mind should be on good government — why dwell on yellow-white matters?" Song Taizong gave him the title **Xiyi xiansheng** and called him 方外之士, "a man beyond the bounds." He died in 989, aged nearly a hundred by the court's estimate, after some forty years on Huashan. The standard biography is Song shi ch. 457, the Recluses.
- **The name-record.** His chosen names are themselves the tradition's map: **Tuan** (抟) and **Fúyáozi** (扶摇子) both come from the [Peng bird passage](/stories/peng-bird/) of Zhuangzi ch. 1 (抟扶摇而上者九万里 — "braces on the whirlwind and climbs ninety thousand li"); **Xiyi** (希夷) comes from [Daodejing ch. 14](/texts/daodejing/) — 视之不见名曰夷，听之不闻名曰希, "looked at and not seen, it is called *yi*; listened for and not heard, it is called *xi*" — the book's words for the Way's invisibility. A man whose given honorifics are the two foundational texts' names for flight and invisibility: the site keeps the etymology because the names are the tradition's own reading of him.
- **The legendary record, kept separate.** The folklore (sleeping for months; the *shuigong*, sleeping-practice, attributed to him; the 无极图 lineage claims linking Zhou Dunyi's *Taiji tushuo* and Shao Yong's *Xiantian tu* back to him; the numerous divination books attributed to him — most likely false attributions) is recorded as legend, not biography — exactly the [layering the laozi page](/people/laozi/) applies. What is *historical* is the outline: the failed candidate, the two imperial audiences, the refusals, the long Huashan reclusion, the near-hundred years.

## Why he belongs in this knowledge base

Because he is the site's late-tradition anchor for the **practitioner's refusal**: two emperors, two refusals — no alchemy for the court, no office for the hermit — and the [invisible-ruler chapter](/translations/daodejing-17/)'s ladder read backwards: the man who stays unknown to power is the one the power comes to find. And because his *sleeping* is the tradition's most radical answer to the site's [drowsiness question](/questions/falling-asleep-during-meditation/): what the modern sitter calls a failure (falling asleep), the Sleeping Immortal called the practice — a fact the site records *and* refuses to flatten into permission, per its [sleeping-safety discipline](/problems/drowsiness-in-sitting/).

**Uncertainty preserved:** dates and court episodes follow the standard sources (Song shi ch. 457, Tang caizi zhuan); the birthplace is disputed across sources and recorded as disputed; the age "nearly a hundred" is the court's estimate, not a birth certificate; the sleeping folklore, the diagram lineage-claims, and the divination attributions are registered as legend/tradition, never as history.
