**The short answer:** in the Daoist sources, stillness (静) is **the mind's condition of non-disturbance** — not the absence of movement but the absence of *being moved*. The Daodejing defines it as "returning to the root" (归根曰静): things grow outward, then return; the return *is* stillness, and stillness is "returning to life" (复命). The Zhuangzi pictures it as a mirror: "responding without storing" (应而不藏). It is a real, named, trainable state — and the term itself is settled enough in the sources that this question can be answered at the terminological level.

## What the sources say, chapter by chapter

- **DDJ ch. 16:** 致虚极，守静笃 — "reach the utmost emptiness, keep steadfastly to stillness"; then 万物并作，吾以观复 — "the ten thousand things rise together, and I watch their return." Watching the return *is* the stillness practice: attention resting on how everything comes back to its root, and coming back with it.
- **DDJ ch. 45:** 静胜躁 — "stillness overcomes agitation," paired with 清静为天下正 — "the clear-and-still governs the world." Stillness is the *stronger* term of the pair: effective, governing, not passive.
- **Zhuangzi ch. 7:** the perfected person's mind "works like a mirror — neither welcoming nor escorting, responding without storing, able to prevail over things without being harmed." Stillness as perfect responsiveness with zero residue.
- **Qingjing Jing:** "the human spirit is fond of clarity, but the mind disturbs it; the mind is fond of stillness, but desires draw it" — the religious version's diagnosis, and its prescription: let the desires be released, and clarity-and-stillness return.

## The three misunderstandings, corrected

1. **"Stillness = sitting motionless."** No — the body sits, but stillness names the mind; the texts praise the mirror *because* it responds to everything. Physical quiet is the condition, not the content.
2. **"Stillness = blankness or sleep."** No — the tradition keeps [昏沉](/patterns/drowsiness-vs-stillness/) (dull sinking) strictly separate from 静; stillness in the sources is *clear*, aware, and light. The seeing-light [problem page](/problems/seeing-light-in-sitting/) shows the tradition even uses light as its image for it.
3. **"Stillness = doing nothing in life."** No — ch. 45's stillness *governs*; in the meditative context, stillness is the source of effective action ([wuwei](/concepts/wuwei/)), not its opposite.

## The boundary

The *definition* of stillness is settled in the primary sources — hence answered. The *causal claims* (stillness returns you to the root of life; stillness makes you effective) are traditional doctrine, not measured findings, and remain marked as such throughout this site. What modern research documents is the near-relative: meditation programs reduce stress and reactivity (Goyal 2014, [here](/research/mindfulness-meta-analysis-2014/)) — consistent with, but not proof of, the classical account.
