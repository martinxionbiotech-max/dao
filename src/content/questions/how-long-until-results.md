**The short answer:** there is no single timetable, and the tradition's own texts do not offer one — they offer *ladders* (schematic stages, not schedules), while community teachers offer *years*, and the modern record offers *weeks-to-months for symptom outcomes only*. The site's line: expect the [body and attention changes](/experiences/patterns/warmth-and-qi-sensations/) in weeks-to-months, the tradition's deeper claims in years or never, and promise yourself nothing — the tradition's own word for the path is [perseverance](/translations/daodejing-33/), not a deadline.

## The three registers, kept apart

- **The classical ladders are schematic.** The Zhuangzi's stage-numbers — [three, seven, nine days](/translations/yingning-passage/) for the woman Yu's ladder, [three, five, seven days](/translations/artisan-qing-passage/) for the woodcarver's fast, [ten-day units](/translations/wooden-rooster-passage/) for the fighting cock — are the book's literary arithmetic, not timetables: they mark the *shape* of progress (stages build, then a turn), never a promise that you will be through by Thursday. The site's translation pages flag each one as schematic, per the [text-interpretation discipline](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/): reading a stage-number as a schedule is the one misreading the book itself never commits.
- **The teachers estimate in years.** The community record the site archives is candid about scale: access concentration "within two or three years" ([EXP-009](/experiences/notes/exp-009-dissolving-void/)), three years of the full program to reach it ([EXP-010](/experiences/notes/exp-010-lotus-pain/)), a four-hour threshold for the first dhyana ([EXP-016](/experiences/notes/exp-016-nine-dhyanas-map/)), and — the series' standing line — "no attainment without ten years of work" ([EXP-020](/experiences/notes/exp-020-false-first-dhyana/)). The site records these as one teacher's system, not as fact: the same discipline as the [posture doctrine](/experiences/notes/exp-014-double-lotus-doctrine/) — a map, registered; a promise, not adopted.
- **The research measures weeks-to-months, for symptoms.** The [modern record](/research/mindfulness-meta-analysis-2014/) is the honest floor: eight-week programs show moderate effects on anxiety, depression, and pain — symptom outcomes, not the deep states the tradition names. Nothing in the literature tests "three days to 外物" — and the site's standing rule applies: absence of evidence is not evidence of absence, but it is absence of a timetable.

## The practical line

- **Measure the practice, not the state.** The site's [how-long question](/experiences/questions/how-long-should-i-sit/) holds the tradition's only consistent metric: continuity beats duration, and the [settled sitting](/experiences/questions/i-feel-nothing-when-i-sit/) page holds the only standard the classics endorse — 绵绵若存, continuous and fine, not intense and fast. Results worth measuring are the ones you can actually verify: did you sit today, did the body [settle](/problems/drowsiness-in-sitting/), did the [sensations](/experiences/questions/warmth-tingling-when-sitting/) stay benign.
- **The teacher's own honesty is the model.** The archive's most useful timetable data is the series' self-correction: the practitioner who claimed "first dhyana at last" was told — not yet, that's just settling ([EXP-020](/experiences/notes/exp-020-false-first-dhyana/)); the teacher himself says "I haven't achieved constant concentration yet" ([EXP-016](/experiences/notes/exp-016-nine-dhyanas-map/)). The site keeps these as the tradition's own realism: even inside a system with a map, the map's owner does not claim to have arrived. Progress claims deserve the same candor from you.
- **What is safe to expect.** Weeks-to-months: the [body's settling](/experiences/patterns/warmth-and-qi-sensations/), the [attention's steadiness](/experiences/patterns/drowsiness-vs-stillness/), the plain [health-adjacent benefits](/experiences/questions/can-meditation-cure-illness/) the research supports. Years-or-never: the tradition's deep states, which no source the site trusts has ever dated. The [sick question](/experiences/questions/should-i-meditate-when-sick/) applies the same line to illness: rest is the practice, medicine is the treatment — and neither has a timetable that comes from the cushion.
