**The short answer:** you are almost certainly not doing it wrong — and the archive's teacher gives one first answer that the site has verified across its whole collection: *most beginners who fall asleep while sitting are simply sleep-deprived, and stillness is the first thing that shows it to them.* The site's [drowsiness pages](/problems/drowsiness-in-sitting/) hold the same answer with the same first step: check sleep debt before you check anything else. Then the deeper point, which both the archive and the site's [stillness page](/questions/what-is-stillness/) agree on: drowsiness and stillness look identical from the outside — closed eyes, low motion, quiet — and are opposites from the inside. One is the body falling, the other is the body settling.

## What the sources say, layer by layer

- **The archive layer: the first answer and the inversion.** The archive's [EXP-001](/experiences/exp-001-falling-asleep/) preserves the beginner report and the teacher's first answer: most people who fall asleep when they sit are not failing at meditation; they are carrying sleep debt, and sitting simply removes the noise that was masking the exhaustion. Then the inversion from the [breathing chapter](/experiences/exp-028-abdominal-breathing/): evening sitting makes sleep *harder*, not easier — 睡前打坐反而不容易睡 — and the teacher's sleep-reduction doctrine (more oxygen, less brain fatigue, sleep drops) is registered on this site exactly as what it is: a lay-physiology claim, not endorsed. The site keeps both halves: sitting does not cause the sleep debt, and sitting is not a sleep aid.
- **The teacher's physiology layer: registered, not endorsed.** The teacher's ch. 6 (the basis of this site's [EXP-029](/experiences/exp-029-the-human-body/)) gives the full machinery: sitting entry is a state of being *awake and clear* (入定是在人非常非常清醒的情况下进入的); the brain needs oxygen and glucose; breath-rate is the index (呼吸次数多 = 消耗能量多 = 妄想多 = 烦恼多 — more breaths, more energy spent, more distracted thoughts, more distress); and the practice-adjacent claim that trained breathing supplies the sitting brain (犯困时也清醒). The site's [health-claims rule](/questions/can-meditation-cure-illness/) applies: registered as the teacher's account, not endorsed, and the [breath-stopping file](/experiences/exp-008-breath-stopping/) sits on the site precisely to hold the modern-research side of breath-related claims.
- **The classical layer: an honest absence.** The Daoist texts on this site have no term for sleepiness-in-meditation — the book's stillness vocabulary (the [withered-tree posture](/translations/qiwulun-wu-sang-wo/), the fasting-of-the-heart, the sitting-and-forgetting) describes states that the tradition explicitly distinguishes from sleep, but the beginner's *drowsiness* is not named there. The site registers the absence honestly rather than filling it with a borrowed term (the Buddhist 昏沉, torpor, exists but is not verified on this site, and per the site's [source discipline](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/) an unverified term is not a source). The closest the classical layer comes is the boundary itself: stillness is *described as* wakefulness — the [stillness page](/questions/what-is-stillness/) holds the distinction the tradition did draw.
- **The site's own pages: the protocol.** The site's [drowsiness problem page](/problems/drowsiness-in-sitting/) and its [pattern page](/patterns/drowsiness-vs-stillness/) hold the working protocol, verified across the archive: check sleep debt first (the archive's own first question); then posture (the [bed question](/questions/can-i-sit-in-bed/) holds the site's answer on the worst place to confuse the two); then time of day (the archive's evening-sitting caution); then the distinction test itself — sleepiness *recovers* (you wake up groggy and want more), stillness *restores* (you open your eyes clearer than before). And the site's standing statement, kept from its drowsiness pages: sleepiness during sitting is not a failure signal — it is information.

## The two honest declarations

- **What the site does not claim.** That the teacher's oxygen-and-brain-cells physiology explains drowsiness in any medical sense, that sitting causes or cures sleep debt, or that the classical texts have a doctrine of the beginner's torpor. The archive's physiology claims stay inside the archive's frame, flagged on every page that carries them.
- **What the site does claim.** That the archive and the site agree on the practical layer: if you fall asleep when you sit, the first thing to check is how much you actually sleep — and the difference between the two identical-looking states is the single most useful distinction a beginner can learn. Both are on the site, on the pages linked here, and neither requires believing anything.
