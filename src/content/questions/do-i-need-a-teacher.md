**The short answer:** neither requirement is the site's. The Zhuangzi's own transmission passage says the Way can be *transmitted* but not *received* — and its transmission chain runs through ink and recitation, not through living hands alone. The archive's teacher says the same thing in one line: take the precepts as teacher, self-study without a teacher also works — and these years are full of swindlers. What both sources converge on is not a requirement but a discipline: try, and describe honestly.

## What the sources say, layer by layer

- **The source-text layer: the chain runs through ink.** The [transmission passage](/glossary/zhao-che/) is the book's most quoted statement on this exact question, and the site holds its two halves: 可传而不可受 — the Way can be transmitted but not received (you cannot be *handed* it; the receiver must do the receiving) — and the chain that follows: 闻诸副墨之子 — heard it from the *son of ink* — who heard it from the *grandson of recitation*, and so on. The chain is a genealogy of *texts and repetitions*: the book's own answer to "who taught you?" is "writing." The book does contain living teachers (the [woman teacher](/glossary/zhao-che/) who taught the ladder of forgetting; Huzi teaching Liezi) — and it contains the transmission-by-book as the norm. The site's [faith question](/questions/does-practice-need-faith/) holds the consequence: what the tradition transmits is a *text-and-practice corpus*, not a private wire.
- **The archive-teacher layer: the sutras are the teacher, and the frauds are real.** The newly collected [EXP-027](/experiences/exp-027-finding-a-teacher/) holds the archive's most quoted answer on the subject, given to the direct question "is practice without a teacher all demonic?": 以戒为师，深入经藏，智慧如海，无师自学也可 — take the precepts as teacher, go deep into the canon, wisdom like the sea; self-study without a teacher also works. And the same answer's other half: "read the sutras yourself, research yourself — these years are full of swindlers, few true persons." The episode also carries the teacher's fraud analysis, which the site's [display line](/experiences/exp-021-prostrations-channel-circuit/) extends: those who get cheated are those who wanted shortcuts — attainment cannot be bought, and the man who sells quick advancement is the swindle.
- **The middle layer: what a teacher does, when there is one.** The same episode preserves a reader's own teacher's words (reposted with permission, paraphrased per the archive's rule), and the site elevates one sentence of it: 对老师最好的尊敬，就是按老师说的好好实践，并超过老师 — the best respect for a teacher is to practice what the teacher says, well, and surpass the teacher. That is the site's working definition of the teacher-student relation when it exists: a teacher is someone whose instructions you *do*, not someone whose approval you collect — which is also why a teacher is not, strictly, necessary: the instruction is the transferable part.
- **The safety layer.** The site's [safety-without-teacher page](/questions/zuowang-safety-without-teacher/) holds the standing boundary: the site's own materials (a sitting protocol, archived practitioner reports, translated passages) are text-transmission — the son-of-ink chain in modern dress — and for psychological difficulties they are not a substitute for clinical help. The archive's [innovation episode](/experiences/exp-025-lying-down-innovation/) adds the other boundary from the teacher's side: practice follows predecessors' footprints, not personal style — self-study is legitimate *within* the transmitted forms, not as a license to invent them.

## The two honest declarations

- **What the site does not require.** No page on this site requires a living teacher as a condition of practice. The [sitting protocol](/tools/sitting-protocol/) and the [practice pages](/practices/zuowang/) are written for the reader without one — that is the site's entire purpose — and the [reading order](/tools/reading-order/) is, in the passage's own terms, the son-of-ink chain.
- **What the site does require.** The description discipline, held on the [faith page](/questions/does-practice-need-faith/) and applied to teachers here: if you learned from a teacher, say so; if you learned from books and forums, say that. The site's pages name their sources for exactly this reason — a self-study practitioner who claims a lineage is making the one claim the sources actually forbid.
