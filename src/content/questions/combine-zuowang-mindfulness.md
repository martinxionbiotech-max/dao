**The short answer:** yes, in the practical sense — they can be combined, and many practitioners do. They share the same basic mechanics (still sitting, attention, non-interference), and no classical source on either side prohibits the other. What cannot be combined is their *accounts of what is happening*: zuowang's tradition describes emptying-and-forgetting with Daoist language (qi, the Dao, returning to the root), while mindfulness describes attention and acceptance in clinical language. Practitioners who combine them successfully usually run the practice like this site's [comparison page](/comparisons/zuowang-vs-mindfulness/) describes: one body, two descriptions, kept separate.

## What combining looks like in practice

- **Sequential, not simultaneous.** Sit as a zuowang session *or* a mindfulness session — not both at once. Trying to hold "empty and forget" and "attend and note" as simultaneous instructions produces a tangle, because they're two different relations to mental content: the one releases it, the other observes it.
- **Different sessions, different aims.** A practitioner might use mindfulness's instruction set for daytime stress (evidence: the [Goyal 2014 meta-analysis](/research/mindfulness-meta-analysis-2014/) — moderate effects on anxiety/depression/pain), and zuowang's framework for the contemplative depth the classical texts describe (no clinical evidence, stated plainly on the [zuowang page](/concepts/zuowang/)). The combination is a *division of labor*, not a fusion.
- **Mindfulness can be the entry, zuowang the tradition.** Many Chinese practitioners reach classical sitting through modern mindfulness apps, then find the [texts](/texts/daodejing/) describing what they were doing all along. The reverse also happens: classical practice gains vocabulary from the modern literature — the [drowsiness](/problems/drowsiness-in-sitting/) distinction is clearer because the modern research named fatigue.
- **Safety notes transfer.** The [Farias 2020 review](/research/farias-adverse-events-2020/) applies to meditation generally, including mixed practice: adverse events are uncommon but real, and combining systems does not make them less so.

## What combining does not mean

- **It does not mean the traditions agree.** The Daoist account (qi, dantian, returning to the root) and the clinical account (attention networks, stress response) are different languages for different audiences, and this site's discipline is to keep them separate — the [patterns](/experiences/patterns/warmth-and-qi-sensations/) pages show the separation in action.
- **It does not create a "new system."** Mixed practice is a personal arrangement, not a lineage; nothing on this site presents it as one.
- **It does not fix the evidence gap.** Mindfulness has clinical evidence; zuowang does not have dedicated trials. Combining them transfers neither status: the mindfulness evidence remains about mindfulness (per [Goyal 2014](/research/mindfulness-meta-analysis-2014/)), and zuowang remains evidence-free in the clinical sense. This answer is therefore marked answered at the *practical* level only — the compatibility question is answerable; the question of what combining them *does* is not, and no page here pretends otherwise.
