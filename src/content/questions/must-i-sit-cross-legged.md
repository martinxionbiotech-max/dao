**The short answer:** no — **the posture is an instrument, not a requirement.** The manual tradition grades posture by *stability*, and the received record includes chair-sitting, the kneeling seat, half-lotus, and full-lotus as legitimate forms. The hard line that "only full lotus counts" exists — but it is **one lineage's claim** within the community Q&A record, and it conflicts with the manuals' own priority (settled body, not heroic legs) and with the modern consensus on injury risk. This question is answered at the practice level: the documented spectrum *is* the tradition; the choice is yours within it, and the answer's conditions are stated below.

## What the manuals actually say

- **The Xiao Zhiguan's posture section** (the site's reference manual, [here](/texts/xiao-zhiguan/)) treats the seated form as one of the five adjustments, and the adjustment's standard is *settling* — the body arranged so the breath can be tuned and the mind addressed. A posture that produces constant pain fails the manual's own test: the body is not settled, and the [leg-numbness pattern](/experiences/patterns/leg-numbness-pain/) documents the result.
- **The tradition's grading principle.** The [jiafuzuo glossary](/glossary/jiafuzuo/) records the classical precision about the full form (which leg crosses on top, the insteps on the thighs) — and the same record shows the tradition *grading*: the half-form, the kneeling seat, and the chair all appear historically. Posture is the support, not the gate.

## Where the hard line comes from — and why it's one voice

The community Q&A record this site archives (the [Taishi column](/experiences/notes/exp-010-lotus-pain/)) contains the strongest version of the hard line: "single-lotus cannot open the channels — it doesn't count, only sitting; full-lotus is required," with a training program of endurance ("endure the pain, endure the time"), prostrations, and a one-year timeline to form the posture. That is a real, articulated position — and it is **one teacher's system**, with its own metaphysics (channel-opening) that this site reports but does not adopt. Against it stands everything else in the archive: the manual tradition's stability standard, the modern consensus that forced full-lotus is the fastest road to knee injury, and the site's own [protocol](/tools/sitting-protocol/) policy — **use the form that lets you sit settled for the planned duration, and add load gradually.**

## The practical answer, stated

- **Start where you are:** chair or kneeling seat if the legs can't cross comfortably; half-lotus if they can; full-lotus only when it arrives without force — and never let pain be the teacher of posture.
- **If your lineage requires full-lotus:** it also provides the graduated path (the endurance tradition's own answer is *gradual*: prostrations, movement, months to a year). Follow the path, not the gate.
- **The unchanged core:** every layer of the tradition agrees that *what the posture serves* — the settled body, the tuned breath, the quieted mind — is the actual practice. The legs are the least of it.

**Uncertainty preserved:** the "channels" claim inside the hard line is traditional physiology, not measured fact; the injury-risk claim is modern consensus, not a studied outcome for this population; the page keeps both as the sources state them, and its recommendation is the documented spectrum, not a judgment between metaphysics.
