**The short answer:** the classical manuals do not prescribe clock times — they prescribe *regulation*. A session is the right length when posture, breath, and mind remain adjusted and the sitting is not being forced; that usually means starting short (10–20 minutes) and extending gradually. The community consensus matches: **short and daily beats long and occasional.** This question is therefore answered at the practical level, with the classical caveat stated: the tradition's unit is the quality of regulation, not the minute.

## What the classical texts actually say

- **The manuals' silence is the answer.** The [Xiao Zhiguan](/texts/xiao-zhiguan/) gives a graded posture and a graded breath and says to re-check body, breath, and mind before, during, and after sitting — but no duration. The [Zuowang Lun](/texts/zuowang-lun/)'s stages are graduated by *content* (what is forgotten), not by time. The tradition measured progress in stability, not minutes.
- **The implicit unit: the breathing cycle, not the clock.** The manuals' instructions are breath-based ("expel stale breath three times," then fine continuous breathing). A classical session is a span of regulated breaths; duration follows regulation, never leads it.
- **The one classical duration-warning is about forcing.** The [tiaoxi](/glossary/tiaoxi/) frame — harmonize, never force — applies to time itself: a session held past the point where posture collapses or the breath is fought has left the manual's method, however many minutes it lasts.

## What practitioners report

Community discussions of sitting duration (search-indexed threads, anonymized per this site's privacy rules) converge on a consistent picture:

- **Beginners: 10–20 minutes.** The reported sweet spot for starting; the [drowsiness](/problems/drowsiness-in-sitting/) and [restlessness](/problems/restlessness-in-sitting/) pages both note that most beginner problems surface here, and that shortening is the standard first fix.
- **The climb is gradual, not heroic.** Practitioners who sustain practice report extending by small increments over months, not doubling overnight; the "two-hour sit" threads exist and are explicitly framed as years-in achievements, not beginner targets.
- **The consensus rule: regularity over length.** The community formula repeated across threads is daily-short rather than weekly-long — and this matches the manuals' own logic, since regulation is a state rebuilt each session, not a quantity accumulated.

## The boundary

No classical text fixes a number, and no modern study on Daoist sitting exists to fix one either. The practical answer above is community-and-manual consensus — real guidance, not research. Beginners with health conditions should additionally apply the [medical boundary](/experiences/questions/can-sitting-go-wrong/) that governs every practice page on this site.
