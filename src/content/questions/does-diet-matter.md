**The short answer:** the Daoist core texts do not prescribe a diet, and the Zhuangzi's most famous fasting passage exists precisely to *replace* food-fasting — but the site's sources also hold the one point where diet does matter for practice, and it is much smaller than either camp claims.

## What the sources say, layer by layer

- **The source-text layer: the mind-fast replaces the food-fast.** The [xinzhai passage](/translations/xinzhai-passage/) is the book's direct answer to this exact question, and the site holds it as such: Yan Hui reports his abstinence — no wine, no meat, for months — and asks if this counts as fasting; Confucius answers 是祭祀之斋，非心斋也: that is the fasting of the sacrifice, not the fasting of the mind. The passage then installs the real fast: 若一志，无听之以耳而听之以心… 唯道集虚，虚者心斋也 — unify the intention, listen with the heart not the ear, emptiness collects the Way, and emptiness is the fasting of the mind. The [wuwei page](/glossary/wuwei/) holds the general rule this instance follows: the book spiritualizes the ritual, and the body-level requirement is the thing the spiritualization replaces.
- **The archive-teacher layer: diet as the first gate, in a Buddhist frame.** The newly collected [EXP-026](/experiences/notes/exp-026-diet-gate/) holds the archive's other answer: meat-eating blocks even the first dhyana, the dream-test measures whether the meat-habit is extinguished (about seven years, in the teacher's frame), and the teacher's own regime is one meal a day, ten years vegetarian. The site registers the *frame* with the discipline established in [EXP-013](/experiences/notes/exp-013-buddhist-daoist-boundary/): this is Buddhist monastic teaching (four heavenly kings, karma, the Shurangama's 杀心不除尘不可出), coherent within its own tradition, and the site's [borrowing boundary](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/) keeps it at arm's length — the archive records the teaching as a teaching, not as the site's requirement.
- **The classical-moderation layer.** The one classical statement near this question is [ch. 12](/translations/daodejing-12/): the five colors blind the eye, the five flavors dull the mouth — 爽 there meaning *damage*, not delight (the translation page holds the trap). That is a statement about satiation, not a menu; the site registers it as the only classical text that touches diet, and notes the distance between "the senses dull themselves" and "thou shalt not eat meat."

## The one convergence

The site's sources agree on exactly one dietary point, and it is the useful one: **the sudden change is the mistake.** The archive's teacher says it in the nutrition exchange of EXP-026 (the body needs a transition period; cutting everything at once leaves you starry-eyed; tofu is fine daily) — and the archive's own community records the failure mode (a forum reader's report of an eight-year unbalanced vegetarianism ending in diabetes, registered in the same page). The Zhuangzi's side is quieter but points the same way: the book replaces the food-fast rather than sharpening it — the physical fast is *demoted*, not intensified. The site's [progress page](/experiences/questions/what-counts-as-progress/) applies the standing rule: change diet, if you change it, the way you would change any practice — gradually, and for a reason you can name.

## The two honest declarations

- **What the site does not require.** Nothing. No page on this site prescribes a diet, and the site's own [sitting protocol](/tools/sitting-protocol/) contains no food rule. The closest the practice pages come is ordinary prudence (the archive's 少吃多动, "eat less, move more," from [EXP-024](/experiences/notes/exp-024-standing-and-sitting/), and the [digestive page](/experiences/notes/exp-007-digestive-release/)'s note that a heavy meal before sitting makes drowsiness likelier) — registered as convergence with common caution, not as doctrine.
- **What the research record does not establish.** The site has no verified study on diet and meditation outcomes, and says so: the absence is registered, not filled with the archive's claims. The Buddhist-framed diet doctrine in EXP-026 is exactly the kind of claim the site's [claim discipline](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/) keeps labeled as a teaching rather than a finding.

The archive chapter that treats diet as a practice gate is at [From Zero to the Desire-Realm Dhyana](/experiences/notes/exp-032-from-zero-to-desire-realm/).
