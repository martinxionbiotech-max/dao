**The short answer:** most quitting is two fixable mistakes stacked — an all-or-nothing goal, and a meter that measures the wrong thing. The tradition's own texts diagnose both, and the site's answer follows them: shrink the goal, fix the meter, and treat restarting as the practice, not the failure.

## The two mistakes, and the tradition's own words for them

- **Mistake one: the goal is built to break.** The [ch. 64 translation](/translations/daodejing-64/) holds the book's ladder: 合抱之木生于毫末，九层之台起于累土，千里之行始于足下 — the great tree begins as a sliver, the tower rises basketful by basketful, the thousand-mile walk begins under your feet. A practice goal set at "an hour daily, forever" is a tower demanded without baskets — and the book says the failure then is *structural*. The same chapter names the failure pattern as the normal one: 民之从事常于几成而败之 — people's undertakings usually fail *when they are nearly complete* — because care thins at the end (慎终如始则无败事: be as careful at the end as at the start, and nothing is ruined). The [archive's teacher](/experiences/notes/exp-012-restless-body/) says the same thing in one image: the Buddha's lute-string — too tight, it snaps; too loose, it is silent; practice a while, rest a while. An all-or-nothing goal is the snapped string.
- **Mistake two: the meter is wrong.** The [progress page](/experiences/questions/what-counts-as-progress/) holds the site's asymmetric rule: you can measure practice, nobody can certify attainment — so measure practice. Quitting usually happens at the moment the *other* meter disappoints: no stage reached, no experience arrived, nothing "happening." The archive's honesty file holds the tradition's own corrections of exactly this: the [false first dhyana](/experiences/notes/exp-020-false-first-dhyana/) (what felt like attainment was, by the teacher's own standard, only settling), and the site's [feel-nothing page](/experiences/questions/i-feel-nothing-when-i-sit/) (nothing happening *is* the practice). When the meter is "days sat, not states reached," the third week stops being a verdict.
- **What quitting sometimes is: a correct verdict on a wrong object.** The site's [dragon-slaying page](/stories/tu-long-zhi-ji/) holds the counterweight: a skill pursued with no real object is a dragon — expensive, finished, and useless. If the practice being abandoned was *for* a certificate (a stage, a comparison, an image of oneself), the tradition's own joke says the abandonment was the healthy part. The honest question before restarting is the one the site's [progress page](/experiences/questions/what-counts-as-progress/) asks: what is this practice *for* — and is that object real?

## The restart rule

- **The wait is not a break in the practice; the wait is the practice.** The [Lord Ren page](/stories/ren-gongzi-fishing/) holds the site's sharpest image: the great fisherman baited fifty oxen and caught nothing for a year — 期年不得鱼 — and the year was not the cost of fishing; it was the fishing. Applied to the sitting practice: the gap between attempts is not evidence against you, provided the attempts resume. The tradition's own word for this is 慎终如始 — the care is the same at the restart as at the start; the restart *is* the start.
- **The pheasant's installments.** The [marsh pheasant](/stories/marsh-pheasant/) lives free and pays for it ten steps at a time — 十步一啄，百步一饮 — and the site reads the image as the tradition's own anti-streak doctrine: the free life is made of small, repeated installments, not heroic runs. Ten minutes daily is the pheasant's life; the hour-long streak is the cage.
- **The site's own floor.** The [how-long page](/experiences/questions/how-long-should-i-sit/) holds the community consensus: 10–20 minutes to start, daily beats occasional. The [archive's daily rule](/experiences/notes/exp-024-standing-and-sitting/) — alternate the legs, keep the seat — exists to remove the last excuse, not to raise the bar. And the [drowsiness page](/experiences/patterns/drowsiness-vs-stillness/) keeps the one real boundary: a sitting that slides into sleep is not a sit — so the floor is *short and awake*, never *long and fogged*.
