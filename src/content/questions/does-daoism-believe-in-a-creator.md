## Direct answer

**At the source-text layer: no.** The foundational texts are not agnostic on this question — they *raise it and decline it*. The tradition's own word for the driver is framed as an as-if: 若有真宰，而特不得其朕 — "**as if** there were a true driver, and yet its traces cannot be found" ([Zhuangzi ch. 2](/glossary/zhen-zai/)). The world-process is called the **great clod** — a mass with no face and no plan — and the tradition's own commentary on the clod makes the refusal explicit: things are born 块然而自生, "clod-like, **of themselves**" ([the da-kuai page](/glossary/da-kuai/)). The site's answer is therefore not "maybe" but the source texts' own no — with the important precision that it is a *refused* question, not an unasked one.

## The three words the refusal uses

- **真宰 — the true driver, as-if.** The [zhen-zai page](/glossary/zhen-zai/) holds the full record: the Zhuangzi proposes the steward (宰 — the one in charge, the butcher-priest) under the shadow of 若, "as if" — and then searches the body's hundred bones and nine orifices and finds no master among them. Guo Xiang's commentary draws the conclusion: search for the traces and find none, and it becomes clear that things are all *ziran* — **no thing makes them so** (无使物然也). The creator-question is the tradition's own question, asked in order to be answered by unfindability.
- **大块 — the great clod.** The [da-kuai page](/glossary/da-kuai/) holds the tradition's positive name for the world-process: a clod — chosen precisely because a clod has no face, no plan, no mind. It breathes out the wind (ch. 2) and carries you through form, life, age, and death (ch. 6) — and the labor was never personal, which is why death in ch. 6 is *rest*, not punishment.
- **天机 — the mechanism with no driver known.** The [tian-ji page](/glossary/tian-ji/) holds the third word: the heaven-mechanism spreads itself (天机自张), and no one knows its driver (莫知其宰). The tradition's answer to "then what makes it run?" is: *it runs* — and the not-knowing of the driver is stated, not hidden.

## What this does and does not mean

- **It is not atheism in the modern sense.** The texts do not argue against gods — [the Zhuangzi's world](/people/zhuang-zhou/) is full of spirit-figures and strange beings. The refusal is narrower and sharper: no *maker* of the whole, no *steward* of the process. The [hundun story](/stories/hundun/) is the tradition's only candidate for such a being — the shapeless one — and the story's point is that the moment well-meaning others try to give him the driver's face, he dies.
- **Later Daoism is more complex.** The site's scope discipline applies: the foundational refusal is what this page answers. Later religious Daoism developed pantheons, deified figures (see the [people pages](/people/laozi/)), and internal-alchemical cosmologies — those belong to the later tradition's own records, and the site does not use them to revise the source-text layer's answer, nor vice versa.
- **The practice consequence is the site's actual interest.** Why the refusal matters for [sitting practice](/practices/jingzuo/): the tradition's meditation does not address a person; it addresses the process — the breathing [clod](/glossary/da-kuai/), the [self-running](/concepts/ziran/) order. The [empty boat](/stories/xuzhou-empty-boat/) is the same doctrine in practice form: no one to be angry at, because no one is at the wheel — of the river, or of you.
