**The short answer:** no belief is *required* — the core instructions the site documents are techniques with observable checks, not acts of faith. But the traditions are the *reason the techniques survived*, and one rule follows: practice what you like, describe what you practice. The two sides of the answer, kept apart:

## What the instructions actually presuppose

- **The core instructions are godless and testable.** The site's central passages — [Kongzi teaching Yan Hui](/translations/xinzhai-passage/) to listen with the qi, the [Zuowang stages](/texts/zuowang-lun/) (trust in the method, continuity, the quieting descent) — contain no deity, no devotion, and no article of belief. What they presuppose is *conditions you can check*: that attention can be gathered, that the body settles, that the breath slows. The site's [is-sitting-religious page](/questions/is-sitting-religious/) already drew the line; this page draws the next one: the load-bearing parts of the practice are belief-free in the same way a recipe is.
- **What belief adds — and what it does not.** The honest, three-part statement: (1) the *frameworks* (qi, the alchemical body, the cosmology) are the traditional containers that kept the techniques alive and gave practitioners a map to read experience through — the site's [qi question](/questions/qi-belief-necessary/) covers the borrowing rules; (2) there is a *psychology of belief* — a meaning-frame can affect motivation and expectation, and the traditions themselves are explicit that [trust in the method](/glossary/zuowang/) is a stage, not a creed; (3) there is no evidence the site archives that practice *outcomes* depend on held beliefs — the [research record](/research/mindfulness-meta-analysis-2014/) measures practice effects without measuring belief, and the site states this as an absence of evidence, not a proof of irrelevance.
- **The asymmetry rule (the site's own, stated plainly).** You may practice without belief — and you must describe without dishonesty: if you practice sitting with the [Zuowang instructions](/practices/zuowang/) and none of the cosmology, say "I practice sitting meditation with techniques drawn from the Daoist tradition"; if you claim you practice *the* tradition, the tradition comes with it (deities, [alchemical stages](/glossary/jing-qi-shen/), [ritual context](/questions/is-sitting-religious/)). The rule is the same one the site applies to itself: the [progress question](/questions/what-counts-as-progress/) measures the practice, not the stage — and this page measures the technique, not the creed.
- **What the tradition itself says about belief.** The [Daoist record](/glossary/dao/) this site documents has a standing suspicion of *claims* — the [Zuowang's](/glossary/zuowang/) stages are silent on faith, the [experience archive](/experiences/) documents teachers who treat the body-side as checkable and the cosmology as secondary, and the tradition's own history (from [Lu Xiujing's](/people/lu-xiujing/) catalog to [Zhang Boduan's](/people/zhang-boduan/) prefaces) is of men *testing and recording*, not professing. Belief, in this record, is a permitted container, not a required ingredient.
