## Direct answer

**At the boundary layer: no, and the boundary itself is the answer.** Meditation — Daoist or Buddhist sitting included — is not a medical treatment, and the site will not carry any source's claim that it is. The question is answered at the layer the site actually controls: what the practice may *claim*, what the research *supports*, and where the site draws its line.

## The three layers, kept apart

- **What the tradition claims.** The [Tai ping jing](/texts/taiping-jing/) and the [Baopuzi](/texts/baopuzi/) — the site's two healing-text pages — do claim that the practices (shouyi, breathing, the inner work) heal and extend life. Those are **traditional claims**, registered in the tradition's own record, with the site's standing [source discipline](/tools/how-to-verify-sources/): a claim in a classical text is evidence of what the tradition believed, not evidence of a medical effect.
- **What the research supports.** The [mindfulness meta-analysis page](/research/mindfulness-meta-analysis-2014/) holds the modern record: meditation programs show moderate evidence for improving anxiety, depression, and pain at eight weeks — symptom-related outcomes, with the evidence grades moderate to low, and with harms poorly reported. The [adverse-events pages](/research/farias-adverse-events-2020/) hold the other half: meditation has documented unwanted effects, and the honest summary of the modern record is "useful for some, safe for most, but neither a cure nor a risk-free one." Nothing in this evidence supports meditation replacing medical care.
- **What the community record shows.** The [EXP-019 record](/experiences/exp-019-one-remedy-body/) preserves the strongest real-world case: a meditation teacher prescribing breath practice for teeth, gut, and ulcers — including the warning "do not take Western medicine." The site records the teacher's program *and* its own non-endorsement in the same page, and this question page is that record's generalization: whenever a practice source makes a health claim, the site reports the claim, marks it traditional, and refuses the medical role.

## The practical line

- Use meditation for what the research supports (stress, anxiety, depression, pain — as a complement, with the harms-awareness the site's [problems pages](/problems/breath-stopping-anxiety/) document).
- Treat illness medically. The [EXP-019 discipline](/experiences/exp-019-one-remedy-body/) applies to every tradition, the site's own included: the moment a meditation source starts telling you what not to take or what will heal, it has left its competence, whatever its vintage.
- The tradition's own best texts agree in spirit: the [empty boat](/stories/xuzhou-empty-boat/) does not promise safety, and the site's practice pages promise even less — a method of attention, not a pharmacy.
