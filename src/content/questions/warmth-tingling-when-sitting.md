**The short answer:** yes — **common, usually transitory, and nothing to do by default.** Warmth, tingling, and small spontaneous movements during sitting are among the most frequently reported experiences in the practitioner archive this site keeps ([EXP-002](/experiences/reports/exp-002-warmth-rotation/), [EXP-005](/experiences/notes/exp-005-night-qi-movement/), and the [warmth pattern page](/experiences/patterns/warmth-and-qi-sensations/)), and the Chinese tradition has named this whole family for two thousand years. The practical rule from both sides is the same: **notice, don't chase, don't fight.** This question is answered at the practice level (the reports and the vocabulary are documented); the *physiology* of the sensations is not settled by research, and the page keeps that boundary.

## What the practitioner archive reports

- **The forms:** warmth spreading from the [dantian](/glossary/dantian/) area or along the limbs; tingling or "flowing" sensations; small twitches or jerks; a sense of heat at rest.
- **The consensus handling:** the same acts appear in hundreds of independent reports without harm — the community's default is to let them be, and the recurring warning in the archive is against *chasing* them (wanting more of the pleasant ones tends to end the stillness that produced them).

## What the tradition calls them

- **Qi.** The classical name for the felt medium: the Daodejing's and Zhuangzi's [qi](/glossary/qi/) is the body's living flow, and sitting manuals describe sensation as qi "arriving" (the [dantian](/glossary/dantian/) system's home region). The eight tactile responses (八触) of the sitting-manual tradition — heat, cold, movement, itch, weight, lightness, roughness, smoothness — are the formalized catalog of exactly this family.
- **The tradition's own boundary:** these are signs on the road, not the destination. The [tiaoxi](/practices/tiaoxi/) ladder and the [zuowang](/practices/zuowang/) texts both treat sensation as something the practice passes through, never something to hold.

## The safety line, stated plainly

- **Fine to ignore:** transient warmth, tingling, small movements — especially when they fade with the session.
- **Worth attention:** pain that persists or worsens (see the [leg-numbness pattern](/experiences/patterns/leg-numbness-pain/)), heat that becomes distressing, or sensations accompanied by dizziness, chest discomfort, or fear that lingers after sitting. The [Schlosser 2019](/research/schlosser-2019/) survey documents that a minority of practitioners do report unpleasant meditation-related experiences (about one in four, ever) — mostly transient, but real, and the [breath-anxiety problem page](/problems/breath-stopping-anxiety/) applies the same screening to sensation reports.
- **Not a medical claim:** none of this page is medical advice. Sensations that worry you — especially chest, head, or persistent pain — belong with a clinician, not a knowledge base.

**Uncertainty preserved:** the reports and the classical vocabulary are documented; the *mechanism* (qi or vasodilation or relaxation or attention itself) is not settled — the three-layer reading (practitioner / traditional / physiological) is kept separate on the pattern page, and this page inherits that separation.
