**The short answer:** yes — sitting up in bed is fine, and the tradition's own texts are more relaxed about posture than its later manuals suggest. The one line the archived teachers draw, and the site repeats: *sit up*. Lying down is where the practice merges with sleep, and the sleep boundary is the one boundary every source on this site agrees about. The rest is tools and honesty.

## What the record actually says

- **The teachers' line: sitting up is sitting; lying down is the boundary.** The archive's [lying-down page](/experiences/exp-025-lying-down-innovation/) holds the teacher's full position: lying makes dreams, not stillness — and the bed's one sanctioned form, the auspicious reclining posture (吉祥卧), is a *provisional* method for the stage before the channels open, not a main practice. The [episode-24 material](/experiences/exp-018-halflotus-to-full-lotus/) adds the physiological claim, registered as the teacher's own lay-physiology, not anatomy: lying practice raises blood pressure and reddens the eyes — "do not practice lying down." The site's [drowsiness pattern](/patterns/drowsiness-vs-stillness/) is the same line from the practice side: the bed is where sitting slides into the sleep boundary, and the tradition's own texts (this site's [falling-asleep page](/questions/falling-asleep-during-meditation/)) treat the slide as the classic beginner's event, not a failure.
- **The classical counterweight: the armrest.** The book's founding stillness scene — Nan-Guo Ziqi at the [opening of the Qiwu Lun](/glossary/wu-sang-wo/) — is a man sitting *leaning on an armrest* (隐机而坐), breathing up to heaven: the tradition's own record that the state it prizes arrived in a casual, supported posture. The site's [posture page](/questions/must-i-sit-cross-legged/) draws the principle: posture is a *tool*, not a requirement — the lotus, the chair, the armrest are instruments for the same work. Sitting on a bed with pillows is the armrest's descendant, not a departure.
- **The mechanics note, kept small.** The archived teacher's sitting-mechanics (from [episode 24](/experiences/exp-018-halflotus-to-full-lotus/)): a soft surface tilts the pelvis, a tilted pelvis shortens the sit — so a firm base matters more than the location. The practical translation for beds: sit on a folded blanket or a cushion on the bed, not into the mattress, and let the knees fall below the hips as on a floor cushion. The site registers this as the teacher's own mechanics-plus-experience, not a requirement: the [sitting protocol](/tools/sitting-protocol/) already holds the site's posture range.
- **The description rule (the site's own).** The rule this site has stated on its [faith page](/questions/does-practice-need-faith/) applies to posture too: practice what suits your body — and *describe what you practice*. Sitting in bed is "sitting meditation, in bed, supported by cushions"; lying down is "reclining practice," which the teachers in the archive call a different, sleep-adjacent thing. The rule protects the record's honesty on both sides: it does not forbid your posture, and it does not let a reclining session be reported to yourself — or to a teacher — as a sit.
