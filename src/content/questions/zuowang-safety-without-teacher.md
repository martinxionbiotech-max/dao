**The question:** is quiet sitting of the Daoist type safe to practice alone, from books, without a teacher or lineage?

## What the tradition itself says

- **It built in exit conditions.** The *Taiping Jing* (Han) instructs: if agitation arises during practice, leave the room; do not force the sitting. This is a safety counsel from the tradition's own core.
- **It assumes transmission.** The *Zuowang Lun* opens with 敬信 (reverent faith) and presupposes a teacher-disciple context; the seven stages are a transmitted program, not a self-help checklist. The tradition never sold itself as teacherless.
- **It warns against forcing.** The recurring counsel across texts is subtraction and non-forcing — the opposite of strenuous mental manipulation.

## What the research literature says

- **Meditation in general** has a modest evidence base for benefit (see the [research page](/research/mindfulness-meta-analysis-2014/)), but **adverse-event reporting in meditation trials is poor** — the 2014 meta-analysis could not quantify harms because studies mostly did not report them.
- **No studies exist on zuowang or jingzuo specifically** — safety data for these practices simply do not exist.
- **Known risks elsewhere in contemplative practice** (from the mindfulness and retreat literature): distress, disorientation, and exacerbation of trauma-related symptoms, especially in intensive settings. Whether and how these apply to Daoist sitting is unstudied.

## What remains unanswered

There is no research on the safety profile of classical Daoist sitting practice per se. The honest answer state of this question is **open**: the tradition supplies conservative counsel (stop when agitated, don't force), but empirical safety data are absent. A modern practitioner acting conservatively — short sessions, non-forcing, stopping when distressed, professional help for clinical conditions — is following both the tradition's own advice and the limits of current knowledge.

**Not medical advice:** this page is informational. If you have a mental-health condition or trauma history, consult a qualified professional before beginning any contemplative practice.
