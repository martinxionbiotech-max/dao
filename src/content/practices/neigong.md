**Direct answer:** Neigong (内功, "internal work") is the counter-term to *waigong* (external exercise): work done *inside* the body — breath, intention, qi — as against work done on its outside (muscle, bone, movement). The term lives mostly in the martial-arts and internal-cultivation traditions, where it names the training that turns a movement practice into a cultivation practice. It is a *category* term, not one practice with one manual.

## The term's territory

- **Internal vs. external.** The pair neigong/waigong sorts training by what it works on: the inside (breath, intention, the jing-qi-shen economy) or the outside (form, strength, speed).
- **The overlap with this site's vocabulary.** What the martial traditions call neigong, the classical texts call by older names — [tiaoxi](/glossary/tiaoxi/) (tuning the breath), [taixi](/practices/taixi/) (the fine breath), and the [jing-qi-shen](/glossary/jing-qi-shen/) triad. Neigong is the later umbrella those older terms live under.

## The honest uncertainty

- **Attestation is loose.** Unlike daoyin (named in the Zhuangzi) or taixi (defined in the Baopuzi), neigong's early textual history is not firmly established on this site, and this page does not invent one. Its wide use dates to the Ming-Qing martial literature; claims of earlier antiquity circulate but are not verified here.
- **The term bundles claims.** "Internal" work is often marketed as the deeper or truer training; that ranking is a lineage claim, not a textual fact, and the site does not reproduce it.

## What the site does with it

- Keeps the term for what it is: a modern-facing category for the internal side of the movement and cultivation arts.
- Routes its actual practices to the pages that hold their sources: the breath arts ([tiaoxi](/glossary/tiaoxi/), [taixi](/practices/taixi/)), the body art ([daoyin](/practices/daoyin/)), and the triad ([jing-qi-shen](/glossary/jing-qi-shen/)).

 The term layer, with the modern-history caveats, is the [neigong glossary entry](/glossary/neigong/).
