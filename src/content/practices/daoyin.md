**Direct answer:** Daoyin (导引, "guiding and pulling") is the classical art of guiding the body and breath — stretching movements paired with breathing, aimed at suppleness and long life. The Zhuangzi already knows it as a practice class in the 4th century BCE, naming its signature moves: "the bear's strides and the bird's stretch." The Mawangdui silk chart (sealed 168 BCE) shows 44 figures in movement postures — the oldest surviving visual record of a Chinese movement practice. Daoyin is the ancestor of what the modern world calls qigong.

## What the sources say

- **Zhuangzi ch. 15:** the text lists daoyin among the "nurturers of the body" — practitioners who "blow and breathe, expel the old and draw in the new" and move in animal patterns, "all for long life." The passage is a description, not an instruction — but it shows the practice was already *named and recognizable* in the 4th century BCE.
- **The Mawangdui chart (168 BCE):** a silk manuscript from a Han tomb shows 44 human figures in postures, many imitating animals, with captions. It is the earliest material witness that the tradition *moved* the way the Zhuangzi says it did.

## The shape of the practice

- **Movement plus breath.** The defining pair: the body is guided (stretched, turned, extended) while the breath is tuned — the same breath hierarchy the [tiaoxi](/glossary/tiaoxi/) entry treats, applied to motion.
- **Animal patterns.** The Zhuangzi's bear and bird became a whole vocabulary — the Five Animal Frolics (wuqinxi) attributed to the Han physician Hua Tuo descend from exactly this line.
- **Health, not transcendence.** The Zhuangzi's phrase is "all for long life." Daoyin is the health-cultivation cousin of the seated practices, not a rival to [zuowang](/concepts/zuowang/).

## The honest boundary

- The classical sources *describe* daoyin; the specific sequences practiced today are reconstructions and lineages, not transcriptions of the Zhuangzi.
- The modern term **qigong** (1950s) absorbed daoyin into a larger modern system — the [qigong page](/practices/qigong/) states that relationship.

 The term layer for this practice lives in the [daoyin glossary entry](/glossary/daoyin/).
