**Direct answer:** *Shouyi* (守一, "guarding the One") is the holding-pole practice of the Daoist sitting tradition: keeping attention steadied on unity — carried from the Daodejing's "carrying the soul, embracing the One" (ch. 10) into a concrete method by the [Taiping Jing](/texts/taiping-jing/) and ranked supreme by the [Baopuzi](/texts/baopuzi/)'s economy argument. It is the classical *addition* pole of training — one thing held — and the tradition's standard beginner anchor, in contrast to [zuowang](/practices/zuowang/)'s subtraction. The [comparison page](/comparisons/zuowang-vs-shouyi/) maps the two poles; this page gives shouyi's method and conditions.

## The method, from the sources

- **The root saying.** "Carrying the soul, embracing the One — can you keep them from parting?" (DDJ ch. 10). The question form is the practice: the work is *keeping* soul and One together, against the daily tendency of essence-qi-spirit to scatter.
- **The Taiping Jing's protocol.** The Han scripture makes it operational: "guarding the One and contemplating the spirits" — a quiet room with images of the five viscera displayed, sitting in contemplation with fasting and incense, letting the seasonal qi enter and the "five viscera spirits" take form. The One here has a *body-map*: the unity being guarded is the unity of the body's own centers (the [jing-qi-shen](/glossary/jing-qi-shen/) triad, the [dantian](/glossary/dantian/) system's ancestor).
- **The Baopuzi's economy.** Ge Hong's ranking gives the method its classical prestige: among "thousands" of visualization methods, "knowing the One completes the ten thousand affairs" — one held thing doing the work of the whole library. This is the passage that licenses the modern minimal-practice reading: guard one anchor, and the rest follows.
- **The paired discipline.** The Baopuzi's shouyi has *numbered versions* (guarding the One in one, two, three... senses) and a strict condition: consistency. The One guarded occasionally is not guarded — the tradition's version of "daily practice beats heroic practice," which the [sit-length question](/experiences/questions/how-long-should-i-sit/) restates.

## What guarding actually guards against

- **Scattering.** The classical diagnosis: the spirit leaks outward through the senses (the seven holes of the [Hundun story](/stories/hundun/)); guarding is the counter-motion, gathering attention back to the center. The "parting" of DDJ ch. 10 is the failure mode the whole method exists to prevent.
- **The contrast with forcing.** Guarding is not clenching; the same manuals that prescribe it prescribe *gentle* holding — the breath section's rule (harmonize, never force) applies to the attention exactly as to the [breath](/glossary/tiaoxi/): a steadied gaze, not a grip. The [restlessness problem page](/problems/restlessness-in-sitting/) documents what happens when the guard becomes a fight.

## Modern boundary, stated plainly

The practice is documented; its effects are traditional claims (the Taiping Jing's "returning the spirit," the Baopuzi's protective promises), not measured findings. No dedicated modern research exists on shouyi; the nearest evidence (meditation programs' general stress effects, Goyal 2014 [here](/research/mindfulness-meta-analysis-2014/)) is adjacent, not confirmatory, and this page keeps that boundary explicit.

**Uncertainty preserved:** the chamber details come from a fragmentary Han text; the internal visualization descriptions are traditional doctrine, not anatomy; the page reports the method as the sources state it, conditions and all.
