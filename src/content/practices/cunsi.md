**Direct answer:** *Cunsi* (存思, "holding images in mind") is the imagistic family of Daoist meditation — deliberately visualizing the body's inner geography, its palaces, colors, and resident spirits, as laid out in the [Huangting Jing](/texts/huangting-jing/). It is the *opposite pole* from the emptying traditions this site centers ([zuowang](/practices/zuowang/), [xinzhai](/concepts/xinzhai/)): where zuowang empties the inner field of images, cunsi fills it with them, precisely and systematically. The two poles coexisted for the entire history of the tradition — and practitioners often held both in one program, cunsi for building the map, stillness for letting the map go.

## The method, as the sources describe it

- **The Baopuzi's summary** (4th c.): "The various scriptures of the Way contain thousands of methods of holding-and-visualizing (思存念作) for dispelling harm and guarding the body" — an explicit statement that visualization was already a *crowded* field, thousands of variants, by Ge Hong's time. The same passage makes the comparison this site preserves: "if you know the way of guarding the One, all of these can be set aside — knowing the One completes the ten thousand affairs."
- **The Huangting Jing's system** (3rd–4th c.): the body read as a landscape — the Yellow Court at the center, three cinnabar fields (see [dantian](/glossary/dantian/)), residences for spirits, colors and directions keyed to the five phases. The practice: *see* the inner court, hold the image, let it stabilize.
- **The Shangqing elaboration** (4th c. onward): cunsi becomes the school's central method — visualization of spirits descending from the stars into the body, the practitioner's identity merging with the image.

## What cunsi is for, in traditional terms

- **Building the map.** Before the dantian can be "returned to," it must be *built* — given form, color, and name. Cunsi is the construction phase of the inner geography.
- **Protection and transformation.** The Baopuzi's purpose is explicitly protective ("dispelling harm and guarding the body"); the Shangqing purpose is transformative (the visualized spirit merges with the practitioner).
- **The contrast with stillness.** Zuowang's account of forgetting the body presupposes a body worth forgetting — an organized inner self. Cunsi organizes it. The tradition's own geometry: cunsi constructs the inner world; zuowang dissolves it; both belong to one path.

## Modern boundary, stated plainly

Cunsi's images are **traditional doctrine** — contemplative geography, not anatomy. No modern research establishes the dantian or the inner palaces as physiological findings, and this page claims none. Practitioners who use cunsi today use it as the tradition transmitted it: as structured imagination with traditional aims. The evidence for its effects is traditional and anecdotal; the site's [research pages](/research/farias-adverse-events-2020/) cover the modern evidence base, which does not include cunsi-specific studies.

**Uncertainty preserved:** the sources describe methods in coded, partial language (the Baopuzi repeatedly says the essentials are oral and secret); this page describes the family of practices as the texts state it, not a complete working protocol.
