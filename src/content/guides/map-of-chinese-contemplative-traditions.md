**Direct answer:** "Chinese contemplation" is four families under one roof, and they borrowed from each other for two thousand years. The four: **Daoist** (union with the Dao — zuowang, xinzhai, shouyi, cunsi, neiguan), **Buddhist** (seeing through self-clinging — Tiantai stages, Chan sitting), **Confucian** (quiet sitting as the servant of study and conduct — jingzuo), and the **health-cultivation arts** (breath and body work — tiaoxi, taixi, daoyin). The borrowings are so deep that clean boundaries are editorial convenience, not history.

## The three borrowings that shaped everything

1. **Tang Daoism borrowed Buddhist stages.** The Zuowang Lun's ladder reads Buddhist, but its summit is merger with the Dao — same scaffolding, different building. See the [Buddhist-vs-Daoist comparison](/comparisons/daoist-vs-buddhist-meditation/).
2. **Song Confucians borrowed Buddhist sitting and redefined it.** Jingzuo keeps the posture, drops the doctrine — [jingzuo vs zazen](/comparisons/jingzuo-vs-zazen/).
3. **The breath manual this site cites most** (Xiao Zhiguan) is Buddhist, which is why its breath analysis is labeled by affiliation wherever it appears.
