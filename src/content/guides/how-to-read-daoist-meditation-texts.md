**Direct answer:** Daoist meditation texts come in three kinds, and the first reading skill is telling them apart: **parables** (the Zhuangzi — descriptions of states, not instructions), **manuals** (Baopuzi, Zuowang lun, Neiguan jing — instructions with conditions attached), and **commentaries** (the tradition reading itself — interpretation, not source). Most errors in the popular literature come from reading a parable as a manual, or a commentary as a source.

## The three kinds of text

| Kind | Examples | What it can tell you | What it cannot |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parable | Zhuangzi chs. 4, 6 | What states the tradition valued; its vocabulary | Step-by-step technique (none is given) |
| Manual | Baopuzi; Zuowang lun; Neiguan jing | The tradition's actual instructions and conditions | What "the ancient masters did" — manuals are late, layered texts |
| Commentary | Guo Xiang; Cheng Xuanying; Wang Bi | How the tradition interpreted itself | The "original meaning" (that is exactly what is in dispute) |

## The dating and attribution risks

- **The Zhuangzi is a layered anthology** (4th–2nd c. BCE), attributed to a probably-historical Zhuang Zhou; chapter 6's sitting-and-forgetting is one voice in a conversation, not the manual of one author.
- **The Daodejing's chapters** exist in several recensions (received, Mawangdui, Guodian) with real differences — see the [Guodian](/timeline/guodian-daodejing/) timeline entry.
- **Practice manuals accrete.** The Baopuzi and the Zuowang lun contain earlier material, and the tradition itself attributes technique to sages who left no manuals.

## The reading rules this site follows

1. **A parable is a description, not a prescription.** When this site says zuowang "forgets the body," that is what the text *describes* — not a technique it teaches.
2. **Conditions travel with instructions.** Baopuzi's breath work comes with diet rules; dropping the rules and keeping the breath is how summaries go wrong.
3. **Commentaries are evidence about the tradition, not about the text's "original" meaning.**
4. **No modern research validates an ancient claim by association** — the site's research pages are bounded to what they studied.
