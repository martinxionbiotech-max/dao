**Direct answer:** Daoist meditation is not one practice but a family: zuowang (sitting-and-forgetting), xinzhai (fasting of the mind), shouyi (guarding the One), cunsi (visualizing inner bodies and deities), and neiguan (inner contemplation) — plus the breath work (tiaoxi, taixi) that threads through all of them. They differ in aim — merger with the Dao, receptivity, unity, longevity, clarity — and share a root vocabulary: emptiness, qi, forgetting, the One. This guide is the Daoist-specific entry point; the broader map of all Chinese traditions is at [Chinese meditation explained](/guides/chinese-meditation-explained/).

## The five practices at a glance

| Practice | Period | Source | Stated aim |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Xinzhai](/concepts/xinzhai/) 心斋 | 4th c. BCE described | Zhuangzi 4 | empty the mind so the Dao gathers |
| [Zuowang](/concepts/zuowang/) 坐忘 | 4th c. BCE described; 8th c. systematized | Zhuangzi 6; Zuowang lun | merge with the Great Thoroughfare |
| [Shouyi](/concepts/shouyi/) 守一 | Han–Six Dynasties | Daodejing 10; Taiping Jing | keep jing-qi-shen united |
| [Cunsi](/practices/cunsi/) 存思 | Han–Tang | Shangqing/other lineages | visualize inner bodies and deities |
| [Neiguan](/practices/neiguan/) 内观 | Tang | Neiguan jing | contemplate within, clarify the mind |

## What the sources actually are

1. **Philosophical parables** (Zhuangzi 4, 6) describe states; they are not manuals. Reading step-by-step technique into them is an interpretive move, not a reading.
2. **Practice manuals** (Baopuzi, Zuowang lun, Neiguan jing, Xiao Zhiguan) are the closest thing to instructions — and they carry conditions (diet, patience, teacher lineage) that modern summaries drop.
3. **Lineage transmission** is the tradition's own answer to "how do I learn this" — see [do I need a teacher?](/experiences/questions/do-i-need-a-teacher/).

## The evidence situation, stated plainly

- **No modern clinical research exists on Daoist sitting specifically.** The site's [research](/research/farias-adverse-events-2020/) pages are about meditation generally — useful boundaries, not Daoist evidence.
- **The archive material** (practitioner accounts) is one-person reporting, kept with its limitations stated on every archive page.
- **What the tradition offers is a long textual and lineage record** — which is evidence for what the tradition *taught*, not proof of what it *delivers*.

## Honest expectations

- Early sitting is usually [restless](/problems/restlessness-in-sitting/), [sleepy](/problems/drowsiness-in-sitting/), or [noisy with thoughts](/problems/thoughts-become-stronger/) — the sources name these as the starting condition, not failure.
- The tradition's promised changes are described in its own vocabulary (the One, emptiness, the fine breath) — see [what counts as progress](/experiences/questions/what-counts-as-progress/).
- **Nothing in the tradition requires belief** — see [does practice need faith?](/experiences/questions/does-practice-need-faith/).
