**Direct answer:** The Chinese contemplative vocabulary is a small set of terms that keep reappearing, and every one of them means something narrower than its English translation suggests. This page gives the one-line meaning of each, with the page that holds the full treatment. The standing caveat: these are not dictionary entries but terms embedded in cosmologies — the one-liners below are orientation, not definition.

## The root vocabulary

| Term | Reading | One-line meaning | Full page |
|---|---|---|---|
| 道 dao | dào | the Way — the pattern of all things, not a creator god | [concept](/concepts/dao/) |
| 德 de | dé | the power a thing receives and carries — the Way as it runs through a particular thing | [concept](/concepts/de/) |
| 气 qi | qì | breath-air-vitality — the felt medium of body and world, not a measurable gas | [concept](/concepts/qi/) |
| 精 jing | jīng | essence — the concentrated basis of life that qi and spirit depend on | [concept](/concepts/jing/) |
| 神 shen | shén | spirit — the luminous presence, most refined of the three | [glossary](/glossary/shen/) |
| 无为 wuwei | wúwéi | acting without forcing — the skill of not pushing, not a policy of doing nothing | [concept](/concepts/wuwei/) |

## The practice terms

| Term | Reading | One-line meaning | Full page |
|---|---|---|---|
| 坐忘 zuowang | zuòwàng | sitting in oblivion — dropping body and mind to merge with the Great Thoroughfare | [concept](/concepts/zuowang/) |
| 心斋 xinzhai | xīnzhāi | fasting of the mind — letting the mind fast of its own busyness until it can listen with qi | [concept](/concepts/xinzhai/) |
| 守一 shouyi | shǒuyī | guarding the One — holding body, breath, and spirit undivided | [concept](/concepts/shouyi/) |
| 静坐 jingzuo | jìngzuò | quiet sitting — the Song–Ming practice of collecting the mind for study and conduct | [concept](/concepts/jingzuo/) |
| 调息 tiaoxi | tiáoxī | regulating the breath — making the breath even and fine, the Xiao Zhiguan's fourfold analysis | [practice](/practices/tiaoxi/) |
| 胎息 taixi | tāixī | embryo breathing — the fine, "as if absent" breath of deep stillness | [practice](/practices/taixi/) |
| 内观 neiguan | nèiguān | inner observation — watching the body-mind itself, not holding images | [practice](/practices/neiguan/) |
| 存思 cunsi | cúnsī | visualization — holding inner images of deities, landscapes, or stars | [practice](/practices/cunsi/) |

## The distinctions that matter most

1. **Zuowang is not mindfulness.** The comparison [zuowang vs mindfulness](/comparisons/zuowang-vs-mindfulness/) holds the whole distance: forgetting versus noticing, dropping versus observing.
2. **Neiguan is not cunsi.** Observation of what is there versus visualization of what is not — the [what is neiguan](/guides/what-is-neiguan/) guide draws the line.
3. **Wuwei is not passivity.** The [wuwei vs non-action](/comparisons/wuwei-vs-non-action/) comparison unpacks what "not forcing" licenses and what it does not.

## The honest limits

- Every one-liner above is a compression; the full pages carry the sources, the disagreements between commentators, and the uncertainty. When a claim depends on a term, read the term's page.
- Romanization here follows the site's convention (pinyin without tone marks); historical texts use no romanization at all, and translations vary.
- The [map of Chinese contemplative traditions](/guides/map-of-chinese-contemplative-traditions/) places these terms inside their traditions; this page only names them.
