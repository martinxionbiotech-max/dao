**Direct answer:** Shouyi (守一) means "guarding the One" — the practice of holding the person *together*: body, breath, and spirit kept undivided. The term comes from the Daodejing's question, "Can you keep the soul embracing the One without separation?" and grew into one of the main practices of early Daoism, taught in the Taiping Jing and Baopuzi as the road to health, clarity, and long life.

## Where the term comes from

- **Daodejing ch. 10:** "Can you keep the soul embracing the One without separation?" — a question, not an instruction, and the seed the whole practice grows from.
- **Taiping Jing (Han):** shouyi becomes a full practice — keeping the One means keeping jing (essence), qi (breath), and shen (spirit) from scattering.
- **Baopuzi (4th c.):** Ge Hong ranks shouyi among the most important methods of the inner chapters.

## What guarding means in practice

- **The object is unity, not an image.** Unlike [cunsi](/practices/cunsi/) (visualization), shouyi does not picture a deity or a landscape — it holds the *undivided state* itself.
- **The work is settling.** Attention stops jumping; the person's pieces stop scattering; what remains is the One. The ordinary first step is the same as everywhere on this site: sit, settle, and stop chasing.
- **The failure mode is separation** — the Daodejing's own word (离): the One dividing into scattered thoughts, scattered breaths, a scattered person.

## The honest limits

- The earliest sources describe the *state* more than the technique; the practice details grew in the Han manuals, and modern reconstructions are editorial, not ancient.
