**Direct answer:** Neiguan (内观) means "inner observation" — turning attention back from outer things to the body-mind itself, and watching what is there. The term's scriptural anchor is the Neiguan Jing's single instruction, "observe within one's own body, to clarify the mind" (内观己身，澄其心也), and its practice rule: stillness of spirit, settled mind, scattered thoughts not arising.

## Where the term comes from

- **Neiguan Jing (Sui–Tang dating):** the method in one sentence — inner observation of one's own body with the purpose of clarifying the mind; the object of observation is the person's own state, not an image.
- **Dingguan Jing commentary:** "the mind of wisdom shining inward is called neiguan" (慧心内照名曰内观) — observation as an inward light, not an outward gaze.
- **Tang identification:** the Daoshu records Zhongli Quan's identification of neiguan with [zuowang](/concepts/zuowang/) — the Tang tradition read the two as one family: observation emptying into oblivion.

## What inner observation means in practice

- **The object is what is already there.** Unlike [cunsi](/practices/cunsi/) (visualization — holding images of deities or landscapes), neiguan does not picture anything. It observes the body-mind as it is: breath, sensations, the coming and going of thoughts.
- **The work is watching without adding.** The Neiguan Jing's rule names the failure mode: scattered thoughts arise, deluded images intrude (乱想不起，邪妄不侵) — the practice is to keep the inward light steady without chasing what it sees.
- **The tone is clarifying, not achieving.** The scripture's purpose verb is 澄 — to clarify, to let settle. Observation is how the water clears.

## The honest limits

- The scripture gives the principle and the state; it does not give a minute-by-minute modern protocol. Reconstructions that add detailed steps are editorial, not ancient.
